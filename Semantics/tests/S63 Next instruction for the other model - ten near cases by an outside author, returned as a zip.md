## The ten cases

**O15 - Sam's inherited marks** (E, row 57: "every required starting representation"; "complete grounding of representation"). Sam starts from two diagrams. Every line on both has a recorded preparation: who drew it, from which trial, on which day. One symbol appears on both, a small arrow meaning "the blank catches here", and nobody recorded who first drew it or from what. Sam cuts his new blank by following those arrows. *Thoughtful person:* Sam's account of his own work is complete. Where the arrows came from is a hole in the history of the diagrams, and it is a hole in his account, because his cut followed them.

**O16 - The worn key and the diary** (E, row 52: "no binding can acquire a provenance solely because its own later representation presupposes that provenance"). Sam's present diagram matches a worn trial key found in his drawer. To date the trial key, Sam wrote a diary last week, working the dates out from the stages visible in the diagram. *Thoughtful person:* The worn key is evidence on its own. The diary is the diagram talking about itself. Sam has one independent witness, and the diary is a second copy of the first.

**O17 - The robot's log and the maker's manual** (I, row 94: "Do not define that ownership by the very Can claim whose owned realization it is meant to ground"). A repair robot keeps a log of every comparison it runs. The log shows which routine, running inside the robot, produced today's diagnosis. The maker's manual says the routine is the robot's own "because the robot can diagnose". *Thoughtful person:* Ownership here rests on the log, which shows the routine running inside the robot. Strike the manual's sentence and nothing is lost; keep only the manual's sentence and there is nothing.

**O18 - The one tooth** (E, row 58: "Relay alone supplies neither Build nor Origin"). A learner receives a complete key diagram from Sam. At a new lock she finds a catch the diagram never covered, files one tooth to fit it, and keeps every other tooth as the diagram gave it. *Thoughtful person:* The one tooth is hers. The rest is relay. She built something, small, and it is exactly the tooth.

**O19 - The diagram in his hand** (E, row 51: "Earlier represented contents may constrain later construction"; "earlier occurrences and a stated starting stock"). Sam holds his old spacing diagram open while cutting a new blank. Every measurement he uses, he has just taken from the lock with his own gauge. The diagram's spacings agree with them. *Thoughtful person:* The diagram was in his hand and did no work; the lock did it. Had the diagram said something different, he would have followed the lock.

**O20 - Two valves in one second** (H, row 90: "genuinely entangled episodes"; "a separate historical argument"). Ana and Ben each close a different valve within the same second. The leak stops. The records are complete: either valve alone would have stopped it. Each claims the repair. *Thoughtful person:* Both did something that would have stopped it. Neither can claim it alone. The record shows two sufficient acts and no way to say whose water stopped.

**O21 - The expert's question** (I, row 96: "supplies or executes that very capacity outside the stated continuing system"; "relative to the capacity being attributed"). A remote expert sends the robot two candidate diagnoses and the one test that would separate them. The robot runs the test on its own evidence and picks the right one. *Thoughtful person:* The robot chose, with its own evidence. The expert did the part that took understanding, knowing what would separate the two. The choosing is the robot's; the diagnosis as an achievement is mostly the expert's.

**O22 - Two knobs, one linkage** (G, row 69: "unless that was independently required by the question"; "coherently ask about changing two things together"). Lea asks what happens when two knobs are turned together. On this machine the linkage lets one knob lead the other by a fraction of a second. *Thoughtful person:* Her question is about the pair. The brief lead is part of what "together" means on this machine, and it is a fact about the machine, not a second question about one knob.

**O23 - "The seal was tight"** (G, row 79: "inventing a new dimension of variation"). An investigator's earlier notes record knob settings and temperature, and mention in passing that "the seal was tight". Later she measures seal compression as a variable and finds it matters. *Thoughtful person:* She added a dimension. Mentioning tightness in passing did not make it something that could vary in her account; measuring it did.

**O24 - The unused joint setting** (J, row 107: "at least one solution-changing alternative at the unseen pair"). Two wiring arrangements agree on every tested setting and on almost every untested one. They differ at a single joint setting of two switches, one nobody has used. The setting is reachable by hand. *Thoughtful person:* They are two arrangements, and the record has not chosen between them. One reachable setting is enough.
