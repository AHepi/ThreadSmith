## The protected condition, stated first (H, row 83, beside row 82)

**O38 - Written on the job sheet.** Before the repair, the job sheet states the protected condition: the kitchen tap runs at all times during the work. During the repair the tap stops for four seconds while the mains is switched over; nobody draws from it. The plumber's report lists the interruption as incidental. *Thoughtful person:* The condition was written down first, and it was broken, for four seconds. Calling it incidental changes nothing. Whether four seconds mattered to anyone is a different question from whether the condition held.

## Two more attribution tests

**O39 - Two routes, both running** (H, row 90, for H64). A leak is stopped when a bypass opens and a valve closes in the same second. Either alone would have stopped all of the flow. Both operated fully. The meter shows the flow dropping to zero once and cannot tell which route took it. *Thoughtful person:* Both operated and both were sufficient. The record cannot divide it and does not need to: the leak was stopped by both, and neither can claim it alone.

**O40 - Two hands on the test** (I, row 96, for I64). The expert sends the robot two candidate diagnoses and the test that separates them. The result fits neither. The robot notices that the test assumed a sensor was reading true. The expert, watching, proposes swapping to the second sensor. The robot does it, runs the test again, and finds a third fault. *Thoughtful person:* The reinterpretation was theirs together: the robot saw the assumption, the expert saw the fix. Neither can say "mostly".

## The next nine rows

**O41 - The technician's whisper** (I, row 95: "Delegated processes count only under a stated system boundary that includes them"). The assessment is stated at the start as robot-only. Midway, a technician tells the robot which gauge to read. The robot reads it and completes the diagnosis. The log records the technician's instruction. *Thoughtful person:* The boundary was robot-only, and the instruction crossed it. The diagnosis is a team result under a boundary nobody restated. Robot-only credit is not available for it.

**O42 - New board, old memory** (I, row 91: "the system's continuity and resource boundaries"; "which processes it can retain and execute under them"). The robot's controller board fails mid-task. A technician fits a new board and copies the memory and routines across. The task finishes. At the start, continuity of memory and routines had been stated as what makes it the same robot. *Thoughtful person:* The same robot finished, on the stated terms. The swap repaired the system; it did not substitute another one.

**O43 - Mirrored marks** (E, row 54: "an actual content-preserving transfer"; "preserve the relevant content"). Sam's diagram is translated into raised marks by a reader who writes right to left. Every pairing of tooth to obstruction is preserved, and the reader's convention is stated on the sheet. *Thoughtful person:* The transfer preserved the content. The mirror is a convention, declared, and anyone reading with it recovers every pairing.

**O44 - Half from the source, half from the shelf** (E, row 55: "depend on the source occurrence"; "coincidentally identical marks"). The copier reproduces the diagram from the source. One damaged section it fills in from a stored earlier version, which happens to match the source exactly there. *Thoughtful person:* Most of the copy depends on the source. The patched section does not, and its matching is luck. The copy is part copy and part coincidence, and the record says which part is which.

**O45 - The spring nobody mentioned** (F, row 60: "another already present route"; "genuinely redundant routes"). The account of the door's return contains two springs, both connected, both sufficient. The second is never described as doing anything until the first is deleted from the account; then the author says the second was always there. *Thoughtful person:* The second spring was in the account from the start, connected and sufficient. It was already a route. That nobody said it did work does not make it a later addition.

**O46 - The cable that used to be a spring** (F, row 61: "a separate candidate, with its own assessment"; "supports of a fixed candidate"; "existentially rematched candidate organizations"). After the spring is deleted, the author reassigns the surviving component to a cable and shows the door still returns, then reports that the original spring account survived the deletion. *Thoughtful person:* What survived is a new account with a cable in it. The spring account did not survive; it was replaced, and the replacement's success is its own.

**O47 - One more commitment** (J, row 105: "finite, monotone assumptions"). A finite collection of commitments supports the conclusion, and every earlier addition to it kept the support. The newest addition, that the room was locked, conflicts with a premise every route uses. *Thoughtful person:* The conclusion no longer follows. The collection stayed finite. What broke is that additions could not always be made without loss, and this one could not.

**O48 - The forbidden wire** (J, row 108: "admitted-class and realizability qualifications"). The only arrangement that gives a different response at the unseen setting needs a wire between two terminals, and every device in the stated population is built without that wire. *Thoughtful person:* Then there is no alternative in that population. The different response is imaginable and is not available. The premise is not met.

**O49 - Owned means capable** (J, row 110: "a proposed semantic definition can be meaningful"; "a proved reduction to the advertised primitives"). A definition is offered: a routine is the robot's own when the robot can run it, and the robot can run it when it is its own. Beside it are two histories, a routine learned and an answer relayed, told apart by what happened in each. *Thoughtful person:* The definition goes in a circle and gives nothing. The two histories give the distinction its meaning. The definition adds nothing to them and could be dropped.
