## The three cases

**O50 - The same afternoon, three achievements** (I, row 96, for I64). The expert sends two candidate diagnoses and the separating test. The result fits neither. The robot notices the sensor assumption; the expert proposes the swap; the robot does it and finds the third fault. Three things were achieved that afternoon: the reinterpretation of the test, the discovery of the fault, and the completed inquiry from first question to answer. *Thoughtful person:* Credit each achievement on its own. The reinterpretation was theirs together. The discovery was the robot's: it ran the test and found the fault. The inquiry was mostly the expert's: the expert framed it, supplied the candidates and the test, and supplied the fix. One word for all three would be wrong for at least two of them.

**O51 - The module in the casing** (I, row 93: "spatial location inside its casing alone is insufficient"). A controller runs a diagnostic module bought from a supplier and fitted on a removable processor inside its own shell. The system boundary declared at the start names the controller and its routines and says nothing about the module. The module produces today's diagnosis. *Thoughtful person:* The diagnosis was produced inside the shell and outside the declared boundary. Where the boundary was drawn decides it, and the boundary was drawn without the module. Being inside the casing changes nothing.

**O52 - The route that started and was stopped** (H, row 90, for H64). Two routes to stopping the leak start in the same moment: Ana's bypass begins to open, Ben's valve begins to close. Ana's bypass jams a quarter open and takes nothing; Ben's valve closes and the flow stops. The operating log records both starts, the jam, and the closure, before any meter is read. *Thoughtful person:* Ben stopped it. Ana's route started and did no stopping. The log says so without the meter.
