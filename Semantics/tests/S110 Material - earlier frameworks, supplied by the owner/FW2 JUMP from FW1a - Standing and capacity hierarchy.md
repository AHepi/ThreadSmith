## A recursive semantic theory of explanatory knowledge and universal explanatory capacity, with a stratified class hierarchy and realization constraints

### Purpose and standing

The subject of this theory is how a system can create explanations, expose errors in them, and change the problems, standards, and methods through which it does that. Its target is **explanatory universality**: an organization whose explanatory reach is not permanently confined to a predetermined subject matter, vocabulary, repertoire of answers, or hierarchy of judges.

The kernel is recursive. Recognizing a problem can itself present a problem. Inventing a conjecture, understanding a criticism, interpreting an observation, choosing between explanations, or deciding whether a standard is appropriate can each require the same process of conjecture and criticism. The process can return to any of those activities without installing a final authority above them. This is the substantive structure described in *The Fabric of Reality*, chapter 3, particularly printed pages 67 to 68. [F3]

This is a semantic theory, not an execution procedure. No algorithm for generating explanations, computing verdicts, recognizing every error, or completing an inquiry is required. Whether a particular physical realization is computable is neither a premise nor a conclusion. Recursive self-application must not be reduced to a computational fixed point, and it does not, merely by being recursive, establish a mathematical uncomputability result.

**Standing.** This document is a candidate successor to *FW1a* (SHA-256 `823f508867d779cbea9b8f878d876dab94d635b97bdb17512942a2ac9619e41e`). It is canonical for any specification that explicitly adopts FW2. FW1a remains the authority for specifications that adopt it; supersession is by adoption, not by fiat. # Part I-A. The explanatory core

## Reality is not conferred by a judgment (K-REAL)

An explanation attempts to say how something is, works, or follows from a structure, and why the relevant phenomenon or relation has the character it does. Its content is not exhausted by the observations it fits, the responses it produces, or the approval it receives. A represented explanation, an agent's assessment of it, and what it actually explains are different things. This distinction applies to an assessment of the present theory as much as to any other. [F1; B1]

Errors can be real when nobody has identified them. An objection can be mistaken when nobody has answered it. No authority, procedure, record, tradition, or successful history turns a fallible judgment into a guarantee. Fallibility does not imply that all explanations are equally good or that knowledge is impossible; it means that explanatory improvement is not final certification. [F3; B1]

## A problem is an interpreted difficulty that matters (K-PROBLEM)

An active problem arises when a system recognizes a tension, inadequacy, or unresolved relation among ideas and directs attention to it as worth addressing. The difficulty may be inexplicit. It need not be a contradiction, an anomaly, or an emergency; a wish to understand more deeply can be enough. Recognition and values affect which difficulty receives attention; neither makes the recognized difficulty automatically genuine. [F3; B1]

The world does not supply a ready-made partition into the problems a thinker must solve. Background explanations, recognition, purposes, and standards contribute to that partition, and each can subsequently become problematic. Attention therefore belongs inside the theory rather than being an unexplained selector outside it. A frame in which facts are arranged is such a background: the same facts arranged differently yield different difficulties and different conclusions, and the choice of arrangement carries content and is itself criticizable. (FW2: the last sentence, from A1.5 and C2.5.)

## Conjecture precedes criticism of that conjecture (K-CONJECTURE)

A conjecture supplies a proposed content, interpretation, or organization to criticize. It need not be justified before it is entertained. Observation may occasion its invention and existing knowledge may contribute to it, but neither repetition nor deduction from observational instances supplies the missing explanatory content by itself. [F3; B1]

The order is a dependence of roles: criticism has something it criticizes. It is not a requirement that every conjecture be written out before a difficulty can be noticed. An inexplicit expectation can be the prior conjecture. A criticism is also conjectural content; once proposed, it too can be criticized. "Conjecture before criticism" does not appoint an uncriticizable first conjecture.

## Criticism has a reason-bearing role (K-CRITICISM)

Criticism proposes that some content, inference, application, standard, or practice has a defect relevant to a problem. A reason-sensitive response depends on what the objection says and on how the alleged defect bears on the problem. Negative feedback, punishment, disagreement, failure, or rejection is not criticism merely because it changes behavior. Such events can become evidentially significant through an interpretation that connects them to a conjecture and an alleged error. [F3; B7]

A response may improve an explanation, reject it, retain it against an objection, investigate further, reinterpret evidence, alter a standard, reframe the problem, or suspend judgment. It can also be mistaken. The semantic role of a critical response and its epistemic success must remain separate. (FW2: three mechanisms that resemble criticism and are not it are fixed in Part III: a prediction-error signal that repairs a model, suppression of a candidate by an attested competitor, and settlement among partial models. Each changes behavior; none represents a defect with grounds and bearing.)

## The same kernel applies to its own working parts (K-RECURSION)

Anything doing explanatory or adjudicative work is eligible to become the subject of further conjecture and criticism: a problem formulation, a criticism, the interpretation of a reason, an inferential connection, an observation model, a comparison, a novelty attribution, a system boundary, a stopping decision, an evidential practice, and the methods being used to assess them. [F3]

Recursive scrutiny must be able to matter. A criticism of a standard can change how arguments using that standard are assessed; a criticism of an interpretation can change what an earlier utterance is taken to have said; a criticism of an attribution boundary can change who receives credit. Merely allowing comments about a method while permanently insulating its consequences would not satisfy this commitment.

Recursion does not require an infinite chain of prior authorizations. A system can reason provisionally with its present resources while examining a particular part of them, suspend the examination, act under a qualified judgment, or discover a better starting point. No requirement to solve every subsidiary problem first is imposed. On the realization side the same point is forced: no organization consults rules all the way down, so at some point a rule is executed rather than consulted, and provisional reasoning is both semantically legitimate and physically necessary. (FW2: the last sentence, from C1.5.)

## Generation confers no standing (K-STANDING)

(FW2, new.) A content's being generated by a system, whether by conjecture, by a lawful rule, or by any other route, confers no standing on it: it is not thereby available as a premise, an endorsed account, or an actual form. Standing is conferred by an appraisal in a specified respect, and the appraisal is itself content eligible for the kernel. Conversely, denial of standing is not deletion: a generated content may be retained as an occurrence available for criticism while having no standing in any application.

This commitment is stated separately because it is what makes conjecture safe and criticism possible. It makes conjecture safe because an organization that generates freely but grants nothing standing by generation can overgenerate without harm; this is how a learner reconstructs an open-ended target from positive evidence alone, where no datum can refute a superset conjecture. It makes criticism possible because a conjecture must be retained as a target while a rival is in use; an organization that either grants standing on generation or discards what it generates has no such target. Admission to a collection, use in an argument application, and truth are three things, and the kernel lives in the distinction. [P2; from C2.2 and A3.2]

## Creation has a provenance; understanding can be reconstructive (K-ORIGIN)

Attribution of creativity concerns the production or reconstruction of problem-specific explanatory organization, not the emission of an unfamiliar token. A prior explanation can contribute to a new creative act. A learner can reconstruct an explanation that exists elsewhere without having possessed it beforehand. A group can create through contributions that no isolated participant supplies in full. [F1; B7; B16]

Newness, authorship, understanding, correctness, and historical priority are distinct. A communicated explanation is not automatically a disqualification, and absence of a communicated complete answer is not automatically proof of authorship. Most originative acts are small at the relevant grain; an act need not be large to be one. (FW2: the last sentence, from C1.5.)

## Knowledge growth is explanatory improvement, not persistence (K-PROGRESS)

Successful explanatory knowledge creation requires an actual improvement in the relevant problem situation through explanatory work and error correction. A new conjecture need not achieve it; a critical response need not; retention alone does not. Improvement need not consist in a replacement theory: exposing a defective question, correcting a standard, or establishing a principled limitation can improve the situation. [F3]

The resulting knowledge remains fallible and can generate new problems. Improvement is assessed with its scope and dependencies visible. Changing the standard to declare victory does not establish improvement; neither does requiring every old commitment to survive. Improvement has no global direction and no terminus: a later theory may replace an earlier one by transport rather than incorporation, and no stage is one at which everything is known. (FW2: the last sentence, from A1.6.)

## Universality is a capacity claim, not a collection of successful episodes (K-UNIVERSALITY)

The target universal class contains systems capable, under suitable non-question-begging enabling conditions, of understanding explanations and conducting creative, critical inquiry across explanatory subject matters without a fixed parochial barrier. The capacity includes extending the represented vocabulary, revising problem formulations, revising the constraints on what the system can conjecture, and improving the methods needed for unfamiliar domains. [B6; B7; B16] (FW2: "revising the constraints on what the system can conjecture," from C2.3 and E1.)

This is stronger than an ability to repeat criticism, than performance across a large finite menu, and than permission to discuss anything. It is different from already knowing everything, succeeding on every attempt, solving a physically impossible demand, possessing unlimited resources now, or surviving indefinitely. Its formal obligations appear in Part II and are constitutive of the universal class.

K-UNIVERSALITY is an explicit substantive target commitment, not derived from K-RECURSION. Whether a particular system possesses the capacity requires an explanatory account of its organization; a finite performance record does not settle the question, in either direction. A checklist of learning attributes, however general the learning, does not settle it either: a system can learn continuously, by movement, with many complementary models and general representational frames, and instantiate no explanatory activity. (FW2: the last sentence, from A1.1.)

## Physical embodiment constrains without replacing explanation (K-PHYSICALITY)

When this theory is applied to an actual system, its contents and activities must have a physically tenable realization. Physical embodiment does not make semantic organization dispensable. An account of particle trajectories and an account of why a theory was revised answer different questions, although they must be compatible. [F1]

Constructor theory contributes a language of possible and impossible transformations, information-bearing substrates, and retained capacities. It does not, just by calling a process a constructor, explain the origin of its knowledge or the meaning of its representations. Physical knowledge that contributes to its own preservation and explanatory knowledge that improves understanding are related but nonidentical. [M5; M7] Part II states what any realization of the recursive-capacity class must be able to do, without naming a substrate. (FW2: the last sentence.)

# Part I-B. Why the independent commitments are needed

The following explain why these commitments are adopted and which distinctions would be lost without them. They are not claims of ultimate justification, nor proofs that no alternative formalism could use a different primitive vocabulary. A commitment is independent when it is not supplied by the other displayed definitions and must be stated rather than smuggled into a theorem.

**Explanatory realism cannot be supplied by a record of endorsements.** Two communities can endorse the same sentence for different reasons, or endorse incompatible sentences. Recording endorsements determines what they accepted, not which account explains the phenomenon. Omitting K-REAL permits a model in which "good explanation" means "accepted by the selected judge." Realism provides the distinction between being judged adequate and being adequate; it provides no infallible test. [F1; B1]

**Problem recognition cannot be supplied by an external task label.** A thermostat, a student, and an investigator can be exposed to the same reading. Calling the input a "problem" does not establish that each recognizes a difficulty. K-PROBLEM requires an interpretation of the role the discrepancy plays for the system, and values explain why attention goes to this difficulty rather than others. [F3]

**Conjectural origin is not a consequence of causal dependence on data.** A system may causally depend on observations without extracting an explanation from them. K-CONJECTURE prevents a causal input relation from becoming an undeclared rule of epistemic instruction, leaving room for observation, deduction, analogy, imagination, and inherited knowledge to participate without any of them being an automatic source of warrant. [F3; B1]

**Criticism is not supplied by variation and selection alone.** Differential persistence can occur without a represented objection, and a person can understand an objection and decide, for reasons, not to change. K-CRITICISM adds a content-sensitive relation not recoverable by counting revisions or outputs. A declaration that a signal was a reason is insufficient. [F3; B7]

**Recursion is stronger than maintaining an attackable archive.** An archive can contain criticism of a standard while the institution continues to apply it unconditionally. K-RECURSION requires a possible return from scrutiny to the operative use of the target and blocks a fixed hierarchy in which the final judge's judgments can never acquire a defect. [F3]

**Standing is not supplied by conjecture or by criticism.** (FW2, new.) K-CONJECTURE says criticism has something to criticize; it does not say that what is generated lacks standing. K-CRITICISM says what a criticism is; it does not say that its target must be retained. A theory with both and without K-STANDING permits two organizations it should distinguish: one that grants standing on generation, so that every conjecture is an endorsement and overgeneration is error; and one that discards its conjectures on use, so that nothing it generates is ever a target. The first cannot learn safely; the second cannot criticize itself. Both are excluded by the one commitment. [P2]

**Provenance is not supplied by output quality.** An answer can be accurate because its organization was created by the system, supplied by a collaborator, or retrieved from a store. K-ORIGIN locates the contribution rather than inferring it from surface quality or excluding it from a transmission history. [B7; B16]

**Improvement is not supplied by either change or retention.** Changing a theory may make it worse; retaining one may preserve an error; removing a local defect can damage the explanation elsewhere. K-PROGRESS adds the relation between the contribution and the whole relevant problem situation. [F3]

**Universality is not supplied by unrestricted self-reference.** A system might examine its standards indefinitely while confined to one kind of puzzle; another might name every discipline and learn none. K-UNIVERSALITY requires explanatory capacity across an independently specified domain, not reflective vocabulary. Nor is it supplied by general learning, however continuous or flexible, since learning a model of the world is not explaining it. [B6; B7] (FW2: last sentence.)

**Physical realization is not supplied by abstract consistency.** A coherent description of a capacity need not be realizable; reliable physical transformation does not establish semantic authorship. K-PHYSICALITY supplies the realization question and prevents either inference. [M5; M7]

# Part II. Semantic structure

## The model and the three standpoints

A model is an interpreted structure

\[
\mathcal M=\langle S,C,\mathcal H,\mathcal X,\mathrm{Rep},\mathrm{Prov},\mathrm{CF},\mathrm{Account},\mathrm{Bearing},\mathrm{Progress},\mathrm{Capacity},\Theta\rangle .
\]

\(S\) is a domain of systems and attributed contributors; \(C\) a domain of contents; \(\mathcal H\) a nonempty family of histories and admitted counterfactual histories; \(\mathcal X\) a domain of problem situations. The remaining entries interpret representation, provenance, counterfactual dependence, explanatory accounting, the bearing of reasons, improvement, and capacity. \(\Theta\) supplies physical assumptions when physical attribution is being made; it is not needed to turn every semantic comparison into a physical experiment.

The signature is an organizational vocabulary, not a derivation of its own interpretations. \(\mathrm{Account}\), \(\mathrm{Bearing}\), \(\mathrm{Progress}\), and \(\mathrm{Capacity}\) carry substantive content; calling one of them true is not a substitute for explaining why it holds.

Three standpoints remain distinct. **Model satisfaction**, \(\mathcal M,\xi\vDash\phi\), states what holds in a specified interpretation at situation \(\xi\). **Situated judgment**, \(\mathrm{Judges}(s,\phi,\xi)\), states what the system takes to hold. **Attribution evidence** is what an investigator uses to conjecture that the model and the situated judgment describe a target. None is identified with either of the others. An occurrence of a claim does not assert that claim as a premise of the metatheory; a model can describe an agent entertaining inconsistent proposals without asserting both. Ordinary mathematical consequence is available to the author of a theorem about the model; it is not an instruction that the modeled thinker execute a calculus. A check of a content's internal coherence, whether its own stated operands satisfy a relation it asserts, concerns the situated judgment; a check of its agreement with a designated source concerns something else; neither is model satisfaction. (FW2: the last sentence.)

## Contents, occurrences, and interpretations

A content can play the role of a problem, conjecture, explanation, observation claim, criticism, response, standard, method, inference, interpretation, value, or attribution. Roles overlap: a criticism is a conjecture about a defect; an observation claim contains assumptions; an account of a standard may be an explanation.

A content occurrence is a particular token, artifact, event-associated representation, or embodied state. Its identity is distinct from its interpreted content. Several occurrences may express one content; one occurrence may support several defensible interpretations; an interpreter may be mistaken.

An interpretation is indexed by a semantic grain \(\ell\), a context, and an attributed system. A relation \(x\equiv_\ell y\) may be used where genuine equivalence at that grain is intended. Similarity is not silently promoted to equivalence: a tolerance relation that is not transitive remains a similarity relation, and a similarity space supplies no grain. Categories recorded by similarity among observed features and categories derived from an account of the laws that produce the clusters are different kinds of category; the second can override the first, and a definition within a theory is not refuted by instances the theory idealizes away. Changing a notation can preserve explanatory organization; identical wording can conceal a changed problem, scope, or inferential role. (FW2: the two sentences on categories, from C1.5 and C2.5.)

An explicit sentence, a diagram, an action, and an inexplicit disposition can all carry content. No complete formal translation is required. The interpretation must nevertheless explain systematic use, discrimination, reconstruction, or correction by the system. A formal representation is a conjectural interpretation of the content; it does not erase the prose, artifact, or disposition it represents. Where the formal representation loses a distinction on which the explanation depends, it is inadequate in that respect. [B14; B16]

## Histories and causal order

Each history \(h\in\mathcal H\) contains occurrences with a causal precedence relation \(\prec_h\). A reference to an actual earlier occurrence requires that occurrence to exist in the relevant history; a conjecture about a future event refers to proposed content, not to an accomplished occurrence.

Temporal location and explanatory dependence must not be conflated. Earlier knowledge can be relevant to novelty even when its acquisition is not a direct premise of a later conjecture. A historical cut is a portion of a history selected for a question. A finite archive can describe an unbounded history; neither histories nor the semantic domains are required to be finite or completely represented in an observer's records. Possibilities that cannot jointly occur are represented in different histories; incompatible conjectures can coexist as contents of one actual inquiry. [F1]

## Problem situations and episodes

A problem situation is represented schematically as

\[
\xi=\langle P,B,V,K,A,J,L\rangle .
\]

\(P\) contains the live problems and their formulations; \(B\) the background contents in use; \(V\) the values and priorities affecting attention; \(K\) the standards, methods, and interpretation practices in use; \(A\) the actual alternatives under consideration; \(J\) situated judgments; \(L\) links to relevant histories, dependencies, and retained resources. These describe semantic roles, not explicit lists inside a thinker. A frame in which facts are arranged belongs to \(B\) and \(K\). (FW2: last sentence.)

A situation index identifies the respects relevant to a claim. Abbreviating a situation to a problem-background pair does not assert that omitted components are unchanged; a comparison that changes a material component must expose that change. A represented problem is a difficulty experienced or recognized by the attributed system; a problem imposed by another can become the system's own through understanding. Problem-origin autonomy, candidate-origin autonomy, criticism-origin autonomy, and autonomy in changing standards are separate attributions.

An episode \(q\) is a connected course of attention to a difficulty, including reformulations and subproblems; the connectivity is causal and explanatory, not topic similarity. Episodes can overlap, split, resume, or be absorbed. An episode's engagement state and its epistemic disposition are separate: work can be active, suspended, interrupted, or closed for now; closing does not make a conclusion immune; an observer's decision to stop recording is not the system's decision.

## The recursive kernel as a relation, not a scheduler

Write \(\mathcal K(s;q;\xi,\xi')\) for a connected instance of problem-directed conjecture and critical activity by \(s\), relating situations \(\xi\) and \(\xi'\). The relation prescribes no fixed number of stages, order, winner, or termination. Some occurrences are incomplete engagements. The actuality of a critical engagement does not entail the correctness of its objection.

A complete critical episode includes a represented difficulty, a target content already available for criticism, a conjectural objection represented as bearing on that difficulty, and a reason-sensitive response that leaves a resulting problem situation, which may contain unresolved questions or qualified retention. A creative critical episode additionally contains an attributed originative explanatory contribution connected to the engagement.

The recursive commitment has three parts. **Target closure.** Any content, method, interpretation, or relation doing work in an instance of \(\mathcal K\) may become the represented subject of another instance; the system need not possess an exhaustive self-description to question the rule by which it preferred a result. **Role preservation.** Scrutiny of a method uses the same distinctions between conjecture, reason-bearing criticism, response, and improvement; no criticism is correct by position. **Return relevance.** A result of the subsidiary inquiry can revise the parent's use of the target: withdraw a premise, alter an interpretation, change which comparison is appropriate, narrow a conclusion, redirect attention. It need not change the conclusion when the objection fails, and chronological succession does not establish effect.

These are conditions on semantic organization. A realized capacity claim adds modal obligations. An actual failure of recognition remains a possible failure, not a contradiction manufactured by the definition of fallibility.

## Methods can be criticized from within inquiry

A system's operative method at \(\xi\) is represented by contents in \(K\) together with their roles. It can conjecture a description of that method, criticize it, and introduce a different practice at \(\xi'\); the account of the transition belongs to the same history as the inquiry it changes. This does not require a mechanism to rewrite a currently executing evaluator; it requires that method change not be intelligible only as an unexplained external intervention.

**Generator constraints.** (FW2, new, from E1.) Among the contents of \(K\) are those whose standing determines which contents the system can deploy as attempts. Let \(K^{\mathrm{gen}}_\xi\subseteq K\) be the contents \(d\) such that there is a problem \(p\) and a content \(x\) for which \(\mathrm{Attempt}(s,x,p,h',e')\) occurs in some admitted counterfactual history \(h'\in\mathcal H\) whose situation differs from \(\xi\) only in that \(d\) lacks \(\mathrm{Standing}_j(d;\cdot)\), while no such attempt occurs in any admitted history from \(\xi\). A generator constraint is a standard, method, or practice whose withdrawal would change what the system can conjecture. Linking rules and narrow classes in a grammar, an evolved similarity space, a learned attention method, and the rules of a formal system are generator constraints for the organizations that have them; so are inexplicit dispositions. The definition is counterfactual because the histories are, and it makes no claim about mechanism.

Target closure ranges over contents doing work in an instance of \(\mathcal K\). A generator constraint does not do work in an instance; it fixes which instances can begin. An organization can therefore criticize every standard it applies and remain unable to represent or withdraw the constraint that determines which conjectures it can form. Whether generator constraints are eligible targets is a further condition, stated in the class hierarchy below.

The mathematical language used to describe this theory is not a perfect internal truth predicate for a thinker. A representation of "my present method is adequate" is a conjecture in the model, not the metatheoretic assertion that it is adequate.

## Chronology does not decide opposition between reasons

Causal order belongs to occurrences; explanatory dependence, support, opposition, and undercutting belong to interpreted contents and applications, and need not inherit acyclicity. Two arguments already available can each be recognized, later, as conflicting with a premise of the other; both relations are recorded after both arguments exist, with no backward causation. The most recently stated unanswered objection is not privileged by its date. Circular opposition does not imply equal merit; circular support does not create independent warrant by circulating it.

## Attempting an explanation and actually explaining

An attempted explanation is content the system deploys as an account of a represented difficulty. It can be false, incomplete, poorly articulated, or based on a mistaken problem, and need not have answered every objection to count as an attempt. A useful interpretive description is

\[
E=\langle W,\Gamma,\Delta,\Sigma,B_E\rangle ,
\]

where \(W\) describes the explanatory job, \(\Gamma\) the commitments, \(\Delta\) the proposed connections, \(\Sigma\) the scope, and \(B_E\) the auxiliaries. This exposes what a proposal does; it is not a mandatory artifact. The substantive relation

\[
\mathrm{Account}_{\mathcal M}(e,p,b;\Sigma)
\]

holds when the interpreted content accounts for the relevant feature of the problem in the stated respect and scope. An admissible interpretation must identify the explanatory work done by the commitments, explain the bearing of the connections, and distinguish that work from associating an input with an output. A model that predicts what will be sensed under movement, and can be run to see what happens, is not thereby an account of why; prediction is not explanation, and a hypothesis that "explains away" lower-level signals in a statistical sense stands in a relation among representations, not in \(\mathrm{Account}\). (FW2: last sentence, from A1 and A2.) An account may invoke a mechanism, geometry, constraint, structure, intention, institution, or another appropriate mode; no single microscopic vocabulary is imposed. A false attempted mechanism is still an explanatory conjecture. [F1; B1]

An interpreter need not establish that every commitment is consciously used at one instant. Use may be distributed across an episode, embodied in a disposition, or shared by a group.

## Reason-bearing criticism and counterfactual uptake

A criticism is interpretable as

\[
c=\langle z,p,b,\delta,g,\lambda\rangle ,
\]

where \(z\) is the target, \(\delta\) the alleged defect, \(g\) the grounds, and \(\lambda\) the proposed bearing of those grounds on the target in problem \(p\). A standard used by the criticism belongs among its dependencies. A test or proposed investigation may be part of the grounds; an empirical experiment is not mandatory.

\(\mathrm{Bearing}_{\mathcal M}(g,\delta,z,p,b)\) is a substantive relation. A criticism occurrence can allege it without its holding; bad criticisms and mistaken rejections are represented without being validated. For a response \(\alpha\),

\[
\mathrm{UsesReason}_{\mathcal M}(s,c,\alpha;\mathfrak C)
\]

requires an explanatory account of how the represented reason participates in the response across a relevant contrast family \(\mathfrak C\). Content-preserving recodings should preserve uptake; changes to the alleged defect should make a content-appropriate difference; irrelevant hostility should not count as the same criticism because it causes an output change. Two objections can rationally receive the same action for different reasons. An empty or uninformative contrast family supplies no discrimination between reason use and generic responsiveness. Performed interventions can be evidence for the relation, but a fixed battery is not part of its identity. Where evidence is inadequate, attribution remains unresolved. [B7]

A mismatch between a model and its input that draws attention and repairs the model has no represented defect, grounds, or bearing, and its response is fixed by the mechanism rather than by what any objection says; it is not a criticism, however reliable it is within its scope. Its disappointment of an expectation becomes criticism only through an interpretation connecting the event to an alleged error. (FW2, from A1.2.)

## Argument dependencies and the effect of criticizing a premise

An argument occurrence \(u\) has a conclusion, a scope, an inferential application, and the premises on which that application essentially depends. A source cited for background is not necessarily essential; a standard, interpretation, observation model, or comparison can be essential without being packaged as a certificate. Essentiality is application-relative:

\[
\mathrm{Essential}(d,u)
\]

means this application cannot sustain its stated conclusion in its stated way without \(d\); several independent arguments can have the same conclusion. For a situated appraisal \(j\), \(\mathrm{Standing}_j(d)\) means \(d\) is treated as an available premise in the specified respect, and

\[
\mathrm{Usable}_j(u)\iff\mathrm{Licensed}_j(u)\land\mathrm{InScope}_j(u)\land\forall d\,[\mathrm{Essential}(d,u)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Standing}_j(d)] .
\]

\(\mathrm{Licensed}_j\) concerns the inferential application, not infallible authorization. The condition expresses an obligation of coherent appraisal: one cannot withdraw an essential premise and continue to claim support from the same dependent application. It applies uniformly to theories, criticisms, criticisms of criticisms, standards, and progress claims. A system can violate it; the semantics then represents an error in appraisal.

Standing is indexed by use and respect; where one symbol would conceal different uses, write \(\mathrm{Standing}_j(d;u)\). A premise can be available for one qualified application and unavailable for another. Ordinary conjectures, observations, comparisons, reasons, background assumptions, and standards can all be dependency targets. Provisional background assumptions are allowed; their provisional use and their vulnerability to criticism must both be represented. Evolved constraints on generalization are provisional background in this sense: physical knowledge in the organism, deployed as premises, withdrawable, and not warrant. (FW2: last sentence, from C1.3.)

The obligation concerns coherent appraisal, not an instruction that any newly entered objection defeats its target. Whether an alleged defect bears, whether its grounds are acceptable, and which applications depend on the premise are criticizable judgments; a schematic attack edge cannot supply them. A defeated argument does not make its conclusion false; a withdrawn criticism does not make the criticized claim true; a criticism of one application of a standard need not defeat every use. A support cycle supplies no new reason merely because it is a cycle; mutually constraining theories and reciprocal explanatory relations are nonetheless permitted.

## Appraisal is also conjectural content

An appraisal can propose that an explanation is preferable, a criticism succeeds, a standard is defective, a question is ill-posed, or a comparison is unresolved. These are contents eligible for the same kernel; the appraisal's method and premises must be recoverable, though no storage format is prescribed.

A record may distinguish "no case supplied," "a positive case supplied," "a negative case supplied," and "cases supplied on both sides." These are optional descriptions of the record, not four kinds of truth; the presence or absence of an unanswered attack does not determine merit. A well-formed account of a failed test is not evidence that the test succeeded. Keeping a defective argument available for examination is different from counting it as usable; assessing an argument as unusable does not require erasing it.

No complete or unique ranking of alternatives is assumed. Incomparability, disagreement about criteria, and unfinished assessment are permitted. Moving from them to a reasoned preference may require creating a new explanation, not selecting a different rule for aggregating the same labels. A mechanism that yields one stable state from many partial models, by intersection of candidate sets or by suppression of the rare by the common, represents no appraisal and supplies no warrant; on an input the constraints do not decide it selects, where the semantics permits leaving the matter open. (FW2: last sentence, from A1.3.)

## Newness is relative to an attributed system and a semantic grain

Let \(R^{\ell,\beta}_{<e}(s,h)\) contain the explanatory contents that the system, under attribution boundary \(\beta\) and its declared historical continuity, had previously been able to deploy in a relevant explanatory role before event \(e\). Initial understanding belongs to this baseline, as does previously possessed but forgotten understanding when the claim is first creation by the continuing system. Storage of a string is not understanding; not all logical consequences of an accepted theory are in the repertoire. [F1] Define

\[
\mathrm{New}_{\ell,\beta}(s,x,e,h)\iff\neg\exists y\in R^{\ell,\beta}_{<e}(s,h)\;(y\equiv_\ell x) .
\]

Historical novelty replaces the system-relative baseline with an independently specified historical one. Neither baseline is switched to make an attribution come out favorably. The relation concerns the actual repertoire, not an investigator's archive; an incomplete archive can leave newness unestablished. "Previously able to deploy" means actual possession at an earlier time, not acquirability after further learning.

**Initial repertoire domain.** (FW2, from E1.) For an event \(e_0\) taken as the start of an attribution, let \(D_0(s;e_0)\) be the set of subject matters \(D\subseteq\mathfrak E_\Theta\) such that \(R^{\ell,\beta}_{<e_0}(s,h)\) contains contents deployable in an explanatory role for some element of \(D\). This is fixed at \(e_0\) and is not the set the candidate later succeeds on.

## Authorship and distributed contribution

Write \(\mathrm{Authors}_{\beta,\ell}(s,x,p,h,e)\) when the causal-explanatory account attributes the construction or reconstruction of the relevant problem-specific organization in \(x\), in the contribution completed or delimited at \(e\) in \(h\), to \(s\). This is not "every incoming channel lacked equivalent content"; it requires an account of the contribution. The contribution index is essential: a system may forward an explanation without understanding it and later reconstruct it; later authorship does not make the earlier forwarding originative. A received explanation may be understood only after the learner invents an interpretation, tests it, and corrects it; sender and learner then receive different attributions. Prior influence is compatible with authorship; mere causal involvement is not sufficient. [B16]

The boundary \(\beta\) identifies the system whose contribution is claimed. Nested attributions to a person, a collaboration, and an institution are permissible with separate accounts. A later discovery that a tool or collaborator did essential work revises the attribution without moving the event. Every use of authorship and newness displays \(s,\beta,\ell\), the history, and the contribution index; a definition must not search for a favorable boundary while presenting its result as an answer about a fixed one.

## Originative acts and critical processes

\[
\mathrm{OCA}_{\beta,\ell}(s,x,p,h,e)\iff\mathrm{Attempt}(s,x,p,h,e)\land\mathrm{New}_{\ell,\beta}(s,x,e,h)\land\mathrm{Authors}_{\beta,\ell}(s,x,p,h,e) .
\]

\(\mathrm{Attempt}\) requires a represented problem and the use of \(x\) as an attempted account of it. No truth, success, retention, critical episode, or universal capacity is a conjunct; a one-time false conjecture can be an originative act.

A **critical episode** requires connected, reason-bearing criticism and uptake, whether the target was authored by the responder or inherited. A **creative critical episode** additionally contains an \(\mathrm{OCA}\) that contributes to the connected inquiry; the contribution may be a candidate explanation, a reconstructed understanding, an explanation of a defect, or a new account of the problem or method. The \(\mathrm{OCA}\) need not originate the content first criticized; the inherited target must precede criticism of it, the new contribution need not. A lineage-specific revision process is a special case, not the definition.

A **critical result** is the resulting problem situation with the contents and dispositions the response makes available. Suspension can produce a better articulated uncertainty; rejecting a criticism can reveal a misunderstanding; requesting evidence can formulate a discriminating question. A **reason-sensitive setting aside** is distinguished from a content-insensitive refusal; an unexplained refusal must not be given a fictional reason.

Productivity is not creativity. (FW2, from A3.2.) An organization with a lawful generator of possible contents that denies them standing and discards them on use can extend an inherited system without any instance of \(\mathcal K\); its generated contents are never targets, so it cannot criticize what it generated and cannot satisfy \(\mathrm{OCA}\) for it. An originating organization must retain its conjectures as targets without granting them standing.

## Continuity through reframing, replacement, and absorption

Ancestry of occurrences is one kind of provenance, not the whole of continuity. A theory can be replaced by an independently originated rival; an episode can preserve a difficulty while changing its formulation, background, or standards. A contextual transport

\[
T:\langle p,b,k\rangle\rightsquigarrow\langle p',b',k'\rangle
\]

is an interpreted account of which difficulties, commitments, and reasons are preserved, revised, abandoned, or rendered inapplicable; its validity is a substantive claim. A connected critical history using \(T\) must show how the earlier difficulty bears on the later one and must neither evaluate all descendants against the original problem by fiat nor discard the original obligations by renaming. Absorbing an earlier result into a later episode changes the later episode's resources; the earlier event keeps its historical location. Reuse of history is not rewriting history.

## Explanatory quality, constraint, and reach

A current good explanation is an account that addresses the relevant problem and withstands the applicable criticism sufficiently well to merit preference among the actual alternatives in the stated scope. This does not require that its user originated it, and it does not make the user's preference self-validating. [F3; B1]

A useful analysis of "hard to vary" asks which content-bearing details are constrained by the work the explanation does. An apparently harmless alteration is a substantive criticism when it shows that the alleged connection contributed nothing. For a commitment \(d\), job \(p\), background \(b\), and variation family \(\mathcal V\), write

\[
\mathrm{FreeVariation}_{\mathcal V}(e,d;p,b)
\]

when a change in that family preserves the claimed account without a new reason or compensating explanatory change. The family must contain informative contrasts; an empty, renaming, or objection-excluding family establishes nothing. The diagnostic is comparative and respect-relative. Two independently reasoned explanations can address one problem; a false explanation can have tightly constrained details. Constraint is neither uniqueness nor truth certification. An account that simultaneously meets many independently discovered constraints does more explanatory work and merits provisional preference among actual alternatives; it acquires no probability of correctness thereby, since the constraints are themselves conjectures that can fail together and the alternatives are not enumerated. (FW2: last sentence, from A1.4.) [B1]

Explanatory reach is a relation between the commitments of an explanation and further problems for which they do work; it can exist before any agent recognizes it and is not restricted to enumerated applications. An application claim must identify the unchanged commitments, the additional background, and the explanation of relevance; a new bridge theory is an additional contribution, not a free consequence. Reach of an explanation is distinct from the reach of a thinker's inferential machinery across domains; the two are different relations with one name. (FW2: last sentence, from C1.5.) [B1]

## Progress, possession, and historical assessment

\(\mathrm{Progress}_{\mathcal M}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\) means that contribution \(\Delta\) produces an actual explanatory improvement in the stated problem situation. The interpretation must identify what deficiency was removed or clarified, what work survives or is superseded, what losses matter, and why the new situation is better in the claimed respect. Replacing a false mechanism, explaining why an approximation worked, finding that a conflict was an artifact of interpretation, finding a better question, and recognizing a limit are not equivalent successes, and no common scale is imposed. Local progress does not establish improvement everywhere; changed standards must be part of the comparison, not its concealed premise.

**Explanatory knowledge creation** by a system consists of a connected creative critical contribution, actual explanatory progress attributable to it, and the resulting knowledge being available for use or reconstruction by the attributed system or identified community. Availability is not endorsement and does not require perpetual survival; whether an insight is created, retained, communicated, or lost are distinct historical questions, and loss does not make creation not to have occurred. (FW2: last clause, from A1.6.) [F3; M5]

A historical occurrence of "we judged this a correction" remains an occurrence after the judgment is rejected. A claim that a correction improved \(\xi\) relative to \(\xi'\) is a truth claim about indexed situations; discovering it was wrong does not make it formerly true.

## What explanatory universality requires

The domain of a universality claim must be specified independently of the candidate's successes. Let \(\mathfrak E_\Theta\) range over explanatory contents across every subject matter admitted by the adopted account of reality, subject to physical possibility of representation under \(\Theta\); let \(\mathfrak P_\Theta\) range over the corresponding coherently posed challenges. These include unfamiliar subjects and explanations not yet invented; they are not a test suite or "whatever this system can already handle." A restriction to a subject \(D\) yields a domain-relative capacity that must be reported with that restriction; even universality within two large independently chosen subjects is not full universality. The full claim may not exclude a subject because the candidate lacks its concepts.

A challenge can concern why a transformation is impossible. A demand for unrecoverable accidental details is not automatically a demand for a general explanation.

Let \(\chi\) specify enabling conditions: background learning, evidence, time, memory, communication, tools, and other resources. \(\mathrm{Enable}_\Theta(s,p,\chi)\) requires that these be physically admitted where a physical claim is made and that their specification not assert that the discovery has already occurred. Each challenge specifies an **achievement respect** and the permitted contributions of its environment, fixed by an explanatory account of the challenge. Understanding a supplied explanation, reconstructing absent understanding, and originating an unsupplied solution are different achievements; a condition suitable for one cannot discharge another. Communication is compatible with creativity; the restriction concerns where the claimed organization was created.

Let \(\mathrm{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(s,p,\chi;\Phi)\) mean that the continuing organization \(\Omega\), at boundary \(\beta\), has the capacity to perform activity \(\Phi\) on \(p\) under \(\chi\). This is a substantive modal relation. Its interpretation must explain how the organization contributes, how variations in the problem or reasons affect it, and why the activity is not a preadapted answer supplied by an unacknowledged creator. A capacity may admit failed attempts; robustness claims state their perturbations. Mere absence of witnessed failure does not establish capacity; one failed attempt does not establish incapacity; an explanatory argument about an architectural barrier can refute an attribution without enumerating continuations. A record of failure, however long, is evidence about enabling conditions and formulations tried; it is not itself a barrier. (FW2: last sentence, from C1.2.)

The same \(\Omega\) across domains means continuity of the learning and critical organization, not frozen contents. It may acquire languages, change standards, build tools, and reorganize. Replacing it with an unrelated competent system does not establish that the original had the capacity; an account may not choose a new competent author for each problem. The attribution boundary and continuity criterion are held fixed across the quantifiers. Relabeling an answer-bearing device as background, or postulating a capacity separately for every conclusion, does not meet the burden.

**Learnability as a template.** (FW2, from C2.1.) A learning problem specified by a class of targets, an environment, a learner, and a criterion of success is an instance of this schema restricted to reconstruction: the class is a subject \(D\), the environment is \(\chi\), the learner is \(\Omega\), and the criterion is the achievement respect \(\mathrm{Reconstruct}\). A negative learnability theorem is an organizational barrier of the kind that can count against an attribution; it bounds reconstruction under the stated environment and says nothing about origination or about what the organization can do when \(\chi\) includes tools and communication that are not positive text.

### Universal understanding

\[
\mathrm{UU}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)\iff\forall e\in\mathfrak E_\Theta\;\exists\chi\;\bigl(\mathrm{Enable}_\Theta(s,\mathrm{understand}(e),\chi)\land\mathrm{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(s,\mathrm{understand}(e),\chi;\mathrm{UnderstandAndCriticize})\bigr) .
\]

\(\mathrm{understand}(e)\) is the challenge of reconstructing the relevant explanatory organization, not producing its words. Communication of \(e\) is allowed and confers no invention credit. The quantifier does not claim all explanations can be understood simultaneously or within one lifetime.

### Universal creative inquiry

For challenges for which explanatory improvement is possible in principle,

\[
\mathrm{UC}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)\iff\forall p\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\;\exists\chi\;\bigl(\mathrm{Enable}_\Theta(s,p,\chi)\land\mathrm{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(s,p,\chi;\mathrm{CreativeCriticalAdvance})\bigr) .
\]

\(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\) is selected by the independent claim that an advance is possible, not by a claim about \(s\). The causal organization of the advance must be attributable to \(s\) or the declared composite; copying a supplied complete answer does not witness creative advance. The strength of \(\mathrm{UC}\) is intentional: permission to inquire and topic-independent syntax are weaker results and must be reported as such.

### The stratified hierarchy

(FW2: this subsection replaces FW1a's three-class statement with five classes and a comparison class; the three original definitions are unchanged.)

**The first-layer class (comparison, outside the hierarchy).** An interpretation in the base semantic class that attributes to \(s\): retention of representations with fidelity correction (settling among partial models, redundancy, suppression of a candidate by an attested competitor, attestation); a lawful generator of possible contents; reconstruction of an inherited target under positive evidence; standing denied to generated contents by default; and no instance of \(\mathcal K\). The class is disjoint from the recursive-capacity class on its last clause. It is named because every mechanism this theory has shown to be "not criticism" lives in it, and because one physical system can host a first-layer subsystem at one boundary while being in a higher class at another.

**The base semantic class.** Interpretations respecting the distinctions and structural commitments stated here. It can describe mistaken judgments, failed episodes, and institutions that obstruct criticism without declaring them rational.

**The recursive-capacity class.** Interpretations in the base class that additionally satisfy the kernel's three modal conditions, target closure, role preservation, and return relevance, as capacities of an attributed continuing organization: \(\mathrm{RecursiveCapacity}_\beta(s,\Omega)\). They are not supplied by a separate observer who can write comments after the target has ceased functioning.

**The extensible class.** (FW2, new.)

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{EX}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)\iff{}&\mathrm{RecursiveCapacity}_\beta(s,\Omega)\\
&\land\ \forall\xi\ \forall d\in K^{\mathrm{gen}}_\xi\ \bigl[d\text{ is an eligible target of }\mathcal K\text{ for }s\text{, with return relevance to its standing}\bigr]\\
&\land\ \exists D^\ast\subseteq\mathfrak E_\Theta,\ D^\ast\not\subseteq D_0(s;e_0),\ \exists\chi\ \bigl[\mathrm{Enable}_\Theta(s,\cdot,\chi)\\
&\qquad\land\ \forall e\in D^\ast\ \mathrm{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(s,\mathrm{understand}(e),\chi;\mathrm{UnderstandAndCriticize})\\
&\qquad\land\ \exists p\in D^{\ast\,\mathrm{adv}}\ \mathrm{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(s,p,\chi;\mathrm{CreativeCriticalAdvance})\bigr]\\
&\land\ \text{the extension into }D^\ast\text{ is attributable to }\Omega\text{ under the fixed continuity criterion and is accounted}\\
&\qquad\text{for by a criticism of some }d\in K^{\mathrm{gen}}\text{, a change to its standing or to the represented vocabulary,}\\
&\qquad\text{and the effect of that change.}
\end{aligned}
\]

The conjuncts are E0 (recursive capacity), E1 (generator constraints are eligible targets with return relevance), E2 (domain-relative understanding and creative-inquiry capacity in at least one independently specified domain outside the initial repertoire), and E3 (the extension is the organization's own and traceable). E1 is not supplied by E0, since target closure ranges over contents doing work in instances and a generator constraint fixes which instances can begin. E2 is not supplied by E1, since constraints can be revisable while every revision stays within \(D_0\). E3 is not supplied by E2, since domain-relative capacity can be present because a competent component was added from outside or the boundary was silently redrawn. E0 is not supplied by E1 to E3, since an organization can revise constraints and enter new domains by variation and uptake alone.

**The universal explanatory class.**

\[
\mathrm{UED}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)\iff\mathrm{RecursiveCapacity}_\beta(s,\Omega)\land\mathrm{UU}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega)\land\mathrm{UC}_{\Theta,\beta}(s,\Omega) .
\]

The inclusions are

\[
\mathrm{UED}_{\Theta,\beta}\subseteq\mathrm{EX}_{\Theta,\beta}\subseteq\mathrm{RecursiveCapacity}_\beta ,
\]

the first because a universal organization whose generator constraints were fixed would be barred at any domain they exclude, and an extension not attributable to \(\Omega\) counts for neither class; the second by E0. Whether either inclusion is proper is left open and is the subject of the jump conjecture in Part IV.

The three-conjunct decomposition of \(\mathrm{UED}\) and the four-conjunct definition of \(\mathrm{EX}\) are modeling proposals intended to make the universal claim substantial and contestable; they are not theorems that every instance of creativity has these capacities. Understanding, progress, and continuing capacity have substantive interpretations, so the displayed quantifiers do not by themselves explain their realization. A theory of a universal organization should explain why its way of creating, reconstructing, and criticizing content is not tied to a finite preadapted repertoire, and in particular why the constraints that make its domains learnable are contents it can withdraw. [B6; B7]

## Physical realization and constructor-theoretic scope

For physical attribution, an interpretation must connect content roles to physically discriminable states, the claimed activities to physically admitted processes, and counterfactual differences in reasons to corresponding differences in the organization's use of them, using the same interpretation throughout. The boundary includes the stores, collaborators, tools, and environmental contributions that matter to the claim; distributed realization is allowed, and a list of contributors without an account of their integration is not enough. Resources and error correction matter where repeatable capacity is claimed; the ability to continue indefinitely is not inserted into the definition of a one-time act.

For a realization module, *substrate*, *attribute*, and *task* denote a physical system, a relevant set of its states, and specified input-to-output transformations. The important physical distinction is between a transformation occurring once and an organization retaining the ability to cause it. Marletto develops that distinction through generalized and abstract catalysts. Physical knowledge is information with a causal capacity for preserving its instantiation; an inherited adaptation can have that character without representing an explanation, and the merit of an explanatory discovery is not the duration for which one copy survives. [M5; M7] Universality of construction and universality of explanation are separate attributions. No claim about consciousness, personhood, moral standing, or species membership follows from a lone episode or a constructor description.

**Realization constraints for the recursive-capacity class.** (FW2, new; from C1.1, A2, A3, C2.2.) The following hold of any \(\Theta\) module for an organization in the recursive-capacity class or above. They are consequences of the semantics, not conditions of membership, and they name no substrate.

1. **Medium.** The realization must support representation of particular contents distinct from any other with the same properties, composition of contents, binding of one content across roles, and embedding of contents in contents to unbounded depth. The kernel's contents are contents about contents with shared targets: a criticism is about a target, a criticism of it embeds it, and reason use identifies the same criticism across a contrast family. A medium that cannot do these things cannot instantiate target closure as a capacity. This does not require that the medium be a language, that its operations be discrete, or that an interpreter produce a formal translation. [P1]
2. **Two layers of error correction.** A realization contains a layer that corrects errors of representation, making a content's instantiation stable under noise, partial input, and movement, by redundancy and settling; and a layer that corrects errors of content, treating an instantiated content as a target and changing its use on the basis of a represented objection. The first is necessary for the second, because the second operates on retained representations; it supplies no warrant, because its object is the fidelity of a token. The second cannot be obtained by iterating the first: consensus among consensuses is still consensus. This is stated as a conjecture in Part IV and as a constraint here to the extent the semantics already requires both objects. [H1; CT1]
3. **Retention.** The realization retains attested contents with provenance, and retains the organization's own generated contents as available targets without standing. A reconstructing organization needs only the first; an organization in this class needs both, since its own conjectures and constraints must be targets. It must also be able to instantiate a content without enacting it, to represent the set of live alternatives and the currently deployed one as an object, to reify a mismatch as a content with target, defect, grounds, and bearing, and to change the use of a content without erasing it. [P2]
4. **Standing discipline.** Generated contents are denied standing by default (K-STANDING). This is a constraint on the relation between a generator and a store on any substrate, and it is what makes generation safe where no datum can refute an overgenerating conjecture.
5. **Constraints as copyable contents.** For membership in the extensible class, the generator constraints must be instantiated in a medium in which they can be copied, compared, and withdrawn; constraints that are parameters of a component rather than contents of the organization do not satisfy E1. For humans, notation and explicit grammar are what supply this.
6. **Nothing first-layer crosses a boundary.** Consensus, blocking, discarding, attestation, and uptake, whatever their value for stable representation, conventional systems, or cultural transmission, do not move an organization from the first-layer class into the recursive-capacity class, or from that class into the extensible class.

Substrate-level proposals, such as an account on which contents are stored at locations in reference frames within many partial models settled by voting, are candidate \(\Theta\) modules and are assessed against these constraints. Satisfying such a proposal is neither necessary nor sufficient for any semantic attribution defined here. [H1; H2]

# Part III. Consequences and discriminating examples

## Historical acyclicity does not imply semantic acyclicity

**Claim.** An inquiry with no backward causal reference can contain mutual semantic opposition. **Construction.** Two available arguments conclude \(p\) and \(\neg p\); at later occurrences the inquirer records both directed rebuttals; both refer only to existing contents; precedence remains acyclic while opposition contains both directions. **Consequence.** Removing mutual opposition to preserve a chronological graph loses a legitimate situation.

## Losing a premise loses an argument, not automatically its conclusion

**Claim.** If an essential premise of \(u\) loses standing in appraisal \(j\), \(u\) is not usable in \(j\); the conclusion need not be false or unsupported by every other argument. **Derivation.** The universal clause of \(\mathrm{Usable}_j\) fails; a second argument without that dependence is unaffected; nothing establishes the negation. The consequence applies when \(u\) is a criticism; exempting criticisms would violate the condition.

## Recursion is not universality

**Counterexample.** An organization can conjecture about one subject, criticize those conjectures, and criticize its own standards within that subject, with arbitrarily many levels, while its organization prevents representation or critical learning of an independently specified \(p^\ast\) under every admitted enabling condition. The recursive condition holds while \(\mathrm{UU}\) or \(\mathrm{UC}\) fails at \(p^\ast\).

## Recursion is not extensibility

(FW2, from E1.) **Claim.** An organization can satisfy E0 and fail E1. **Construction.** Take the preceding counterexample and add that the constraint confining it is wired in: no content in \(K\) is such that withdrawing its standing widens the set of attempts. Then \(K^{\mathrm{gen}}\) does not contain the confining constraint as an eligible target. **Consequence.** The existing counterexample is a member of \(\mathrm{RecursiveCapacity}\setminus\mathrm{EX}\); what it lacks is not recursion but a constraint that is a content.

## Provisional reasoning does not require a completed hierarchy of judges

**Construction.** An investigator uses a provisional instrument model; a criticism identifies a vulnerability; a revised model is compared using other available explanations while unresolved assumptions are retained; the comparison changes the use of the original model. There is a finite episode with a genuine return from method criticism to object-level inquiry, and no premise has been justified for all purposes.

## A finite record does not logically determine universal capacity

**Claim.** A finite record of successful episodes, without organizational premises, does not entail \(\mathrm{UED}\). **Construction.** Two candidate models agree on the observed episodes; one admits a universal organization; the other has an unobserved barrier at an uncovered problem. Both fit. The conclusion is underdetermination by the record alone, not a ban on rationally accepting a universal explanation.

## Explanation, creativity, and success do not collapse

A person can invent a false account (\(\mathrm{OCA}\) without progress); a critic can respond to a real issue and introduce a worse theory (uptake without progress); a copier can transmit a correct explanation (possession without authorship); a learner can reconstruct an old explanation (newness and reconstructive authorship without historical firstness). None claims that copying, deduction, search, randomness, or communication is forbidden inside a creator.

## General model learning is not explanatory organization

(FW2, from A1.1.) **Claim.** An organization can satisfy a demanding set of conditions for general-purpose learning of predictive models while no interpretation in the base class assigns it any instance of \(\mathcal K\); it then lies outside the recursive-capacity class. **Construction.** Let \(s\) modify its models continuously, learn by moving its sensors, hold many complementary models of each object stored at locations in learned frames, and settle on a percept by consensus among them. Suppose its history contains no occurrence interpretable as a recognized difficulty, no content deployed as an account of one, and no occurrence alleging a defect with grounds and bearing. Then \(\mathrm{Attempt}\), \(\mathrm{Bearing}\), and \(\mathrm{UsesReason}\) have no instances and no instance of \(\mathcal K\) exists; \(s\) may nevertheless carry physical knowledge. **Consequence.** A checklist of learning attributes is a restrictive profile: passing it establishes general learning and not explanatory organization; failing it does not establish absence. The case sits between "Recursion is not universality" (an explainer confined to one subject) and the biological contrast (a lineage without criticism): an individual general learner with no explanatory activity. [H1]

## A prediction error is not a criticism

(FW2, from A1.2.) **Claim.** A mechanism that detects every mismatch between a model and its input and repairs the model does not instantiate reason-bearing criticism. **Construction.** Represent input, predictive state, mismatch event, and model change. To interpret it as \(c=\langle z,p,b,\delta,g,\lambda\rangle\), \(\delta\) would have to be a represented defect, \(g\) grounds, \(\lambda\) a bearing claim. The mismatch is an event; no bearing is represented; the response is fixed by the mechanism; the only variable in any contrast family is the presence of mismatch. **Consequence.** An expectation-and-surprise cycle is not the kernel; the disappointment of an inexplicit expectation becomes criticism only through an interpretation connecting it to an alleged error. The mechanism supplies enabling conditions for criticism, including evidence and attention; it is not criticism. [H1]

## Consensus by suppression is not warrant

(FW2, from A1.3.) **Claim.** A mechanism yielding one stable state from many partial models supplies no warrant, represents no appraisal, and produces determinations where the semantics permits none. **Construction.** Under one reading the mechanism intersects candidate sets; under another the common suppress the rare; under both the output is one state selected for action, and on a bistable input the mechanism picks. Elimination by joint constraint can be criticism when the constraints are contents represented as reasons; here nothing alleges a defect and the intersection is computed. Agreement among models is a count. On the bistable input the mechanism selects where "incomparability, disagreement about criteria, and unfinished assessment are permitted." **Consequence.** The strongest engineering case for a consensus mechanism, that it binds partial models into one percept and stabilizes it under moving sensors, is not epistemic. A theory taking convergence as warrant adopts a perceptual convenience as epistemology. The two readings must be kept apart because elimination by represented reasons can be criticism and majority suppression cannot. [H1]

## Satisfying many constraints is a reason for preference, not a probability of truth

(FW2, from A1.4.) **Claim.** An account meeting many independently discovered constraints may merit provisional preference without any probability of correctness being licensed. **Construction.** The crossword analogy: the more intersecting answers fit, the more certain the solution. A crossword has a finite grid, an answer key, and independent clues by construction; explanatory inquiry has none. \(\mathrm{FreeVariation}\) is comparative and respect-relative, and constraint is neither uniqueness nor truth certification. **Consequence.** The correct reading is that such an account does more explanatory work and is harder to vary; the incorrect reading is a confidence functional, which this theory does not have. [H1]

## A reference frame is a background, and choosing one is a conjecture

(FW2, from A1.5.) **Claim.** That the same facts arranged in different frames yield different conclusions is an instance of K-PROBLEM; a frame is a component of \(\xi\); choosing one is an explanatory act. **Construction.** Historical facts on a timeline suggest causal relations by temporal proximity; on a map, by spatial proximity; a good frame for an abstract subject may need dimensions nobody has named. In \(\xi\) a frame belongs to \(B\) and \(K\); a change of frame is a change to a material component and must be exposed. The description of a discovery as finding a better frame for the same facts misdescribes it: a new theory is a conjecture that can change what counts as a fact in the relevant respect. **Consequence.** The physical vocabulary of frames corroborates the theory; treating a frame as neutral scaffolding slides into inductivism, and K-CONJECTURE denies that arrangement supplies content. Lexical instances exist: one event admits alternative sparse schematizations licensing different constructions, neither recoverable from the scene's correlational texture. [H1; P2]

## Blocking is not criticism

(FW2, from A3.1.) **Claim.** Retreat from an overgenerated form on acquisition of a listed competitor for the same slot is first-layer competition among forms. **Construction.** A slot, two fillers, one attested; the attested suppresses the generated. Nothing represents a defect; the attested form is a competitor, not a reason; the outcome is fixed by attestation and slot uniqueness; the only variable is attestation. **Consequence.** Blocking is a correct policy for a conventional system, where attestation is the only ground there could be. It transfers to explanatory content only as a mistake: an account is not displaced by a competitor's being attested, repeated, or older. [P2]

## Discarding is not criticism, and it marks the line between reconstruction and origination

(FW2, from A3.2 and C2.2.) **Claim.** An organization that uses generated forms once and discards them, retaining only witnessed forms, corrects overgeneration without criticism; it can reconstruct and be productive; it cannot criticize what it generated and cannot originate. **Construction.** Productively formed entries are not listed; they are regenerated on demand; only witnessed forms are listed. Overgeneration is harmless because nothing generated enters the store with standing. The generator's products are never targets, since a target must be "already available for criticism." The organization can satisfy a domain-relative reconstruction attribution and can extend an inherited system; it cannot satisfy \(\mathrm{OCA}\) for its generated forms or an advance attributable to itself. **Consequence.** Reconstruction is compatible with a discard policy for generated candidates; origination is not. This is a retention-policy discriminator between \(\mathrm{UU}\)-type and \(\mathrm{UC}\)-type capacity at the level of the substrate, and it is why K-STANDING requires retention without standing rather than discarding. [P2]

## Uptake is not criticism

(FW2, from A3.3.) **Claim.** The process by which an innovation that violates current constraints becomes an actual form of a language over time is variation and selection at the cultural level. **Construction.** Many speakers generate possible forms; some are repeated; forms repeated enough become listed for the community. No speaker represents a defect in a rejected form or a reason for an adopted one. Substitute forms for variants and uptake for reproduction in the biological contrast; it applies unchanged. **Consequence.** Language change by uptake is real and is a source of novelty; it is reason-free and outside the kernel, and it is the mechanism by which false models spread when what spreads is a belief. [P2; H1]

## Reconstruction is not extensibility

(FW2, from E1.) **Claim.** An organization can extend into a new domain without satisfying E0. **Construction.** The reconstructing learner acquires a second inherited system from positive evidence by the same generator and acceptance policy; its repertoire now covers a subject it did not; no instance of \(\mathcal K\) occurred. **Consequence.** Extension of repertoire is not extension in the sense of E2, which requires \(\mathrm{UnderstandAndCriticize}\) and \(\mathrm{CreativeCriticalAdvance}\); the first-layer class can grow without entering \(\mathrm{EX}\).

## Extension by replacement is not extension

(FW2, from E1.) **Claim.** An organization can come to satisfy E2 for some \(D^\ast\) by a change that fails E3. **Construction.** A designer adds a new sensor modality and its learning modules to a modular sensorimotor system, or an operator adds a new collection to an organization of language models under a host. The composite now learns, and with additions may criticize, in the new domain. The route was the designer's act, not a criticism by the organization of a constraint in its own \(K^{\mathrm{gen}}\). **Consequence.** Membership in \(\mathrm{EX}\) is established only by an extension the organization brings about itself, traceably; enlargement of \(\chi\) is admissible, but the extension must be the organization's. [H5; H6]

## The organization of language models under a host

(FW2, from E1, Case 4.) **Claim.** A composite of language-model participants under a deterministic host that retains contributions as targets without standing, separates examination from adoption, records dependencies and case descriptions over uses, and enacts mediated use changes is a candidate for the recursive-capacity class; it fails E1 as constituted, for a specific and repairable reason. **Construction.** The constraints determining which contents a participant can deploy as attempts are its weights and training. Within a run these have no standing the organization can withdraw; they are not contents in the composite's \(K\). The host's revisable attention methods constrain selection and supply, not generation. So \(K^{\mathrm{gen}}\) for the composite contains constraints that are not eligible targets. **Consequence.** The composite is at most in \(\mathrm{RecursiveCapacity}\) and a specification adopting this authority should not claim more for it. Repair requires that the organization be able to represent, criticize, and change what its participants can conjecture, within the run and traceably; adjustment by the operator between runs is extension by replacement. Whether an organization with frozen generators can satisfy E2 through E1-satisfying constraints elsewhere in \(K\) is an open question.

## The combinatorial medium is a realization constraint

(FW2, from C1.1.) **Claim.** Any realization of an organization in the recursive-capacity class must support individuals distinct from kinds, composition, binding across roles, and embedding to unbounded depth. **Construction.** A complete critical episode contains \(z\), \(c\) about \(z\), and \(\alpha\) with \(\mathrm{UsesReason}\); target closure allows \(c'\) about \(c\); role preservation gives \(c'\) the same structure. Representing \(c\) requires identifying \(z\) as a particular content bound to two roles; representing \(c'\) requires embedding \(c\) as a target; representing \(\mathrm{UsesReason}\) requires identifying the same \(c\) across \(\mathfrak C\). An organization whose states cannot do these cannot instantiate target closure as a capacity. **Consequence.** A constraint on \(\Theta\) modules, not on the semantics; stated without a substrate; more general than any proposal about symbols, frames, or networks. [P1]

## Cognitive closure is a well-formed criticism of an attribution, not of the class

(FW2, from C1.2.) **Claim.** The proposal that a human mind is constitutionally unable to solve certain problems because they do not decompose combinatorially is a criticism of the attribution of \(\mathrm{UED}\) to humans; it is compatible with the class definition; the authority fixes what a reply must show. **Construction.** The proposal characterizes a domain \(D^{\ast\ast}\) independently of human successes, asserts an organizational barrier, concedes the barrier is a property of one organ of one species so that a universal explainer is coherent, and states a refutation condition. This is the form a challenge must take. In the stratified hierarchy it is the claim \(s\in\mathrm{EX}\setminus\mathrm{UED}\). **Consequence.** Three obligations bind any reply and any defense. A record of failure is not a barrier; the argument must be organizational. The attribution is to the continuing organization under admissible enabling conditions including external representation and tools, so claims about the unaided organ do not engage it, while the claim that a supplied solution could not be understood does. The problem formulations in \(D^{\ast\ast}\) are themselves criticizable, so a demonstration of closure must show that no admissible reformulation dissolves them, and a defense by reformulation must show the difficulty is preserved. [P1; B3; B6]

## Evolved constraints on generalization are provisional background, not warrant

(FW2, from C1.3.) **Claim.** An organism's evolved constraints on similarity and generalization are contents with \(\mathrm{Standing}\), deployable as background and withdrawable under criticism; they supply no inductive warrant. **Construction.** A lineage instantiates physical knowledge without representing a problem or a reason; the constraint is present as an inherited disposition; when it contributes to a judgment it occupies the role of a background content \(d\) with \(\mathrm{Standing}_j(d;u)\); it can be wrong for the case and withdrawn, and the generalization it licensed then loses that application. **Consequence.** Inborn expectations are prior conjectures, which is all K-CONJECTURE needs and all this theory grants. A similarity space supplies no grain; a promotion of similarity to identity must state one.

## The no-negative-evidence constraint

(FW2, from C2.2.) **Claim.** An organization reconstructing an open-ended target from positive evidence alone cannot use evidential refutation against overgenerating conjectures; it must have a constrained generator, endogenous withdrawal, or a distinction between formation and acceptance. **Construction.** A superset conjecture is compatible with every positive datum, so no datum bears on it as criticism and \(\mathrm{Bearing}\) has no instance. A learner that nevertheless arrives at the exclusions does so by denying standing to generated forms, listing only witnessed ones, and letting development be the acquisition of more accurate meanings rather than the narrowing of a rule. **Consequence.** K-STANDING is what makes generation safe under positive evidence; the constraint is substrate-independent. [P2]

## The learnability-universality tension

(FW2, from C2.3.) **Claim.** Constraints on a generator that make reconstruction from positive evidence possible are, if fixed, parochial barriers; an organization claimed to be universal must hold them as contents in \(K\), eligible for the kernel and withdrawable. **Construction.** The first horn of the no-negative-evidence problem is a constrained generator. A fixed constraint is the counterexample "Recursion is not universality." K-RECURSION requires that methods be criticizable with effect on operative use; the constraints that make a domain learnable are methods in that sense once they are contents. Revision at the community level over history, by innovation and uptake, is variation and selection and is not the kernel. **Consequence.** An attribution of universality to a learnable organization must show that its generator constraints are revisable within the continuing organization under admissible \(\chi\); tools, notation, and metalinguistic knowledge are what convert a constrained generator into one whose constraints have standing. This is E1 of the extensible class, seen from the learner's side. [P2]

## A worked inquiry: two balances and a changing question

A stipulated thought experiment. An object is placed on two balances reading 10 and 12. The investigator trusts the second and proposes the first reads low. A colleague asks why the second is trusted; if only because its reading agrees with the proposed mass, the account uses its conclusion to select its standard, and the problem becomes what licenses treating either as reference. Suppose constant additive offsets: \(m_A=x+b_A\), \(m_B=x+b_B\), with \(10=x+b_A\), \(12=x+b_B\). Both \((12,-2,0)\) and \((10,0,2)\) fit, and for any \(\lambda\), \((x+\lambda,b_A-\lambda,b_B-\lambda)\) leaves both readings unchanged: the readings determine \(b_B-b_A=2\), not \(x\). Repetition does not remove the ambiguity; an independently relevant constraint is needed. The useful result is a better problem situation, not a certified mass. The subsidiary result is criticizable (offsets may not be constant); a later calibration certificate is evidence only through an account of what was calibrated and why it applies, and a criticism of applicability affects the argument using it whichever side it serves. A collaborator's discovery of the invariance while the investigator repeats the result yields a different attribution from the investigator's own reconstruction.

## Explanatory constraint and reach without a list of outputs

Deutsch's seasons example: axial orientation, orbital relations, and illumination geometry constrain the account; substituting an agent's mood does not preserve the work. The explanation applies beyond the locations considered because of what it says. Its reach must still be stated accurately; discovering the need for a further explanation is the next problem, not a reason to conceal the distinction. [B1]

## Inexplicit and artistic inquiry

An artist can recognize that a passage does not achieve an intended effect, vary it, and distinguish improvement from irrelevant alteration without an explicit theory. The burden is to explain that discrimination and its use, not to require a prior formal specification; a claim that the work discovers objective aesthetic knowledge requires the further aesthetic interpretation. [B14]

## Distributed inquiry and biological contrast

In a collaboration, one may identify the problem, another invent a candidate, a third expose a defect, and the group preserve a revision; a system-level attribution requires the causal connections and shared use that make these one inquiry. Agreement or membership alone is not such a connection. A biological lineage can acquire an adaptation through heritable variation and differential reproduction without representing the difficulty as a problem or proposing a reason against a variant; it instantiates physical knowledge without explanatory criticism. Blind lower-level variation does not eliminate the explanatory significance of higher-level thought. [F3; M5]

# Part IV. Adequacy, scope, and remaining questions

## What an application must explain

A proposed application must identify the system and boundary, the represented difficulty, the relevant content, and the causal organization of the contribution. Where criticism is claimed it must explain bearing and uptake; where progress, the improvement; where recursive capacity, how scrutiny affects operative methods; where extensibility, which generator constraint was criticized, how its standing changed, and what the change effected; where universality, the domain, enabling conditions, continuity, and capacity clauses directly. No transcript of a mind is required; no certificate format is required; no absence in an archive settles a claim about everything the system could do.

## What would count against the theory

The explanation-centered core would be challenged by a defensible account of explanatory knowledge creation with no problem-directed conjectural or critical organization at the relevant level. The reason-use analysis would need revision if it excluded genuine understanding or included content-insensitive compliance after the causal differences were made explicit. The recursive account would be inadequate if it allowed an operative standard to remain immune while claiming criticism had effect, or required a final evaluator for local progress. The transport account would fail if it disconnected legitimate revolutions or admitted arbitrary changes as solutions. The universality conditions would need revision if their resource clauses covertly supplied every achievement, if their domains were selected by successes, or if the understanding-creating distinction misdescribed universal ability. An attribution of \(\mathrm{UED}\) would be challenged by a genuine organizational barrier to an independently characterized domain under enabling conditions that do not remove the barrier by replacing the system.

**K-STANDING** would need revision if a defensible account of self-criticism were found in an organization that grants standing on generation or discards what it generates, or if the distinction between retention and standing could not be applied to any realization without a theory of mechanism. **The extensible class** would need withdrawal if E1 could not be stated without a mechanistic notion of a generator, if E1 turned out to be entailed by target closure after all, or if E2 and E3 added nothing to domain-relative \(\mathrm{UED}\) as already reported. (FW2.)

Renaming an inconvenient case, changing scope silently, or declaring every negative case "not a real problem" would not answer any of these.

## Named criticism routes and conjectures

(FW2, new.) A commitment that names its strongest published opponent is more contestable than one that does not.

**Cognitive closure.** The named challenge to the attribution of \(\mathrm{UED}\) to humans is the proposal that problems lacking part-whole and lawful-relation structure are constitutionally beyond a combinatorial mind. It concedes the coherence of the universal class and contests membership; in this theory's terms it asserts that humans are in \(\mathrm{EX}\setminus\mathrm{UED}\). Its obligations are stated in Part III. Its published counter-argument is that any explanation comprehensible to a universal explainer is comprehensible to a person, so closure would require that the barred problems have no explanation; that argument is itself a conjecture about reach and is eligible for criticism on the same terms. [P1; B3; B6]

**The jump conjecture.** \(\mathrm{EX}_{\Theta,\beta}=\mathrm{UED}_{\Theta,\beta}\): an organization that has extended itself into one independently specified domain by criticizing and revising its own generator constraints, traceably, has the capacity to do so for any admitted domain, the remaining differences between domains being differences in \(\chi\). This is this theory's form of the claim that universality arrives as a jump. It is refuted by an organization shown to be in \(\mathrm{EX}\) and shown, by an organizational argument meeting the closure obligations, to be barred from some domain. If it holds, the extensible class is not proper on that side and its value is to name the condition that makes the jump possible; if it fails, closure has a member. [B6]

**Two layers of error correction.** Any physical realization of the kernel contains a fidelity-correcting layer over representations and a content-correcting layer over contents, the second not obtainable by iterating the first. This is refuted by an organization with a single error-correcting layer that instantiates the kernel, or by evidence that content-level criticism co-varies strictly with representational stability so that no dissociation can be produced. The research program that tests it is external to this authority. [H1; CT1]

## What is and is not established

The theory supplies a recursive organization of semantic roles, an explicit universal target class, a stratified hierarchy between recursion and universality, and distinctions that prevent false identifications: history with judgment, criticism with rejection, understanding with transmission, explanatory progress with persistence, recursion with universality, recursion with extensibility, productivity with creativity, general learning with explanatory organization, consensus with warrant, and physical task capacity with creativity.

It does not derive the existence of an actual universal explainer from definitions. It does not give a complete reductive account of explanation, meaning, causal authorship, progress, or creative capacity. The constructions make additional commitments explicit; their success must be assessed by the explanations they support and the criticisms they survive. The remaining question is explanatory: what organization makes the attributed capacities possible, how do its parts do the claimed work, and why do the distinctions hold. The same kernel applies to attempts to answer it.

## Refinement without semantic substitution

A specification adopting this theory distinguishes a **semantic assertion**, an **attribution judgment**, a **record fact**, and a **machine-check result**. A machine may check that a document has required fields, that a finite derivation uses stated premises, that a counterfactual experiment met a stated comparison, or that a content's own stated operands satisfy a relation it asserts. None of these establishes that its interpretation of explanation, reason use, authorship, progress, or capacity is correct.

Let \(A\) be the interpretation of this authority and \(D\) a dossier or implementation state. A refinement provides a partial interpretation relation \(\mathcal I(D,A)\), not a definition of \(A\) as whatever the implementation accepts. For every projected claim it states subject, scope, interpretation, dependencies, and whether the projection concerns a record fact, a situated judgment, or semantic satisfaction. A machine-level classification may entail a semantic proposition only under an explicit bridge whose adequacy is open to criticism. A restrictive profile can require an explicit graph, an intervention family, a finite event vocabulary, or a witness format; failure to satisfy it is failure of that check, not absence of the phenomenon; passing it is not a definition. The connection must be substantive in both directions and must test legitimate cases against unintended exclusion: inherited targets, communicated reconstruction, inexplicit contributions, method change, nonreplacement results, provisional closure.

Admission, use, and truth are distinct (K-STANDING). Recording a proposed case does not make its body satisfy its checks; satisfying checks does not make premises true; a mechanically failed body must not be reported as passed because nobody objected. Noninductivism forbids manufacturing warrant from a count of endorsements or repetitions, from agreement among instances or components, from attestation, or from a count of satisfied constraints; it does not make different arguments or new information irrelevant because they share a conclusion. Operational stopping and epistemic immunity are different. A specification may itself be criticized through the inquiry it supports; a replacement can be enacted by a person, institution, or mechanism without a self-rewriting evaluator, and the account must identify the criticism, replacement, decision, and effects. Version labels establish traceability, not return relevance.

## Further discriminating cases

**Delayed reconstruction.** A relay emits a received explanation at \(e_1\) without reconstructing it; the system develops the understanding at \(e_2\). Authorship at \(e_2\) cannot combine with novelty and attempted use at \(e_1\) to establish \(\mathrm{OCA}\) at \(e_1\).

**Inherited target, original criticism.** An investigator inherits a theory, notices a defect, and creates an explanation of it. A creative critical episode is possible though the investigator did not originate the theory and the new explanation is not its descendant; a definition requiring lineage excludes this.

**Ordinary premise under criticism.** A criticism depends essentially on an observation interpretation rather than a labeled standard; withdrawing it removes that application's support without refuting the criticism's conclusion or reinstating the theory.

**Same target, different achievement.** A tutor supplies a complete explanation and the learner reconstructs it; this creates knowledge for the learner without establishing the capacity to originate it under an achievement condition excluding a supplied solution.

**One system, two boundaries.** (FW2.) A person's grammar-acquisition subsystem is in the first-layer class: it reconstructs an inherited system from positive evidence, denies standing to what it generates, and instantiates no kernel. The person, at the boundary that includes notation, explicit grammar, and communication, is a candidate for the extensible class. Attributions are made at boundaries; the same physical system can host members of different classes at different \(\beta\).
