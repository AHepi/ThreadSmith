## Part A. The mechanisms, one by one

### A1. Where error sits: the commitments (Part I)

- [TEXT] Realism. "Whether a transport is faithful on a contract is independent of whether anyone accepts it. Systems can be wrong about their transports, their observations, their criticisms and their own capacities." (F11:L69 = D3:L67)
- [TEXT] Fallibility. "A theory may contain an accurate scoped dependence together with errors elsewhere. What cannot count as explanation is an error in the very dependence alleged to do the work." (F11:L71)
- [TEXT] Draft 3 adds (D3:L69): "'Explanation' in this commitment means an account … a false theory offered as an answer is an explanatory candidate, which ordinary usage may still call an explanation, and how hard an account is to vary is a separate matter (Part VI)."
- [TEXT] "An idea may be entertained without justification. A criticism has a target and is itself conjectural. A thinker may act on an appraisal without certifying it." (F11:L73)
- [TEXT] "Recursive scrutiny with operative return. Any aspect of a system's practice … can become a target, and the result must be able to change how the system proceeds." (F11:L75)
- [FOLLOWS] The fallibility commitment tolerates error *outside* the working dependence. So a false part that does no work, such as the sun god, is tolerated by name, and a false part that does work is not.
- [FOLLOWS] "Must be able to change how the system proceeds" is said of a *system's practice*. It is formalized as scrutinizability (F11:L479: "criticism can be directed at it, and the result can affect its operative use. Contracts and transports are among the scrutinizable aspects") and (RC) (F11:L481–485). Both define the recursive class (F11:L520). Neither is a condition on Account.

### A2. What a failed test does to an Account (Part V, K3, the historical index)

- [TEXT] (A): "For every (a,b)∈C, Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b)) = Ans_p(a,b)" (F11:L249–253). (F1) and (F2) are likewise quantified over every (a,b)∈C (F11:L235–245). "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in C." (F11:L267)
- [TEXT] K3: "For T∧B∧I⇒O, an established ¬O yields ¬(T∧B∧I) and nothing narrower." (F11:L391 = D3:L389). File 12 widens this (F12:L411): "An intervention whose predicted outcome fails refutes the conjunction of the causal model, the background and the claim that the intervention realized the intended edit; which conjunct fails is a further question."
- [TEXT] Historical index: "A proposition indexed to a contract remains that proposition when a later theory changes the current contract. A new index is a new claim." (F11:L365 = D3:L363)
- [TEXT] Derivation 7: "The claim 'E is an account on C' and the claim 'E is not an account on C′' are claims at different indices and may both be true." "Goalpost-moving is the act of changing the index without recording the change, and it is a failure of the record, not a licensed operation of the semantics." (F11:L596–598)
- [FOLLOWS] Account is fixed by the world on the whole contract, tested or not. A failed test at a pair *inside* C does not make E stop being an Account. It shows that E never was one on C, provided B and I hold (K3). A failure at a pair *outside* C leaves the Account on C standing: "it does not answer a broader question that failed" (F11:L161).
- [FOLLOWS] K3 does not say which conjunct to revise, and file 12 says outright that this "is a further question". So the text gives no rule against saving T by revising B or I.

### A3. Criticism (Part IX)

- [TEXT] Bearing (K1): "A criticism has target z, alleged defect δ, grounds g, and a connection. Let p_δ be the question about the defect. Then Bearing(c,z,p) ⟺ Account(E_c,p_δ). … A criticism occurrence can exist when (K1) fails. An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents how it bears." (F11:L373–379)
- [TEXT] Draft 3 defines the pieces (D3:L371): "Let p_δ be the question whether z has δ in respect of p, and E_c the criticism's connection from g to δ, interpreted as an explanatory candidate (Part V) for p_δ". Its last sentence (D3:L377) reads "…until an organization represents it as grounds for an alleged defect in a target."
- [TEXT] Reason use: "…lands on an active route. Using an invalid objection does not make it valid." (F11:L381) Standing (K2): "Withdrawing a premise removes a license; it does not make the conclusion false." (F11:L389) Receipts: "missing evidence stays missing. A record reconstructed from the claim it is meant to support is not a receipt for that claim." (F11:L393)
- [TEXT] "(E) does not exclude a true mechanism guessed for bad reasons; the reasons for adopting it are assessed elsewhere (Part IX)." (F11:L279)
- [FOLLOWS] A criticism *bears* when its own connection is an Account of the defect. So criticism is held to (E), the same standard as explanations. Whether a criticism bears does not depend on whether anyone accepts it.
- [FOLLOWS] Part IX assesses *reasons and receipts*. Nothing in Part IX feeds back into (E). A candidate adopted for bad reasons, or patched for bad reasons, is not thereby any less an Account.

### A4. Violation, surprise and the two responses (Part IV, Derivations 3, 4 and 10)

- [TEXT] File 11: "Let t be selected on history H with contract C … a **violation** occurs when fidelity fails at (a,b); **surprise** is a violation at (a,b)∉H." (F11:L219–223) "Two responses to a violation are distinguished. A **selection response** extends H and lets μ act: the transport is re-tuned within the population. A **construction response** introduces a new organization or a new transport with a construction witness. Only the second can be originative under Part X." (F11:L227)
- [TEXT] Draft 3, from W35 (b′):
  - "Let t be a transport to the simulation layer S, with contract C and, where t is selected, history H." (D3:L217)
  - "**surprise** is a violation of a selected transport at (a,b)∉H." (D3:L221)
  - "Expectation and violation are defined for every transport to the simulation layer, surprise only for a selected one: a constructed one that fails at a pair of its contract is violated, and the failure is not surprise; a violation the system represents can be a recognized difficulty (Part X)." (D3:L223)
  - "A **selection response** extends the history H of a selected transport" (D3:L225).
- [TEXT] File 12 widens further: surprise for any transport "whose fidelity has been checked on a finite **tested history** H" (F12:L217, L221). Its responses are "A **re-tuning** extends H and alters values within the model's existing components. A **rebuilding** introduces a new organization or a new transport, with a discovery witness" (F12:L225).
- [TEXT] Derivation 4: "A system can be surprised only if it holds a transport selected on a history H strictly smaller than the contract C" (F11:L570). Its consequence: "The two responses, extend H and re-tune, or construct a new transport, are the difference between learning and creating" (F11:L574).
- [TEXT] Derivation 3, qualified: "the value of t at (a,b) is underdetermined by H" only where some co-surviving t′∈T differs there, and "Where T contains no such transport, H is silent on the value at (a,b) and the population fixes it." (F11:L562–564) Draft 3 adds that t′ "must be a member of T, a transport the physics and the stated construction admit (Part XII), and it must survive on H (Part IV)" (D3:L566). It also defines survival (D3:L195): "fidelity on H without membership in T is not survival."
- [TEXT] Derivation 10 is the theory's own worked example of error correction. A selected S₀ is surprised by occlusion. Under a selection response, "If the population's transports can only predict from occupancy, no member survives the extended history: the fidelity failure is structural, not parametric" (F11:L616). A construction response builds S₁. "If it repairs the obligation … while protecting 'predict displacements correctly,' then (P) holds; and since the account is adequate on its contract and deployable, (EK) holds." (F11:L618)
  - Draft 3 drops F11:L616's gloss "and this is Derivation 3's qualification seen from the other side…" (D3:L620; CL:L2207–2214, an erratum).
- [FOLLOWS] In file 11 a *constructed* account that fails a test has no "violation" in the text's terms. It meets its error only through K1 and K3 and an undefined "recognized difficulty". Draft 3 gives it a violation and routes a represented violation into a critical episode. File 12 goes further and calls it surprise.
- [FOLLOWS] Selection is error elimination bounded by the population: a test removes members, and what stays open is only where co-survivors differ. Past the population ("structural, not parametric"), only construction corrects.

### A5. Critical and creative episodes (Part X)

- [TEXT] "A complete critical episode contains a recognized difficulty, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a content-sensitive response. A creative critical episode contains an instance of (G) connected to its inquiry. Closing an episode is a decision, not a proof." (F11:L421; F12:L439 is the same)
- [TEXT] Draft 3 adds (D3:L423): "A **recognized difficulty** is a failure of a claimed obligation, or a conflict in which what the system holds meets a claimed obligation only by failing a protected one (Part XI), when the system represents it."
- [FOLLOWS] In draft 3, a failed test becomes the start of an episode only through the obligations of Part XI: the failure of a *claimed obligation* (for example, "answer correctly at this pair") that the system represents. Error correction is thus routed through declared obligations, not through (E).

### A6. Repair (Part XI)

- [TEXT] (P): "For claimed obligations O and protected obligations P, fixed for the comparison, Repair_{O,P}(ξ,ξ′;Δ) ⟺ ∃o∈O[¬o(ξ)∧o(ξ′)] ∧ ∀r∈P[r(ξ)⇒r(ξ′)] ∧ ProducedBy(Δ,ξ,ξ′;O)." (F11:L427–430)
- [TEXT] "The obligations are declared inputs: O says what is to be repaired and P what is to be protected, each as a stated condition over stated occasions, and a protected condition is lost exactly when it fails on an occasion it covers. Their declaration makes no claim that the aims are worth pursuing, and (P) does not rank alternatives. Losses outside P must be exposed. ProducedBy holds when an active route (Part IX) runs from Δ to the repair … A correct account that produced nothing, an act that repaired without an account, and a repair produced through use of an account are three different attributions." (F11:L433)
- [TEXT] Draft 3 adds two things.
  - "The contribution Δ of a repair is a subhistory together with the changes of content it makes." (D3:L429)
  - "In (P), accordingly, r(ξ′) says of a protected condition r that it held on every occasion it covers from ξ to ξ′, not only at ξ′." (D3:L435)
- [TEXT] Worth: "Repairing an obligation establishes that it was repaired; it establishes nothing about whether the obligation, or the question that led to it, was worth having." (F11:L447)
- [TEXT] Active routes: "A route that started and did no work, or that was already at rest when the result occurred, is not active for that result" (F11:L371). Draft 3 ties this to Part VI: ProducedBy "credits a repair to the contributions whose active routes ran to it, not to the routes a candidate contains" (D3:L307).
- [FOLLOWS] Repair is a relation between states of a *system* relative to obligations fixed in advance. It is not an operation on an account.
  - A patch that fits a failed test is a Repair of whatever agreement obligation was declared, if it protects P and produced the change.
  - It is a repair of an *epistemic* obligation only through (EK), which needs Account.
  - The anti-trade-off force of (P) is exactly as strong as the declared P.

### A7. Created explanatory knowledge (EK) and newness

- [TEXT] (EK) needs CreativeCriticalEpisode ∧ Repair_{O,P} ∧ an epistemic o repaired ∧ Origin(s,c,p_c,h,e_c) ∧ Account(c,p_c) ∧ c∈Result(Δ) ∧ Deploy ∧ ProducesVia (F11:L437–442). "The scope of Account(c,p_c) is the contract fixed at e_c. A later narrowing of that contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (EK)." (F11:L445) Draft 3 defines Result(Δ) and ProducesVia (D3:L447).
- [TEXT] Newness: New(s,c,h,e) ⟺ ¬∃d∈R_{<e}(s,h), d≡_ℓ c (F11:L405–409). Draft 3 defines the equivalence (D3:L407): "d≡_ℓ c when there are transports from d to c and from c to d, both faithful on c's contract at grain ℓ. The equivalence is structural at the stated grain, not string equality or similarity."
- [TEXT] Build: "Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not. … A small binding newly prepared inside received content is construction of that binding, and the rest of the content keeps its inherited provenance." (F11:L401) "A dimension of variation mentioned in passing is not thereby a port of the account; it becomes one when the account admits changes to it, and adding it is construction." (F11:L417)
- [FOLLOWS] A patch fitted after a failure and built in an owned episode is New, Built and an Attempt, so (G) holds. If it is also an Account on the contract fixed at e_c and repairs an epistemic obligation, (EK) holds. The history of "fitted after the fact" is the normal route to (EK), not a bar to it.
- [INFERENCE] Under draft 3's ≡_ℓ, swapping one idle part for another (both constrain nothing) plausibly gives contents that are structurally equivalent at the grain. The result is not New, so it has no Origin and no (EK). The theory would then be blind to idle variation in newness as well as in (E).

### A8. Historical index, scope and records: everything that treats "after the fact" differently

- [TEXT] Scope: "An account at a stated scope answers the question asked at that scope; it does not answer a broader question that failed, and a narrowing adopted after a failure is a new claim at a new index (Part VIII). What makes a restriction appropriate to the question asked is a substantive, criticizable part of the claim; the semantics records the restriction and supplies no rule that certifies it." (F11:L161)
- [TEXT] Draft 3 adds (D3:L159): "Meeting the conditions of an account (Part V) on the restricted contract does not certify the restriction as appropriate. Where the claim states the ground of its restriction, a verdict on the restriction is given with that ground; where it states none and a verdict turns on one, the ground is a missing declared input (Part XIV)."
- [TEXT] "What is prohibited is changing C or Q during an assessment without recording that the claim has changed. Supplying a meaning for a replacement query can make a coherent new question; it does not answer the original one." (F11:L163)
- [TEXT] Grievance 4: "A contract that quietly excludes physically possible changes to protect an account is not caught by a rule about intentions; it is caught by the requirement that the exclusion be stated (non-vacuity, Part V), and then by any criticism that supplies the excluded change. Objectivity lives in the physics and the fidelity facts; scope-honesty lives in the record." (F11:L45)
- [TEXT] Grievance 10: "An assessment is an event with a frozen contract. An episode is a history in which contracts change, and every change carries a provenance record." (F11:L57)
- [TEXT] Part VI, after a deletion: "a component reassigned to a new target after a deletion belongs to a new candidate with its own assessment, and the new candidate's success is not the old one's." (F11:L309 = D3:L307) Interference: "Success of a subset does not imply success of the whole; b is not made 'not a commitment' to repair this." (F11:L311)
- [TEXT] Supports: file 11 has "the supports assessed are the ones actually written, not a support someone could write in their place" (F11:L301). Draft 3 has "the supports assessed are subsets of the written Γ, whether or not anyone has set them out, not a support someone could write with commitments outside Γ" (D3:L299).
- [TEXT] "A changed rule is a changed component." (F11:L105)
- [TEXT] Inputs fixed before the verdict:
  - obligations "fixed for the comparison" (F11:L427);
  - boundary and continuity "declared before the attribution, not chosen after it" (F11:L465);
  - "where the input is missing, the verdict is unsettled and the semantics says so rather than choosing the input from the verdict wanted" (F11:L514).
- [TEXT] The predecessor was more explicit on two points, neither of which is carried word for word into file 11 or draft 3 as far as I found:
  - "This distinction is part of the interpreted claim, not permission for an assessor to rescue a failed argument by selecting whichever fragments happen to be true." (F00:L162)
  - "It must not be dismissed by inventing a favorable new grain after the fact." (F00:L1380)
- [FOLLOWS] Every "after the fact" rule in file 11 and draft 3 applies to one of four things:
  - an index (contract, query): L161, L163, L445, L598;
  - a declared input (obligations, boundary, continuity): L427, L465, L514;
  - the identity of a candidate (a reassigned component is a new candidate): L309, L311;
  - the record (an unrecorded change is a failure of the record): L163, L598.

  None applies to the *content* of a candidate. A part fitted after a failure is judged by (E) exactly as a part written first.
- [FOLLOWS] The historical index gives "a new claim". It does not give "a false claim" or "no explanation". Whatever verdict the new claim gets comes from (E) on its own index.

### A9. Provenance: selected, constructed, declared; "blind"

- [TEXT] Selected: "No member of the history represents t, H, or the survival condition. … The population T is part of the claim: what H leaves open about t is what T leaves open (Derivation 3)." (F11:L197) Draft 3's Part 0 now says selection has "no represented target in that history" (D3:L13). File 10 said "blind" (F10:L13). Neither file 11 nor draft 3 uses the word.
- [TEXT] "Declared. Neither of the above. The transport is entered into the model by its author." (F11:L201) "Declaration is permitted only as a modelling convenience, and a claim about creativity cannot rest on it." (F11:L15) "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does." (F11:L213)
- [TEXT] "Neither provenance is reducible to the other: a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both." (F11:L203) "Construction is not selection." (F11:L403)
- [TEXT] "The population is the set of transports the physics and the stated construction admit; a transport that would need a part every member of the population is built without is not in it." (F11:L473)
- [TEXT] Part XV (A) needs "a candidate meeting all four conditions of (E) on a physically admitted contract, **with a non-declared transport**, that plainly provides no account" (F11:L528).
- [FOLLOWS] (E) has no provenance conjunct (F11:L233–265). A declared patch can therefore meet (E), but it cannot ground (R), Origin or (EK), and it cannot serve as a Part XV (A) counterexample.
- [FOLLOWS] Criticism belongs to construction (F11:L15: "an episode of conjecture and criticism"; L203). Deutsch's "selection by criticism and experiment" is not selection in this theory. The S89 audit says as much (AU obs 9), and so does draft 3's sources note (CL: W38.1, "Here selection is blind").
- [INFERENCE] Derivation 3 is the theory's only formal statement about "alternatives that fit the same tests", which is the core of Deutsch's easy-to-vary. It applies to selected transports and their populations. For constructed candidates the nearest analogues are Part VI's Pres (variants that stay Accounts) and Derivation 2 (same anchors, one account). Both report; neither grades.

### A10. Idle parts and hard-to-vary (Part VI)

- [TEXT] File 11: "**Hard-to-vary.** For explanatory jobs F⊆F′, Pres(F′)⊆Pres(F) … More reach constrains variation; the containment need not be strict; counting jobs is not a warrant." (F11:L315) Also "It does not prefer an elegant account to a less elegant one with the same fidelity." (F11:L279)
- [TEXT] Draft 3, W33.1 (D3:L313): "The **reach** of E is the set of jobs f with Account(E,f); it is fixed by E and the world, not by which jobs anyone has checked. … (E) has no condition that each commitment do work. A commitment d does no work by itself in the candidate when every support stays a support after d is added to it and after d is removed from it: a candidate carrying d then meets (E) exactly when it meets (E) without d, and {d} is critical in no support (B). When Γ is infinite, a block of such commitments can still be critical (Infinitary support). How hard an account is to vary is a separate matter, shown by Pres, and it grades nothing."
- [TEXT] Draft 3 types Γ (D3:L231): "The commitments Γ are components of E, those the candidate offers as doing the work, whether or not anyone has described their work".
- [TEXT] Non-circular dependence in draft 3 (D3:L255) adds two things.
  - "Identity of that assertion with the target's answer is structural at the declared grain".
  - S3 requires a pair of C and a block G "such that the answer profile at (a,b) differs from its value at (1,b₀) … and this contrast is lost when the components of G are deleted from E".
  - The change list reads S3 as meaning "An idle block can never witness" (CL:L875).
- [TEXT] The change list states the loss openly: "The theory now says openly that it tolerates idle parts, which the source counts as defects." (CL:L1141)
- [FOLLOWS] By D3:L313, an idle part is *inert both ways*: adding or removing it never changes whether the candidate meets (E). So an idle part can never be what rescues a failed candidate, and it can never be what sinks one. Any part that rescues does work on the new contract, because it fails the removal half.
- [INFERENCE] If a candidate has no support (it fails on C), "every support stays a support…" holds vacuously, so every commitment "does no work by itself". W33.1's test tells you nothing about a failed candidate. Idleness must be read on a contract where the candidate is an Account.
- [INFERENCE] A part that is idle on C is not therefore idle on a wider C′. If it constrains anything at a new pair, it can do work there, or interfere there (F11:L311), and sink the full candidate. Idle *false* parts are harmless on C and can be a liability on C′.

### A11. Scope and appropriateness (Parts III, V, XIV)

- [TEXT] Non-vacuity: "every physically admitted edit excluded from C is excluded by a stated scope, not silently. A contract consisting only of relabelings, or excluding every change under which the active commitments could matter to Q, does not satisfy non-circular dependence and is therefore not a contract on which an account can be claimed." (F11:L259)
- [TEXT] Table: "A **table of observed answers** has no component whose relation is replaced by an intervention; it fails (F1) under any contract containing one." (F11:L271)
- [TEXT] Draft 3 on inputs and grading (D3:L516): "The semantics supplies no probability of truth, no merit function and no ranking of thinkers, and a claim that needs one is unsettled." Front matter (D3:L25): "a claim that needs any of the others is unsettled". File 11 (F11:L27) says instead that the semantics "takes it as a **declared input** and marks the place".
- [TEXT] Draft 3's Part XV preamble (D3:L528): "A case whose verdict turns on an input the case does not state, where the input is one of the declared inputs Part XIV lists or the normative relation, is a case with a missing input, not a refutation."
- [TEXT] Left out of draft 3: W32(c), "a series of loose limits adopted after failures is a retreat on the record" (WL:L717), with the note "W32(c) is left, so O1 stays SILENT" (PL:L274).
  - An earlier clause (A-prime) that would have tested limits by swapping them failed. The project story's round-4 entry says why: "a swap that works shows a limit is loose, not that it was fitted to failures; and the clause would refuse honest limits that simply say what is being asked about" (records, project story entry 29).
- [FOLLOWS] The one rule that exposes an account at all is non-vacuity's last sentence: the contract must contain some change under which the commitments could matter. A narrowing to the tested history, if stated, meets it.

### A12. Error correction as a computational claim (unbounded knowledge)

- [TEXT] (T2): "Without a modulus, no accumulated bound follows. An exact question is not silently replaced by an approximate one." (F11:L361; restated with hypotheses at D3:L359) (RC) is at F11:L481–485. Universality: "Recursion does not entail universality; a historical extension does not certify it; a finite performance record does not establish it." (F11:L501)
- [TEXT] The only occurrence of "correction" in file 11 and draft 3 is in (EK): "An epistemic obligation requires a correct account, or the correction of a use through one, to be deployable." (F11:L435 = D3:L437)
- [TEXT] W42 ("unbounded composition at a fixed accuracy grade needs a correction step", WL:L869–880) is left out of draft 3: "W42 is left, so error correction has no lemma yet" (PL:L274; PL:L132). N20 rests "on the case's own reasoning" (CL:L1236).
- [FOLLOWS] **Audit observation 14 checked: confirmed for file 11 and draft 3.** AU:L240 and L244 say error correction is "present for ideas and missing for computation". I would sharpen the "for ideas" half. The theory has error detection (K1, K3, violation), error elimination (selection on H) and repair (P, EK). It has no claim that correction is what makes knowledge growth unbounded (D p.140), and no requirement that a content be built so that its errors *can* be corrected. Deutsch's p.25 point, that testing matters only for hard-to-vary explanations, has no counterpart.
- [TEXT] Transmission, audit M9 (AU:L284): no theory file connects hard-to-vary with detecting and correcting transmission errors. Build treats a learner's fix of a relayed slip as "a small binding newly prepared inside received content" (F11:L401).

### A13. Part XV: what would refute the theory here

- [TEXT] "None is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label." (F11:L526) "A candidate that satisfies all four while plainly explaining nothing counts against it too." (F11:L19)
- [TEXT] (A) Sufficiency, with a non-declared transport (F11:L528). (D) Genesis includes "a demonstration that every construction witness can be rewritten as a selection history without loss" (F11:L534).
- [FOLLOWS] The owner's worry has exactly one refutation route in Part XV, attack (A): a candidate patched after a failure that meets (E) on a physically admitted contract, has a non-declared transport, and plainly explains nothing. Draft 3's preamble (D3:L528) removes any such case whose verdict turns on an unstated declared input, and the appropriateness of a contract's restriction is one (D3:L159, L516). Part XV lists no attack for "(E) counts an easily varied or after-the-fact candidate as an Account", except through (A).

## Part B. Answers

### (1) An account meets a failed test. What does the theory say happens?

- [TEXT] Standing is fixed on the whole contract and independent of acceptance (F11:L69, L249–253, L267). The test refutes only T∧B∧I (F11:L391). The claim keeps its index (F11:L365, L596–598).
- [FOLLOWS] If the failed pair lies inside C, E is not an Account on C, and never was, provided B and I hold. If it lies outside C, E is still an Account on C and does not answer the broader question (F11:L161).
- [TEXT] What comes next is a history.
  - For a selected transport: violation or surprise, then either a selection response (re-tune within the population) or a construction response (F11:L227).
  - For a constructed one in draft 3: a violation, which "can be a recognized difficulty" (D3:L223, L423) and so opens a critical episode.
- [FOLLOWS] The account is not "repaired" as an object. The system's content changes (Δ, D3:L429). The changed content is a new candidate: F11:L105 and L309 say a changed or reassigned component makes a new candidate "and the new candidate's success is not the old one's". A narrowing is "a new claim at a new index" (L161) and "does not retroactively satisfy (EK)" (L445).
- [FOLLOWS] **The repair is constrained in three ways.**
  1. The new candidate must meet (E) on its own contract. (F1), (F2) and (A) quantify over every pair of that contract, tested or not. So a patch that fits only the failed pair fails wherever the contract holds a change at which the patch's anchor behaves differently. A patch that just writes in the observed answer fails as a table (F11:L271) or under non-circular dependence (D3:L255).
  2. If the repair is a Repair_{O,P}, it must keep every declared protected condition on every covered occasion (F11:L433; D3:L435).
  3. Nothing about indices may change unrecorded (F11:L163, L598).
- [FOLLOWS] It is *not* constrained by any requirement that the new content keep the old parts, be minimal or principled, or be hard to vary (F11:L279, L315; D3:L313). Nor is it constrained on which conjunct of T∧B∧I to revise (F11:L391; F12:L411).

### (2) What happens to an idle part (draft 3's "does no work") during this?

- [FOLLOWS] **Changing or adding an idle part cannot rescue the account.** By D3:L313, a candidate carrying an idle d "meets (E) exactly when it meets (E) without d". Adding or varying d leaves the verdict where it was.
- [FOLLOWS] **A part that rescues is, by definition, no longer idle.** Suppose d is re-worded into d′ so that the candidate now fits the failed pair.
  - d′ is a changed component (F11:L105), which makes a new candidate (L309's logic).
  - d′ does work on the new contract, since removing it loses the support.
  - d′ is then held to (F1): its relation must equal its anchor's "for every (a,b)∈C" (F11:L235–237).
  - A god who "intervenes where needed" has no such anchor. On any contract with the relevant changes, it fails (F1) or (F2), or it is the answer written in and fails non-circular dependence.
  - A d′ that does track an anchor across the whole contract is, by Derivation 1, of one kind with that anchor on C, "whatever labels anyone attaches". It is a faithful component under another name (F11:L13, L546–550; D3:L119).
- [FOLLOWS] **Would the theory count the result as an Account?** Yes, whenever it meets (E) on its contract, whatever its history and however it was motivated (F11:L279: "does not exclude a true mechanism guessed for bad reasons").
- [FOLLOWS] **What it records.**
  - A new claim at a new index, if the contract changed (F11:L161, L365).
  - A new candidate (L309).
  - The patch's provenance: constructed at e, with a witness (F11:L199, L401, L411–415). This is the route to (G) and (EK), not a mark against the patch.
  - The Part VI profile: which parts are critical, and Pres. Pres "grades nothing" (D3:L313).
- [FOLLOWS] **It records nothing that marks the fit as arbitrary or evasive.** Grievance 4 says so for evasion: it "is not caught by a rule about intentions" (F11:L45). The loose-limit retreat measure W32(c) is left out, and O1 is SILENT (PL:L274).
- [INFERENCE] The real gap sits on thin contracts. On a contract narrowed (and stated) to the tested history, an easily varied candidate and its after-the-fact patch can both meet (E) if neither restates the answer at the grain. Pres is then large, and it grades nothing. On a contract that holds the physically admitted changes, (F1) over untested changes does most of the work hard-to-vary does for Deutsch. The easily varied myth fails there because it is unfaithful, not because it is variable.
- [INFERENCE] An idle *false* part is harmless on C (F11:L71). On a wider C′ it can start to constrain and interfere (F11:L311), and deleting it then leaves a support of the written Γ (D3:L299) while the full candidate fails. That is the one way an idle part matters to error correction in this theory.

### (3) Is there a rule that an explanation must be correctable, or one that stops an unconstrained save?

- [TEXT] **No correctability condition on Account.**
  - (E) has five conjuncts, none about variability (F11:L263; D3:L261).
  - "(E) has no condition that each commitment do work … it grades nothing" (D3:L313).
  - A claim needing a merit function is unsettled (D3:L25, L516).
  - "Recursive scrutiny with operative return" (F11:L75) and scrutinizability (F11:L479) are about a system's practice and define the recursive class (F11:L520), not Accounts.
- [TEXT] **The nearest thing to exposure** is non-vacuity: a contract "excluding every change under which the active commitments could matter to Q … is not a contract on which an account can be claimed" (F11:L259).
- [TEXT] **What does stop an unconstrained save:**
  - (a) unrecorded index changes: "What is prohibited is changing C or Q during an assessment without recording" (F11:L163), and goalpost-moving (F11:L598);
  - (b) backdated adequacy (F11:L445) and borrowed success (F11:L309, L311);
  - (c) inputs chosen from the verdict: obligations "fixed for the comparison" (L427), boundary and continuity "declared before the attribution, not chosen after it" (L465), "rather than choosing the input from the verdict wanted" (L514);
  - (d) patches that are tables of observed answers (L271) or restate the answer at the grain (L257; D3:L255);
  - (e) patches unfaithful anywhere in the contract (F1, F2, A).
- [FOLLOWS] **What is not stopped:**
  - (f) a stated narrowing after failure. It is a new claim, "recorded" and not certified (L161; D3:L159), and O1 stays SILENT.
  - (g) a faithful patch fitted after the fact.
  - (h) idle parts.
  - (i) blaming B or I instead of T. K3 leaves this open, and file 12 says it "is a further question".

  So the theory's discipline against evasion is a discipline of *records and indices*, plus fidelity over the whole contract. It is not a discipline on how content may be revised.

## Part D. Bearing on the owner's answer [INFERENCE throughout]

"It depends on how error correction is handled" can be answered from the text. The theory handles error correction by:

- **indexing claims**, so that a failed claim stays failed and a save is a new claim;
- **holding every candidate to fidelity over every admitted change of its contract**, so that a point patch fails wherever the contract reaches;
- **fixing obligations before a repair**, so that trade-off fixes fail against a declared P.

It does not handle correction by any property of content that makes errors correctable, and it keeps no mark of after-the-fact, arbitrary or evasive fitting.

The yes-or-no test therefore does Deutsch's work exactly where contracts are rich, and not where they are thin and stated. The places to press are three:

- stated narrow contracts: O1 is SILENT, and the appropriateness of a restriction is an input;
- idle parts, which are tolerated;
- K3's open choice of which conjunct to revise.

A grading would enter as a merit function, which draft 3 calls "unsettled" (D3:L25, L516). A non-grading alternative would be a record-level rule on revisions, in the style of the index rules: "a component introduced after a failure is held to …". Nothing like it is in the text.
