## 1. How error correction is handled now

**What corrects an account that fails.**
- [TEXT] Standing is fixed by the world, not by anyone's acceptance, on every pair of the contract, tested or not: realism (D3:L67); (F1), (F2), (A) quantify over every pair of C (D3:L233–253); "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in C" (D3:L265).
- [TEXT] A failed test refutes the conjunction of theory, background and the claim that the test did what was intended, "and nothing narrower" (K3, D3:L389). File 12 adds "which conjunct fails is a further question" (F12:L411).
- [TEXT] The failed claim stays failed: "A proposition indexed to a contract remains that proposition … A new index is a new claim" (D3:L363). A narrowing after a failure "is a new claim at a new index" (D3:L159). Changing the index without recording it is goalpost-moving, "a failure of the record" (D3:L602).
- [TEXT] A patched account is a different candidate: "A changed rule is a changed component" (F11:L105); a reassigned component "belongs to a new candidate … and the new candidate's success is not the old one's" (D3:L307).
- [TEXT] Detection and response: a violation for every transport to the simulation layer; surprise for a selected one outside its history; a selection response (re-tune within the population) or a construction response (D3:L217–225). A represented failure of a claimed obligation is a "recognized difficulty" that opens a critical episode (D3:L423). Derivation 10 is the worked example (D3:L620).
- [TEXT] Repair: claimed obligations O and protected obligations P are "fixed for the comparison"; a protected condition must hold "on every occasion it covers" (D3:L429–435). Created knowledge (EK) needs an account on the contract fixed when the content arose; "A later narrowing … does not retroactively satisfy (EK)" (D3:L447).
- [TEXT] Criticism is held to the same test as explanation: it bears only if its own connection is an account of the defect (K1, D3:L371–375). "A record reconstructed from the claim it is meant to support is not a receipt for that claim" (D3:L391).

**What this constrains.**
- [FOLLOWS] The content of any patch: every part must match the piece of the world it is tied to, on every change of its contract (F1 is part by part: "(F1) prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition", D3:L245). No part may carry the answer (D3:L255).
- [FOLLOWS] The record: every change of question is a new, recorded claim; the old verdict stands.
- [FOLLOWS] Repairs: they are measured against obligations declared first, so a fix that trades away a protected condition fails (P).

**What it leaves open.**
- [TEXT] Which of theory, background or test to blame (K3; F12:L411).
- [TEXT] A stated narrowing after a failure. It is recorded, "the semantics … supplies no rule that certifies it", meeting (E) on it "does not certify the restriction as appropriate", and with no stated ground a verdict that turns on appropriateness has "a missing declared input" (D3:L159), so that verdict is unsettled (D3:L516).
- [TEXT] A choice between candidates the contract does not tell apart: "the contract does not contain the distinction … The remedy is a finer contract, which is a new question" (D3:L562; F11:L558).
- [TEXT] Idle parts, which are tolerated: "(E) has no condition that each commitment do work … it grades nothing" (D3:L313).
- [FOLLOWS] Nothing marks a save as having no reason behind it, and nothing reads a run of saves. O1 ("the series as a whole is a retreat") is SILENT because W32(c) was left out (PL:L274).
- [TEXT] No lemma that the growth of knowledge needs correction (W42 left, PL:L274; AU obs 14).

## 2. The crux: can an idle or newly added adjustable part save a failed account unnoticed?

Three situations. The first two change a part; the third changes the question as well.

### Situation 1. The saint and the Saturday bread (an idle part given a job)

*A baker explains why her bread sells out by noon on market Saturdays: the market brings crowds past the door. She adds, "and Saint Honoré watches over the shop." Nothing else depends on the saint. One market Saturday, a third of the bread is left at closing. She says: "The saint was displeased that week." Now the saint's mood decides the sales, and the only sign of the mood is the unsold bread.*

- **Before the patch** [TEXT] the saint does no work: adding or removing it leaves every support a support, so "a candidate carrying d then meets (E) exactly when it meets (E) without d" (D3:L313). [FOLLOWS] As an idle part it could not have rescued anything. Deutsch p.24 says the same of the sun god: "no story about how anyone felt would put it right."
- **The patch** is a changed component, so it makes a new candidate (F11:L105; D3:L307).
- **Does it pass (E)? No.** Every component must be tied to a piece of the world (λ "assigns each component of E a subnetwork of D", D3:L189). If the mood is tied to nothing real, its claimed relation differs from its anchor's and (F1) fails (D3:L233–236). If it is tied to the sales themselves, the target's answer appears "as a component", and non-circular dependence fails (D3:L255; F11:L257). Both failures hold on any contract that contains the failed Saturday, however narrow.
- **Index:** no change of question, so there is nothing to record beyond the new candidate.
- **Provenance:** constructed or declared makes no difference to (E), which has no provenance conjunct (D3:L261). A declared patch could not ground (EK) or a Part XV (A) counterexample (F11:L528).
- **Repair:** she may repair an agreement obligation for that Saturday, but (EK) needs an account (D3:L437), so no knowledge is credited.
- **Criticism:** "the mood is read off the sales" bears (K1), since its connection is an account of the defect non-circular dependence names. The mood's only record is the unsold bread read as displeasure, which is a record reconstructed from the claim and so "not a receipt" (D3:L391).
- **Result: blocked, by (E) itself.** [FOLLOWS] If she ties the saint to something real ("the saint frowns when it rains"), the label drops out ("None inspects a label", D3:L265; Derivation 1), and the case becomes Situation 2.

### Situation 2. The north bed, with the question left open (a new adjustable part)

*A gardener explains why the tomatoes on the south wall ripen first each year: the wall gets the most sun. It has held for four summers. This year the north bed ripens first. She adds a part: "in a wet spring the north bed ripens first", and this spring was wet. (In fact the neighbour who shares the north bed planted an early variety there this year, and rain has nothing to do with it.) The new part fits all five summers.*

- **Contract.** [TEXT] She states no scope, so every physically admitted change not excluded by a stated scope is in the contract (D3:L257), for example soaking the north bed in a dry spring.
- **Does it pass (E)? No.** [FOLLOWS] (F1) is checked part by part at every pair. At "soak the north bed in a dry spring" the rain part says north first, and the world says south. So (F1) fails and the patched account is not an account.
- **But nothing in the record marks it.** [FOLLOWS] All five tested summers fit. The verdict is true as a fact about the world, but nothing in the theory lets an assessor who holds only those summers say it. Deutsch would reject the patch before any test (D p.25: "rejected out of hand without any experiment"), because "rain" could as well be "the late frost" or "the new compost".
- **Index:** same question, same index. [FOLLOWS] Every after-the-fact rule applies to indices, declared inputs, candidate identity or the record, never to the content of a part (01, A8).
- **Provenance:** constructed after a recognized difficulty (D3:L423). That is the normal route to Origin and (EK), not a mark against the patch (D3:L399–415).
- **Repair:** with O = "say which bed ripens first" and P = "keep the four earlier summers right", the patch repairs O and keeps P, so (P) holds for that agreement obligation (D3:L429–435). Protected obligations block only trade-off fixes, and this is not one.
- **Criticism:** [TEXT] "(E) does not exclude a true mechanism guessed for bad reasons; the reasons … are assessed elsewhere (Part IX)" (D3:L277), so "you fitted it to one spring" names no defect under (E). A criticism that supplies the separating change bears if it is right (Grievance 4, D3:L43).
- **Receipts:** the wet spring is a genuine record, and no rule discounts the data a part was fitted to (D3:L391).
- **Non-circular, non-vacuity:** both met. Rain is an independent condition, and deleting the part loses the contrast.
- **Result: blocked as a fact, unmarked in the record.** [INFERENCE] On a question left open, the fitted patch fails because it is unfaithful, not because it is easy to vary. Its swaps fail the same way, so the set of variants that stay accounts (Pres) is small.

### Situation 3. The same patch, with the question narrowed after the failure

*As in Situation 2, but she also says, after the failure: "My account is about this garden as the seasons come, with nobody changing the watering or the planting."*

- **Index:** [TEXT] a new claim at a new index (D3:L159), recorded. The old claim stays failed (D3:L363). Nothing forbids it: only an unrecorded change is goalpost-moving (D3:L602).
- **Does it pass (E)? Yes, in draft 3.** [FOLLOWS]
  - The contract now holds only the seasons as they came. Rain and the early variety came together once, so (F1) is checked only there, and the rain part matches the world (the north bed did ripen first in the wet spring). (F2) and (A) hold.
  - Non-circular dependence: draft 3's third sentence accepts a contrast from inputs only. The change list records this as a gain: "accounts of input-only contracts can now witness" (CL:L858), and O5 needed it (CL:L872). The wet-against-dry contrast is lost when the rain part is deleted, so the condition is met (D3:L255).
  - Non-vacuity: the exclusions are stated, and changes remain under which the parts matter (D3:L257).
  - [INFERENCE] In file 11 this is less certain. Its clause asked for a pair "that removes or replaces a nonempty block of Γ" (F11:L257), which input-only pairs meet "on one identification and not on another" (CL:L870).
- **Criticism cannot reach it.** [FOLLOWS] "It was the variety" needs a change that separates rain from the variety. The narrowed contract excludes every such change, so the criticism bears on the old, broad question, which "failed" (D3:L159). That is D3:L562 at work: the contract "does not contain the distinction".
- **Draft 3's marks:** [TEXT] (E) on the narrowed contract "does not certify the restriction as appropriate". With no ground stated, a verdict that turns on appropriateness has a missing input (D3:L159), and is unsettled (D3:L516).
- **Repair:** [FOLLOWS] if the knowledge obligation was fixed at the broad question ("fixed for the comparison", D3:L429), the narrowed claim does not meet it, and (EK) cannot be backdated (D3:L447). If the obligation is declared at the narrow question, (EK) can hold, and the fitted patch then counts as created explanatory knowledge.
- **Variability:** [FOLLOWS] every rival that co-occurred with this spring (frost, compost, variety) also stays an account, so Pres is large, and it "grades nothing" (D3:L313).
- **A run of such saves:** next year she can narrow again. Each narrowing is a new claim, and nothing reads the series. This is O1's pattern: SILENT.
- **Result: passes (E).** It is recorded as a new claim, and its appropriateness is unsettled. Nothing marks the failure as absorbed or reads a series of such saves.

### What the three situations show

- [FOLLOWS] **An idle part cannot save anything** (D3:L313; D p.24 agrees). The owner's worry, taken literally, is already answered.
- [FOLLOWS] **A part given work, or a new adjustable part, is caught in the verdict whenever the question is left open.** Fidelity is checked part by part over every change of the contract. But it is caught only as a fact that a later test would show. Nothing in the record marks it as fitted.
- [FOLLOWS] **Every successful save goes through the question, not the part.** Narrow the contract, stated, after the failure, and the fitted patch passes. The theory records the new claim and leaves its appropriateness unsettled. It neither blocks the save nor marks it, and it does not read a run of saves.
- [INFERENCE] So the theory corrects parts by fidelity and changes of question by records. The gap is where the record says what happened but not what it amounts to. This also means the change list's reason for N2, "the patch was fitted after the fact, which is the historical index" (CL:L1139), credits the index with a verdict it does not give. N2's No rests on (E) alone (01, Part C).

## 3. The sources' reason

- [TEXT] **Deutsch gives both kinds of reason and never ranks them** (02, section 5a).
  - *For correction:* the "fundamental flaw" is that "one could just as easily vary it to make different predictions" (D p.21). "Experimental testing is almost useless for correcting their errors. I call such theories bad explanations" (D p.22). "It is only when a theory is a good explanation – hard to vary – that it even matters whether it is testable" (D p.25). A refuted good explanation leaves its defenders "nowhere to go" (D p.25).
  - *For its own sake:* the glossary defines good and bad by variation alone (D p.31). Superfluous assumptions make a theory "bad by definition" (D p.26). Preferring one of many equal variants "is irrational" (D p.21). And we should choose "not on the basis of their origin, but according to how good they are as explanations: how hard to vary" (D p.209, checked in the text).
  - *Marletto* runs the arrow the other way: the finished work "is hard to change further … because it has been obtained by tentatively removing flaws" (M p.15). Correction produces the trait.
- [TEXT] Neither book makes hard-to-vary a condition of *being* an explanation: the myth "does constitute an explanation" (D p.19).
- [INFERENCE] **What follows on the correction reading.** What matters is what happens when an account fails: whether a cheap change can absorb the failure. Section 2 shows that the theory already stops a cheap change of a *part* on an open question, through fidelity. The theory needs no grade. It needs only to see a save made *through the question*, which is option (b). Idle parts are beside the point: they cannot absorb anything.
- [INFERENCE] **What follows on the own-sake reading.** The theory would need a grade, option (c), beside the yes-or-no test. The draft forbids one ("no merit function … a claim that needs one is unsettled", D3:L516; D3:L25). Even Deutsch keeps it beside the question of *being* an explanation, not inside it.
- [INFERENCE] The main argument of Deutsch's chapter 1 (pp.21–25) is the correction reading. D p.209 is the strongest line for the other reading. It also cuts against any history-based mark, which option (b) is.

## 4. Options

### (a) Stay neutral: no grading, no mark

- **Available only in part.** Section 2 shows that saves by changing a part are caught (Situations 1 and 2), but saves by changing the question are not (Situation 3).
- **Gained:** no new text or judgement; verdicts depend only on content, as D p.209 asks; the refusal of a merit function stands (D3:L25, L516).
- **Lost:** O1 stays SILENT. The retreat Deutsch describes (D p.22, "reinterpret the omens, pick a new date") has no counterpart. N2 rests on (E) alone, so CL:L1139's reason must be corrected. On a thin stated contract, N2 would turn on the grain reading of non-circular dependence and on a missing input.
- **Cases:** none move. N2's recorded reason changes.

### (b) A correction-based record, not a grade (recommended)

**Draft wording.** Part VIII, a new paragraph after "Historical index" (D3:L363):

> **Absorbed failure.** Let a claim fail at a pair of its contract, and let a later claim on the same question meet the conditions of Part V at that pair, or exclude it by a stated scope, because a limit was added or a component was added or changed. The later claim **absorbs** the failure when it states no ground for the change other than the failure it removes. A ground is a change in the later claim's own contract, other than the pairs that failed, at which a component of the claim, the added or changed one or the one from which the limit follows, makes a difference the claim predicts. The record keeps each absorbed failure with the index it left. An absorbed failure does not make the later claim false and does not stop it from being an account on its own contract. It is not a correction of the earlier claim until a ground is stated, and a ground stated later ends the mark from then on without backdating it. A run of absorbed failures on one question is a retreat on the record. A selection response is not a claim and absorbs nothing.

Two pointer sentences:
- Part III, at the end of D3:L159: "A narrowing that absorbs a failure is recorded as such (Part VIII)."
- Part VI, at the end of D3:L313: "What the measure bears on is correction: a commitment that does no work cannot absorb a failure, and a part varied or added to absorb one is recorded (Part VIII)."

**What kind of change it is.** A record of history, in the family of the historical index and Derivation 7. It is not a condition of (E), and no (E) verdict changes. It is not a Part VI measure, and it grades nothing. It reads the ground from the claim's own content, so it adds no declared input. A missing ground is recorded, not treated as unsettled. That leaves D3:L159's missing input about *appropriateness* as it is.

**Gained:**
- O1 becomes statable.
- N2 gets the history reason CL:L1139 already assumes.
- Situations 2 and 3 are marked, as Deutsch's pre-test rejection would mark them, but as a record rather than a verdict.
- Hard-to-vary is tied to correction, as D pp.21–25 tie it.
- The mark is keyed to history, not to looseness. That avoids the failure of the earlier clause A-prime: "a swap that works shows a limit is loose, not that it was fitted to failures; and the clause would refuse honest limits" (project story entry 29).

**Lost:**
- A new judgement, "is a ground stated?", with room for dispute, as "appropriate" had (entries 28–29).
- Identical final accounts can differ in record according to how they were reached. That goes against D p.209, though it matches the theory's own practice of telling provenance by history (F11:L15).
- It reaches only accounts that have failed. An easy-to-vary account that never failed (N3, N25) is judged by (E) alone.
- No computational correction lemma (W42; N20 unaffected).
- Blaming the background or the test (K3) stays open unless the paragraph is extended, with O27 as the guard.

**Cases** [INFERENCE, untested]:
- **O1** moves SILENT → toward, at least on "the series as a whole is a retreat". Whether "Bruno has no explanation" also moves is unclear.
- **O5** holds AGREE, on firmer text: her limit follows from the yeast part, which is a stated ground. S81 contested it (a retry ruled SILENT; S81 results row L352).
- **N2** holds, with its reason corrected.
- **O46** holds: the mark says the cable account is not a correction of the spring account, as its verdict does.
- **O45** holds: nothing was added or changed.
- **Guards, unmarked because no failure was absorbed:** O8, N6 (Iras), N7 (Maya).
- **O23** holds: the seal was measured, which is a ground.
- **Unaffected:** O27 (a re-test, not a change of claim); O76 (D3-T, selection); N1, N3, N25 (nothing absorbed); N18, N19.
- **Risk:** an honest, correct patch made with no ground stated (D3:L277's "true mechanism guessed for bad reasons") is marked "not yet a correction". Its standing as an account is untouched.

### (c) Full grading from Pres

- **Definition.** Fix a declared family V of variations of E and a set of jobs F. For candidates that share V, order them by inclusion of Pres(F) = the variants that stay accounts on every job in F: smaller means harder to vary. A number, or any comparison across different families, needs a measure on V.
- **Costs:**
  - V becomes an assessor's input, open to "choosing the input from the verdict wanted" (D3:L516).
  - A measure, or any cross-family comparison, is a merit function, which is "unsettled" in draft 3 (D3:L25, L516).
  - File 00 rejected the general comparison: "not a valid comparison between arbitrary contents" (F00:L1300).
  - Pres counts variants nobody wrote, against O36's verdict and D3:L299.
  - Pres does not see idle parts. Adding or removing one never changes (E) (D3:L313), so every account has those variants. Grading the sun god down would need a second measure, a count of commitments critical in no support.
  - On an open question, fidelity already keeps Pres small for every account (Situation 2). A Pres grade would mostly re-measure how narrow the question is, which the index already records.
  - It says nothing about how an account was saved, which is the source's main concern.
- **Gained:** Deutsch's comparative usage ("most constrained", D p.26; D p.306; D p.342) gets a home.
- **Cases** [INFERENCE]: possibly toward on N25's second question and N7's "fuller" (though that is reach, not variation). Risk away on O36, and on O8 and N7 if limits are counted as variants.

### Recommendation: (b)

The owner's condition decides it. Section 2 shows that the theory already corrects a fitted *part* by fidelity, so grading is not needed for correction. The one unguarded route is a save through the *question*. That is exactly where the source's reason for hard-to-vary applies: testing is useless when a failure can be absorbed. (b) closes that route with a record. It keeps the test yes-or-no, adds no merit function, is keyed to history rather than looseness, and makes O1 statable. Take (c) only if hard-to-vary is wanted for its own sake. The sources support that reading less, and the draft's own rules forbid a grade.

## 5. The one decision

When a failed account is rescued by a change whose only reason is the failure it removes (a new limit on the question, or a new or re-set part), should the theory's record say so, and call a run of such rescues a retreat?

- **Yes** is option (b): a record, no grade, and the yes-or-no test unchanged.
- **No** is option (a): the theory judges only whether the rescued account is faithful on its new question, and Bruno (O1) stays without a verdict.

Grading is a separate choice, needed only if hard-to-vary is wanted for its own sake.
