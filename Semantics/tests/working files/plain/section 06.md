## 6. Narrowed questions, and why a failed answer stays failed

### Example: Greta's dough

Greta's rule says her bread dough doubles in two hours. It fails in January. She restates it for rooms warmer than twenty degrees, and says why: the yeast that makes the gas works slowly in the cold. "On days other than Tuesdays" would come with no such why.

The **fixed verdict**: a legitimate narrowing; the limit follows from a piece of her explanation. The **ruling** on file 11: the theory **agrees**, though contested. In the **round**, four of five views found it **silent**, and one **outside reader** (an AI cross-examiner) called the ruling wrong; a second look upheld it, at medium confidence.

### The idea in plain words

A narrowed question is a different question and rescues no failed answer. A limit is recorded, not vouched for. A wrong answer at a setting stays wrong there, whoever gives it.

### What the theory says

#### A limit makes a different question

Greta's first rule covered every kitchen; her restated rule, warm ones. A question's **range** is the settings it covers (a **setting**: one allowed change in given circumstances), chosen from the **possible** changes, those physics allows; a stated **limit**, saying which are left out, makes it a choice.

An **account** (a **candidate**, an explanation offered for a question, meeting section 2's four requirements) answers the question within its stated limit, not a broader one that failed. A **narrowing**, a limit added after a failure, makes a **different question**; the January failure stands on the first.

#### Recorded, not vouched for

Greta's restated rule may meet all four requirements on warm rooms without showing twenty degrees is the right line. What makes a limit right belongs to the claim, open to criticism; the theory records the limit, and nothing in the theory vouches for it.

Where a claim gives its **stated reason for the limit**, a verdict on the limit comes with that reason. Greta's is tied to a piece of her explanation, the yeast, whose behaviour changes in the cold; on draft 4's view, untried in a round, "legitimate" comes with it. "Not on Tuesdays" gives none. Where none is given and a verdict turns on one, the reason is a **missing input** and the verdict **unsettled**; the theory never picks a reason to suit the verdict wanted.

The limit belongs to the claim's **frame**, what it is relative to (level of detail, boundary, sameness rule, range), neither true nor false. It is also a **stated input**, recorded and never supplied by the theory. A case turning on a missing one is no **defeat** (a finding that would show the theory wrong).

#### Not "anything goes"

Suppose Greta had silently dropped January. The fourth **complaint** the theory expects: if everything is relative to a range, nothing is objective. The answer: physics fixes what is possible, a range is a stated choice from it, and within it whether a candidate **matches** (behaves as the world does at every setting) is a fact. A silent leaving-out is caught, not by judging motives, but by **no dodging** (section 2: every possible change left out must be left out by a stated limit; a candidate whose only real contrast no possible change brings about fails it too), then by any criticism naming the change. Honesty about limits lies in the **record**.

#### Example: the forecaster who keeps finding honest limits

Bruno's rule predicts when the village stream floods. It fails in March; he restates it for months outside the thaw. It fails after a dam release; he adds "no release". It fails again; he adds "west wind". Anyone can check each limit, each restated rule has been right so far, and he records every failure openly.

The fixed verdict: "Bruno has no explanation of the floods. Each limit is honest taken alone, and the series as a whole is a retreat." The ruling found files 10 and 11 silent.

#### A failed answer stays failed

In March the stream, the question's **target**, did not do what Bruno's first rule said. Any candidate giving that answer there is no account of the flood question: the first rule, the same rule offered again, a rival offered in its place, or a changed candidate keeping that answer. Answering the question asked, required at every setting, settles this for every question with the same target and **what is asked** (the exact thing read off the target) whose range holds March.

Once a **judge** holds a **usable receipt** for the stream's March answer (reasoning over evidence the judge may use, section 5), every such candidate's failure is **established** for that judge, each by its answer there, with no record of who failed before or how anyone changed a candidate.

**Blame is shared**: the March test counts against a candidate only with its other assumptions and instruments. If one is no longer **still held** (say the gauge proves faulty), the failure stops being established for all alike, though none is thereby shown to be an account; what lapses is the establishing, not the fact.

Leaving March out, or changing what is asked, makes a different question, whose account does not answer the first; the failure stands, and the first rule stays the claim it was: **a claim keeps its frame** when a later theory changes the range in use.

Your decision of 24–25 September on hard to vary called a record redundant: once an explanation is rescued, "the mistake shouldn't be able to creep back in". Draft 4 shows why on a fixed question: only a receipt for what the target did is needed.

#### Bruno, on draft 4's view

On draft 4's view, untried in a round, the verdict's first half now follows: each restated rule answers a different question, not when the stream floods, and the flood failures stand, so Bruno has no account of the floods. The second half does not: nothing in the theory looks at a series of narrowings as a whole, and his limits give no reason, so a verdict on them is unsettled.

#### Fixed judging, moving questions

Greta's first rule is no account on the all-kitchens range, and may be one on warm rooms. The tenth complaint calls freezing the question while judging, yet letting questions change, having it both ways. The theory agrees, on purpose, keeping the two apart.

An **assessment**, one act of judging a candidate, holds its range fixed. An **episode**, a stretch of inquiry from a difficulty to a response, can replace the range with one built with **construction evidence**, a record of how it was built, each change carrying its **history**. The theory proves the two consistent: "an account on the old range" and "not an account on the different one" are claims in different frames, and can both be true.

So where an explanation meets the world moves between episodes, because ranges are built, never within an assessment, where changing the range or what is asked unrecorded is forbidden (section 1). **Moving the goalposts**, changing a claim's frame without saying so, is a failure of the record, not permitted by the theory.

### What changed in this revision

- On limits: meeting the requirements on a narrowed range does not vouch for the limit; a verdict on it comes with the stated reason, and without one the reason is a missing input.
- The paragraph "A failed answer stays failed".
- The list of defeats now excludes a case with a missing input.

**Departure**: both source books say a limit must itself be explained; here it is recorded, not vouched for.

### What is open

- Bruno's "retreat" is not settled.
- The failed-answer paragraph says nothing beyond the failed setting: other settings (the same mistake at a neighbouring setting goes unnoticed until tested there), runs of rescues, changes of level of detail, a coarser what-is-asked the same receipt would settle.
- "The stated reason for the limit" is undefined; the text never says if it means the **grounds** of a criticism (section 9).
- Your decision calls a record of rescues redundant; the analysis of 24 September found a record still tells a retreat from a limit honest from the start. Draft 4 does not settle this.
- The outside readers have not seen the failed-answer paragraph; no round has used it.
