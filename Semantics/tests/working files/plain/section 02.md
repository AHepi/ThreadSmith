## 2. When an explanation counts: the four requirements

### The example: the tide table with a real bell attached

Carla is asked why the harbour water is high at noon today. She offers two things together: the printed tide table, and a real float that, rising past a mark, tips a lever and rings the harbour bell. Take the float away and her explanation no longer says the bell will ring, so something in it seems to make a difference.

The **fixed verdict**, written in advance: Carla has explained the bell and nothing about the tide; the table is the answer written down.

### The idea in plain words

Any explanation offered for a question, even a false one, is a **candidate**. It is an **account** when it meets four **requirements**, all about what happens when things are changed: its pieces behave as the world's do; it gives the world's answer; it does not assume that answer; and it does not quietly leave out awkward changes.

### What the theory says

#### A candidate, its link and its commitments

A candidate for the pole and its shadow says that sunlight fixes the shadow's length from the heights of pole and sun. Its **link** from the world says which state goes with which, which change with which, and which circumstances with which. It ties each of the candidate's pieces to an **anchor**, the **cluster** (connected bunch) of the world's pieces that it must behave like, with a **slot pairing** saying which slot goes with which. The link **carries over** each setting of the world to the candidate's.

A candidate, then, is a set-up, a link to it from the **target** (the set-up in the world the question is about), and its **commitments**: the pieces it offers as doing the work, whether or not anyone has described their work. The rest is **background**, held fixed: the values of its circumstances and its other pieces, including any that just gives an input its value. Here sunlight is the commitment; the pieces putting in the two heights are background.

#### The four requirements

All four must hold at every **setting** (one allowed change, in given circumstances) of the question's **range** (the settings it covers). None inspects a label.

1. **Matching**, in two halves. **Piece-by-piece matching**: each commitment allows exactly what its anchor allows, once the anchor's **hidden slots** (those the commitment does not mention) are set aside. **Whole matching**: the world's **consistent states** (those all its pieces allow at once), carried over, are exactly the candidate's, and chains of changes carry over as chains. Raise the pole, and in both the shadow lengthens. The first half stops a whole that behaves right from hiding a wrong split into pieces; the second stops pieces that each behave right from hiding a lost tie between them. Piece-by-piece matching already makes each commitment respond as its anchor does, which is all "the same kind" means here (section 3).

2. **Answering the question asked.** At every setting the candidate gives the target's **answer**, with **what is asked** (the exact thing read off the target) held fixed. An account of something else asked is no account of this.

3. **No assumed answer**, in two halves. First, the answer must come from working the candidate out, in circumstances independent of it. The target's answer may not appear, at the stated **level of detail** (how finely things are described), as an unexplained input or as a piece, even one called a "law". Sameness with the answer is judged by form at that level of detail, not by counting mathematical truths that imply one another as one. Second, some setting's answer must differ from that at the **untouched setting** (no change, ordinary circumstances), or not be fixed there in the way claimed, and removing some **group** of commitments must lose that contrast. A **removed piece** restricts nothing: without the group, the two answers come out fixed and equal, or one is fixed no longer.

   Carla fails the first half: her only piece about the tide is the table, the answer written in. Her bell mechanism, answering a different question, does not repair this, and removing it loses no difference in the tide. The **ruling** on file 11 (the project's settled view of what the theory says) agrees. In draft 4, since no group of her commitments makes an account (she has no **support**, section 4), whether the bell is an **idle commitment**, making no difference added or removed, does not arise.

4. **No dodging.** The target has a consistent state at the untouched setting, and the range is a stated selection from the **possible** changes (those the laws of physics allow): each possible change it leaves out is left out by a stated **limit**. A range of mere **renaming changes**, or one leaving out every change where the commitments could matter, fails the third requirement.

#### What the requirements exclude

- A **table of observed answers** has no piece that responds when the world is changed, so it fails piece-by-piece matching on any range holding such a change. A **full response table**, recording the response to every allowed change, passes that half, and is an account if it meets the rest too.
- A **calculation run backwards** (the pole's height worked out from the shadow and offered as what produces it) fails whole matching on the **producing question**, whose range holds changes to the pole and sun: raise the pole, and the world's shadow lengthens, the calculation's does not. It may match on the **pinning-down question**, "given this shadow, how tall is the pole?", a different question.
- "It is so because it is so" fails no assumed answer, as Carla does.
- A candidate whose only real contrast no possible change brings about fails no dodging: its range was narrowed silently.

#### What they do not exclude

Ines says the ice on her step melted because she spread salt. Spread salt and it melts within the hour; sweep it off at once and it stays. She knows nothing finer and uses no instrument. The fixed verdict: a genuine explanation, a coarse one; the salt produced the melting. The ruling on file 11: the theory agrees, since a coarse account is an account of the coarse question, its strength fixed by its range, not by what a finer range would add. Draft 4 states the reason more directly (its view, a move toward the fixed verdict, not yet tried in a **round** of the cases): the piece that puts the salt on or sweeps it off just gives an input its value, so it is background; the salt's action is the commitment, and removing it loses the difference.

Nor do the requirements exclude a true mechanism guessed for bad reasons (the reasons are judged in section 9), or prefer an elegant account to a less elegant one that matches as well. A proof meets the four for "why does this follow, given these starting points?" without answering "what caused this?". Depth is relative to the question; the four measure none.

#### Mathematics: the odd-sized tables

Why can no square table of numbers with an odd number of rows, each number the negative of its mirror image across the diagonal, be undone by another table? The range drops the mirroring, the oddness, or both, with arithmetic held fixed. Working out in full the one number that says whether a table can be undone meets all four. It matches: the theory proves this; the proof is technical. Each change in the range brings in a table that can be undone, so it gives the answer and assumes none: without the mirroring, the three-row table with ones down the diagonal and noughts elsewhere; without the oddness, a two-row mirrored table with a one and a minus one. Such tables exist, so it does not dodge. A three-line proof is shorter; shortness is not a fifth requirement.

#### Explanations of absence

An old house creaks at night, and the family says it is haunted. "Why is there no ghost effect here?" asks for an **explanation of absence**. Its target lacks the pieces a **rival** (a candidate offered in its place, section 5) would need; its range holds changes that would add them. The account matches if adding them would change its answer as it would the target's, and their absence leaves both unchanged. The rival's ghost is tied to a removed cluster, which restricts nothing. The theory calls this adequate, but names it as a place where the requirements may wrongly exclude a real explanation (section 11).

Why the family seems to hear footsteps is a different question, which an account of the absence need not answer. A **bare denial** ("there is no ghost; you are mistaken") offers no piece that responds to a change in what produces the appearance, so it is no account of that question. **Departure** from the source books: Deutsch asks an explanation that denies something to explain why it seems to exist.

#### Two principles behind the requirements

Whether a link matches is a fact, accepted or not; a system can be wrong about its links, its **readings** (slots that report others), its criticisms and what it can do. A theory may hold a correct dependence within its range and errors elsewhere; what cannot count as explanation is an error in the very dependence said to do the work. "Explanation" there means an account; a false theory offered as an answer is a candidate, though ordinary speech may call it an explanation. **Departure**: Deutsch counts a false myth as an explanation. Whether an account is **easy to vary** is a separate matter (section 5).

### What changed in this revision

- Commitments are defined, and the rest is background.
- The third requirement now removes a group of commitments; file 11 asked for a change in the range that itself removed some, and on one view the pole and the salt had none. Sameness with the answer is judged by form.
- A full response table is an account only if it meets the rest; file 11 called it one outright.
- Why an absent thing seems present is a separate question; a bare denial is no account of it.
- The principle on errors now says that "explanation" there means an account, a false theory being a candidate.

### What is open

- Nobody has yet offered a candidate that meets all four and plainly explains nothing; an **outside reader** (one of two AI systems outside the project) tried and offered none. One would **defeat** the first of the theory's five **main claims**, those it names for attack: that the four are enough (section 11).
- No round has tried the treatment of absence, which may be inadequate; a **proposed case** built on it, "Denying the inner experience", waits for one.
- The text calls commitments "active" without defining the word. If that means only "the commitments", background pieces are matched only as a whole.
