## 7. Narrowed questions, and why a failed answer stays failed

### Greta's dough

Greta's rule says her dough doubles in two hours. It fails in January, and she restates it for rooms above twenty degrees, saying why: the yeast works slowly in the cold. "On days other than Tuesdays" would come with no such why. *Fixed verdict:* a legitimate narrowing; the limit follows from a piece of her explanation.

### Limits

"For rooms above twenty degrees" is a limit: it says which possible changes Greta's range leaves out (cold rooms). A range is a stated selection from the possible changes, and a stated limit is what makes it one. An account at a stated limit answers the question at that limit. It does not answer a broader question that failed.

A **narrowing** is a limit added after a failure. It makes a different claim in a different **frame**. A frame is what a claim is relative to: its level of detail, its range, and, for a claim about a system's ability, the system's boundary and sameness rule (section 11). A frame is neither true nor false; it is chosen and stated.

What makes a limit right for the question belongs to the claim, and is open to criticism. The theory records the limit and gives no rule that certifies it as right. Meeting the requirements on the narrowed range does not certify it as right either. (The fourth requirement does check two things: that the limit is stated, and that the range is not left empty.)

Where the claim states its reason for the limit, the theory's verdict on the limit is given with that reason. Where the claim states none, and a verdict turns on one, the reason is a **missing input**, and the verdict is **unsettled**. The theory never picks a reason to suit the verdict wanted.

**Back to Greta.** *Ruled on file 11: agrees*, though contested. Four of the five marks in the round were silent, one outside reader called the ruling wrong, and a fresh reading of the texts upheld it, though not with full confidence (section 1 says how rulings are made). Greta states her reason, and it is tied to a piece of her explanation, the yeast; "not on Tuesdays" states none. Draft 4 now says outright that a verdict on a limit comes with the stated reason. Her narrowed claim is still a different question, and the January failure stands on the first one.

A limit stated from the start is no narrowing. In **Two bakers**, Maya and Bea both explain why dough rises: yeast gives off gas, which the stretchy dough traps. Maya adds limits of 18 to 35 degrees, found by experience; Bea gives the same limits and also says why. *Fixed verdict:* both explain; Bea also explains the limits.

*Not yet ruled.* Maya's limits are an honest stated range. Bea's candidate is also an account of the further question why the rising stops outside the limits. The two conflict nowhere, so they are not rivals. That would change only if Maya's explanation said something outside her limits that conflicted with Bea's there.

The fourth complaint says that if everything is relative to a range, nothing is objective. But the physics fixes what is possible, and within a stated range there is a fact about matching. A range that quietly leaves out changes to protect an account is caught by the fourth requirement, that every exclusion be stated, and then by any criticism that supplies the change left out. Objectivity lies in the physics and in matching. Honesty about limits lies in stating them openly. (That is a different thing from your "record", which was a record of past failures and rescues.)

### A failed answer stays failed

**The forecaster who keeps finding honest limits.** Bruno's rule for when the village stream floods fails in March, and he limits it to months outside the thaw. It fails after a dam release upstream, and he limits it to days without one. It fails again, and he limits it to days with a west wind. Each limit is described in ordinary terms anyone can check, each narrowed rule has so far been right inside its limits, and he records every failure openly. *Fixed verdict:* "Bruno has no explanation of the floods. Each limit is honest taken alone, and the series as a whole is a retreat."

Draft 4 adds a paragraph, the theory's form of your "the mistake shouldn't be able to creep back in". Take Bruno's first rule, on the question "when does the stream flood?", whose range holds that day in March. On that day the rule gave an answer that the stream did not give. Then any candidate that gives that same answer there is no account of that question. That holds whether it is the rule that failed, the same rule offered again, a rival, or a changed rule that keeps that answer on that day. It holds too on every question with the same target and the same thing asked whose range holds that day.

Once a judge has established what the stream did there, this is established for that judge of every such candidate alike, from the answer each gives there. No record is needed of who failed before, or of how anything was changed.

Because blame is shared (section 6), the finding can lapse. If an assumption behind the test that established it, about its background or its instruments, stops being held, then the exclusion lapses for all such candidates alike. None of them is thereby shown to be an account. What lapses is the establishing, not the fact.

A range that leaves the failed setting out, or a change in what is asked, makes a different question, and the failure on the first question stands. **A claim keeps its frame**: it stays the claim it was, even when later thinking changes which range is in use.

**The test that fitted neither.** A remote expert sends a robot two possible diagnoses and the one test that would separate them. The robot runs the test, and the result fits neither. It notices that the test assumed a pressure sensor was reading true, swaps to a second sensor, runs the test again, and finds a third fault that neither diagnosis named. *Fixed verdict:* the achievement is mostly the robot's. *Ruled on file 11: silent*, on "mostly" (section 10). On the test itself, *not yet ruled*: once the sensor is in doubt, the exclusion of both diagnoses reopens for both alike, and the rerun establishes a further result.

**Back to Bruno.** *Ruled on file 11: silent. Not yet ruled on draft 4.* The first half of the verdict now follows. Each narrowed rule has a narrower range, so it is a different question, which does not answer when the stream floods, and the failures on that question stand. The second half does not follow. Nothing in the theory judges a series of narrowings. And Bruno's limits say where his rule applies, not why it fails outside them: the thaw and the dam release are where it failed, not a reason drawn from a piece of his explanation, as Greta's yeast is. So a verdict on his limits lacks a stated input.

So, on a fixed question, a correction sticks with no record of past narrowings, only a receipt for what the target did. That is how draft 4 renders your "the mistake shouldn't be able to creep back in". It does not reach other settings: the same mistake at a neighbouring setting goes unnoticed until that setting is tried. Nor does it reach a series of narrowings, a change of level of detail, or a coarser thing asked. The project's analysis of 24 September, on whether correction sticks, found that a record still does one job: telling a retreat like Bruno's from a limit that was honest from the start. Draft 4 does not settle whether that job is needed.

### Fixed judging, moving questions

The tenth complaint says that holding the question fixed while judging, yet letting questions change, is having it both ways. It is, on purpose, and the two are kept apart. An assessment holds its range fixed. An episode of inquiry can replace a range with a constructed one, provided every change is recorded.

The theory's **proof that fixed judging and moving questions agree** shows these are consistent. "An account on the old range" and "not an account on the replacement range" are claims in different frames, and both can be true. **Moving the goalposts**, changing a claim's frame without saying so, is a failure of the record, not something the theory permits.

A case whose verdict turns on one of the stated inputs listed in section 12, or on the standard of worth, when the case does not state it, is a case with a missing input, not a refutation.

*Departure:* both source books hold that a limit of scope needs its own explanation. Here a limit is recorded, and not certified as right.

## 8. Where links come from: selection, pictures and surprise

### The discarded lamp controller

Each lamp controller has two switches, A and B. A testing machine keeps a controller if, with B left off, its lamp is dark with A off and lit with A on; it discards the rest. Only two designs exist, and both can be built. The machine never tries the setting with both switches on, and the two designs behave differently there. One design also fails a setting the machine does try: its lamp stays dark with A on. It was discarded, and the other kept. Ivo reasons that since the two designs differ at the untried setting, and the machine never looks there, passing cannot tell you what a kept controller does there; he points to the discarded design as proof. *Fixed verdict (high confidence):* no. The discarded design failed at a setting the machine does try, so it says nothing about controllers that pass. Passing tells you which design you have, and that design does one definite thing with both switches on. Ivo would need two designs that both pass and still differ.

### Carriers, contents and two layers

Sam's key diagram on paper, and the same diagram turned into raised marks that can be read by touch, are two carriers of one content. A **carrier** is a physical thing or event, at a place and time, that bears content. A **content** is a set-up together with what it claims on its range.

Carriers are not the same because they carry the same words: two copies of one page are two carriers. And contents are not the same because they give the same outputs: the parallel and priority wiring of section 5 give the same outputs with different pieces.

A **recoding** writes a carrier's content in another code, with the key stated. It keeps the content when a reader using the key recovers every pairing of the original, such as which tooth of a key goes with which catch in the lock. In **Mirrored marks**, Sam's diagram is turned into raised marks by a reader who writes right to left, with the convention stated on the sheet. *Fixed verdict:* the content was kept, and anyone using the convention recovers every pairing. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

The theory describes a system as having at least two set-ups, called layers.

- In the **thing layer**, the slots are lasting things with edges and identity. The pieces are the **threads** that keep each thing the same thing from moment to moment, such as a ball's path as it rolls. The allowed changes include **moving** a thing, **hiding** it, and **telling which is which again**. It is the layer at which there are objects, rather than a blur of sensation.
- The **expectation layer** sits over it. Its pieces are dependences among things, and it makes predictions.

The two layers are a description the theory uses to say what it would be for a system to have objects and expectations. The theory does not claim that any real system has them.

To **picture** something is, roughly, for a carrier to stand for it by matching it, with a history of the right sort. The exact sense comes below, after the three histories.

### Three histories of a link

A link between set-ups of a physical system has exactly one of three histories, fixed by what physically happened.

- **Selected.** There is a **pool** of candidate links. There is **variation**, a physically possible process that makes fresh members of the pool. There are **trials**, the settings the link actually met while being shaped. And the surroundings decide what survives: a link **survives** when it is in the pool and matches on every trial. Nothing in the trials pictures the link, the trials, or what counts as survival. The pool belongs to the claim: it is the candidate links that the physics and the **stated design** (how the members are built) allow, not only ones that were ever built.
- **Constructed.** The link is made in an episode of inquiry (section 10) that prepares it, as its construction evidence shows (section 9). In that episode the system pictures the link, or what the link leads to. Episodes of inquiry hold criticism, so a constructed link has both a pictured target and criticism in its history.
- **Declared.** Written into the model by its author. The theory allows this only as a convenience for building models, and no claim of creativity may rest on it.

Selected links are to be expected at the thing layer and constructed ones above it, though this is not required. Construction can work on selected material, and selection can go on beneath it. Neither history reduces to the other: a selected link has no pictured target and no criticism in its history, and a constructed one has both. Creativity lives in construction; selection supplies its raw material.

**Dark moths.** Where smoke has blackened the tree bark, moths become mostly dark-winged over generations, as birds eat more of the pale ones. *Fixed verdict:* the wing colour, inherited and put there by selection, tells the bark's colour, though no moth has any idea of it. *Not yet ruled:* this holds under draft 4's wording of selection.

Two complaints are answered here. To "'selected' is simply laid down, like 'is a cause'": selection is a physical history, and whether it happened can be checked, at least in principle; a kind-label claims nothing. To "you have replaced explanation with evolution": selection appears once, at the bottom, producing the thing layer that explanation works on, and every claim of creativity needs construction.

### Pictures

Take the dark moths again (this document's illustration). Their inherited wing colour is a carrier. At the level of detail of "dark or pale", the set-up it embodies (dark wings where the bark is dark) has a link to the content "the bark here is dark". That link matches on the content's range, blackened bark or clean, and its history is selection. So the wing colour pictures the bark's colour.

In general, a physical thing **embodies** a set-up when, described at some level of detail, it has that set-up. A carrier **pictures** a content, at a level of detail, when the set-up it embodies at that level has a link to the content that matches on the content's range, with a history of selection or construction. So what a carrier stands for is a fact about matching with a history, not something given.

- A system can picture a false explanation: the link from carrier to content matches, while the content's link to the world fails.
- A declared link makes no picture. It makes only a modeller's claim that there is one.
- A carrier keeps its history when access to it is lost. A later record worked out from it is no second, independent witness to that history (Sam's diary, section 6).
- Which set-up a physical carrier embodies is the one thing here taken from outside: the physics supplies it.

**The card nobody can read.** A forecast card's ink has faded, and the one person who could read its raised marks by touch has died. A copy made earlier gives its content. *Fixed verdict:* the marks still say what they said; the card's history is intact, and access to it is not. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

In **Half from the source, half from the shelf**, a copier fills one damaged patch of a diagram from an old stored diagram that happens to agree with the source. *Fixed verdict:* that patch's agreement is luck, and the record says which lines came from where. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.* Whatever is filled into a carrier from another source keeps that source's history.

### Expectation, miss and surprise

**Two footbridges on opening day.** Rhea designed her footbridge from a worked-out theory of sway, calculated for crowds of the size that came. Dov copied an eighty-year-old village footbridge, not knowing it had never carried more than a few dozen people at once. Under large opening-day crowds both bridges sway badly. *Fixed verdict:* the swaying went against what both expected, but contradicted only what Rhea had reason to expect.

The theory's words for this. For a link to the expectation layer, at a setting that actually happens:

- the **expectation** is what that layer gives;
- a **miss** is the link failing to match there;
- a **surprise** is a miss by a selected link at an **untried setting**, one not among its trials.

With no link, or with trials that cover the whole range, there can be no surprise. A constructed link that fails has a miss, not a surprise. A miss that the system pictures can be a noticed difficulty (section 10).

*Not yet ruled.* Rhea's link was constructed, and its range covered that crowd, so she has a miss that contradicts her grounds. Dov's copy is taken as a link shaped by the village bridge's eighty years of use, which serve as its trials; that is why it counts as selected, not constructed. The large crowd was a setting outside those trials, so Dov has a surprise, contradicting nothing he had grounds for. The list of changes marks this half as watched: it depends on taking Dov's link as selected, and a reader might not.

A miss by a selected link can be met by **re-tuning**: adding the setting to the trials, and letting variation adjust the link within its pool. Any miss can be met by a **response by construction**: building a fresh set-up or link. Only the second can originate anything (section 9).

### What survival leaves open

The theory's **proof on what survival leaves open** says this. Suppose some other member of the pool also survived the trials, and behaves differently at an untried setting. Then the trials leave the selected link's value there **left open**. An untried setting alone does not show that such a survivor exists: it must be in the pool, and it must survive. Where there is none, the pool itself fixes the value.

So a selected link matches where it was tried and, wherever its pool allows an alternative, is unconstrained elsewhere. That is why the thing layer can be wrong, and why surprise can happen. The older blanket claim, that every untried value is open, is given up (section 12, "The forbidden wire").

**Back to the lamp controller.** *Not yet ruled.* The pool is the two designs, and the trials are the settings the machine tries. The discarded design failed a trial, so it did not survive. "Both on" is not left open: passing tells you which design you have. In the terms of section 6, the discarded design does not fit what is established, so no problem arises. Whether one could arise is a fact about the pool, not about which designs anyone points to.

This answers the third complaint, that selection makes truth a matter of survival. Survival is how a link got there; whether it matches at changes it never met is a fact about the link and the world. The theory's **proof on surprise** adds that a system can be surprised only if it holds a selected link whose trials fall short of the settings the world allows. Surprise is not a feeling added to the theory. Re-tuning and construction are the difference between learning and creating, and they are told apart by their evidence, not their outcomes.

If a programmer designed the pool, success at untried settings reflects the programmer's knowledge. The theory cannot yet say so (section 14).

### The hidden thing: the theory's worked example

A row of cells holds two things, each with a position and a speed. The allowed changes are: move a thing, set its speed, hide a cell behind a screen, and swap the two things. The senses show only which cells are filled, not which thing fills them.

An expectation layer was selected on trials of moves and speed changes, but never of hiding. It predicts filled cells from recently filled ones. Then a thing passes behind a screen and keeps moving. The layer expects nothing there, and misses when the thing comes out where its speed puts it: a surprise. Re-tuning fails if every member of the pool predicts from filled cells alone: no member survives the wider trials, so the fault is in how the whole pool is built, not in any one member.

Construction builds a fresh layer, with a **tracker** for each thing: a piece that carries the thing's position and speed through the time it is hidden, linked to that thing's thread. The fresh layer matches on the wider range, and by the proof that kinds add nothing, the trackers have the response pattern of things. Nobody declared them "objects"; they are objects because they respond as objects do. If the system built the layer in a stretch of events that it owns (section 11), with a real join of tracker to thread (section 9), and the layer was new to it, then it originated the layer. Section 10 returns to this example, as created knowledge.

Swapping the two things changes nothing the senses show. Take a range that allows each change for both things alike. Swap which thread each tracker is tied to, and you get a second link. Swapping the two things carries the first link's matching and answers over to the second link: that is the **proof on rewriting**, below. Then pair each tracker in the first link with its swapped partner in the second: each pair is tied to one thread, so they have the same anchors, and by the proof on same anchors (section 6), so far as matching and answering go, the two are one account. "Tracker one is really thing one" needs a change that tells them apart, and on this range there is none: at this level of detail, which thing is which is nothing more than its path.

The example shows that the theory's definitions can all hold at once. It is not a claim about any infant, animal or program.

**Nadia and the cards she has never seen.** Nadia learns a card game only by watching cards played. For weeks she predicts the next card from those just played, and is often surprised. Then she starts keeping a tally of cards "waiting somewhere", and her predictions improve sharply. *Fixed verdict:* "Nadia built something new: a part that stands for cards she has never observed. That is construction, and it is her own." *Ruled on file 11: agrees.* The tally is a response by construction. A first picture can be constructed from a challenge, without ever observing what it pictures. Whether her early misses were surprises in the theory's sense does not affect the verdict. "Her own" is taken up in section 11.

The theory's **proof on rewriting**: **rewriting** everything (carriers, behaviours, links, histories, ranges) by renaming piece for piece, with every connection kept, keeps accounts, originations, repairs and created knowledge. It does not cover **blurring** (describing with less detail), changed boundaries, or descriptions that no longer tell which event is which.

### Departures

- Deutsch calls criticism and experiment a sort of selection; here selection has no pictured target.
- For Deutsch a problem is a situation in which conflicting ideas are experienced, and it can arise with no observation. Here surprise is kept for selected links. A problem for a question, in section 6's sense, is narrower than his: two rivals that both fit what is established. A problem in his wider sense can be a noticed difficulty (section 10).

## 9. Building something new: understanding, construction and finding questions

### The apprentice and the third lock

On Monday an apprentice tries keys from a ring until one opens the lock. On Tuesday, at another lock, she feels where a blank catches and files it until it turns. On Wednesday, at a third lock, she picks a key from the ring at once, saying it looks like Tuesday's. *Fixed verdict:* "Monday is blind fitting. Tuesday is making something new. Wednesday is neither: she is reusing what Tuesday gave her."

### Understanding

**The phrasebook.** After a month abroad, Kofi can order the same dishes with his phrasebook shut. Asked for a sentence that was never in the book, he has nothing to say. *Fixed verdict:* "Kofi has kept a narrow skill by his own effort. He has yet to understand the language." *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

A system **understands** a content when three things hold. It holds a picture of the content. It uses that picture in its work on challenges. And it keeps that use as a **lasting** ability for a stated **use task**, the task by which a claim of understanding is judged. For Kofi, ordering those dishes is one use task, and he keeps it. Saying new sentences would be another, and he does not have it.

A system's **stock**, at a moment, is the contents it understands for some real use. A system may understand a false explanation. A narrow lasting use is just that: it shows neither a wider understanding, nor that the wider one can never come.

### Construction

Take the apprentice's Tuesday. She feels where the blank catches, forms a picture of what the lock needs, and files the blank to fit it. That stretch of events is hers, it prepares a pictured set-up (the shape the key must have) for use in explaining why a key turns, and it holds a real join, between her picture of the lock and the shape she files.

In general, **construction** is an actual **stretch** of events, with a start and a stated end, owned by the system (section 11). It prepares a pictured set-up for explaining with the content. It holds a real **join**, a connection the system makes, relevant to that use. And it is not merely a chain of passings-on that keep the content as it was.

**Construction evidence** names the processes, the carriers that came in, the joins made and the picture that came out. **Reconstruction**, working received content out afresh, is construction. **Relay**, passing content on without making any join, is not. A small join made inside received content is construction of that join, and the rest keeps its **inherited history**.

**The parrot in the lecture hall.** A parrot repeats a professor's sentences word for word, answers questions about them with another sentence or a squawk, and would have repeated recipes as readily. A student learns the ideas by working out what the parrot's sentences mean. *Fixed verdict:* the parrot has only the sounds; in the student's learning it was a carrier. *Not yet ruled:* the parrot relays, since its responses follow wording, not content; the student reconstructs.

**The one tooth.** A learner receives a full key diagram from Sam, finds a catch it never covered, files one tooth for it, and keeps the rest as given. *Fixed verdict:* the tooth is hers; the rest is relay. *Ruled on file 11: agrees*, on the sentence about small joins, which file 11 added.

### Unspoken pictures

**The order of adjectives.** Ngozi always says "a lovely little old green French knife", never another order, even in phrases she has never heard. She cannot state any rule, and nobody taught her one. *Fixed verdict:* she knows the rule tacitly, and formed her grasp of it herself.

The theory's own illustrations are like this: a pianist imagines a passage without playing it; a geometer handles a spatial relation without naming every piece; an investigator notices an inconsistency before she can say what it rests on. An **unspoken picture** is not an absent one. Picturing, understanding and construction ask for no written form. They ask only whether the distinctions and moves are there, with matching and history, and are used where use is asked.

A picture can be partial, spread out, or drawn out over time. It can be kept in memory, imagery, rehearsed action, or a notebook. Keeping a criticized item in mind can mean being able to bring back the distinction that matters, rather than storing every word.

So construction evidence may pick out a join by its use. With Ngozi, for instance:

- her responses keep what the join ties together: size comes before age, whatever the words;
- she treats the same content alike in another code: the order is the same whether she speaks or writes;
- a change in content brings the change the join calls for: a new word of colour takes its place after age and before origin;
- and the use lies on a route that ran: her grasp of the order actually produced her sentences.

That is how using an objection is judged too (section 10). But use alone does not construct. Received content used as it was received keeps its inherited history, and passing it on without such use is relay.

*Not yet ruled.* Ngozi was given examples, never the rule, and her use of it on phrases she has never heard picks out the join. One outside reader (of two) named this as a move toward the fixed verdict on an earlier draft; the other called the passage faulty, and the project kept the text.

### New, origination and finding questions

On Wednesday the apprentice picks a key that works like Tuesday's. Two contents are **equivalent**, at a level of detail, when links run both ways between them, each matching on the content's range. That is sameness of structure, not of wording or looks. A content is **new** to a system when nothing already in its stock is equivalent to it.

A system **originates** a content for a question when three things hold: it **used** the content **on the question**, the content was new to it, and it constructed the content. What is originated can be a set-up, a link, or a range with what it asks; each can be treated as a content.

**Back to the apprentice.** *Ruled on file 11: agrees.* Monday is selection: a pool (the ring), trials, and a surviving key, with nothing picturing the lock. Tuesday is construction, a real join made to her picture of what the lock needs, and the key is new to her, so she originates it. On Wednesday the key is equivalent to one in her stock, so she originates nothing.

**"The seal was tight."** An investigator's notes mention in passing that the seal was tight. Later she measures the seal's compression as something that varies, and finds it matters. *Fixed verdict:* she added a dimension (something that can vary: in this document's words, a slot), by measuring it, not by mentioning it. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.* A matter mentioned in passing becomes a slot of the account only when the account allows changes to it, and adding it is construction.

**The rota.** A team argues for weeks about who is lazy, until Noor asks which tasks have no owner; the arguing stops, and the work gets done. Nobody had said that every task should have an owner. *Fixed verdict:* "Noor found a better question, and its merit is real, even though the need it met was only recognised afterwards."

Originating a range, with what it asks, is **finding a question**. Noor's range can itself be treated as a set-up. Its slots are the changes and circumstances it holds (which tasks, who does them). Its pieces settle which changes belong. And its own allowed changes add or remove a change, or alter what is asked, as Noor altered "who is lazy?" to "which tasks have no owner?". The theory's **proof that a range is a set-up** says this. So construction and newness apply to questions, and finding one can, where the rest holds, create knowledge (section 10).

Finding the right question is creative on the same footing as answering one, and a theory that takes questions as given cannot say so. Part 0 of the theory puts it strongly: finding questions is "at least half of creativity". The theory measures nothing here; the phrase says how much weight it gives to finding questions.

*Ruled on file 11: silent*, on the merit only. If Noor constructed the range, it was new to her, and she used it, then she originated a question. Whether it was better is a claim of worth, which needs a standard of worth from outside, and the case supplies none. On draft 4 (*not yet ruled*), the last clause of the verdict, "the need it met was only recognised afterwards", fits the theory's episodes: the difficulty the team pictured was that the work was not getting done, and the demand her question met was stated only afterwards.

This gives the second half of "The tilt and the midnight sun" (section 6). Explaining, as something a system does, needs the idea used on the question, and nobody had used it.

That questions can be found is the fifth main claim. It would be defeated by a showing that treating a range as constructed, new and originated either cheapens creativity, or misses a real case of finding the right question.

*Departure:* Deutsch holds that grasping any idea, even one received from someone else, takes a guess, so that every acquisition is creative. Here working a received idea out afresh is construction, and merely passing it on is not. The likenesses to Deutsch on relay and on unspoken knowledge are named as likenesses, not claimed as borrowed.
