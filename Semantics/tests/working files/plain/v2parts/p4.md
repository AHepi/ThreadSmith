## 10. Criticism, repair, credit and created knowledge

### Two plumbers

Ana inspects a leak, says the washer has cracked, and explains why that lets water past. Before she can replace it, Ben tightens a nearby joint at random, and the leak stops, because the tightening shifted the washer back into place. *Fixed verdict:* "Ben's act stopped the leak. Ana's explanation is correct and produced nothing. Nobody here made a repair that came from an explanation."

### Routes that ran

**The route that started and was stopped.** (Ana and Ben again, in a different story.) Ana's bypass and Ben's valve begin to move at the same moment. The bypass jams a quarter open; the valve closes, and the flow stops. The operating log records it all before any meter is read. *Fixed verdict:* Ben stopped it; Ana's route started and did no stopping; the log says so without the meter. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

The **course of events** is everything that actually happened, as events in an order that never loops back, with the physics saying which processes took place and which connections were really made. A **route that ran** is a connected cluster of actual events with three marks:

- it joins something the system pictures to an outcome;
- its pieces behaved as they should;
- along it, the outcome really depended on the pictured difference: had that difference been otherwise, among the alternatives the claim states, the outcome would have differed.

A route that ran is made of events. A support (section 5) is made of commitments, and it runs when events like these carry it out. A route that started and did no work, or had already stopped when the outcome came, did not run for that outcome. This is read from the course of events, not from the outcome.

In **The diagram in his hand**, Sam holds his old key diagram while cutting a blank, but takes every measurement from the lock itself. *Fixed verdict:* the diagram did no work; had it said something different, he would have followed the lock. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.* A change in the diagram would have changed nothing Sam did, so no route ran from it.

Credit for a repair follows the routes that ran, not the supports a candidate contains (below).

### Criticism

Three principles of the theory come first. An idea may be entertained before anything justifies it. A criticism is aimed at something, and is itself a **guess**, an idea put forward without proof. And a system may act on a judgement without first proving it.

**Two students, ten drafts each.** Jana hands each draft to her teacher, gets back a bare mark, and in the end hands in the best-marked draft. Kasia shows nobody; after each draft she writes down its faults, such as a claim with nothing to back it, and fixes them in the next. The final essays are about equally good. *Fixed verdict:* both created what is good in their essays; for Jana, the teacher chose which draft was handed in.

Take one of Kasia's notes, "this claim has nothing to back it" (this document's illustration). A **criticism** has four things:

- a **criticized item**: here, the claim in her draft (in general, a candidate, question, range, link or method);
- an **alleged fault**: that the claim is unsupported;
- **grounds**: she looked, and her draft gives no evidence for it;
- a **connection**, the argument from grounds to fault: no evidence is given, so the claim is unsupported.

A criticism **bears on** its item when its connection, taken as a candidate for the **fault question**, is an account. The fault question is: does the item have that fault, in respect of the original question (here, the essay's)? So a criticism is judged by the same four requirements as any candidate. A criticism can exist without bearing on anything.

A **warning sign** is something that tells against an item. It is not a criticism until something pictures it as grounds for an alleged fault.

*Not yet ruled.* Kasia's written faults are criticisms. Jana's bare marks allege no fault, so they are at most warning signs. And the teacher, who pictures what she wants as she chooses among Jana's drafts, is not a selection history in the theory's sense (section 8): selection has nothing in it that pictures its target. This keeps "the teacher chose" from being read as selection.

An **objection** is a guess that something is wrong. The theory uses the word for what a response in an episode answers, and does not define it further. A response **uses an objection** when the objection shapes it in the right way and it lies on a route that ran. "In the right way" has three parts. With Kasia's note, for instance:

- each role in the objection keeps its role in the response: the claim criticized is the claim she fixes;
- the objection put in other words brings the same response: "no evidence for this" would lead to the same fix;
- a change in what the objection says brings the change the responder's way of deciding calls for: had the note said "this claim is false", she would have cut it rather than backed it.

Using an objection that is not valid does not make it valid.

### Episodes and noticed difficulties

**The novelist's two demands.** Odile wants two things from every chapter: rich, lyrical prose, and a story that keeps readers turning the pages. Planning chapter five, before writing it and before anyone has read it, she sees that a long description of a valley at dawn would stall the story at its tensest point. Her first draft cuts the description to one plain sentence. Her second has the heroine flee across the valley at dawn, described through what she sees as she runs: rich and fast at once. *Fixed verdict:* she had a problem as soon as she saw the clash, before anyone read the chapter; only the second draft solves it. ("Problem" is the case's word, in its everyday sense: in this document's words, a challenge.)

The theory speaks of an episode loosely, as a stretch of inquiry from a difficulty to a response, and defines only a complete one. A **complete episode of criticism** holds four things: a noticed difficulty; an item that was there before it was criticized; an objection; and a response that depends on what the objection says.

A **noticed difficulty** is one of two things, when the system pictures it: a failed demand; or a clash in which what the system holds meets one demand only by breaking a safeguard (demands and safeguards are explained under "Repair", below). A **creative episode** is an episode of criticism holding an origination connected to its inquiry. Closing an episode is a decision, not a proof.

*Not yet ruled.* Her two stated demands clash in the chapter before anyone reads it, which is a noticed difficulty. It needs no reader and no observation. The first draft meets one demand by breaking the other, so it is no repair. The second draft is a repair.

### Repair

In **Written on the job sheet**, the job sheet for a plumbing repair states in advance that the kitchen tap runs at all times during the work. During the work the tap stops for four seconds while the mains is switched over; nobody draws from it. *Fixed verdict:* the safeguard was broken, for four seconds, and calling it incidental changes nothing. *Ruled on file 11: agrees*, because the moments it covers were stated.

A **contribution** is a stretch of events, with the changes of content it makes. **Demands**, what is to be repaired, and **safeguards**, what must go on holding, are **stated inputs**: the theory records them without supplying them, and stating them claims nothing about their worth. Each is stated with the **occasions** it covers ("at all times during the work"), and a safeguard is lost exactly when it fails on one of them.

A contribution makes a **repair** when three things hold:

- some demand that failed before holds after;
- every safeguard that held before held on every occasion it covers in between, not only at the end;
- and the repair was **produced by** the contribution, meaning that a route that ran joins the two.

The theory ranks no repair above another. Losses outside the safeguards must be put on the record.

In **Four seconds**, the same stop happens, but the case names only "the protected tap". *Fixed verdict:* the safeguard held in every way that mattered. *Ruled on file 11: silent.* Whether the safeguard covers every moment, or only moments when someone draws water, is not stated, so the verdict turns on a missing input.

### Credit

**Two valves in one second.** Ana and Ben each close a different valve in the same second, and either alone would have stopped the leak. *Fixed verdict:* both did something that would have stopped it; neither can claim it alone; there is no way to say whose water stopped. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

**Credit** is which contribution an achievement is attributed to. The theory credits each contribution whose route ran to the achievement, as the course of events shows. Where two contributions that were each enough both ran, both are credited. It gives no **sharing of credit** that the course of events does not contain.

Three attributions stay apart: a correct account that produced nothing (Ana, among the plumbers); an act that repaired without an account (Ben); and a repair produced through the use of an account (nobody there). *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

Three cases have fixed verdicts that call an achievement "mostly" someone's.

- In **The expert's question**, a remote expert sends a robot two possible diagnoses and the one test that would separate them; the robot runs it on its own evidence and picks the right one. The verdict: the diagnosis, as an achievement, is mostly the expert's.
- In **The test that fitted neither** (section 7), the verdict calls the achievement mostly the robot's.
- In **The same afternoon, three achievements**, the robot sees a faulty assumption in the test, the expert proposes the fix, and the robot carries it out and finds the fault. The verdict credits each of the three achievements separately, and calls the whole inquiry mostly the expert's.

*Ruled on file 11: silent on all three.* "Mostly" needs a **weighting of credit** beyond what the course of events contains, a stated input that none of them states.

In **Two hands on the test**, the robot saw the faulty assumption and the expert saw the fix. The fixed verdict, "Neither can say 'mostly'", is *ruled on file 11: agrees*: with no weighting stated, neither has grounds for it.

### Created knowledge

Return to the hidden thing (section 8). Suppose the system originated its new layer, with its trackers, and that the layer met the demand "hold, and be able to use, an account of how things reappear after hiding", while keeping the safeguard "predict moves correctly". Then it made a repair. And since the layer is an account on its range, and the system understands it, it created knowledge. Among the plumbers, by contrast, nobody created knowledge: Ana holds a correct account that produced nothing, and Ben repaired without one. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

A **demand for knowledge** is a demand that a correct account be understood, or that some way of using something be corrected through such an account. **Created knowledge** needs all of these:

1. a creative episode (above);
2. a contribution in it that makes a repair;
3. among the demands repaired, a demand for knowledge, which failed before and holds after;
4. a content that the system originated, for some question, by the end of the episode;
5. that content is an account of its question, on the range fixed when it was originated;
6. it is among **what the contribution prepared**;
7. the system understands it afterwards;
8. and its join lies on the route that ran to that repair, so that the repair was **produced through the account**.

A later narrowing of the range, to rescue the account, is a different claim and does not count.

### Worth

Noor's question stopped the arguing, and the work got done (section 9). Whether it was a *better* question is another matter. Repairing a demand shows that it was repaired; it does not show that the demand, or the question behind it, was worth having.

Where a claim invokes **worth**, the theory takes the **standard of worth**, one of its two foundations, from outside, and marks the place. The eighth complaint asks where beauty and art come in. The theory keeps three things apart in the artistic case:

- a work's **effect**, what it does in a context;
- its **purpose**, what it aims at;
- and its **artistic value**, taken from the standard of worth, with its **reasons of taste**, whenever artistic value is claimed.

**Achieved purpose** is defined from the first two: a work achieves its purpose when it has some effect, and every effect falls within the aim. None of the three is defined as another. The theory does not pretend to know which reasons of taste are true, and no view of beauty follows from achieving a stated effect. Whether Odile's rich prose is beautiful, for instance, is a claim of artistic value, which needs the standard of worth.

*Departure:* Deutsch holds that there are objective truths about beauty; the theory abstains.

## 11. Owning an ability: boundaries, tasks, barriers and the universal class

### The technician's whisper

An assessment is stated at the start as robot-only. Midway, a technician tells the robot which gauge to read, and the robot completes the diagnosis; the log records the instruction. *Fixed verdict:* the boundary was robot-only, and the instruction crossed it; "The diagnosis is a team result." *Ruled on file 11: agrees*, as on file 10.

### Owning

A stretch of events is the system's **own** when its processes run inside the **boundary** stated for the system, which says which processes and resources are its own, and within its stated **resource terms**, which say which resources count as the system's. Work supplied from outside the boundary, such as a diagnosis, a decisive question, or an instruction about what to read, stays an **outside contribution**, however it is carried out inside.

A process run inside the boundary is the system's own, whoever wrote it. Where the boundary is drawn decides, not where the process sits in the casing. Credit for content and owning a process are different things: a routine written outside and run inside is the system's process, and its content is still its writer's contribution.

Three more cases are *ruled on file 11: agrees*.

- In **The routine uploaded that morning**, a robot compares pressures using a routine a remote person uploaded that morning. The comparison is the robot's work; who wrote the routine is a separate fact.
- In **The module in the casing**, a bought module sits inside a controller's shell but outside its stated boundary. It is not the controller's.
- In **The robot's log and the maker's manual**, the log shows which routine, running inside the robot, produced today's diagnosis, and the manual says the routine is the robot's own "because the robot can diagnose". Owning rests on the log, which shows the routine running inside. It never rests on the ability being attributed, as the manual's sentence would have it.

An ability is attributed under a stated boundary and a stated **sameness rule**, what makes the system the same system through change. Both are stated before the attribution, not chosen after it. In **New board, old memory**, a robot's controller board is replaced mid-task, and its memory and routines, stated at the start as its sameness rule, are copied across. *Fixed verdict:* the same robot finished. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

Where the boundary is not stated outright, two sorts of statement are told apart.

- A statement that names the system and says whether a process runs inside it or outside it states the boundary for that process. "The robot compares the pressures itself" says the comparison runs in the robot.
- A statement that a process is the system's own, without saying where it runs, is the attribution itself, not the boundary. "The routine is the robot's own" is one.

Nadia's case (section 8) states no boundary outright. But "she starts keeping a tally" names her and places the process in her, which under draft 4 states the boundary for that process (*not yet ruled*). On file 11, the project's own readers in the round had noted a doubt there, since the ruling read "her own" in the ordinary sense.

### Tasks, and what is possible

Take a kettle, as an everyday illustration. Boiling water is a task: a physical thing, the water, goes from a starting property, cold, to an end property, hot, using stated resources (electricity), with stated side effects (warm air). The kettle is the doer. Switching it on and waiting is the procedure. One boil is a carrying-out.

The theory states its physics in terms of tasks. (The sources note adds that this wording is from Deutsch's 2013 paper on "constructor theory"; the paper is not one of your two source books.)

- A **physical thing** has **properties**, each a set of its states.
- A **task** changes a physical thing from a stated starting property to a stated end property, with its resources and side effects stated.
- A task is **possible** when no law of physics limits, short of perfection, how well it can be done and how well the doer can be kept ready. This is how the physics decides what "possible" means for tasks: the word keeps its one meaning, allowed by the laws of physics. One success does not make a task possible.
- A **doer** performs a task by a stated **procedure**, and is left able to do it again. A **carrying-out** is one run of the procedure.
- In a **lasting performance**, every carrying-out finishes with a right end property and leaves the doer ready again: from any start where the doer is ready, whatever it starts from within the task's starting property, and with the aids given. A kettle that never switches off is **getting stuck**, and getting stuck is not success.
- **Staying able** is the widest set of ready states from which every carrying-out finishes and brings the doer back into that same set. The theory's **proof on staying able** shows that a widest such set always exists.

An **ability** is an owned lasting performance, or an owned, possible, finite way of **making** one, under the same sameness rule and resource terms. A theorist's description of a procedure is not the system's having it.

A system is **able to advance** a challenge when, from each starting point in a set stated in advance, every carrying-out ends with the challenge advanced and the system still in shape to go on. A first discovery cannot be repeated: Nadia's first idea of hidden cards can happen only once. So what must last is the set-up that lets the system go on inquiring, not the discovery.

The physics also supplies **degrees of accuracy**, ever more demanding and short of perfect, for how exactly a task is done and how reliably the doer is kept ready. At each degree, what a system can do never exceeds what the physics allows at that degree. A task that a system can do at every degree is possible. But an ability at some degree does not make a task possible. For instance, a machine might cut to within a millimetre, while the physics forbids doing that cut better than some finer limit; then the task is not possible, though some system can do it at the coarser degree.

### Criticism at any depth

Kasia criticizes her draft. She could also criticize the way she criticizes, say her habit of looking only for missing evidence, and then criticize that criticism in turn. A principle of the theory: any aspect of a system's practice (a question, a range, a link, a method, what it attends to) can be criticized, with an outcome able to change how the system goes on.

An aspect is **reviewable** when some owned way of going on makes it pictured, criticized, and able to change how it is used. Ranges and links are among the reviewable aspects. A system has **criticism at any depth** when, for every finite chain of such items (a criticism of a criticism of a method, and so on), it has an owned way to go on.

### Barriers and the universal class

An **aid** is help a system may be given in doing a task. A **fair aid** is a legitimate aid that does not assume what is to be shown.

A **barrier** is a field of tasks, described on its own terms and not by anyone's failures, for which every fair aid leaves the ability unavailable. A finite list of failures proves no barrier. And a narrow lasting use, like Kofi's phrasebook (section 9), shows no lasting inability to go further. A **way round** refutes a proposed barrier.

**Two sealed sorts of matter.** A universe has two sorts of matter that pass through each other without effect. Each sort's people have built a computer for any calculation their sort allows, and some calculations are possible only in one sort. Asha says the two computers together make a machine for any calculation the laws allow; Bram says none can exist, since a question written in one sort can never be read by the other's computer. *Fixed verdict:* Bram is right, and his reason largely explains why, together with the stated fact that each sort allows calculations the other cannot.

A principle of the theory is that any sort of matter may carry any set-up, and that every claim that a physical thing has a set-up must be allowed by the physics. Draft 4 adds a limit. This holds only so far as the physics lets contents pass between sorts of matter. Where two sorts cannot exchange what they carry, what only one sort can carry is a barrier for a system built of the other.

*Not yet ruled.* For a system of either sort, the calculations only the other sort allows are a barrier, so no system is in the universal class. Bram's reason explains this, together with the stated fact. Asha's weaker claim, that every calculation can be done somewhere, answers a different question, so she and Bram are not rivals.

The **universal class** is the systems, each with its boundary and sameness rule, that have three things. They have criticism at any depth. From their starting state, with some fair aid, they can understand every explanatory content, by doing its use task. And with some fair aid they can advance every **well-posed challenge**, one stated clearly enough for progress to be judged. The list of all explanatory contents and the list of all well-posed challenges are both fixed without reference to the system being judged.

Criticism at any depth does not bring membership. A growing history does not certify it, and a finite record does not establish it. The theory shows no person, machine, institution or line of descent to belong.

*Likenesses and departures:* draft 4's limit on what sorts of matter can carry is like Marletto's principle of exchange between sorts of matter, a likeness named, not claimed as borrowed. Deutsch holds that people are universal explainers; the theory abstains. Marletto defines knowledge as information that can keep itself embodied in physical things; the theory leaves that to the physics, outside its classes.

## 12. What the theory rests on, and how it could be refuted

### The uncited textbook

An explanation builds up the ideas it uses, step by step, from a starting base. It justifies every idea that way except one, which it justifies by a later theorem; but the proof of that theorem, as the explanation gives it, uses that same idea. A separate proof of the theorem exists in a textbook the explanation does not cite. *Fixed verdict:* as written, the order has a circle in it; the textbook proof would break it, and has not been used; the order is not settled until it is. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

### Foundations, frames and stated inputs

Recall "Four seconds" (section 10). Whether the safeguard was kept turns on which moments it covers, and the case does not say. The theory does not fill that in; it leaves the verdict unsettled. That is how the theory treats what a claim takes as given.

The theory has two **foundations**.

- **The physics.** It says what states and properties physical things have, which processes are possible, which processes a system controls (what counts as its deliberate action, used in construction evidence), what resources are, the degrees of accuracy, and which set-up a physical carrier embodies at a level of detail.
- **The standard of worth.** It is used only where a question invokes worth, is taken as given, and is never derived.

Every claim is relative to a frame (section 7), which is neither true nor false. A **stated input** is something a claim takes as given and states; the theory records it and does not supply it. The stated inputs are:

- a repair's demands and safeguards, with their occasions;
- a range's limit, and what makes the limit right;
- an attribution's boundary and sameness rule;
- a weighting of credit beyond what the course of events contains.

Boundary, sameness rule and limit belong at once to a claim's frame and to its stated inputs. A verdict that depends on a stated input, or on the standard of worth, is given with it. Where the input is missing, the verdict is unsettled, and the theory says so rather than choosing the input to suit the verdict wanted. No probability of truth, merit score or ranking of thinkers is supplied, and a claim that needs one is unsettled.

### Everything else is built from these

Roles come from allowed changes; kinds from response patterns; pictures from matching and history; histories from what physically happened; accounts from matching under change; and from these, everything else, up to criticism at any depth and the universal class. Nothing rests on a basic term meaning "really explains", "is a cause" or "is knowledge". The theory's **proof that nothing rests on "really explains"** follows each definition down to confirm this. The foundations are a theory of matter (the physics) and, where needed, a standard of worth; neither is about explanation.

The **building order** says which definitions rest on which.

- Frames and stated inputs rest on nothing in the theory; they are stated, not derived.
- Kinds rest on set-ups and ranges; accounts on matching; a criticism's bearing on accounts.
- Pictures rest on matching and physical history; understanding on pictures and lasting performance.
- Owning rests on the course of events and a boundary; an ability on owning, lasting performance and a sameness rule.
- Construction rests on owning and accounts; origination on understanding and construction.
- Repair rests on origination, accounts, understanding, routes that ran, and the stated demands and safeguards with their occasions; created knowledge on all of those and on repair.
- Criticism at any depth and the universal class rest on all of the above.

The order has **no circles**. A picture justified only by the construction that made it, or an owning and an ability justified only by each other, has no place in it. A separate proof counts only once the explanation uses it, as with the uncited textbook.

In **Owned means capable**, a routine is defined as the robot's own when it can run it, and runnable when it is its own. *Fixed verdict:* the definition goes in a circle, and only the histories of what happened give the distinction meaning. *Ruled on file 11: agrees.*

The theory's definitions leave places to be filled for any particular system: its set-ups, links, histories, boundary, and so on. A **filled-in model** is those places filled in for a real or imagined system. Its **classes** are these.

- The **base class**: every place filled with the right sort of thing, and every physical claim allowed by the physics.
- The **creative-episode class**: with an origination connected to an episode of criticism.
- The **knowledge-creation class**: with created knowledge.
- The **criticism-at-any-depth class**.
- The **universal class**.

The creative-episode class lies inside the base class, and the knowledge-creation class inside the creative-episode class. The universal class lies inside the criticism-at-any-depth class. The others are not ordered. A single origination does not put its author in the universal class.

### How the theory could be refuted

The five **main claims**, in order of how much would fall, each with what would **defeat** it. None is protected by the theory's notation, or by the label of any draft.

1. **The four requirements are enough.** Defeated by a candidate that meets all four on a range of possible changes, with a link that is not merely declared, and plainly explains nothing. The known tricks each fail one requirement (section 3). A full response table passes matching; like any other attempt, it would defeat this claim only if it met all four and still explained nothing. An outside reader tried to build such a candidate, and offered none (section 14 notes one boundary case).
2. **They are needed.** Defeated by a real explanation whose set-up no link can match on any range of possible changes. Explanations of absence are the most exposed place.
3. **Kinds can be dropped.** Defeated by a label that tells apart two accounts which no allowed change tells apart, where the difference does real explaining.
4. **The two histories, selection and construction, are kept apart.** (The third history, declaration, supports no claim of creativity.) Defeated by any of three things. A selected link fixed at an untried setting by its trials, although its pool holds a survivor that differs there. (A pool with no such survivor is a limit the claim itself states, not a refutation.) A showing that every construction can be rewritten as a selection without loss. Or a showing that explanation does not work on the thing layer.
5. **Questions can be found.** Defeated by a showing that treating a range as constructed, new and originated cheapens creativity, or misses a real case of finding the right question.

A mathematical error also counts. That means a counterexample, under their stated assumptions, to one of these: the proof on smallest supports (section 5), the proof on pinning down (section 2), the proof on blocking (section 2), the proof on error build-up (section 4), the proof on staying able (section 11), the proof that kinds add nothing (section 4), the proof on same anchors (section 6), or the proof on what survival leaves open (section 8).

A case whose verdict turns on one of the stated inputs listed above, or on the standard of worth, when the case does not state it, has a missing input, and is not a refutation.

### The record so far

**The forbidden wire.** The only arrangement that would respond differently at the untried setting needs a wire between two terminals, and every device in the stated pool is built without it. *Fixed verdict:* "Then there is no alternative in that population. The different response is imaginable and is not available." *Ruled: file 10 disagrees; file 11 agrees.*

File 10's proof on what survival leaves open said that trials always leave every untried setting open. This case refuted that, and the list of defeats counted it. File 11 corrected the proof by requiring a differing survivor in the pool, and draft 4 keeps that, adding that rivals are not a pool, so a merely imagined arrangement reopens nothing. The cost: the corrected claim now holds almost by its wording, so it says less and is hard to defeat. The theory does not say how much that matters.

Three more defects, found in September, are corrected in draft 4.

- The old proof on same anchors was false as written, so the entry for a mathematical error stands triggered for files 10 and 11 (section 6).
- The proof on error build-up lacked assumptions it needs (section 4).
- "No assumed answer" left open which pieces are commitments (section 3).

On the cases, file 11 agreed with 46 of the 52 fixed verdicts, was silent on 6, and went against none.

### Three older views

The fifth complaint says the theory is three older views renamed. The first holds that what a sign stands for comes from a history of selection. The second, that science captures the structure of the world. The third, that a mind is defined by what it does, not by what it is made of.

The theory takes from each and differs from each. Links have a history, but selected and constructed histories are kept apart, with creativity only in construction. Structure is kept, but piece by piece and under change, and the theory does not require an explanation to copy the world's whole structure at once. Any sort of matter may carry a set-up, but every claim that a physical thing has one must be allowed by the physics. Whether the mix is new is a question for scholars.
