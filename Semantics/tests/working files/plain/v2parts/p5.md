## 13. What draft 4 changed

Draft 4 is file 11 with 57 changes. The main ones are gathered here by section, each said in terms a newcomer can follow.

**Questions (section 2).** Only one change touches the theory's text on questions, and it concerns limits (section 7 below).

**The four requirements (section 3).**
- It now says which pieces are the commitments: those offered as doing the work, whether or not anyone has described their work. The rest, including any piece that gives an input slot its value, is background.
- The second half of the third requirement changed. File 11 asked the range to hold a change that itself removes or replaces some commitments. Draft 4 asks instead that deleting a group of commitments lose a difference between two settings of the range. File 11's wording also left open which pieces were commitments, and, on one way of taking it, found no account where the fixed verdict finds one, as in the salt on the step.
- Whether a statement is the answer written in is judged by its form at the stated level of detail, not by treating all mathematically equivalent truths as one.
- File 11 said outright that a full response table is an account. Draft 4 says it passes matching, and is an account only if it also meets the other three requirements.
- On absence, it adds that why the absent thing seems present is a separate question, and that a bare denial is no account of it.
- The principle on errors now says that "explanation" there means an account, that a false theory offered for a question is a candidate, and that whether an account is easy to vary is a separate matter.

**Kinds (section 4).** It says how a piece of one candidate is compared with a piece of another. It says that response patterns are built from behaviour, not from values, and that a change that sets a slot replaces only the piece that fixes it. The proof on error build-up now states the assumptions it needs; file 11 left them out, a drafting slip.

**Which pieces do the work (section 5).** File 11 compared which changed forms of an account still pass as more "explanatory jobs" are demanded of it (the jobs were never defined), and used the word "reach" without defining it. Draft 4 drops the comparison and the word, so nothing counts, grades or ranks candidates. It adds the idle commitment, with its test applied only to a candidate that has a support. It says that a cut-down candidate's commitments are the ones it keeps, and that the supports judged are groups of the written commitments. And it separates a support from a route that ran, crediting by routes that ran.

**Rivals and problems (section 6).** The two paragraphs "Rivals" and "Problems" replace file 11's paragraph on hard to vary (the comparison just described). The proof on same anchors is narrowed: it now needs the same anchors.

**Narrowed questions (section 7).** Meeting the requirements on a narrowed range does not certify the limit as right. A verdict on a limit comes with the reason the claim states for it, and where none is stated, the verdict lacks a stated input. The paragraph "A failed answer stays failed" is new. And the theory's list of defeats now says that a case with a missing stated input is no refutation.

**Where links come from (section 8).**
- Surviving the trials is now defined: being in the pool and matching on every trial.
- Expectation and miss now cover every link to the expectation layer, whatever its history. File 11 gave a failed constructed link no miss.
- The pool is described as candidate links, not only ones that were built.
- The swap in the hidden thing now runs through exchanging the two things (the proof on rewriting), and then the narrowed proof on same anchors, not through the old unnarrowed proof.
- Part 0 no longer uses the word "blind" for selection. It says only that selection's history holds no pictured target, as the body of the theory does.

**Building something new (section 9).** Unspoken pictures and joins picked out by their use are added, with the guard that use alone does not construct. "Equivalent" is defined.

**Criticism, repair and knowledge (section 10).** It spells out the fault question and the connection of a criticism, and when a warning sign becomes a criticism. It defines a noticed difficulty and a contribution. It makes a safeguard hold on every occasion it covers, not only at the end. It defines "what the contribution prepared" and "produced through the account". And it makes the standard of worth a foundation, where file 11 had called it a stated input.

**Owning an ability (section 11).** File 11 said a process run inside the boundary is the system's own "today"; draft 4 drops "today", to agree with the theory's Part on the physics. It separates credit for content from owning a process. It says how a boundary is stated when it is not stated outright. It defines degrees of accuracy and fair aid. And it limits the principle about carriers to what can pass between sorts of matter.

**What the theory rests on (section 12).** Part 0 and the theory's Part on foundations now say that everything else is built from the two foundations, the frames and the stated inputs; file 11 said only that everything else is built. Frames and stated inputs are told apart. A weighting of credit joins the stated inputs. A missing standard of worth leaves a verdict unsettled, as does a claim that needs a probability of truth, a merit score or a ranking of thinkers. The building order now places owning, abilities and repair. A case with a missing stated input is no refutation. And the full response table is no longer set outside the list of defeats: like any candidate, it would defeat the first main claim if it met all four requirements and still explained nothing.

## 14. What is still open

**The state of draft 4.** It is still being changed. The outside readers have not seen its paragraphs on rivals, problems and failed answers, which were tried only on small made-up examples worked through by computer programs. No round has used draft 4, the 23 proposed cases, or the lamp controller.

**Your decision on hard to vary.** Draft 4 carries it (sections 6 and 7). Still open:

- "Easy to vary" applies to both rivals alike. Making it point at one of them would need something more in the theory, which risks becoming a ranking (section 6).
- A candidate nobody has challenged with a rival is never called easy to vary, however loose it is (section 6).
- The project's analysis of 24 September found that a record still does one job: telling a retreat like Bruno's from a limit honest from the start. Draft 4 does not settle whether that job is needed. The failed-answer paragraph does not reach other settings, a series of narrowings, changes of level of detail, or a coarser thing asked (section 7).
- Your own example, "Demeter grieves" against "Persephone is underground", gives rivals only where some allowed change, such as a veil of dust dimming the sun, sets the warmth received on its own; otherwise they conflict nowhere (section 6).
- Two partial explanations, each exactly right about a different step, can be rivals outside the range, and the finer question's test can show both wrong (section 6).
- Idle commitments are no defect. Making them one would be a change of claim (section 5).

**Verdicts the theory does not give**, each for want of something the case does not state: Bruno's "retreat" (section 7); the merit of Noor's question (section 9); "mostly" in three robot-and-expert cases (section 10); and the safeguard in "Four seconds" (section 10).

## 17. The mathematics, for readers who want it

**Pinning down, for sums (section 2).** Suppose each reading, and the feature asked about, is a sum of the unknowns with fixed weights. Then the feature is pinned down exactly when every shift of the unknowns that leaves all the readings unchanged also leaves the feature unchanged. For the two balances, the shift "one gram more mass, one gram less error on each balance" leaves both readings unchanged and changes the mass, so the mass is not pinned down. The same shift leaves the difference of the two errors unchanged, and every shift that leaves both readings alone is a multiple of it, so that difference is pinned down. Repeating a weighing adds a reading with the same weights, so it rules out no shift. A calibration, against something already known, can rule one out.

**Odd-sized tables (section 3).** Take square tables of numbers with an odd number of rows, where each number is the negative of its mirror image across the diagonal. The question is why none of them can be undone. The range drops the mirroring, drops the oddness, or drops both, holding ordinary arithmetic fixed, and holding fixed the tie between being undone and the table's **determinant**, one number worked out from the table that is nought exactly when the table cannot be undone.

- The full working-out of the determinant, with the mirroring put in, matches: each step is a smaller working-out whose hidden slots are set aside.
- It gives the answer: in the family of tables asked about, none can be undone, and under each dropped assumption the family holds one that can.
- It assumes none. Without the mirroring, the three-row table with ones down the diagonal and noughts elsewhere can be undone. Without the oddness, the two-row mirrored table with a one above the diagonal and a minus one below it can be undone.
- It does not dodge: a mirrored table of odd size that is not all noughts exists, so the untouched setting has a consistent state.

So it is an account. A three-line proof is shorter, but shortness is not a fifth requirement.

**Error build-up (section 4).** Choose a distance between states. Suppose that at every state within stated bounds, one step of the candidate lands at most a step error away from where one step of the target lands, carried over. And suppose one step of the candidate never enlarges the distance between two states more than a fixed number of times over, the stretch factor. Then, from one shared start, as long as the target's states stay within the bounds, the gap after a number of steps is at most this: the step error once for the latest step, plus the step error times the stretch factor for the step before, plus the step error times the stretch factor times the stretch factor for the one before that, and so on back to the first step. With a stretch factor of one, the ceiling is simply the step error times the number of steps. Without any stretch factor, no ceiling follows.

**Smallest supports (section 5).** Suppose three things: the commitments are finitely many; adding a commitment to a support always leaves a support; and the whole candidate is a support. Then a single commitment is load-bearing in some support exactly when it belongs to some smallest support, and it is needed in every support exactly when it belongs to every smallest support. Where adding can break a support (interference), the second assumption fails, and the rule does not apply.

**The tipping edge (section 5).** Take a stated family of changes to a candidate. The tipping edge is the set of pairs of changed candidates of which one is an account and the other is not. The theory defines it and uses it nowhere else.

**Spread-out support (section 5).** Take endlessly many commitments: the first says a quantity lies within one of nought, the next within a half of nought, the next within a third, and so on. Any endless selection of them puts the quantity at exactly nought; no finite selection does. So no single commitment and no smallest group does the work; the whole group does, together. For the same reason, with endlessly many commitments, commitments that are each idle can together be load-bearing.

**Staying able (section 11).** Call a set of the doer's states "safe" when every carrying-out from a state in it finishes and returns the doer to a state in it. Put all the safe sets together, and the result is again safe. So there is always a widest safe set, and that is staying able.
