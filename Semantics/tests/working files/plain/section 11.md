## 11. What the theory rests on, and how it could be refuted

### Example: the uncited textbook

A write-up traces every **picture** a system holds (section 7) back to its start, except one, justified by a later theorem whose proof in the write-up uses that same picture. A textbook the write-up never cites holds a separate proof.

The **fixed verdict**: as written, a circle, which the unused textbook proof would break; nothing is settled until it is.

The **ruling** on file 11 **agrees**: its definitions rest on one another with **no circles**; a picture justified only by the construction that made it has no place, and a separate proof counts only once the justification uses it. Draft 4 keeps this.

### The idea in plain words

Everything rests on two given things plus what claims state openly; gaps are marked, not guessed; refutations are named in advance.

### What the theory says

#### Two foundations

Can a kettle boil water? Physics says. Is a song beautiful? A standard from outside says. The two **foundations** are **the physics** (states and properties of physical things, allowed processes, what counts as deliberately making a change, resources, degrees of accuracy, and which **set-up** a physical thing embodies at a level of detail) and, only where a claim turns on worth, the **standard of worth**, taken as given, never derived.

#### Frames and stated inputs

"The shadow is ten metres long" holds at a level of detail, over a range of changes. Every claim is relative to such a **frame** (level of detail, **boundary**, **sameness rule**, **range**), neither true nor false.

A **stated input** is what a claim must state, recorded but never supplied by the theory: a repair's **demands** and **safeguards**, with their **occasions**; a range's **limit** and what makes it right; a system's boundary and sameness rule; a **weighting of credit** beyond what the course of events shows. Boundary, sameness rule and limit are both frame and stated input.

Three cases call an achievement "mostly" one party's, stating no weighting; the rulings on file 11 are **silent**. A verdict resting on a stated input comes with it; without it (a **missing input**) the verdict is **unsettled**, and the theory says so rather than pick one to suit a wanted verdict. So too for worth. Claims needing a probability of truth, merit score or ranking of thinkers, never supplied, are unsettled.

#### The building order

Everything else is defined from these three, in a **building order** of what rests on what. Frames, stated inputs, set-ups and questions rest on nothing; then, each on earlier items and the foundations: kinds; matching and answering the question asked; accounts; supports, load-bearing groups, the tipping edge; pictures; understanding; owning and ability (also on stated boundary and sameness rule); construction; newness and origination; repair and created knowledge (also on "produced by" and stated demands and safeguards); last, criticism at any depth and the universal class.

Like the textbook's picture, an ownership and an ability justified only by each other have no place in it. No basic term meaning "really explains", "pictures", "is a cause" or "is knowledge" is given or depended on, as the theory proves, tracing each definition down. Its foundations are a theory of matter and, where needed, of reasons, neither about explanation.

#### The classes

A **filled-in model** fills the theory's blanks for a real or imagined system. Its **classes**, widest first: the **base class** (blanks rightly filled, every physical claim allowed by the physics); those with a creative episode; with created knowledge; with criticism at any depth; and the **universal class**, which one origination does not earn.

#### What would defeat it

What would count against the **central guess** (section 0) is a **defeat** of one of five **main claims**, listed by how much falls. None is shielded by symbols, this document's existence or a draft's label; anything else is a detail.

1. **The four requirements are enough.** Defeat: a **candidate** meeting all four on a range of possible changes, its link not merely declared, plainly explaining nothing. Known tricks each fail one requirement (section 2). A full response table passes matching, and defeats the claim only by passing all four and explaining nothing.
2. **They are needed.** Defeat: a real explanation whose set-up no link can match on any range of possible changes; explanations of absence are most exposed.
3. **Kinds can be dropped.** Defeat: a label telling apart two accounts no allowed change tells apart, the difference doing real explaining.
4. **The two histories, and what trials leave open.** Defeat: a selected link fixed by its trials at an untried setting though its pool holds a survivor differing there; every construction retold as selection without loss; or explanation shown not to work on the thing layer.
5. **Questions can be found.** Defeat: showing that treating a range as constructed, new and originated cheapens creativity or misses a real case of finding a question.

A **mathematical error** counts too: a counterexample, under their stated assumptions, to the smallest-supports theorem, the proofs on pinning down with sums and on blocking, error build-up, staying able, or the first three numbered proofs. A case turning on a stated input it omits, or on worth, has a missing input: no defeat.

### Example: the forbidden wire

Selection shapes a **pool** of devices on its **trials**. The only arrangement responding differently at an **untried setting** needs a wire between two terminals; every device in the pool lacks it.

The fixed verdict: the pool holds no alternative; a different response is imaginable, not available; the claim's assumption is not met.

File 10's third numbered proof said trials always leave every untried setting **left open**; its ruling **disagrees**: a counterexample its list of defeats named. File 11's one declared change requires the differing link to be in the pool and **survive**; its ruling agrees; draft 4 keeps it, the pool being what the physics and stated design allow.

That is the list at work: a named finding came, and the claim changed. The cost: "left open" now needs a differing survivor in the pool, close to true by wording alone, so hard to defeat.

#### Defeats so far

The old second numbered proof said candidates matching and answering alike on a range are one account, pieces pairing kind for kind. A counterexample shows the pairing false as written, triggering that entry for files 10 and 11; draft 4 narrows the proof (section 5). The round on file 11 gave 46 of 52 fixed verdicts, none against.

#### Three older views renamed?

An expected **complaint**: this is three older views renamed (what a sign stands for comes from selection; science captures structure; a mind is what it does, not its matter). The theory borrows from each but keeps selection apart from construction, where creativity lies; keeps structure piece by piece under change, not as a whole copy; and lets any sort of matter serve only as the physics allows. Whether the mix is unprecedented is for scholars.

### What changed in this revision

- Everything else rests on foundations, frames and stated inputs, now told apart, and the proof traces each definition to them.
- A weighting of credit joins the stated inputs; the building order places them, owning, ability, "produced by" and the demands.
- Missing worth, or needing a probability, merit score or ranking, leaves verdicts unsettled; missing inputs are no defeat; the full response table stays inside the first main claim.

### What is open

- Draft 4: not frozen, latest fixes unseen by the outside readers, untried in any round.
- The building order is a summary, omitting rivals and problems, among others; stated inputs omit how a candidate is cut down and the family behind the tipping edge.
