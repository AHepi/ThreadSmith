## 0. What this theory is for, and how to read this

### An example

"The shadow is ten metres long because the pole is five metres tall and the sun is low." Raise the pole, and the shadow lengthens. Shade some of the ground with a board, and the pole does not grow. The changes, not the names, show which depends on which.

### The idea

The theory, named "Claude Fable Semantics", says what makes something an explanation, and what makes a thinker creative at explaining: at making explanations, or questions, that did not exist before. As with the pole, it judges only by changes, and cases could show it wrong.

### What the theory claims

- An explanation is a **set-up** of **pieces** that depend on one another, chosen for a question. A **link** ties it to its **target** in the world, and is judged only by **matching**: whether both respond alike to each change the question covers. The theory never asks whether a piece is "the same kind of thing" as a piece of the world, and proves it need not: a **kind** is just how a piece responds to the changes allowed at a level of detail (section 3).
- A link's **history**, how it came about, makes it **selected** (by variation and survival, with nothing in that history standing for the target), **constructed** (by guessing and criticism) or **declared** by whoever writes the model. Creativity lives in construction; selection supplies its raw material; no claim of creativity may rest on declaring (section 7).
- Questions can be found as well as answered; the theory describes both, as no theory taking questions as given can. Finding them is at least half of creativity (section 8).
- The **central guess**: four things are enough to describe creativity at explaining: set-ups and their allowed changes, links judged by matching alone, the history of links and questions, and their embodiment in physical things. A real achievement these four cannot describe counts against it, as does something meeting all four that plainly explains nothing (section 11).

### What it does not claim

- That matter alone fixes what a thing stands for. A history of selection or construction is needed too.
- That predicting is explaining. The same outputs by different inner workings are different set-ups (section 4).
- That it supplies a true theory of beauty, a probability of truth, a merit score, a measure of worth or a ranking of thinkers. Worth and artistic value come from a **standard of worth** taken from outside; a claim needing the others is **unsettled**. Nor does it share credit beyond what the course of events shows (sections 9 and 11).
- That anyone or anything belongs to the classes it defines (section 10).

### The six principles

1. Whether a link matches is a fact, accepted or not; a system can be wrong about its links, readings and criticisms, and about itself (section 2).
2. A theory wrong in places can still explain, unless the error is in the very dependence said to do the work. "Explanation" here means an **account**: a **candidate** (any explanation offered, even a false one) meeting section 2's four requirements. Whether it is **easy to vary** (roughly: has an offered **rival** differing only outside what the question covers) is separate (section 5).
3. Ideas may be held without proof; a criticism is itself a guess; one may act without proving first (section 9).
4. Any aspect of a system's practice can be criticized, and the criticism must be able to change how it goes on (section 10).
5. Any **sort of matter** may embody a set-up, as far as **the physics** (the adopted theory of physics) allows. Where two sorts cannot exchange what they bear, what only one can bear is a **barrier** for systems of the other (section 10).
6. Selection and construction both make links, told apart by their histories; neither reduces to the other (section 7).

### How to read this

Each special word keeps one meaning, from the word sheet; each section runs from an example to what is not settled.

A **case** is a short everyday situation with a **fixed verdict**, a careful person's, written in advance. File 11, the text in force, was run against 52 cases in a **round**: it **agreed** with 46 fixed verdicts, disagreed with none, and was **silent** on 6, each turning on something the case does not state.

**Draft 4** is file 11 with 57 changes, 51 of them **changes of claim**, letting a reader conclude something file 11 did not.

The **source books** are by Chiara Marletto and David Deutsch; sections name each **departure** from them, made on purpose.

### What changed in this revision

File 11 took every measure the theory does not supply as something a claim must state, though only worth was handled so. Now worth comes from the standard of worth, and a claim needing a probability, a merit score or a ranking is unsettled.

### What is open

Draft 4 is not frozen. The **outside readers**, two AI systems outside the project, have not seen the fixes made after their cross-examination, or the paragraphs on rivals and failed answers. No round has used it. The rest is in section 12.
