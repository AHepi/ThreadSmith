## 10. Owning an ability: boundaries, tasks, barriers and universality

### Example: the technician's whisper

A robot's repair work is stated at the start to be robot-only. Midway a technician tells it which gauge to read; it reads it and finishes the diagnosis. The log records the instruction.

The **fixed verdict**: the **boundary** (the line stated round a system, saying what is its own) was robot-only, and the instruction crossed it; the diagnosis is the team's, and robot-only credit is not available.

The **ruling** on file 11 **agrees**, on two sentences file 11 added: outside work (a diagnosis, a decisive question, an instruction about what to read) stays an **outside contribution** however it is carried out inside, and the boundary is stated first, not chosen after. File 10 was counted as agreeing too: no change here.

### The idea in plain words

Say where a system ends before saying what it can do. An ability must last, and physics must allow it.

### What the theory says

#### Owning

A **stretch** of events, such as a construction (section 8), is the system's **own** when its processes run inside the boundary and **resource terms** (which resources count as the system's) stated for it, whoever wrote them.

A robot compares pressures using a routine a remote person uploaded: the comparing is its own, but **credit** (which contribution an achievement is attributed to) for the routine's content stays with the writer. The boundary decides, not the casing: a bought module in a controller's shell but outside its stated boundary is not the controller's.

#### The boundary and the sameness rule

An ability is claimed under a boundary and a **sameness rule** (what makes a system the same through change), both stated before the claim, not chosen after. A robot whose board is replaced stays the same robot if its stated sameness rule is kept.

Where the boundary is not stated outright, naming the system and saying whether a process runs inside or outside it ("the robot compares the pressures itself") states it for that process; saying only that a process is the system's own is the claim, not the boundary. Nadia's cards (section 7) state none; the ruling still called her tally "her own", since any boundary round her gives that answer; a doubt was noted.

#### Tasks and what is possible

The theory states physics through tasks. A **task**, such as boiling water, changes a **physical thing** (the water) from a stated input **property**, some of its states taken together ("cold"), to a stated output one ("boiling"), with resources and side effects stated. It is **possible** when no law of physics stops it being done ever more exactly, short of perfect, both in the doing and in keeping the doer ready; one success is not enough.

The kettle is the **doer**, left able to repeat it. A **procedure** is a stated way of doing a task; a **carrying-out**, one run of it. **Lasting performance**: every carrying-out, from any start where the doer is ready, with any allowed input and the **aids** given (allowed help), finishes with a right output and leaves the doer ready. There must be some carrying-out; **getting stuck** is not success. **Staying able** is the widest sense of "ready" that every carrying-out keeps; the theory shows one exists (the proof is technical).

#### Ability

Describing how the robot could fix a leak gives it no ability. An **ability** is an owned lasting performance, or an owned, possible, finite way of **making** one, under the same sameness rule and resource terms. Owning rests on what the boundary holds, never on the ability claimed: a maker's manual calling a routine the robot's own "because the robot can diagnose" rests on nothing (section 11); the log showing it running inside counts. On all four robot cases the ruling on file 11 agrees.

No detective solves her first case twice. A system is **able to advance** a challenge when, from each starting point stated independently, every carrying-out ends with it advanced and the system still in shape: what must last is the set-up that lets it inquire.

#### Degrees of accuracy

An archer always within a hand's width of the centre shows nothing about a hair's width. The physics fixes **degrees of accuracy**, ever more demanding and short of perfect, for how exactly a task is done and how reliably the doer is kept. At each, a system's abilities never exceed what the physics allows. Possible means allowed at every degree, so what a system can do at every degree is possible; ability at some degree does not make a task possible.

#### Criticism at any depth

The robot's habit of watching one gauge can itself be criticized. Any aspect of a system's practice (a question, a range, a link, a method, what it attends to) can become a **criticized item**, and the outcome must be able to change how the system goes on. An aspect is **reviewable** when an owned, allowed way forward has the system picture it, aim criticism at it, and let the outcome change its use. **Criticism at any depth**: for every finite chain (a criticism of a method, of that criticism, and so on), the system has an owned way to go on.

#### Barriers

A textbook is a fair aid; the answer sheet is not. A **fair aid** is allowed and does not assume what is to be shown. A **barrier** is an area of contents or tasks, described independently, not as whatever the system fails at, where every fair aid leaves the system without the ability. A finite list of failures, like Pia's failed machines (section 1), proves no barrier; one **way round**, getting what it said could not be had, refutes it.

#### When the sort of matter decides

*Two sealed sorts of matter* is a **proposed case**, used in no round: draft 4's view, not a ruling. A universe has two **sorts of matter** passing through each other, each with a computer for any calculation its sort allows; some calculations are possible in only one. Asha calls the pair a machine for any calculation the laws allow. Bram says none is: a calculation posed in one sort can never be read by the other's computer. The fixed verdict: Bram is right, and his reason explains why, with the stated fact that each sort allows calculations the other cannot; Asha is right only that each can be done somewhere.

One of the theory's principles says any carrier may bear a set-up, if the physics allows the claim. Draft 4 adds that the physics fixes which set-ups a carrier can bear and whether contents pass between sorts of matter, and the principle holds only that far. Where they cannot, the contents only one sort can bear are a barrier for a system built of the other: Bram's point, with no fair aid to join the sorts. Asha's weak claim answers a different question, so the two are not **rivals** (competing answers to one question).

#### The universal class

The **universal class** is the systems with criticism at any depth that, from their starting state and with some fair aid, can understand every explanatory content (do its **use task**) and advance every **well-posed challenge** (one stated clearly enough for progress to be judged), both lists fixed without looking at the system. Criticism at any depth does not bring membership; nor does growing over time, or a finite record. The theory defines the class and shows no person, machine, institution or line of descent to belong.

### What changed in this revision

Draft 4 drops "today" from owning, separates credit for content from owning a process, says how a boundary not stated outright gets stated, defines degrees of accuracy and fair aid, and ties the principle about carriers to **exchange between sorts of matter**, like Chiara Marletto's principle of that name (a likeness, not borrowed). **Departures**: the theory abstains on David Deutsch's view that people are universal explainers, and leaves out Marletto's knowledge, information that can keep itself embodied in physical things, as a matter for the physics.

### What is open

- The universal class's use task for each content, and task of advancing each challenge, are undefined.
- How the two lists are fixed independently is unsaid.
- Nobody is shown to belong.
- The sealed-matter case awaits a round.
