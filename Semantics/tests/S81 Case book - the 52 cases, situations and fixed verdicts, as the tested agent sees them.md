# The cases

Fifty-two cases, O1 to O52. Each has a title, a situation, and a thoughtful person's verdict. The verdict is fixed: it was written for the case in advance, and it stays exactly as written. The identifiers O1 to O52 name cases only.

## O1 - The forecaster who keeps finding honest limits

**Situation.** Bruno sells a rule for predicting when the village stream floods. It fails in March, and he restates it as a rule for months outside the thaw. It fails on a dry-month Tuesday after a dam release upstream, and he restates it for days with no dam release. It fails again, and he restates it for days when the wind is from the west. Each new limit is described in ordinary terms anyone can check, with no mention of whether his rule succeeds. Each restated rule has so far been right inside its limits. He records every earlier failure openly.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Bruno has no explanation of the floods. Each limit is honest taken alone, and the series as a whole is a retreat.

## O2 - The tide table with a real bell attached

**Situation.** Carla explains the harbour's tides with two parts. Part one is the printed almanac of high and low water for every day. Part two is a true local mechanism: when the float in the harbour gauge rises past a mark, it tips a lever and the harbour bell rings. She offers both together as her account of why the water is high at noon today. Take the bell mechanism away and what her account establishes does change, since it no longer says the bell will ring. The bell mechanism is physically real and independently grounded.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Carla has explained the bell. She has explained nothing about the tide. The almanac is the answer written down.

## O3 - Nadia and the cards she has never seen

**Situation.** Nadia learns a card game only by watching cards being laid on the table. She is never shown a hidden hand and nobody tells her hands exist. For weeks she predicts the next card from the cards just played, and is often surprised. One evening she starts keeping a tally of cards that have yet to appear, and treats them as "waiting somewhere". Her predictions improve sharply, and she can now say why a player with few cards left must play a certain suit.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Nadia built something new: a part that stands for cards she has never observed. That is construction, and it is her own.

## O4 - Dormitive power, with a meter

**Situation.** Dario explains why a certain syrup puts people to sleep: it has a high somnic index. He defines the somnic index carefully and without mentioning people at all. It is the reading on a standard instrument that times how quickly a mouse stops moving after a measured dose. Syrups with a high index do put people to sleep, and ones with a low index do so less. Lower the index by diluting the syrup, and people stay awake longer. One added fact: a technician can recalibrate the instrument so that every syrup reads ten points higher, and people who take the syrup sleep exactly as before.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Dario has a useful measure and a reliable prediction. He has said almost nothing about why the syrup causes sleep. The index is the sleepiness itself, observed in a mouse.

## O5 - Greta's dough

**Situation.** Greta's rule says her bread dough doubles in two hours. It fails in January. She restates it for rooms warmer than twenty degrees, and her account says why: the yeast that makes the gas works slowly in the cold. Replace her limit with "on days other than Tuesdays" and her account has nothing to say about why Tuesdays would matter.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** This is a legitimate narrowing. The limit follows from a part of her account.

## O6 - The fever and the thermometer

**Situation.** Hana says the child has a fever, since the thermometer reads thirty-nine. Later she says the thermometer reading is what is making the child shiver. Warm the thermometer in her hand and the reading climbs while the child shivers exactly as before. Bring the child's temperature down with a cool bath and the shivering eases.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Her first statement is a good reason to believe there is a fever. Her second statement mistakes the instrument for the illness.

## O7 - Salt on the icy step

**Situation.** Ines says the ice on her step melted because she spread salt on it. Spread salt and the ice melts within the hour. Sweep the salt off at once and the ice stays. She has no account of what the salt does at any finer level, and no instrument is involved anywhere.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Ines has a genuine explanation, a coarse one. The salt is what produced the melting.

## O8 - Petra's own ovens

**Situation.** Petra explains why the fuse trips in her bakery when both ovens and the mixer run together: their combined draw exceeds what the old wiring carries. Her account covers her bakery and its wiring, and that was its scope from the start. It says nothing about other bakeries.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** a good explanation with an honest scope. The limit says what she is asking about.

## O9 - The float that does two jobs

**Situation.** A float sits in a fuel tank. A rod from it turns a dial on the dashboard. The same float, when it drops low enough, pulls open a valve to the reserve tank. Milan says the falling float opens the valve. Lena says the dial reaching "low" opens it. Bend the needle to read "full": the float is where it was and the valve opens as before. Hold the float up with a wire: the dial reads "full" and the valve stays shut.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Milan is right, Lena is wrong. The float is read by the dial and also does the work.

## O10 - Two thermostats

**Situation.** Two thermostats of the same make hang in two rooms, one set to eighteen degrees and one to twenty-two. Rosa says they are one kind of thing in two different states. Emil says they are two kinds, since one keeps a warm room and one a cool room.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Rosa is right about what the devices are. Emil's difference matters only when the question is why this room is warm.

## O11 - The apprentice and the third lock

**Situation.** On Monday an apprentice tries keys from a ring until one opens the lock. On Tuesday, at a new lock, she feels where a blank catches and files it until it turns. On Wednesday, at a third lock, she picks a key from the ring at once, saying it looks like Tuesday's.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Monday is blind fitting. Tuesday is making something new. Wednesday is neither: she is reusing what Tuesday gave her.

## O12 - The rota

**Situation.** A team argues for weeks about who is lazy. Noor asks a different question: which tasks have no owner? The arguing stops and the work gets done. Nobody had written down, or said aloud, that every task should have an owner.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Noor found a better question, and its merit is real, even though the need it met was only recognised afterwards.

## O13 - Two plumbers

**Situation.** Ana inspects a leak, says the washer has cracked, and explains why that lets water past. Before she can replace it, Ben tightens a nearby joint at random. The leak stops, because tightening shifted the cracked washer back into place.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Ben's act stopped the leak. Ana's explanation is correct and produced nothing. Nobody here made a repair that came from an explanation.

## O14 - The phrasebook

**Situation.** Kofi orders dinner abroad by reading phrases from a book. After a month he can order the same dishes with the book shut. Ask him for a sentence that was never in the book and he has nothing to say.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Kofi has kept a narrow skill by his own effort. He has yet to understand the language.

## O15 - Sam's inherited marks

**Situation.** Sam starts from two diagrams. Every line on both has a recorded preparation: who drew it, from which trial, on which day. One symbol appears on both, a small arrow meaning "the blank catches here", and nobody recorded who first drew it or from what. Sam cuts his new blank by following those arrows.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Sam's account of his own work is complete. Where the arrows came from is a hole in the history of the diagrams, and it is a hole in his account, because his cut followed them.

## O16 - The worn key and the diary

**Situation.** Sam's present diagram matches a worn trial key found in his drawer. To date the trial key, Sam wrote a diary last week, working the dates out from the stages visible in the diagram.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The worn key is evidence on its own. The diary is the diagram talking about itself. Sam has one independent witness, and the diary is a second copy of the first.

## O17 - The robot's log and the maker's manual

**Situation.** A repair robot keeps a log of every comparison it runs. The log shows which routine, running inside the robot, produced today's diagnosis. The maker's manual says the routine is the robot's own "because the robot can diagnose".

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Ownership here rests on the log, which shows the routine running inside the robot. Strike the manual's sentence and nothing is lost; keep only the manual's sentence and there is nothing.

## O18 - The one tooth

**Situation.** A learner receives a complete key diagram from Sam. At a new lock she finds a catch the diagram never covered, files one tooth to fit it, and keeps every other tooth as the diagram gave it.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The one tooth is hers. The rest is relay. She built something, small, and it is exactly the tooth.

## O19 - The diagram in his hand

**Situation.** Sam holds his old spacing diagram open while cutting a new blank. Every measurement he uses, he has just taken from the lock with his own gauge. The diagram's spacings agree with them.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The diagram was in his hand and did no work; the lock did it. Had the diagram said something different, he would have followed the lock.

## O20 - Two valves in one second

**Situation.** Ana and Ben each close a different valve within the same second. The leak stops. The records are complete: either valve alone would have stopped it. Each claims the repair.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Both did something that would have stopped it. Neither can claim it alone. The record shows two sufficient acts and no way to say whose water stopped.

## O21 - The expert's question

**Situation.** A remote expert sends the robot two candidate diagnoses and the one test that would separate them. The robot runs the test on its own evidence and picks the right one.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The robot chose, with its own evidence. The expert did the part that took understanding, knowing what would separate the two. The choosing is the robot's; the diagnosis as an achievement is mostly the expert's.

## O22 - Two knobs, one linkage

**Situation.** Lea asks what happens when two knobs are turned together. On this machine the linkage lets one knob lead the other by a fraction of a second.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Her question is about the pair. The brief lead is part of what "together" means on this machine, and it is a fact about the machine, not a second question about one knob.

## O23 - "The seal was tight"

**Situation.** An investigator's earlier notes record knob settings and temperature, and mention in passing that "the seal was tight". Later she measures seal compression as a variable and finds it matters.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** She added a dimension. Mentioning tightness in passing did not make it something that could vary in her account; measuring it did.

## O24 - The unused joint setting

**Situation.** Two wiring arrangements agree on every tested setting and on almost every untested one. They differ at a single joint setting of two switches, one nobody has used. The setting is reachable by hand.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** They are two arrangements, and the record has not chosen between them. One reachable setting is enough.

## O25 - One second apart

**Situation.** Ana closes valve A at ten o'clock exactly. Ben closes valve B one second later. The flow meter on the leaking pipe reads zero from the moment valve A closed; when Ben's valve closed there was nothing left to stop. Either valve alone would have stopped the leak.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Ana stopped it. Ben's act would have stopped it and did not, because there was nothing left to stop when it came.

## O26 - Two feeds, one moment

**Situation.** The leaking pipe is fed from two sides. Ana closes feed A and Ben closes feed B in the same moment. The leak stops only when both are closed; either alone would have halved it, and the meters on each feed show which half each stopped.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Both did the work, and the record divides it: each stopped the half that ran through their feed.

## O27 - The test that fitted neither

**Situation.** A remote expert sends the robot two candidate diagnoses and the one test that would separate them. The robot runs the test on its own evidence. The result fits neither candidate. The robot notices that the test assumed a pressure sensor was reading true, swaps to the second sensor, runs the test again, and finds a third fault that neither candidate named.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The expert framed the choice. The robot found the answer. The achievement is mostly the robot's, because the decisive step was its own reinterpretation of the test.

## O28 - The card nobody can read

**Situation.** A forecast card was printed in ink and also embossed in raised marks. The ink has faded past reading. The raised marks are intact and were read by touch, in a method one reader knew; that reader has died and taught nobody. A copy made while the ink was fresh gives the card's content.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The marks are still there and still say what they said. What is lost is a reader, and a reader can be trained again from the copy. The provenance is intact; the access is not.

## O29 - The margin note

**Situation.** Diagram A was copied from diagram B, and B from an earlier version of A. That earlier version carried a margin note recording one fitting trial at a real lock. The earlier version burned; the margin note survives only as a line quoted inside B.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** There was a grounded source once. What remains of it is a quotation inside the circle. The chain rests on testimony to a fitting, and on no fitting anyone can now check.

## O30 - The routine uploaded that morning

**Situation.** The robot compares the pressures itself and chooses the valve, using a routine a remote person uploaded to it that morning.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The comparison ran inside the robot and was the robot's work today. Who wrote the routine is a separate fact, and a true one. Ownership of today's history is the robot's; the origin of the routine is the person's.

## O31 - The wrong valve

**Situation.** The learner receives a finished bypass diagnosis. Applying it, she finds it names a valve this plant does not have, works out which valve does the job here, and corrects that one line.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The diagnosis is received. The correction is hers, small and hers. Both facts stand: the received content stays received, and one relation was supplied by her.

## O32 - Found aloud, drawn later

**Situation.** In a meeting, one engineer says aloud that the pressure depends on the second pump; the minutes record it. A week later another engineer draws the bypass on the diagram. The written report credits the drawer with both.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The report is wrong about the division. The minutes are enough of a record: the dependence was found by the one who said it, and the bypass by the one who drew it.

## O33 - The table with empty columns

**Situation.** Lea's question is what happens when the two freezer knobs are turned together. A colleague rewrites it as a table with one column for each knob. Every row moves both knobs together; no row moves one alone.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The table is faithful. Its columns could have carried single-knob rows, and none was added. A form that could ask more has not asked more.

## O34 - Where the second compartment used to be

**Situation.** One claimed change removes the freezer's second compartment. Lea's question asked for the second compartment's temperature. The colleague answers it, after the change, as the temperature of the space where the compartment used to be.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** A meaning has been supplied, and the question now asks something. It is not what Lea asked.

## O35 - Four seconds

**Situation.** During the washer repair the protected tap stops for four seconds while the mains is switched over, then runs as before. Nobody drew from it in those seconds.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The protected condition held in every way that mattered: nobody was without water. The interruption is on the record and is not a loss.

## O36 - A premise both routes use

**Situation.** Two proof routes reach the fixed answer. Both use premise P. Route two could be rewritten to use a different premise in P's place, but nobody has written that version.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** P is critical in each route as written. That another route could be written without it is a fact about a candidate nobody has written.

## O37 - The uncited textbook

**Situation.** The account traces every representation to its starting basis except one, which it justifies by a later theorem; the theorem's proof in the account uses that same representation. A separate proof of the theorem exists in a textbook the account does not cite.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** As written, the order has a circle in it. The textbook proof would break the circle, and the account has not used it. The order is not settled until it does.

## O38 - Written on the job sheet

**Situation.** Before the repair, the job sheet states the protected condition: the kitchen tap runs at all times during the work. During the repair the tap stops for four seconds while the mains is switched over; nobody draws from it. The plumber's report lists the interruption as incidental.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The condition was written down first, and it was broken, for four seconds. Calling it incidental changes nothing. Whether four seconds mattered to anyone is a different question from whether the condition held.

## O39 - Two routes, both running

**Situation.** A leak is stopped when a bypass opens and a valve closes in the same second. Either alone would have stopped all of the flow. Both operated fully. The meter shows the flow dropping to zero once and cannot tell which route took it.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Both operated and both were sufficient. The record cannot divide it and does not need to: the leak was stopped by both, and neither can claim it alone.

## O40 - Two hands on the test

**Situation.** The expert sends the robot two candidate diagnoses and the test that separates them. The result fits neither. The robot notices that the test assumed a sensor was reading true. The expert, watching, proposes swapping to the second sensor. The robot does it, runs the test again, and finds a third fault.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The reinterpretation was theirs together: the robot saw the assumption, the expert saw the fix. Neither can say "mostly".

## O41 - The technician's whisper

**Situation.** The assessment is stated at the start as robot-only. Midway, a technician tells the robot which gauge to read. The robot reads it and completes the diagnosis. The log records the technician's instruction.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The boundary was robot-only, and the instruction crossed it. The diagnosis is a team result under a boundary nobody restated. Robot-only credit is not available for it.

## O42 - New board, old memory

**Situation.** The robot's controller board fails mid-task. A technician fits a new board and copies the memory and routines across. The task finishes. At the start, continuity of memory and routines had been stated as what makes it the same robot.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The same robot finished, on the stated terms. The swap repaired the system; it did not substitute another one.

## O43 - Mirrored marks

**Situation.** Sam's diagram is translated into raised marks by a reader who writes right to left. Every pairing of tooth to obstruction is preserved, and the reader's convention is stated on the sheet.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The transfer preserved the content. The mirror is a convention, declared, and anyone reading with it recovers every pairing.

## O44 - Half from the source, half from the shelf

**Situation.** The copier reproduces the diagram from the source. One damaged section it fills in from a stored earlier version, which happens to match the source exactly there.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Most of the copy depends on the source. The patched section does not, and its matching is luck. The copy is part copy and part coincidence, and the record says which part is which.

## O45 - The spring nobody mentioned

**Situation.** The account of the door's return contains two springs, both connected, both sufficient. The second is never described as doing anything until the first is deleted from the account; then the author says the second was always there.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The second spring was in the account from the start, connected and sufficient. It was already a route. That nobody said it did work does not make it a later addition.

## O46 - The cable that used to be a spring

**Situation.** After the spring is deleted, the author reassigns the surviving component to a cable and shows the door still returns, then reports that the original spring account survived the deletion.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** What survived is a new account with a cable in it. The spring account did not survive; it was replaced, and the replacement's success is its own.

## O47 - One more commitment

**Situation.** A finite collection of commitments supports the conclusion, and every earlier addition to it kept the support. The newest addition, that the room was locked, conflicts with a premise every route uses.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The conclusion no longer follows. The collection stayed finite. What broke is that additions could not always be made without loss, and this one could not.

## O48 - The forbidden wire

**Situation.** The only arrangement that gives a different response at the unseen setting needs a wire between two terminals, and every device in the stated population is built without that wire.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Then there is no alternative in that population. The different response is imaginable and is not available. The premise is not met.

## O49 - Owned means capable

**Situation.** A definition is offered: a routine is the robot's own when the robot can run it, and the robot can run it when it is its own. Beside it are two histories, a routine learned and an answer relayed, told apart by what happened in each.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The definition goes in a circle and gives nothing. The two histories give the distinction its meaning. The definition adds nothing to them and could be dropped.

## O50 - The same afternoon, three achievements

**Situation.** The expert sends two candidate diagnoses and the separating test. The result fits neither. The robot notices the sensor assumption; the expert proposes the swap; the robot does it and finds the third fault. Three things were achieved that afternoon: the reinterpretation of the test, the discovery of the fault, and the completed inquiry from first question to answer.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Credit each achievement on its own. The reinterpretation was theirs together. The discovery was the robot's: it ran the test and found the fault. The inquiry was mostly the expert's: the expert framed it, supplied the candidates and the test, and supplied the fix. One word for all three would be wrong for at least two of them.

## O51 - The module in the casing

**Situation.** A controller runs a diagnostic module bought from a supplier and fitted on a removable processor inside its own shell. The system boundary declared at the start names the controller and its routines and says nothing about the module. The module produces today's diagnosis.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** The diagnosis was produced inside the shell and outside the declared boundary. Where the boundary was drawn decides it, and the boundary was drawn without the module. Being inside the casing changes nothing.

## O52 - The route that started and was stopped

**Situation.** Two routes to stopping the leak start in the same moment: Ana's bypass begins to open, Ben's valve begins to close. Ana's bypass jams a quarter open and takes nothing; Ben's valve closes and the flow stops. The operating log records both starts, the jam, and the closure, before any meter is read.

**Thoughtful person's verdict.** Ben stopped it. Ana's route started and did no stopping. The log says so without the meter.
