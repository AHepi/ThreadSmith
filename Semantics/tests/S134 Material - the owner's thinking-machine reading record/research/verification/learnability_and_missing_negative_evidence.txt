## Verdict on the Generalization Paradox

**Claim: Finite positive examples fit multiple hypotheses, including overly broad ones.** Supported. File page 27 states that "an infinite number of hypotheses are consistent with the input sample but differ from each other and from the correct hypothesis." File page 28 adds that when a hypothesis generates a superset of the target language, "no amount of positive evidence can strictly falsify the guess." Faithful paraphrase: a finite sample underdetermines the correct generalization, and hypotheses that are too permissive survive any amount of confirming data.

**Claim: Absence of a heard form is not automatically evidence of impossibility.** Supported. File page 30 explains that a child's not hearing an ungrammatical sentence "could simply reflect adults' never having had an opportunity to utter them in the child's presence," since infinitely many grammatical sentences are never heard. Faithful paraphrase: silence is ambiguous between ungrammaticality and mere non-occurrence.

**Claim: The demanded experience-to-knowledge explanation.** Supported. File page 23 requires "an unbroken path of explanation from children's experience to adults' knowledge," pursued through "the mental representations and processes that make such learning successful." Faithful paraphrase: Steven Pinker demands a continuous causal account linking input to attained competence, not merely a description of either end.

## Distinction of Levels

**Formal reasoning conditional on a learning setup.** File pages 27 and 28 present the general induction argument: constraints restrict the hypothesis space to one containing the target and excluding many alternatives, after which predictions can be compared with input. The four cases of hypothesis–target relation (disjoint, intersecting, subset, superset) and the differential role of positive and negative evidence are stated as logical properties of this setup. This is conditional formal analysis, not an empirical finding.

**Reported claims about children.** File page 28 reports that Brown and Hanlon (1970) found parents do not differentially approve or disapprove based on well-formedness, and that it is "commonly assumed" children do not depend on negative evidence. File page 30 names the specific verb alternations with exceptions (donate, report, resemble, cry, pour). These are empirical reports and inferences about children, distinct from the formal layer.

**Engineering extension to active experimentation.** Not in the source. Nothing in these pages proposes that a learner can act on the world to generate discriminating observations. That is our engineering proposal and must be labeled as such.

## Does This Establish That Learning Is Impossible Without Assumed Restrictions?

**Claim: Some assumed representation or hypothesis restriction is necessary.** Partially supported, carefully qualified. File page 27 says constraints "force" the learner to entertain a restricted hypothesis set including the correct hypothesis. File page 31 identifies three jointly problematic factors—lack of negative evidence, productivity, and arbitrariness—and says proposed solutions deny one or more of these assumptions. However, the impossibility is stated for the specific case of "acquisition of argument structure alternations in the verb lexicon" (file page 31), not for all learning in all domains. The text does not prove a domain-general theorem that no learner can learn without innate restrictions; it shows that within this learning setup, unrestricted generalization from positive evidence alone cannot converge. Language-learning results do not automatically establish general learning laws.

## Support for Engineering Use and What Remains Unsolved

The source supports three design principles. First, hypothesis spaces must be constrained so that the correct hypothesis is reachable and many alternatives are excluded (file page 27). Second, the learner must distinguish evidence that falsifies from evidence that cannot falsify, and handle the superset case where positive data is silent (file page 28). Third, generalization must be productive yet exception-tolerant, since near-synonyms behave differently (file page 31).

The source does not provide: a mechanism for active observation selection, temporal memory of past experience, compositional construction of new representations, goal selection, or model revision. It does not address non-linguistic perception or action. It does not demonstrate that an inspectable non-neural machine is sufficient; that is our engineering hypothesis, unverified by these pages. Historical criticisms of particular neural networks must not be recast as limits on all neural implementations. Nothing here establishes consciousness or general intelligence in any machine.
