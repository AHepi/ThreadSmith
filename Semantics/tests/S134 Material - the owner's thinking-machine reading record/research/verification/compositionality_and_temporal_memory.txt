## Verdicts

**Role binding.** Supported on file page 74. Steven Pinker argues that concepts must be assigned to roles such as eater and eaten, and that a unit per whole thought fails because combinations explode exponentially and because the recurring rows and columns of thinkable thoughts show that thoughts are assembled from concepts rather than stored whole. He then shows that pairing a concept with a role is still insufficient: "The baby ate the chicken when the poodle ate the slug" becomes indistinguishable from its swap. Faithful paraphrase: binding requires more than concept-to-role associations.

**Structured propositions.** Supported on file pages 75 and 76. The four sentences "Baby same-as baby," "Baby different-from slug," "Slug different from baby," "Slug same-as slug" cannot all be satisfied by any set of weights, an exclusive-or problem in another guise. Pinker concludes that the mind needs a representation for the proposition itself, citing a model by Geoffrey Hinton with a bank of proposition units acting like serial numbers that keep concepts in their slots. He adds that all compositional networks need specially engineered parts separating concepts from roles, and that "association-stuff by itself is not enough." File page 76 extends this to variables and quantifiers, admitting "we have no hint as to how this can be done in an associative network."

**Recursive combination.** Supported on file pages 76 and 77. An entire proposition can occupy a role in a larger proposition, yielding trees of propositions inside propositions and, in principle, infinitely many thoughts. Pinker calls hard-wired layers for each nesting depth clumsy and instead describes a recursive transition network: each simple structure stored once in long-term memory, with a processor shuttling attention and storing its itinerary in short-term memory. He reports looping networks by Michael Jordan and Jeff Elman as only "a glimpse" of iteration, insufficient for structured propositions unless specially assembled into a recursive processor.

**Episodic versus semantic memory.** Supported on file page 76. Citing David Sherry and Dan Schacter and the distinction by Endel Tulving, Pinker says memory demands conflict: remembering individual episodes of who did what to whom, when, where, and why requires stamping each episode with a time, a date, and a serial number, while extracting generic knowledge about people and the world requires a different system. He presents this as Sherry and Schacter's suggestion, not as settled fact.

**Theory-sensitive generalization.** Supported on file pages 78 and 79. Pinker distinguishes two modes: fuzzy stereotypes formed by soaking up correlations, and rule systems or intuitive theories defining categories by the rules applying to them. He disputes the automatic generalization claimed for distributed representations: knowing chimpanzees like onions need not raise the estimate for gorillas, because gorillas are herbivores. He names explanation-based generalization as the alternative to similarity-based generalization, with rule systems couching knowledge in compositional, quantified, recursive propositions forming domain modules.

**Time-indexed versus generic knowledge.** Supported on file page 76: episodes carry time, date, and serial number; semantic knowledge abstracts from episodes. The passage does not specify data structures.

**Explicit records and deterministic program construction.** Not prescribed by Pinker. File page 76 does say each proposition could have "its own storage slot and address," and that not all computer design features are silicon curiosities, but this is offered as one way to package information, not a mandate. Deterministic program construction is absent from these pages and is our engineering choice.

**Qualifications preserved.** The network criticisms concern particular historical designs: Neal Cohen and Michael McCloskey's digit-adding network suffered catastrophic forgetting; the McClelland and Alan Kawamoto network produced a chimeric bat interpretation. These are examples of interference or cross-talk in superimposing patterns, not limits on all neural implementations. Pinker explicitly credits auto-associators with prototype-like categorization and credits looping networks with a glimpse of iteration. Language-learning results appear nowhere in these pages.

## Engineering reach

The source supports the need for role binding, proposition-level units, variable binding, recursion with a processor and itinerary, separate episodic and semantic stores, and theory-indexed generalization. It leaves unsolved the actual mechanism for quantification in any network, the representation format of intuitive theories, how theories are acquired or revised, how goals are selected, and how observations are gathered. Explicit records, deterministic construction, active perception, and revisable model revision are our extensions, not demonstrated by these pages. Nothing here establishes consciousness or general intelligence.
