Research purpose

Develop a proposed way to build a thinking machine using the two supplied books by Steven Pinker. MiMo 2.6 Pro is the designated reader of the books and of long source texts. Every reading request supplies the purpose, asks for source locations, and separates source claims from engineering proposals. The main assistant synthesizes the resulting evidence and takes responsibility for the proposed theory.

The question held fixed

Can a concrete, inspectable computational architecture acquire and use representations to interpret observations, retain relevant history, construct new combinations, predict consequences, choose informative actions, and revise mistaken expectations? A proposed construction is the requested outcome. No claim of demonstrated intelligence, consciousness, or human psychological fidelity follows from this research alone.

Requirements and their origins

The user supplied the aim of a thinking machine, the two source books, MiMo 2.6 Pro as the reader, small reading contexts, explicit reading purpose, and full consistent names without abbreviations or numbered functions. The visible surrounding project context motivates a non-neural candidate, temporal memory, and an early circle and square task. These project choices are not attributed to Pinker, and a limited shape experiment must not silently replace the larger aim.

The following test standards are proposed by the main assistant, rather than attributed to the user or the books: actual executable operations must do the cognitive work; supplied structure must be distinguished from acquired structure; behaviour must transfer to untrained changes; an unresolved answer must remain possible; competing explanations should lead to discriminating observations; memory and learned representations must causally affect subsequent action; time and memory costs must be measured; source support and original speculation must remain separate.

Changes the proposed architecture should face

Vary location, scale, orientation, irrelevant colour, observation order and duration separately from class identity. Present identical final observations after different histories. Introduce unfamiliar shapes and insufficient evidence. Make candidate world models agree on past observations while predicting different outcomes for an available action. Require familiar relations to be combined in a new task. Change the observation mechanism separately from the external object. Remove, scramble or substitute internal state without changing the current sensory input. Record which commitments fail and whether repair requires task-specific information from the designer.

Proposed contrasts and rivals

A fixed classifier can demonstrate discrimination but not by itself model construction. A finite history table can reproduce familiar episodes but should be compared on new lengths and combinations under a common resource limit. An exhaustive program search can eventually find a bounded solution but may fail the practical computation budget. A learned statistical implementation may realize the same useful structures as an explicit symbolic implementation; failure to distinguish these routes means the exact implementation is still a design choice, not an established necessity.

Source and method limitations

The initial reading uses text extracted from each document with ordinal file-page markers. Figures, unusual mathematical notation and extraction defects can lose information; MiMo must flag these. Final material attributions will be sent to MiMo again with the original relevant passages. The reading log demonstrates what was submitted and which responses completed; it does not prove perfect comprehension. Independent conceptual criticism is separate from an experiment. No thinking machine has been implemented or evaluated in this task.

Second reading requested by the user

The user requested another reading in roughly thirty-percent sections and explicitly allowed three concurrent MiMo runs with distinct jobs. Each of three readers therefore reads both complete books, divided by cumulative extracted text into three near-thirty-percent sections and a final near-ten-percent section. The jobs are implementable cognitive mechanisms; learning, generalization and temporal memory; and independent criticism with alternative explanations. Previous notes within a job preserve continuity, but the original text is supplied again for every section. After all readings finish, each job will be consolidated by MiMo and material book attributions checked against original passages.
