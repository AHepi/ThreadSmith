# Research Memorandum: Independent Criticism and Alternative Explanations

## Scope and Method

This memorandum covers file pages 362 through 455 of *How the Mind Works*, which comprise the continuation and conclusion of the references section, the complete index, and the table of contents. This section is almost entirely bibliographic and apparatus material. It contains no sustained argumentative prose by Steven Pinker. I therefore do not infer arguments from reference titles or index entries, and I use the index only as a finding aid that confirms topics and printed page locations discussed elsewhere in the book. Where the index supplies a printed page number, I report it as an index claim, not as a page I have verified in the argument. The table of contents at file pages 453 through 455 confirms the chapter structure and section headings, which I use to orient the unresolved questions rather than to reconstruct arguments.

Because this section is apparatus rather than argument, I cannot deliver the same kind of causal-mechanism critique that the previous sections supported. What I can do is three things: flag the extraction gap honestly, use the index to identify where the provisional theory's weakest points are addressed elsewhere in the book so that the next reader knows where to look, and state clearly what the apparatus cannot establish.

## The Extraction Gap

The user requested a second reading in sections of roughly thirty percent of each book with three concurrent readers. The section assigned to me is approximately ten percent of *How the Mind Works* and is bibliography and index. This is a material gap in the evidence base for the current memorandum. I cannot check whether Pinker actually says the things the index attributes to him, and I cannot verify that the printed page numbers in the index correspond to the file pages I have been given. Any claim I make about what Pinker argues must therefore be deferred to a reader who has the argumentative chapters.

## What the Index Confirms About the Provisional Theory's Weak Points

The index at file page 411 lists "Artificial intelligence" with subentries for emotions, goal-seeking, reasoning, robotics, and vision, with printed page references including 15 through 18, 69 through 76, and 373 through 374. This confirms that Pinker discusses artificial intelligence in connection with goal-seeking and emotion, which is directly relevant to the provisional theory's claim that "initial structure and goal priorities are supplied explicitly." The index entry for "Goals" at file page 425 points to printed pages 61 through 62 and 372 through 374. The entry for "Motivation" at file page 437 points to printed pages 15 through 18, 43 through 44, 143, and 372 through 374. These locations suggest that Pinker treats goals and motivation as computational problems, not as static priority lists. The provisional theory's fixed priorities remain vulnerable to the challenge I raised in the previous memorandum: emotions may be the mechanism that sets and revises goals dynamically.

The index entry for "Emotion" at file pages 420 through 421 lists adaptive function, facial expressions, hydraulic model, neuroanatomy, phylogeny, and universality, with printed page references including 363 through 424. The entry for "Doomsday Machine" at file page 419 points to printed pages 407 through 408, 414, 416, 421, 424, 489, and 496 through 497. This confirms that the doomsday-machine argument recurs across multiple chapters, which strengthens the challenge to any architecture that assumes rational revision is always desirable.

The index entry for "Self-deception" at file page 445 points to printed pages 272, 305, 421 through 424, 445, 448, and 543. The entry for "Consciousness" at file page 417 distinguishes access to information, evolutionary benefits of access, evolutionary benefits of lack of access, neural correlates, self-knowledge, and sentience. This confirms that Pinker separates the computational aspect of consciousness from sentience, which supports the provisional theory's refusal to claim consciousness but also confirms that the theory must not imply sentience.

The index entry for "Compositionality" at file page 416 points to printed pages 118 through 122 and cross-references "Combinatorics," which lists language, music, object parts, reasoning, thoughts, social interactions, and visual features. This confirms that compositionality is a cross-cutting problem in Pinker's framework, not a solved one. The provisional theory's "elementary operations and previously constructed subprograms" must specify what the elementary operations are and how they bind, or the composition problem is hidden inside the primitives.

The index entry for "Memory" at file pages 435 through 436 distinguishes episodic from semantic, long-term from short-term, content-addressable, and multiple systems, with printed page references including 90, 104, 124, 136 through 137, 142 through 143, 286, 287, 289, and 294 through 295. This confirms that the episode-versus-knowledge distinction in the provisional theory is at least compatible with Pinker's framework, but the index also lists "multiple systems," which suggests that a single episode store may be insufficient.

## Alternative Mechanisms the Index Leaves Open

The index lists "Production systems" at file page 442 with printed page references 69 through 76, 137, and 373. This is a concrete alternative to the provisional theory's "bounded deterministic program construction": a production-system architecture with condition-action rules and a working-memory buffer. The index also lists "Constraint satisfaction" at file page 416 with printed page references 105 through 106 and 235 through 236, and "Neural networks" at file page 438 with printed page references 99 through 111. These are three distinct architectural families that the source discusses, and the provisional theory has not yet argued why bounded deterministic program construction is preferable to any of them.

The index lists "Case-based reasoning" at file page 414 with printed page reference 543. This is a direct alternative to the provisional theory's model-revision mechanism: instead of constructing and revising general models, the machine could retrieve and adapt specific past episodes. The index also lists "Explanation-based learning" in the references at file page 367 (de Jong and Mooney) and file page 391 (Pazzani), which suggests that Pinker cites learning mechanisms that use existing knowledge to constrain generalization, a possible alternative to the provisional theory's "seek observations on which candidates disagree."

## Discriminating Tests

To distinguish production systems from bounded program construction, give the machine a task requiring rapid context-sensitive rule application with interleaved subgoals, and inspect whether the trace shows condition-action matching or program synthesis. To distinguish case-based reasoning from model revision, present a novel problem superficially similar to a past episode but structurally different, and inspect whether the machine retrieves the episode or constructs a new model. To distinguish constraint satisfaction from sequential search, present an ambiguous percept with many interacting cues and inspect whether the machine settles into a stable interpretation or enumerates candidates.

## Unresolved Questions

The next reader should address: whether Pinker's discussion of production systems at printed pages 69 through 76 actually specifies the working-memory and conflict-resolution mechanisms that the provisional theory lacks; whether the discussion of constraint satisfaction at printed pages 105 through 106 and 235 through 236 offers a mechanism for perceptual grounding that the provisional theory's "calibrated sensor records" does not; whether the discussion of case-based reasoning at printed page 543 is Pinker's own proposal or a citation of someone else's; and whether the discussion of multiple memory systems at printed page 124 supports or undermines the provisional theory's episode-versus-knowledge distinction. The index also lists "Frame problem" at file page 423 with printed page reference 15, which is the classic obstacle to any architecture that revises models after failures, and the next reader should check what Pinker actually says about it.
