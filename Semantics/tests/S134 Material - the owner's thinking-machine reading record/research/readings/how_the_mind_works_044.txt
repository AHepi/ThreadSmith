**Subject and central claims.** This section opens the chapter on the psychology of social relations, arguing that natural selection shaped inborn motives that often put people in conflict, then turns to kinship as the first social bond (file page 250). Steven Pinker reasons that reproduction on a finite planet makes organisms compete to some degree, so humans descend from ancestors who reproduced under competition and are designed to compete at least sometimes (file page 250). He immediately limits this claim: competition does not imply an aggressive urge, death wish, rapacious drive, or territorial imperative. Even harsh competition requires strategy — assessing whether retreat, conciliation, or cooperation serves one's goals (file page 250). Genes, not organisms, compete; sometimes the best genetic strategy is a cooperative organism. Cooperation and generosity are difficult engineering problems, like stereoscopic vision, and require sophisticated programs assessing opportunities and risks (file page 250). Pinker rejects the inference that conflicting motives justify exploitation or make reform futile, citing homicide rates varying from 0.5 per million per year in Iceland to 100 in the United States and Brazil, and noting universal disapproval of violence plus sanctions, mediation, ostracism, and law (file page 250).

**Kinship.** Pinker claims love of kin comes naturally while love of non-kin does not, because relatives share genes, so genes for helping relatives spread; most animal altruism benefits kin, with social insects as extreme cases (file page 251). In humans this becomes two psychological machineries, cognitive and emotional (file page 251). The cognitive part is a desire and ability to learn one's family tree. Genealogy is digital (you are or are not someone's mother), relational (a father is father *of* someone), topological (kin terms read off a web of parenthood, generation, gender — a parallel cousin is a father's brother's child or mother's sister's child), and self-contained (age, status, zodiac sign lie in a different plane) (file pages 251–252). People assess kin indirectly: who grew up together, resemblance, interaction, reliable testimony, logical deduction from other kin relations (file page 252). The emotional part adds solidarity, sympathy, tolerance, trust, doled out by nearness in the tree, confidence in that nearness, and impact on the relative's reproduction; no one consciously crunches genetic data — programs were calibrated in evolution (file page 252). Pinker disputes claims that the nuclear family is an aberration, that primitive marriage was absent, that romantic love was invented by troubadours, or that parental concern is recent, calling these false (file page 252). All societies have marriage centered on a mother and her biological children (file page 252).

**Evidence and disagreements.** Pinker cites Daly and Wilson against the "myth of the cruel stepparent": step-parent villainy appears in folklore worldwide, and stepparenthood is the strongest identified risk factor for child abuse, with a stepparent forty to a hundred times more likely to kill a young child even controlling for confounds (file pages 253–254). He counters the folk claim that relatives are the likeliest killers: only two to six percent of homicide victims are killed by blood relatives, and holding co-residence constant, risk from a nonrelative is at least eleven times greater (file page 254). Homicide is treated as an assay of conflict because it is meticulously documented (file page 254). Chagnon's Yanomamo genealogies show kinship as the cement of villages: close kin fight less and aid more; fission follows declining relatedness (file page 254).

**Engineering relevance.** For a thinking machine, the kinship model offers a concrete representational design: a typed, relational, self-contained graph with digital predicates, plus a separate affect-like weighting module driven by graph distance and confidence — not memorized labels but computed relations. The strategy-versus-instinct distinction maps onto a decision layer that evaluates retreat, conciliation, or cooperation per situation rather than firing fixed aggression. The step-parent and homicide analyses model how to constrain generalization: hold exposure constant before inferring risk. Pinker's claims are about evolved human psychology; they neither demonstrate nor require a non-neural implementation, and nothing here establishes consciousness or general intelligence. The section says little about perception, learning algorithms, semantics, memory, time, causation, analogy, or creativity; those remain unsolved.
