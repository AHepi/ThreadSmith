**Subject and central claims.** This section continues Steven Pinker's evolutionary-psychology account of music and then turns to fiction and the beginnings of humor. On music he catalogs candidate explanations for why it moves us: harmonic tones let the auditory system carve the world into resonating soundmakers, with stable notes grouping securely to a fundamental and minor intervals leaving source assignment unsettled, possibly felt as sadness (file page 310). He also proposes emotional calls, habitat signals, and motor control as sources of musical affect, noting theorists Jackendoff, Clynes, and Epstein on music recreating movement's motivational components (file pages 310–311). A sixth "something else" option is left deliberately open, and Pinker stresses the analysis is speculative: music was chosen because it shows the clearest signs of *not* being an adaptation (file page 311).

**Fiction as simulation and instruction.** Pinker separates fiction's "delight" (pleasure technology) from its "instruction" (a cognitive adaptation), citing Horace and Dryden (file page 311). Fiction delivers a simulation of life: words evoke mental images activating perceptual areas, while costumes, sets, and cinematography exploit perceptual assumptions (file page 311). He explains tearjerkers through Paul Rozin's benign masochism, with conditions of safety, graduated pain, and control (file page 312), and gossip through the strategic value of social knowledge (file pages 312–313).

**Narrative, goals, and reasoning.** Drawing on computer scientist Jerry Hobbs, Pinker argues novels work like experiments: a fictitious character is placed in a hypothetical situation in an otherwise law-governed world, and consequences unfold causally (file page 312). Kafka's *Metamorphosis* adds one counterfactual premise and derives results; only fiction about logic itself, like *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*, lets anything happen (file page 312). Plot is defined as a protagonist pursuing a goal against obstacles — identical to Pinker's earlier definition of intelligence (file page 312). Because social life is combinatorially vast, like chess, general maxims are too vague; a mental catalogue of cases and outcomes is needed, which Pinker names case-based reasoning in artificial intelligence (file pages 313–314). Fiction supplies such catalogues of fatal conundrums and strategy outcomes (file page 314).

**Art, creativity, and humor.** Via Nelson Goodman, Pinker describes "repleteness": in art every feature of the medium may carry meaning, unlike an electrocardiogram where only point positions matter (file page 314). Cole Porter's "Ev'ry Time We Say Goodbye" aligns key change, lyrics, and mood (file pages 314–315). Anomalies in art prompt audiences to resolve puzzles, reinforcing effect — an insight Pinker credits to Arthur Koestler's *The Act of Creation* (file page 315). The humor section opens with Koestler's puzzle of laughter as a purposeless reflex and Robert Provine's finding that people laugh far more with others (file page 315).

**Limits and uncertainty.** Pinker repeatedly flags speculation (music's origins, soundtrack effects) and notes competing explanations remain unresolved (file pages 310–311). **Engineering implications (mine, not Pinker's).** For a thinking machine, this section suggests mechanisms worth implementing: source-segregation of sensory streams into object hypotheses with confidence graded by signal clarity (file page 310); simulation of hypothetical situations under default physical and social laws with a small number of counterfactual premises (file page 312); goal-obstacle plot structures as a driver of planning (file page 312); and case-based retrieval of prior episodes to constrain combinatorial search where general rules underdetermine action (file pages 313–314). Repleteness hints at representing meaning distributed across many features of a representation rather than a single channel (file pages 314–315). None of this is presented as sufficient for human intelligence or consciousness; the source offers functional hypotheses, not a validated architecture. Innate versus acquired distinctions are implicit in the adaptation talk but not formalized here.
