# Health, medicine and public health

Use this file for treatment effects, trials, observational studies, screening, side-effect and safety claims, and health advice.

This skill finds missing tests. It does not give medical advice. Report what the evidence does and does not show, and leave treatment decisions to qualified people.

## Typical claims

- "Drug X lowers blood pressure."
- "The programme reduced admissions."
- "No serious side effects."
- "People who do Y live longer."

## Where the falsifiers usually are

- **Q6 Premise match: who got the treatment.** Without randomization, treated and untreated people differ: health, wealth, age, care-seeking. The falsifier: the "effect" appears for an outcome the treatment cannot affect (a negative control outcome), or before treatment started.
- **Q10 Order of events: registered endpoints.** Was the primary outcome registered before the trial? A switched endpoint, or a subgroup found afterwards, is a new claim.
- **Q9 Draws and size: rare harms.** A trial of a few thousand people for 12 weeks cannot see rare or late harms. "No side effects observed" means unknown beyond what the trial could have detected. State that limit as a number where possible.
- **Q1 Scope: population and duration.** Ages, conditions, other medicines and settings outside the trial are outside the claim.
- **Q7 Same question: surrogate outcomes.** A lower blood marker is not fewer heart attacks. Say which outcome was measured.
- **Q5 Worst single case: subgroups and dropouts.** An average benefit can hide harm in a subgroup. Dropouts that differ between arms can create an effect.
- **Q11 Receipts: independence.** Replication by a different group, in a different setting, under the same protocol.
- **Q13 What is counted: who finished.** Analyses only of people who completed treatment (per-protocol), or only of those still in follow-up, can create an effect. Compare with intention-to-treat.

## The well-tested case

A randomized, blinded, preregistered trial with independent replication has sought its main falsifiers. Say so. List only the real scope limits: population, duration, rare harms, hard outcomes. Do not demand that the main tests be redone.

## Mini example

**Claim:** "The fall-prevention programme cut hospital admissions by 20% compared with the year before."

**Missing:**
- A comparison group in the same year (Q6).
- Regression to the mean, if sites were chosen after a bad year (Q9).
- Changes in admission policy (Q3).

**Next test:** compare with matched sites without the programme over the same year.
