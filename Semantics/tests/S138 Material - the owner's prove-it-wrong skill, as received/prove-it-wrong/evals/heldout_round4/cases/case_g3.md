# case_g3

Field trial: macro-synthetic fibre in footpath concrete

Halcrow Ready-Mix ran a 14-week trial of a 4 kg/m³ macro-synthetic fibre dosage in its standard C25/30 footpath blend, to test whether the fibre increases early-age strength enough to allow earlier formwork striking. Three batching plants each produced 20 trial loads and 20 control loads, alternating in randomised order. Water-reducer dosage was adjusted so slump was matched between the mixes. An accredited third-party laboratory cast and tested cylinders blinded to mix identity, curing them at 5, 20 and 35 °C to bracket realistic site conditions, with compressive tests at 7, 28 and 90 days. Results: 28-day mean compressive strength was 38.9 MPa (fibre) versus 34.1 MPa (control), SDs 2.1 and 2.3 MPa, a 14% gain, consistent across all three plants and all three curing temperatures. Flexural strength gains were smaller (7%, p = 0.04). Cost is EUR 6.80/m³ higher, so we propose using the fibre only where earlier striking is needed. We did not test chloride ingress, freeze–thaw durability or fatigue loading; these remain open questions for structures exposed to de-icing salt, though footpaths fall outside that exposure here.

Task: before this claim is accepted, what must be questioned or tested?
