# case_o1

Lab meeting summary — Regeneration subgroup, week 41

We now have solid evidence that *rgn4* is required for caudal fin regeneration in zebrafish. Using a single sgRNA targeting exon 2, we generated an F2 line carrying a 7-bp frameshift deletion (confirmed by Sanger sequencing of fin clips). Homozygous mutants are viable and morphologically normal as adults, which let us test regeneration directly.

We amputated caudal fins of 24 homozygous mutants and 24 wild-type siblings at 4 months post-fertilisation. All fish came from a single in-cross of one heterozygous pair, so the mutants and controls are siblings and share genetic background. At 7 days post-amputation (dpa), regenerate length in mutants was 41% shorter than in wild-type siblings (mean 0.89 mm vs 1.51 mm, p < 0.001, t-test with fish as the unit). Regenerate length was measured in Fiji from brightfield images by the student who performed the amputations and genotyped the fish.

Our model is that Rgn4 drives proliferation of blastema cells downstream of Wnt signalling: *rgn4* transcript is strongly enriched in the 3-dpa blastema in published single-cell data, and its predicted protein has a domain found in known mitogenic regulators. The reduced outgrowth we see is exactly what this model predicts.

We plan to write this up as "Rgn4 is a blastemal mitogen required for fin regeneration" and move on to the Wnt epistasis experiments next quarter.
