# case_o2

Regional seismic network — quarterly science note

**Wastewater injection at the Harlan disposal field has triggered a rise in local seismicity.**

Injection at the three Harlan disposal wells began in March 2024. Using our new deep-learning phase picker, reprocessed over the full continuous archive, we compared earthquake rates within 15 km of the wells before and after injection began. In the 24 months before March 2024 the catalogue contains 38 events; in the 24 months after it contains 211 events, a 5.5-fold increase. The increase is significant under a Poisson rate comparison (p < 10⁻⁶).

The 15 km radius was chosen after inspecting the epicentre map, because it captures the cluster that appeared after injection while excluding the long-known Sutter fault zone to the north. Most of the new events are small (M0.5–M1.8), consistent with the low-magnitude induced seismicity reported elsewhere.

Because the archive was reprocessed with the same picker throughout, detection is consistent across the whole period, and the rate change cannot be an artefact of changing processing methods.

Hypocentres cluster at 3–6 km depth. We note that the regional network was densified in early 2024 with eight new broadband stations funded under the hazard programme, which has greatly improved location quality in this area.

We recommend that the state regulator treat Harlan as an induced-seismicity case and consider injection-rate limits.

Task: before this claim is accepted, what must be questioned or tested?
