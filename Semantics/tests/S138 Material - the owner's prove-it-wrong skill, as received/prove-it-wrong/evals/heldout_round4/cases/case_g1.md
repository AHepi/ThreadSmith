Marsh Street bus priority: first results

Since the bus lane and signal priority on Marsh Street went live on 12 March, corridor travel times are down and ridership is up. Buses now complete the 3.1 km corridor in a mean of 9 minutes 40 seconds versus 12 minutes 10 seconds in the February baseline, a 21% improvement, measured automatically from vehicle GPS across all scheduled trips. Corridor boardings rose 15% in the eight weeks after opening compared with the eight weeks before, drawn from the automatic passenger counters on the four routes that use the corridor. General-traffic GPS speeds on Marsh Street itself fell only slightly (mean reduction 2.4 km/h), which we take as evidence that the lane did not materially worsen conditions for cars. Passenger satisfaction on the corridor routes rose from 71% to 79% in the April onboard survey. The programme was delivered alongside the citywide signal-retiming project that began rolling out in mid-March and the fare freeze announced in February, which have likely supported these numbers as well. On this basis we propose extending bus priority to the Ferndale Road and Eastgate corridors in the next budget round, projecting similar 15–20% boarding gains there.
