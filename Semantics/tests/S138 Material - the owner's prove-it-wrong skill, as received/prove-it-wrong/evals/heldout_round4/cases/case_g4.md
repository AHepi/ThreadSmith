# case_g4

Beetroot nitrate and 20 km cycling time trial

We tested whether acute nitrate supplementation improves endurance in trained club cyclists. Fourteen members of a regional club (11 men, 3 women, mean VO2max 54) each completed a 20 km laboratory time trial twice, one week apart, in a fixed order: first after a 70 ml beetroot shot (≈6.4 mmol nitrate), then after a volume-matched blackcurrant placebo shot. Mean completion time improved by 1.8% on beetroot (95% CI 0.6 to 3.0%) and mean power output rose 2.1%. Participants were asked to record their diet in a diary on trial days and to avoid caffeine; the diaries showed no protocol violations. All trials took place at 07:00 before work. The head coach, who designed the study and knew which shot was which, administered the drinks and operated the stopwatch timing displayed to riders, because the laboratory's timing rig was being serviced. Riders remarked that the beetroot shot was unmistakable in taste and colour. We conclude that nitrate gives club cyclists a meaningful ~2% time-trial benefit and recommend it for race week.
