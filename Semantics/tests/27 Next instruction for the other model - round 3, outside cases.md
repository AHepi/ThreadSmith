## Case O1 - The forecaster who keeps finding honest limits
**Aimed at:** Amendment A, legitimate scope.
**The situation.** Bruno sells a rule for predicting when the village stream floods. It fails in March, and he restates it as a rule for months outside the thaw. It fails on a dry-month Tuesday after a dam release upstream, and he restates it for days with no dam release. It fails again, and he restates it for days when the wind is from the west. Each new limit is described in ordinary terms anyone can check, with no mention of whether his rule succeeds. Each restated rule has so far been right inside its limits. He records every earlier failure openly.
**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Bruno has no explanation of the floods. Each limit is honest taken alone, and the series as a whole is a retreat.

## Case O2 - The tide table with a real bell attached
**Aimed at:** Amendments B and C, the difference between a disguised answer table and anchored parts.
**The situation.** Carla explains the harbour's tides with two parts. Part one is the printed almanac of high and low water for every day. Part two is a true local mechanism: when the float in the harbour gauge rises past a mark, it tips a lever and the harbour bell rings. She offers both together as her account of why the water is high at noon today. Take the bell mechanism away and what her account establishes does change, since it no longer says the bell will ring. The bell mechanism is physically real and independently grounded.
**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Carla has explained the bell. She has explained nothing about the tide. The almanac is the answer written down.

## Case O3 - Nadia and the cards she has never seen
**Aimed at:** Amendment E, construction and first representations.
**The situation.** Nadia learns a card game only by watching cards being laid on the table. She is never shown a hidden hand and nobody tells her hands exist. For weeks she predicts the next card from the cards just played, and is often surprised. One evening she starts keeping a tally of cards that have yet to appear, and treats them as "waiting somewhere". Her predictions improve sharply, and she can now say why a player with few cards left must play a certain suit.
**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Nadia built something new: a part that stands for cards she has never observed. That is construction, and it is her own.

## Case O4 - Dormitive power, with a meter
**Aimed at:** Amendment B, a commitment specified independently of the answer.
**The situation.** Dario explains why a certain syrup puts people to sleep: it has a high somnic index. He defines the somnic index carefully and without mentioning people at all. It is the reading on a standard instrument that times how quickly a mouse stops moving after a measured dose. Syrups with a high index do put people to sleep, and ones with a low index do so less. Lower the index by diluting the syrup, and people stay awake longer.
**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Dario has a useful measure and a reliable prediction. He has said almost nothing about why the syrup causes sleep. The index is the sleepiness itself, observed in a mouse.
