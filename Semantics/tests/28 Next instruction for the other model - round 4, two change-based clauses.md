## Clause A-prime (proposed, for Amendment A)
A limit on scope is legitimate when the account itself contains a part that says why the excluded circumstances differ. The test is a swap: put a neighbouring limit in its place. If the account does every job as well as before, the limit is loose, and the narrowed claim is fitted to its failures. If the swap leaves some job undone, name the part that holds the limit in place.

## Clause C-prime (proposed, for Amendment C)
A part offered as what produces the outcome must respond as a producer on the question's change list. The list must contain two changes for that part: one that alters the thing itself, and one that alters only how the thing is measured or reported. A producer passes when the first change alters the outcome and the second leaves the outcome as it was. A part that moves with the second change has the response of a measurement, and supports an answer to "what can we tell", which is a different question from "what produces it".

## Case O5 - Greta's dough
**Aimed at:** clause A-prime, as a control that should pass.
**The situation.** Greta's rule says her bread dough doubles in two hours. It fails in January. She restates it for rooms warmer than twenty degrees, and her account says why: the yeast that makes the gas works slowly in the cold. Replace her limit with "on days other than Tuesdays" and her account has nothing to say about why Tuesdays would matter.
**The thoughtful person's verdict.** This is a legitimate narrowing. The limit follows from a part of her account.

## Case O6 - The fever and the thermometer
**Aimed at:** clause C-prime, as a control on the two kinds of question.
**The situation.** Hana says the child has a fever, since the thermometer reads thirty-nine. Later she says the thermometer reading is what is making the child shiver. Warm the thermometer in her hand and the reading climbs while the child shivers exactly as before. Bring the child's temperature down with a cool bath and the shivering eases.
**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Her first statement is a good reason to believe there is a fever. Her second statement mistakes the instrument for the illness.

## Case O7 - Salt on the icy step
**Aimed at:** clause C-prime, as a control that a coarse producer should pass.
**The situation.** Ines says the ice on her step melted because she spread salt on it. Spread salt and the ice melts within the hour. Sweep the salt off at once and the ice stays. She has no account of what the salt does at any finer level, and no instrument is involved anywhere.
**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Ines has a genuine explanation, a coarse one. The salt is what produced the melting.
