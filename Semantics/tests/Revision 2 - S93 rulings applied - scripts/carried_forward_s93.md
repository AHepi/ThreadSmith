The S93 rulings pass these on without an edit. None is an edit in this draft. Each is for the owner, the orchestrator or a later revision, and any that changes the theory text needs an entry of its own. `results/S93 Reading of the replies.md` gives each with its ruling. Line numbers here are draft 4's, as the rulings give them.

**For the owner (rule 6).** Each ruling that met one of these says that the present wording states the matter truly; the choice is the owner's.
- **(c1) The symmetry on the Greeks' question.** On the Greeks' question the tilt and the myth, offered in place of each other and both fitting what an assessor has established, are each easy to vary relative to the other. All four answers (Atria and Mimo, parts B and E) read this HARMLESS, and none proposes words. The X10 ruling adds that on the Greeks' question itself the tilt stays easy to vary relative to the myth until something established shows the myth failing (E) on the Greeks' own contract, which ties (c1) to (c2), and that L317's "has not settled" must then be read as "not settled on \(p\)".
- **(c2) Whether a failure found by examining a candidate counts as established.** Five answers say the text leaves it open (UNSETTLED BY THE TEXT) and one (Mimo B) that it already settles it (ESTABLISHED); no reply argues that such failures should not count. Six settling proposals differ on whether the examination is itself a receipt, whether (K3) qualifies such a result, and where the words go (the X09 ruling, "For the owner"). Any settlement changes what the theory claims and needs a declaration, and L315's "the test that yields it" must agree with it. X14's third sentence stands on either settlement the replies propose (the X14 ruling).
- **(d) Losses the replies name.** A conflict at a pair that only one of the two transports translates is not counted; "easy to vary" never names a swappable pick such as N25's dog and turtle; variations that differ only where no admitted change reaches are never rivals; and "offered as an answer to \(p\) in place of the other" may be narrower than the owner's "discovered" (the X09 ruling, (d); the X03 ruling, (d); the X06 ruling, ruling 7).
