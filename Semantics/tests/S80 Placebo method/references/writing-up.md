# Writing up

Open this when writing up. The aim is a report from which the author can find, understand and correct every problem, most serious first.

## The grades

| Grade | Means | Also say |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Changes a main conclusion, or makes the document unsafe or unusable | what the conclusion becomes if corrected |
| Major | A clear error or inconsistency that damages trust but leaves the main conclusion standing | every place it appears |
| Minor | A small error or unclear passage that costs the reader time or confidence | a suggested rewording |
| Mechanical | Typo, spelling, punctuation, formatting | nothing more; list them |
| Query | You cannot tell whether it is a problem | what would settle it |

## The layout of a finding

**Example.** "Some figures seem inconsistent" gives the author nothing to do. "Summary, sentence 2 and section 5, para 3 (major, inconsistency): costs for 2024 given as £410,000 and £421,000. Suggest checking the table; if £421,000 is right, the summary's 12% is also right" gives them one thing to check and a likely answer.

- **Location:** section, paragraph, line, equation, table or figure; quote a few words if needed.
- **Grade and kind:** critical, major, minor or mechanical; error, inconsistency or presentation.
- **What is wrong:** one or two sentences.
- **Evidence:** your working, the other location, or the source's wording.
- **Proposed fix:** where you can offer one.

## The order of the report

- **Brief, summary, overall assessment,** then findings from most to least serious, then mechanical corrections, then queries, then coverage.
- **Group repeated problems** into one finding with a list of locations.
- **State what you checked and what you did not.** Then one suggested next step.

## Traps

- Listing findings in page order with no grades.
- Reporting a finding without the evidence for it.
- Mixing queries in with confirmed findings.
- Leaving out what was not checked.
