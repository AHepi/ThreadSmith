# Glossary

This file gives plain meanings for the reviewing and editorial terms used in this method, and for terms a reviewer is likely to meet in style guides, journal instructions and editors' notes. It also says where in the method each term is used, so that a step can be traced to the file that explains it.

## Where the conventions come from

- **General editorial practice.** Most of the terms below are in common use among copy-editors, proofreaders, technical editors and journal referees. Their meanings are broadly stable, though house styles differ on detail. Where a house style or a journal's instructions are supplied, they take precedence over this glossary.
- **Mathematical and technical writing.** Terms about proofs, notation and derivations follow ordinary usage in mathematical writing. Where a document defines one of these terms differently, the document's definition applies inside that document, and any difference from ordinary usage is worth noting if a reader might be misled.

## Notes on use

- **Grades are judgements.** Two careful reviewers may grade the same finding differently. Say why you chose the grade when it is not obvious.
- **"Error" and "inconsistency" overlap.** An inconsistency means at least one of the two places is an error. Report it as an inconsistency when you cannot tell which; report it as an error once you can.
- **"Query" is not a softer word for "error".** Use it only when you genuinely cannot tell.
- **Style guides differ.** Where the document follows a consistent style that differs from your own, it is not an error. Where it follows no consistent style, the inconsistency is the finding.

## Traps

- Using a term here in a sense the document defines differently. Inside the document, the document's definition applies.
- Treating "minor" as "optional". Minor findings still need correcting; the grade says when, not whether.
- Reporting a house-style preference as an error when no house style was given.
- Using editorial jargon in the report that the author may not know. Explain it, or use plain words.
