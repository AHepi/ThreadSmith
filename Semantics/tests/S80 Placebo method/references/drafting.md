# Drafting

Open this when the document does not exist yet and you are writing it, or when you are revising your own draft before others check it. The checks in the main file still apply. What changes is that you are now the author as well as the reviewer, and authors are the worst readers of their own drafts: they read what they meant, not what they wrote.

Contents: 1 The order. 2 What to watch. 3 Traps.

## 1. The order

**Example.** Asked to write a short guide, one writer starts with the section they find most interesting and works outwards. Another starts by writing down who the reader is and what the reader must be able to do after reading. The second guide is shorter, and its reader finishes it knowing what to do.

**Point.** Purpose, then reader, then outline, then draft, then review.

- **Write the purpose in one sentence** before anything else: what should a reader know or be able to do after reading?
- **Name the reader** and what they already know. Decide which terms need defining.
- **Outline before drafting.** List the sections in order and say in a line what each is for. Check the order lets each idea arrive before it is needed.
- **Define terms and notation once, early,** and keep a list as you go. The list becomes your inventory at review time.
- **Draft fast, then check slowly.** Do not polish sentences while the structure is still moving.
- **Leave time between drafting and reviewing.** A day is ideal; an hour helps. The gap lets you read what is on the page.
- **Review with the procedure in the main file,** as if the draft were someone else's. Redo every sum. Follow every cross-reference. Compare every quotation.

## 2. What to watch

- **Numbers that were copied.** Every figure pasted from a spreadsheet, an earlier draft or another report is a figure that may have changed at its source. Note where each came from, and check again before sending.
- **Cross-references after edits.** Every time you add, remove or reorder a section, figure or equation, recheck the references that point past it. Use automatic numbering if your tools allow it.
- **The summary, written last.** Write the summary after the body is final, and check each of its figures and claims against the body.
- **Terms that drift.** Search the draft for each defined term and read every use.
- **One version.** If the draft has lived in several files, merge them before review, so that the version checked is the version sent.
- **Ask someone else.** Even a quick read by another person catches things you cannot see. Tell them what kind of review you want.

## 3. Traps

- Reviewing your own draft straight after writing it.
- Writing the summary first and not revisiting it.
- Reordering sections without rechecking cross-references.
- Treating your own figures as checked because you calculated them.
- Sending a draft whose sources you have not reopened since you first read them.
