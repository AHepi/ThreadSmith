## 2. One example: the myth about winter

The ancient Greeks explained winter with a myth: Persephone spends part of each year in the underworld, and her mother's grief brings the cold. The rival explanation is the tilt of the Earth.

Your old question (files 93 and 96): if someone finds, just by reading the myth closely, that it assumes its own answer, does that alone rule it out? The copy you had then said yes, but that sentence went with the written-in test. The readers' jobs had already gone, so before any reply was opened, a short addition to the reading rule put the myth into the round.

The program worked it through:
- **On the Greeks' own question** (their half of the world, June against December), the tilt and the myth both count as explanations; the myth is a bad one.
- **The two disagree only in the southern half**, which that question does not ask about.
- **On a wider question that includes the south**, the myth fails: it says June is warm everywhere, but in Australia June is cold. The tilt does not.

Your words settle it: an explanation with a written-in answer is "an explanation. Just not a good one", and "That "ruling out" is a choice that was made." So finding that the myth assumes its answer rules nothing out by itself. A person may still rule it out on that ground, by their own choice. No question for you, and no change to the words.

## 3. A second example: two changes that were not needed

One sentence says that working backwards from a result does not explain how it is produced. Its example is a flagpole: you can work out its height from its shadow, but make the pole taller and the real shadow grows while the backwards sum does not.

Two readers said the sentence goes too far: carry the change over in a way that also sets the shadow inside the sum, and the sum passes. A checker agreed, and two small changes tied the sentence to one example.

The critical review pointed out that the sentence, after its colon, already says how: a change to the pole is carried over as that same change. Under that, the sum failed every time it was tried; the other way puts the answer into the sum, so it is a different candidate. Both changes were withdrawn.
