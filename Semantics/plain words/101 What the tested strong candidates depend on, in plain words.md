## 1. What was found, and what it might mean

The seven strong candidates that were challenged and kept are steady in their own words. Most of what they depend on is not.

- **Each definition was followed to the ideas it uses, and those to theirs,** down to the theory's starting points: the organization (how the theory describes a thing's parts and the changes it admits), a question and its answers, the range of changes a question covers, the level of detail, the inputs (what the theory takes as stated and does not define), and what the theory takes from physics.
- **The chains meet.** Eleven ideas lie under at least four of the seven.
- **The starting points held still or changed once; the definitions between them and the seven moved a lot,** above all the list of inputs, which grew at every change.
- **On values, the text's own definitions are plain.** One of the seven depends on value, and it only points to where the theory takes value from outside. The definition of a repair, another of the seven, uses aims and not value. Nothing the seven share depends on value.

What it might mean for your idea: if you isolate the strong candidates, the places where they are least isolated are the definitions under them that moved. And on values: in the text as it stands, value sits beside the rest, reached by one pointer and one paragraph. Whether it should stay there is yours to say.

## 2. One sentence traced, step by step

The sentence is the first line of the rule that an explanation must not assume its own answer:

> **Non-circular dependence.** The answer follows by evaluating \(E\) under its independent boundary conditions.

It has read so in every version.

1. **The lines after it moved.** They say what "independent" means, and were added or rewritten after the project's own review in September found, in its words, that "The set \(\Gamma\) of active commitments in non-circular dependence is never typed": the text had never said what the parts "doing the work" of an explanation were.
2. **It uses a proposed explanation**: an organization, a mapping from the thing explained to it, and the parts it offers as doing the work. That definition moved a lot: many proposals were written against its first sentence, and a second sentence was added.
3. **It uses the organization**, which held still (all thirteen of its sentences untouched), **the mapping**, which held still, and **the question's answers, the range of changes and the level of detail**, each changed once.
4. **The mapping is defined "on the stated scope"**, and the scope of a range of changes is one of the inputs. Its sentences moved, and the list that names it as an input is the most altered sentence on any of the seven chains: five wordings, from 57 words to 111.
5. **The other way round, 42 ideas depend on this rule,** because what counts as an explanation includes it. Ten sentences use it by name.

So: a sentence that never changed, heading a rule whose working lines were rewritten, over starting points that held still.

## 3. What most of the seven depend on

Eleven ideas lie under at least four of the seven (two of them are themselves among the seven: the rule against assuming its own answer, and what the theory takes from physics):

## 4. Which starting points moved a lot, and which held still

- **Held still:** the organization, the mapping, and what the theory takes from physics, which is itself one of the seven: a starting point with nothing under it, on which 35 ideas depend.
- **Moved a lot:** the inputs. The list of them grew at every change, and three of the inputs moved in their own sentences: the aims of a repair, a system's boundary and continuity, and the scope of a range of changes.
- **The sharpest case is the rule for when someone can use an argument:**

  > \(\operatorname{Usable}_j(u)\iff\operatorname{Form}_j(u)\land\operatorname{Scope}_j(u)\land\forall d\in\operatorname{Prem}(u),\operatorname{Live}_j(d;u).\)

  The formula has stood since the first version. Its three parts were given meanings only in the last three versions, each marked as Claude's reading, and the person's inputs it depends on joined the list in the repairs of 26 September. Its starting points are the newest part of it. Fourteen ideas depend on it, among them four items of the theory's list of what would rule it out.
- **"A mathematical error" names eight results.** The project's own review in September found three of them at fault, and for one recorded that the sentence's own condition had been met. The results were rewritten; the sentence stayed as it was.
- **The definition of a repair** stood still while the sentences saying what its letters mean moved. The theory's list of what depends on what places it after the definition of an explanation, but its words never use one; the text says: "An account that produced nothing, an act that repaired without an account, and a repair produced through use of an account are three different attributions."

## 5. Values: what the text says

- **The theory takes value from outside.** Of the "appraisal relation" it says: "It is taken as an input and the semantics does not define it".
- **In the whole map, four things use value:** the paragraph on value itself, the answer to "Where is aesthetics?", a rule on cases with a missing input, and the argument that the theory takes exactly two things from outside. No other definition uses it.
- **One of the seven depends on value:** "**8. "Where is aesthetics?"** In Part XI, as a declared appraisal relation." It is a pointer. It calls value "declared", where the list of what the theory takes from outside calls it an input the theory does not define; a proposal to change the word was never carried out.
- **Two of the seven are the repair's own sentences.** They use aims, not value. The text says of the aims: "Their declaration makes no appraisal of the aims, and (P) puts no order on alternatives." And of repair: "Repairing an aim repairs it and says nothing about how anyone appraises the aim, or the question that led to it."
- **The other four, and all eleven ideas the seven share,** depend on neither value nor aims.
- **One loose thread:** every question carries a set of aims of its own, "the set of aims being addressed or protected", and no other sentence of the theory uses them.
- **The text on the two things it takes from outside:** "The two imports are a theory of matter and, when a question requires it, a theory of appraisal."

This bears on your question whether value belongs inside the theory or beside it. It does not answer it.

## 6. What is unsure

- **The links were proposed by a program and kept or dropped by reading;** another reader could draw some differently. One choice matters most: the map treats what counts as an explanation as using only a question's target, range of changes and answers, not the aims and the record of how it was found that a question also carries. Without that choice, nearly everything would depend on nearly everything.
- **The theory's own list of what depends on what, and the words of its definitions, disagree in places.** The map keeps both and says which is which; the repair above is one case.
- **"Moved a lot" counts proposals written against a sentence too,** so a sentence can count as moved while its words never changed.
- **Seven is a small number,** and "at least four of the seven" is a line drawn, not found.
- **Untouched may mean unexamined.** The parts that held still may never have been read closely, and the 29 strong candidates never challenged are not traced here.
