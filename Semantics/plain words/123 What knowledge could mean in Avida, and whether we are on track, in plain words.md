# What knowledge could mean in Avida, and whether we are on track. In plain words

*Log S123, 2 October 2026. Your words: "build a hypothesis of what knowledge could mean in the context of Avida", and "We also need to tell whether Avida is the right environment or not. Because a learning machine also needs constant external input."*

## The main point

We are not yet on track to say what knowledge means in Avida. We have measured one meaning well, your three properties, in Avida's programs. But nothing so far has tested whether that knowledge changes Avida itself, whether anything in Avida is more than adaptation, or whether the execution environment holds knowledge. And as it stands Avida cannot learn from outside data at all: every number its programs read is made by Avida's own code.

## One example, step by step

A word first. A **link**, in the theory's sense, carries the shape of one thing onto another faithfully, so that when the first changes, the second changes in step. (The theory calls it a transport.)

1. An Avida program reads three numbers and hands back a fourth. In one run, most programs came to hand back "NOT" of a number they read (every 0 turned to 1, every 1 to 0).
2. Where do the three numbers come from? From a random-number maker inside Avida's own code, always handed out in the same order.
3. So the link from "the world" to the program starts inside Avida. Whatever a program comes to track, it is tracking Avida's own code.
4. Hand the same programs the same numbers in another order: in one run, 2,370 programs did NOT in the usual order, and only 34 still did with two numbers swapped. Their skill reaches exactly as far as what they met.
5. Now picture Avida fed a recorded signal from outside, say a year of weather readings, a number at a time. A program that came to forecast the next reading on stretches it was never paid for would be tracking something outside Avida. That has never been tried. It needs only a small change to Avida's code, like the one Astra already wrote for its "guess the next number" streams.

## The rival answers, one line each

1. The knowledge is the programs' small circuits, and it is knowledge of Avida's own rules, nothing else.
2. The knowledge sits in the execution environment and its programs together, not in the programs alone.
3. It is not yet knowledge until it changes the execution environment itself, by a route nobody wrote for that purpose.
4. Everything in Avida is adaptation: useful, but unable to reach past what it met, because nothing in Avida builds anything with a goal in view.
5. Learning about anything starts only when data come in from outside the execution environment.
6. Knowledge of the creative kind starts when a single program builds something during its own life, on top of what evolution gave it.

Each comes with the observation that would count against it.
