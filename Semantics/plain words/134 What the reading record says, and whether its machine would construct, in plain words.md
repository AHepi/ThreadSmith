## The main point

The record is the homework your outside coding agent did before building the four-stage experiment: an outside model read Pinker's two books, ten of its claims were checked against the pages, and the agent wrote its own plan for a thinking machine, with a sketch of a machine that builds small formulas and repairs them when they fail. By your own word, that machine would **find** its first rules, as your experiment did; when one fails and it repairs it against everything it has itself seen, the repaired rule could count as **worked out**, but only inside the family of formulas its builders wrote.

## One example: the machine meets an ellipse

The plan's own example: the machine's circle rule really says "any smooth closed outline is a circle", and then it meets an ellipse. The plan says the machine must build a further rule or say "unresolved", and a fix handed over by a person is the designer's repair.

Before calling the machine's repair worked out, the theory would ask to see:

1. **It noticed by itself**: its own record shows the circle rule predicted one reading and the sensor gave another.
2. **The old rule was held when it failed**, with the readings that broke it, before the repair began.
3. **The repair came from the failure.** Shuffle the order in which it tries new formulas: if the repair changes with the order, the failure only filtered a list. Restart from the same record with the failed rule taken out: if the same formula comes out at the same cost, the failure did no work.
4. **It reaches new cases**: ellipses it never saw come out right, piece by piece, and circles and squares still do.
5. **It does the work**: take the new piece out and ellipses go wrong; put it back and they come right; a pretend removal changes nothing.

One limit stays: the machine worked out *which* formula, inside a family its builders chose. It did not work out the family.

## The plan against our theory

- **It has what we lack:** a budget for the search and an honest "unresolved" when it runs out; an actual way of making and repairing formulas; a rule for where to look next (where two surviving guesses disagree); a design for memory; room for noisy readings.
- **We have what it lacks:** three origins for what a machine holds (found by trial, worked out, handed over) where it has two, and the rule that which one depends on where the machine's edge is drawn; who set the question; a test that checks an account piece by piece, not only its answers; and the difference between re-tuning a number and building something new.
- Of its 26 commitments, 9 say the same as ours, 14 overlap with a difference, 3 we have no word for.

## What should go into the constructor experiment

- A check that simply counting actions cannot pass for memory (an earlier Avida run was fooled exactly so).
- An "I can't tell" answer, allowed and credited where nothing the subject could do would decide it; every answer marked "my account supports this" or "a guess".
- A planned moment where two simple accounts fit what was seen and differ on one available action.
- **Build the agent's machine without a language model** in the same box world, with its repair step and without it. It has no training to recall answers from and every step can be read, so it is where our test of "worked out" can be checked before spending about 9,000 calls on the language model. About 1,500 to 2,500 lines of code and a few hours of computer time. Its limit: at most "worked out inside a family we wrote".
- **Keep the sealed box as the world.** Circles, squares, straight-line motion and their usual failures are in every textbook; the box's randomly wired parts are in none.
