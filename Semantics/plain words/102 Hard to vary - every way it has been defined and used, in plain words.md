# Hard to vary: every way it has been defined and used, in plain words

*For the owner, 27 September 2026, answering "can you return a summary of all the different ways it has been defined and used historically, including now." Nothing in the theory was changed, and no change is proposed. The full table is `results/S102 Hard to vary - every definition and use, from the start to now.md`.*

## The answer

Hard to vary and its partner words have been defined or used in ten ways. In the order each first appears:

1. A count that put explanations in order of preference. It was given up before the first version the project holds.
2. A counting rule: the more things an explanation must account for, the fewer changed versions of it still work.
3. Blind selection: versions made and kept by a process that nothing and no one aims. It is never called hard to vary.
4. The skill: a method for criticising an explanation.
5. "How hard an account is to vary": a property of one explanation, set apart from everything else, that puts nothing in order.
6. The source's sense, in Deutsch and Marletto.
7. Three proposals of 24 September built on the counting rule. None was taken.
8. Your sense, of 24 September.
9. "Easy to vary", said through rivals and problems.
10. "Hard to vary" left out of the theory, undefined.

## One example first

The counting rule stood in files 10, 11 and 12 and in the first three drafts of the second revision, as a paragraph headed "Hard-to-vary". It ended: "More reach constrains variation; the containment need not be strict; counting jobs is not a warrant." It counted versions from a set listed in advance, and nothing else in the theory used it. On 24 September you said no one can list all the versions, even in principle. The next day draft 4 took the rule out, and the heading became "Commitments that do no work."

## Each way, in short

**1. The count for preference.** An older version, which the project does not hold, let a count order explanations. The version of 8 September, file 00, withdrew it: "FW5 also withdraws the special permission for a count of conjecturally independent features to order explanations for preference."

**2. The counting rule.** File 00 showed that, in a fixed set of versions, adding things to account for can only shrink the versions that still work. It gave hard to vary its only definition in words in any version of the theory: "“Hard to vary” is therefore an articulated pattern of constrained changes, relative to an explanatory job."

**3. Blind selection.** From file 10 on, one way a link to the world can come about is "produced by blind variation and survival on a history of predictions". The surroundings decide what survives: "a survival condition enacted by the environment." Today's theory keeps rivals apart from it: "Nor are rivals a selection population".

**4. The skill.** It says: "A good explanation is hard to vary: you cannot change its parts and still have it explain what it is meant to explain." It calls itself "a way of criticising, not a truth-meter." This project used it to audit the theory until your verdict of 23 September: "The skill isn't worth it."

**5. A property set apart.** Drafts 1 to 3 said that "How hard an account is to vary is a separate matter", shown by the counting rule, "and it grades nothing." Here hard to vary is something one explanation has, kept away from what an explanation is.

**6. The source.** Deutsch defines it by change alone: "An explanation that is hard/easy to vary while still accounting for what it purports to account for." He argues that it matters because it makes correction work. Marletto has correction produce it.

**7. The proposals of 24 September.** One analysis offered an order of explanations: "smaller means harder to vary". Others offered a record of rescues and a new counting rule for corrections. Your words that day set all three aside.

**8. Your sense.** "A variation is a competitor. Whether anyone can list all variations that still fit is beside the point. If two discovered variations fit, that constitutes a problem." And: "A record is redundant. Once the explanation is rescued, the mistake shouldn't be able to creep back in. Good explanations make bad ones harder to fit by definition."

**9. Easy to vary.** Draft 4 wrote your sense in. A rival is an explanation someone has put forward in place of another, which disagrees with it somewhere. Two rivals that nothing yet rules out make a problem. An explanation is easy to vary when its rival disagrees with it only outside the question asked, so nothing the question covers can tell them apart; the rival is then easy to vary too.

**10. Hard to vary left out.** From draft 4 on, the words "hard to vary" appear in no version of the theory. The plain version of draft 4, file 92, says so: "It does not say why it leaves "hard to vary" undefined."

## Where it sits now

Today's theory defines only easy to vary: "A candidate is **easy to vary**, in the sense used here, when it and a rival pose a problem of the second kind; the rival is then easy to vary too, and the term says nothing about which of them, and not the other, is an account on a finer contract." Its one use is as a criticism: "A criticism that a candidate is easy to vary must supply such a rival". The theory's list of commitments sets it apart: "whether an account is easy to vary is a separate matter". Nothing else in the theory depends on it; it depends on what a judge can use. Your words "the mistake shouldn't be able to creep back in" are in the theory too, as the paragraph headed "A failed answer stays failed". The words "hard to vary" are not in it at all.

## Preference or use: a reading

*This section is Claude's reading of the texts, not a finding of any test.*

From the first version on, the texts kept hard to vary away from preference. File 00 withdrew the count that ordered explanations "for preference"; files 10 to 12 said "counting jobs is not a warrant"; drafts 1 to 3 said it "grades nothing"; and the note on the sources, which no version of the theory carries, said that easy to vary "prefers neither".

But no version gave hard to vary a place in the process instead. In every version it is either something one can point out about explanations (the counting rule, the property, easy to vary) or a method someone applies (the skill). Neither kind drives which versions come about or which survive. The one change that runs with no one aware is blind selection, which is never called hard to vary. The note on the sources records this split as a departure from Deutsch, who calls criticism and experiment a selection.

Your words of 24 September are the one place where hard to vary works by itself: a good explanation, by its own content, makes bad ones "harder to fit", with no list, no record and no one choosing. In the theory they became easy to vary, which works only when someone puts a rival forward, and a failed answer staying failed, which is stated for a judge. After a problem, today's theory says what happens next "is the person's choice".

The source holds both sides. Deutsch uses hard to vary to choose: "we should choose between them not on the basis of their origin, but according to how good they are as explanations: how hard to vary." (page 209). He also finds it where no one chooses at all: "good adaptations, like good explanations, are distinguished by being hard to vary while still fulfilling their functions." (page 78).

## What is unsure

- The older count is known only from file 00's report of it.
- Several ways are neither a preference nor a driver. That third answer is Claude's reading, and another reader could place the counting rule on the side of use, since it holds whether anyone looks or not.
- Blind selection is in the list because you named variation among the words to follow; the texts never call it hard to vary.
- No outside reader has seen this summary.
