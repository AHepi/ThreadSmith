## The main point

On Pinker's account, nothing learns from data alone: a learner needs data from outside that carries a pattern it does not already have, a second view of what the data are about, and built-in limits on what it will guess. Avida gives its programs none of these: what they are checked against is written in Avida's own code, they get nothing but the numbers, and almost any instruction sequence is a possible guess.

## One example, step by step

1. A child hears *give the museum the money* and *give the museum a painting*, and many verbs used the same way.
2. So the child guesses a rule: a verb of giving can take that form. Children really do overreach like this; they say things like *Don't giggle me*.
3. But adults never say *donate the museum the money*. Nobody tells the child that it is wrong: parents correct what is untrue, not what is ungrammatical.
4. Here is the trap. If you only ever hear correct sentences, then a rule that is too broad is never contradicted: everything you hear still fits it. Pinker calls this Baker's paradox.
5. Yet children do stop. Pinker's answer is that they attend to meaning. Which verbs can take the form depends on fine differences in what the verbs mean (and partly on how they sound: long Latin words like *donate* resist it). As a child learns what each verb means, the wrong uses drop away.
6. A test of it: children were taught a made-up verb for a new action. When the action's point was a zigzag movement, they used it one way; when its point was a change of colour, they used it the other way. They had never heard the verb in a sentence.

What this says about learning from outside data: the data alone could never have told the child where to stop. What did it was the data, plus knowing what the words were about, plus built-in links between meaning and grammar.

## What it means for your questions

- **What transports learning needs to begin (S78).** Five, in the semantics' sense: a channel bringing in data from something outside the learner; a second channel to the same events, giving their meaning; a fixed, built-in link from the learner's categories to the data; the learned mapping itself; and a limit on the guesses. Avida has none of the first three in this sense.
- **Is Avida the right environment?** As it stands, for selection yes, for learning from outside data no. Four options are set out in the results file, not chosen: Avida as it is; Avida fed by an outside source; Avida organisms that learn within their lifetime (a published modified Avida did evolve this, when the world changed between generations but stayed the same within a lifetime); or another environment.
- **Knowledge.** Pinker agrees with Deutsch that data alone do not make knowledge, and they agreed in 2023 that children are "forming explanations". He disagrees on universality (he thinks intelligence is specialised, Deutsch that people are universal explainers) and on where the needed limits come from (Pinker: evolution; Deutsch: creative guessing). The S123 tasks should test three meanings of knowledge against each other: yours (copied, resisting change, remaining), Deutsch's, and Pinker's.
