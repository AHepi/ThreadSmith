**Four words first.** A match is *selected* when copying and survival shaped it and nothing earlier in its history already aimed at what it matches. It is *built* when it was worked out with a target in view. It is *declared* when someone simply wrote it in. The *edge* is the line you draw around a system: what counts as inside it.

## What changes

Where a match came from is now judged inside the edge you draw. A task checker that people wrote outside the edge counts, inside it, as part of the world, not as something already aiming at the task. And being copied more often counts as surviving.

## What else this commits the theory to

1. Every claim about where a match came from must name its edge, fixed before the verdict. The same program is selected inside one edge and declared inside a wider one.
2. "Explanation" must be judged edge by edge too. Otherwise your rule that a declared match explains nothing contradicts the theory's central claim on that one program.
3. Bred dogs and crops are selected if the edge is the line of animals or plants, declared if it takes in the breeder; a search scored by code people wrote, likewise, inside the run or with the people.
4. In living things with no chooser, a trait is selected where it raised copying. A trait shaped by mates choosing, like a peacock's tail, stays declared at any edge that holds the choosers.
5. Learning from a teacher within a life still counts as built.
6. New: something learned only by rote stands for nothing inside the learner's edge, so working it out later counts as new to the learner.
7. Two rules need new wording: "exactly one of three histories" now holds edge by edge, and the rule against borrowed help, used when judging what a system can do, becomes empty.
