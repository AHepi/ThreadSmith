# What V5 is missing, in plain words

*Log S136, 7 October 2026. Your words: "No. Get 2 Opus 5.5 agents to read through this and tell me what's missing." Two readers worked separately: one against your aims, one against the theory and the report's own evidence. Nothing was run, because V5's code is on your computer, not here. Everything about V5 below is what its read-me and report say.*

## The main point

Both readers agree. V5 is careful about keeping track: it carries every possibility forward, keeps them apart, and refuses to answer when it cannot be sure. But it works nothing out. When its laws stop fitting the world it notices, then stops for good. The step that would turn that failure into a repair is missing. And its memory design throws away the very observations a repair would have to be checked against.

## One example: the hidden stop

V5 watches one object pushed along. It was handed a list of 648 possible force laws, and it keeps every law that still fits what it has seen.

1. After the 16th observation the object secretly stops.
2. V5's two preferred laws keep predicting motion. By the 24th observation neither fits any more. V5 says, in effect, "my laws have failed", and from then on it answers "unknown".
3. Three other laws from the list of 648 still fit at the end, because the camera only sees a box around the object, not its exact place. The report counts this as a failed test.
4. What a machine that constructs would do next: keep the failed law and the observations that broke it; ask which part of the law was wrong (the speed changed at some moment); build a new law ("moved until about the 17th observation, then stopped"); check it against everything it saw; use it.
5. V5 cannot do this. It has thrown away the observations, and it can only choose among the 648 laws its builders wrote. It never makes a new one.

## Your ladder, rung by rung

- **Recognise objects**: missing. There are always exactly two things, given to it by name.
- **Displacement**: present, and its strongest part. The law family is handed in; which laws survive is found.
- **Object permanence**: only assumed. Nothing ever goes out of sight. There is no screen.
- **Amounts**: missing.
- **Addition**: missing.

## What is missing

**Blocking for your aims:**

- **A rule formed from its own failures.** It latches and stops. The report admits this.
- **A record of what broke a law.** By design it keeps no past observations, so no new law could be checked against what it saw. The report admits only that this costs an audit trail.
- **For the ladder:** recognising objects, keeping things in mind out of sight, counting amounts, and addition.

**Important:**

- It never says which part of a failed law is wrong.
- It never chooses its own test. The next step the report recommends, a push designed to tell the surviving laws apart, would be chosen by its builders. It would only narrow the handed-in list, not work anything out. It is still worth running, because it is how V5 would notice trouble sooner.
- It does not recover after a failure.
- It gave a certified "yes" at the 18th observation, after the world had already left what it was tracking at the 17th. That plan was never carried out, so no harm showed. For the demonstration, the report does not say whether the goal was actually reached.
- The list of 648 laws is never defined in what we have, and the "independent" reviewers are never named.
- The read-me sounds surer than the report. The first functional check failed, and earlier test runs were reused rather than run again.
- Its "memory" is 66 to 180 times larger than the observations it replaces. It runs 42 to 46 times slower than the motion it tracks.

**Where the readers differed.** One said V5 holds its failed law before the world refutes it; the other said nothing in V5 holds that law as something to repair. Both are right. The law is still there, but nothing aims at it.

## What V5 has that your machine lacks

Your machine built addition from its own failures; V5 cannot. But V5 has five things worth carrying into your machine:

- keeping every possibility side by side, for hidden objects;
- telling apart "my rule failed" from "everything I have failed";
- acting only when an answer is certified;
- a second, independent scorer;
- choosing an action that splits rival rules.

Not worth carrying: the memory that discards the past, the list of 648 laws, the grammar, and the save-and-restore machinery.

## Tested, not tested, unsure

- **Tested:** nothing. Two careful readings, and the size and speed figures recomputed from the report's own numbers.
- **Not tested:** any of V5's claims. Everything rests on files we do not have.
- **Unsure:** whether its packets hold raw pictures or only boxes; how its two preferred laws were learned; what a repair step would cost on V5's heavy machinery.

## One next step

I write a brief your outside agent can follow to add the missing step to V5. When its laws fail, it would keep the observations, hold the failed law, work out which part is wrong, build a new law including a moment of change, check it against everything seen, and use it. The tests would be written first, as we did for your machine.
