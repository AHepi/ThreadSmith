# The refined hypothesis of what knowledge could mean in Avida, in plain words

*Log S125, 2 October 2026. Astra's four replies came with no words from you. One agent checked them and merged them into one answer.*

## The refined hypothesis

In an Avida run, knowledge is an arrangement of instructions, spread across the programs, that a task depends on, that the run keeps copying, that matches whatever was actually paid for, and that changes the execution environment through that task. All of it so far is what evolution makes; nothing in Avida has yet been seen building something new with a goal in view.

## One example, step by step

A word first. A **store** is a stock of something in the execution environment that refills slowly and that programs use up when they do a paid task.

1. In one earlier run, the execution environment kept a store for each task. Doing NOT drew on the NOT store, so the more programs did NOT, the less each was paid.
2. After 50,000 updates, about 3,200 programs did NOT, and the NOT store stood near 270, far below the 10,000 it would refill to if nobody used it.
3. The hypothesis says: remove the instructions that do NOT from every program, leaving their copying intact, and the NOT store climbs within a few hundred updates.
4. Remove the same number of other, harmless instructions instead, and the store stays where it was.
5. Put the NOT instructions back, and the store falls again.
6. If all three happen, the programs' knowledge has changed the execution environment itself (its current state). If the store does not move, the hypothesis is wrong here.

## Where the replies corrected us

None of the replies ran Avida, but they caught real mistakes:

- **We said Avida cannot take in outside data at all.** Wrong. A built-in command in a run's script puts any numbers into every program's inputs, so real measurements can be fed in without changing Avida's code. We tried it: the numbers reach the programs. What is missing is a fair way to pay a program for guessing the next measurement before it sees it.
- **We said a skill reaches exactly as far as what the programs met.** Too strong: what they met leaves other cases open, not wrong. With two inputs swapped, 34 of 2,370 programs still did NOT.
- **We said nothing in Avida builds anything, so nothing in it explains.** The theory does not say that. It lets a program shaped by selection count as a narrow explanation of its task, under one answer to your open question about the checking code.
- **We said only the instruction sequence passes on.** A setting lets programs pass their working memory to their copies, and the pay rate passes on by default.
- **We said effects through routes Avida's designers wrote do not count as effects on Avida itself.** That was a reading, not a fact. If "Avida itself" means its current state, they count.
- **Two of our six rival answers were badly built.** "Not knowledge until it acts on Avida by a route nobody wrote" predicts nothing of its own. "Learning starts only with outside data" is already contradicted: programs learned to guess the next number of a stream we made.

## What Avida can and cannot be made to do for learning from outside data

**Can, with nothing new:** take in recorded measurements, three numbers at a time; pass working memory on; let programs sense stores and get feedback (with a wider instruction set).

**Can, with a small addition:** pay a program for a guess it commits to, then show it the answer; give each new program its own cue to learn during its life.

**Needs more work:** moving objects that can be hidden from view; a record fine enough to tell building something new from copying it.

**Cannot, as built:** let a program rewrite Avida's own code; take a live feed without a script in between.

Nothing found so far requires another platform. On "a learning machine needs constant input": the replies argue a recording is enough for a fixed problem, and ongoing input matters for keeping up with change.

## Tested, not tested, unsure

**Tested:** every claim the replies made about Avida's code, and their readings of the theory (all held); eight of their outside sources (all held where they could be opened); seven short runs, under two minutes of computer time, showing that numbers fed in by script reach the programs, that the same numbers give exactly the same run, and that the memory-passing setting works.

**Not tested:** the hypothesis itself; any learning from real outside data; anything about building something new.

**Unsure:** whether the stores move as predicted; the details of one paper (learning evolved in a modified Avida) whose full text could not be opened.
