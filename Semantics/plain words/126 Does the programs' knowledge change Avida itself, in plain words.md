# Does the programs' knowledge change Avida itself? In plain words

*For the owner, after log S126, 2 October 2026. Your question (S76): "The definition of knowledge says it must has a causal affect on its surroundings. Not just other little programs, but Avida itself. Has that been demonstrated yet?"*

## The answer

**Partly, for a short while, and only if "Avida itself" means the state Avida keeps.** When we took one learned skill out of the programs, the execution environment's supply for that skill filled back up; when we put the skill back, the supply was used up again. But the removal was never complete, the programs won the skill back by themselves within a few hundred updates, and the route by which a skill uses up its supply is one Avida's makers wrote.

## One example: the store for NOT

In the "common tasks pay less" execution environment, each logic task pays out of its own store, which refills to 10,000 when unused; the programs had worn the NOT store down to about 290. From one saved population we made four copies:

| What we did to the programs | The NOT store after 100 updates | After 1,000 updates |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing | 280 | 290 |
| A harmless change of the same size | 290 | 250 |
| Took out the instructions that do NOT (copying kept) | **4,300** (it kept rising to 4,500) | 480 |
| Took them out, then, at update 100, put them back | about 4,200 when put back; **275** a hundred updates later | 295 |

So the store follows the skill: out, the store fills; back, it is used up again. Left alone, the store also fell again, more slowly: programs that could still do NOT, or regained it by copying errors, were paid more and spread.

In the second population (the skill OR-NOT), the store rose fourfold for only eight updates. About a quarter of the programs had not been reached by the removal; they took over and wore the store down again.

## What it means for your question

If "Avida itself" means the state Avida keeps, here its stores, then yes: a learned skill has now been seen to change Avida's state, in a way that goes when the skill goes and comes back when it comes back. If "Avida itself" means Avida's rules, then no: nothing a program did changed a rule, and the change ran through a channel Avida's makers built for it. Which reading your definition needs is yours to decide.

One thing we did not expect: the run defends the skill. Once the skill is gone and its store fills up, doing the task pays more, so the skill comes back.

## Tested, not tested, unsure

- **Tested:** two saved populations, one skill each; removal, a harmless matched change, and putting back; every store recorded at every update; about 1.8 hours of computer time.
- **Not met:** the strict tests written before running (a complete removal, a store staying above half full) were not met in either population.
- **Not tested:** whether a program can change Avida's rules; outside data; where the knowledge sits.
- **Unsure:** why the unreached programs in the second population took over so fast (most likely: the removal also cost the reached programs other skills, so they lost running time to the unreached ones).
