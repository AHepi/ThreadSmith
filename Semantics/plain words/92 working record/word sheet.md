# Word sheet for the plain-words version of draft 4

*Binding on every writer. Written 25 September 2026 from the theory text of draft 4 (`Semantics/tests/Revision 2 - file 13 draft 4, theory text.md`), with the plan in `plan.md` beside this file.*

## How to use this sheet

- Each entry gives **the plain word** to use everywhere, the theory's own term in brackets, and one plain sentence saying what it means.
- Use exactly the plain word. Never a synonym, never the theory's term, never a symbol. If you need a thing that is not here, do not invent a word: write it out in ordinary words and flag it to the orchestrator.
- The first time a plain word appears in a section, give its meaning in one plain sentence (the sheet's sentence, or a shorter one), unless the same section has just used an example that shows it.
- The theory's terms, Part numbers, line numbers, Derivation numbers and case ids go only in the small "Where to find it" line that ends each section. The body text names cases by their titles ("the fever and the thermometer").
- Where the theory uses one of its own words for two different things, this sheet gives two plain words and says so (marked **two things**). Where two theory terms would have met in one plain word, one of them was given a different word; the "reserved words" list at the end shows which.

This sheet departs on purpose from the word list of the objections note of 25 September: the four conditions of an account are **requirements** there called "tests"; here **test** has the theory's new meaning (Part VI). Components are **pieces**, because "Part" names the theory's own divisions. "Version" is not used at all: the counting of versions is gone from draft 4.

## 1. The theory and its documents

- **the theory** (the semantics; draft 4 of file 13) — the written claim about what makes something an explanation and what makes a thinker creative at explaining.
- **Part** with a numeral, capital P (Part 0 to Part XVI) — one of the theory's own numbered divisions; the word "part" is used for nothing else.
- **section** — one of the divisions of this plain version.
- **Derivation 1** to **Derivation 10** (Derivations; Part XVI) — the names of the theory's ten numbered proofs; say once that each is a short argument that something follows from what the theory says.
- **proof** (proof; derivation) — an argument showing that something follows from something else, whether one of the Derivations or a proof in mathematics.
- **complaint** (grievance) — one of the eleven objections the theory expects and answers in Part 0.
- **main claim** (load-bearing claim; the five labelled A to E) — one of the five claims the theory names as the places to attack it, in the order of how much falls if each fails.
- **defeat** (refutation; "what defeats this class") — a finding that would show a main claim wrong.
- **central guess** (constitutive conjecture) — the theory's chief claim: that four things are enough to describe explanatory creativity.
- **guess** (conjecture) — an idea put forward without proof and open to criticism.
- **principle** (commitment, in Part I only) — one of the six starting positions the theory takes in Part I.
- **creativity** (explanatory creativity) — making explanations, or questions, that did not exist before.
- **explanation** (ordinary word only) — anything someone offers as the reason something is so; not a term of the theory (see **candidate** and **account**).
- **three older views** (teleosemantics, structural realism, functionalism) — name them once, each in one plain sentence: that what a sign stands for comes from a history of selection; that science captures structure; that a mind is defined by what it does, not what it is made of.

## 2. The world, its pieces and changes

- **set-up** (organization) — pieces that depend on one another, the slots they connect, the circumstances they sit in, and the changes allowed on them.
- **target** (target) — the set-up in the world that a question is about.
- **piece** (component) — one working element of a set-up that ties some of its slots together, such as the sunlight that fixes the shadow's length from the pole's height.
- **slot** (port) — a place in a set-up that holds a value, such as the pole's height or the shadow's length.
- **value** (value) — what a slot holds at a moment: a number, a position, a path, a proof or a history.
- **state** (valuation; state) — the values of all the slots of a set-up, or of a physical thing, at once.
- **behaviour** (component relation; relation) — which combinations of values a piece allows among its slots, under a given change and circumstances.
- **consistent states** (compatible valuations; solutions) — the states in which every piece's behaviour is respected at once.
- **circumstances** (boundary conditions) — the surroundings a set-up is held in while changes are made, such as the time of day or the rules of arithmetic.
- **change** (edit; intervention; organization edit) — anything done to a set-up: setting a slot, removing a piece, altering how a piece behaves, or dropping a starting assumption.
- **allowed change** (admitted edit; admitted change) — a change that the set-up, or the question, counts as one that can be made.
- **possible** (physically admitted; possible, in Part XII) — allowed by the laws of physics; the word is used for nothing else.
- **setting** (edit–boundary pair; "pair") — one change made in given circumstances, the unit at which the theory checks everything.
- **untouched setting** (baseline) — the setting with no change made, in the ordinary circumstances.
- **removed piece** (deleted component) — a piece taken out of a set-up, which then stops restricting its slots at all.
- **sets** (an edit that sets a port) — a change sets a slot when it fixes the slot's value directly, replacing the piece that used to fix it.
- **input** (input) — a slot that some allowed change sets directly.
- **output** (output) — a slot whose value a piece fixes from its other slots.
- **reading** (observation) — a slot that reports another slot and can be altered without altering what it reports.
- **direction** (direction) — which way dependence runs, read from which changes are allowed, not from how an equation happens to be written.
- **level of detail** (grain) — how finely a set-up is described; "coarse" means little detail and "fine" means much.
- **blurring** (coarsening) — describing something with less detail, so that things once told apart merge.
- **cluster** (subnetwork) — a connected bunch of pieces of a set-up, or of events in a course of events.
- **hidden slots** (hidden ports) — slots inside a cluster that the piece tied to it does not mention, set aside when the two are compared.
- **embody** (instantiate; realize; physical realization) — a physical thing embodies a set-up when, described at some level of detail, it has that set-up.

## 3. Questions

- **question** (question) — a target, a range, an untouched setting, what is asked, the demands and safeguards in play, and the history of the range.
- **what is asked** (query; the "respect" of a question) — the exact thing a question reads off the target at each setting.
- **answer** (answer; answer profile) — what the target gives for what is asked, at each setting of the range.
- **range** (contract) — the settings a question covers: a stated selection from the possible changes, with their circumstances.
- **limit** (scope; restriction) — a statement of which possible changes a range leaves out.
- **narrowing** (a restriction adopted after a failure) — a limit added after a candidate failed.
- **stated reason for the limit** (the ground of a restriction) — the reason a claim gives for its limit.
- **producing question** (production question) — a question about what produces an outcome, whose range holds changes to what comes before it.
- **pinning-down question** (identification question) — a question about what the readings settle about a feature, whose range holds changes to the readings.
- **blocking question** (obstruction question) — a question about whether one state can be reached from another at all.
- **status question** (rule-status question) — a question about what status something has under a status rule.
- **purpose question** (purpose-achievement question) — a question about whether a purpose is achieved.
- **pinned down** (identified) — a reading pins down a feature when every state giving that reading agrees on the feature.
- **look-alike states** (fibre) — the states that give one and the same reading.
- **calibration** (independent calibration) — a reading taken against something already known, which can add what repeating the same reading cannot.
- **unchanging quantity** (invariant) — a quantity that no allowed step alters.
- **reachable** (reachability) — a state is reachable from another when some chain of allowed steps leads to it.
- **faulty question** (a question that can be wrong) — a question that misses its target, assumes an untouched setting that cannot be, or asks for things that clash.
- **different question** (a new question; "a new claim at a new index") — a question with a different range or a different what-is-asked; say "different", never "new", because **new** is reserved.

## 4. Candidates, links and the four requirements

- **candidate** (explanatory candidate) — an explanation as offered for a question: its own set-up, its link from the target, and its commitments.
- **commitment** (active commitment; the commitments of a candidate) — a piece of the candidate's set-up that the candidate offers as doing the work, whether or not anyone has described its work.
- **background** (named background) — whatever the candidate holds fixed: its circumstances' values and its pieces that are not commitments.
- **link** (transport; correspondence) — the tie from the target to the candidate, saying which state goes with which, which change with which, which circumstances with which, and which cluster of the target each piece of the candidate is tied to.
- **anchor** (anchor) — the cluster of target pieces that one piece of the candidate is tied to and must behave like.
- **slot pairing** (port translation) — which slot of an anchor goes with which slot of the piece tied to it.
- **carry over** (translate) — what a link does to a setting of the target: it gives the setting of the candidate that goes with it.
- **matching** (fidelity; faithful) — a link matches on a range when, at every setting of the range, the two sides behave alike.
- **piece-by-piece matching** (component fidelity) — each commitment behaves like its anchor at every setting, once the anchor's hidden slots are set aside.
- **whole matching** (global fidelity) — the candidate's consistent states, as a whole, are the target's consistent states carried over, at every setting.
- **the four requirements** (the four conditions of Account) — matching; answering the question asked; no assumed answer; no dodging.
- **answering the question asked** (question fidelity) — the candidate gives the target's answer at every setting of the range, with what is asked held fixed.
- **no assumed answer** (non-circular dependence) — the answer is not written in as an unexplained input or piece, and removing some group of commitments loses a difference the answer shows between some setting and the untouched setting.
- **no dodging** (non-vacuity) — the untouched setting has a consistent state, and every possible change the range leaves out is left out by a stated limit.
- **account** (Account) — a candidate that meets all four requirements on its question.
- **group** (block) — some of a candidate's commitments taken together.
- **renaming change** (relabeling) — a change that only renames things and alters nothing else.
- **table of observed answers** (table of observed answers) — a list of answers seen, with no piece that responds when the world is changed.
- **full response table** (a table that encodes an organization's response to every admitted change) — a table that records how the set-up responds to every allowed change, which is not a table of observed answers in the theory's sense.
- **calculation run backwards** (reversed calculation) — working an earlier quantity out from a later one and offering that as what produces it.
- **explanation of absence** (eliminative explanation; explanation that removes structure) — an account of why something is not there.
- **bare denial** (bare denial) — saying something is not there, with no piece that responds to a change in what makes it seem to be there.

## 5. Kinds

- **response pattern** (signature; edit-signature) — how a piece behaves at each setting of a range, taken all together.
- **kind** (kind) — pieces are one kind on a range when their response patterns agree under some pairing of their slots; the word means nothing else.
- **cause** (causal assignment) — a family of response patterns: changes when its output is set directly or when it is replaced, and is unaffected when a reading is altered.
- **measurement** (measurement) — a family of response patterns: unaffected when the measured slot is set directly, and changed when the measuring is altered.
- **status rule** (rule application; constitutive rule) — a family of response patterns: unaffected by changes to the world, and changed when the rule itself is edited; never write bare "rule" for it.
- **anchoring requirement** (anchoring condition) — the extra requirement the theory rejects: that each piece be tied to one "of the same kind".

## 6. Matching over many steps, and rewriting

- **matching step by step** (functional transport) — if matching holds for each basic change, it holds for every chain of them.
- **two-way matching** (relational transport, forward and backward) — matching that runs in both directions, which is what treating two set-ups as equivalent needs.
- **one-way matching** (forward preservation only; abstraction) — matching in one direction only, which is enough for a simplified version.
- **error build-up** (approximate transport) — how small errors at each step add up over many steps.
- **distance** (metric) — a way of saying how far apart two states are.
- **step error** (one-step discrepancy) — how far the candidate's next state is from the target's next state, carried over, after one step.
- **stretch factor** (Lipschitz constant; modulus) — the most by which one step of the candidate can enlarge a difference between two states.
- **recoding** (declared, invertible recoding of a carrier) — writing a carrier's content in another code, with the key stated, so that it can be read back.
- **rewriting** (transport along a structure-preserving bijection) — redescribing everything (carriers, links, histories, ranges) by a one-to-one mapping that keeps every connection.

## 7. Which pieces do the work

- **cut-down candidate** (restriction; E restricted to W) — the candidate keeping only some of its commitments, with the background held fixed.
- **support** (support) — a group of commitments that, on its own with the background, makes an account.
- **load-bearing** (critical; critical block) — a group is load-bearing in a support when the support stops being one without it.
- **needed in every support** (globally indispensable) — belonging to every support there is.
- **smallest support** (minimal support) — a support from which nothing can be taken without losing it.
- **adding never breaks** (upward closure) — the assumption that adding a commitment to a support always leaves a support.
- **smallest-supports theorem** (finite monotone theorem) — the theorem saying which single commitments are load-bearing, and which needed in every support, when the smallest supports are known.
- **route** (route) — a support of the candidate: a way through it to the answer, whether or not anything ever runs it.
- **redundant routes** (redundant routes) — two or more routes, each enough alone, so that none is needed.
- **interference** (interference) — adding a commitment breaks a support that worked.
- **spread-out support** (infinitary support) — a support spread over endlessly many commitments, with no single one and no smallest group doing the work.
- **idle commitment** (commitment that does no work by itself) — a commitment of a candidate that has a support, such that adding it to or removing it from any support leaves a support.
- **tipping edge** (the set called Boundary in Part VI) — pairs of changed candidates, from a stated family of changes, such that one is an account and the other is not.

## 8. Rivals, problems and tests

- **offered** (offered) — put forward by someone as an answer to a question.
- **rival** (rival) — a candidate offered for a question in place of another, that conflicts with it at some allowed setting both links carry over.
- **conflict** (conflict) — two candidates conflict at a setting when their answers there differ, or when each could match there but nothing the physics allows lets both match.
- **judge** (assessor) — whoever is judging a candidate, for whom results are established or not.
- **assessment** (assessment) — one act of judging a candidate on a question, with the range held fixed.
- **result** (result; established result) — what a test shows about the target.
- **established** (established) — a result is established for a judge who holds a usable receipt for it.
- **fits** (fits what is established) — a candidate fits when no result established for the judge shows it failing a requirement.
- **problem** (problem for a question) — two rivals that both fit what a judge has established, so that what is established has not decided between them.
- **problem inside the range** (a problem of the first kind) — the rivals conflict at a setting the range covers, so a test can decide.
- **problem outside the range** (a problem of the second kind) — the rivals conflict only at settings the range does not cover, so no answer inside it can decide.
- **test** (test) — finding out, by looking or by experiment, what the target does at a setting; used for nothing else.
- **easy to vary** (easy to vary) — a candidate is easy to vary when it and a rival pose a problem outside the range; the rival is then easy to vary too.
- **finer range** (finer contract) — a range that holds more changes, which makes a different question.

## 9. Evidence and standing

- **receipt** (receipt) — a chain of reasoning over evidence items that someone can use for or against a claim.
- **evidence item** (evidence leaf) — a pointer to an event, together with what it is taken to show.
- **usable** (Usable; standing) — an argument is usable by someone when they are entitled to use it, it applies here, and every assumption it needs is still held.
- **assumption** (premise; essential premise) — something an argument needs to be true.
- **still held** (live premise) — an assumption someone has not withdrawn.
- **blame is shared** (what a test refutes) — a failed prediction counts against the claim tested, the test's other assumptions and its instruments together, and against nothing narrower.
- **record** (record) — any written trace of what happened or of what was claimed.

## 10. Frames, narrowing and failed answers

- **frame** (index; declared indices) — what a claim is relative to: its level of detail, its boundary, its sameness rule and its range; a frame is neither true nor false.
- **stated** (declared, when said of indices, inputs, boundaries and limits) — said openly in the claim; never use "declared" for this.
- **a claim keeps its frame** (historical index) — a claim tied to a range stays that claim when a later theory changes the range in use.
- **a failed answer stays failed** (a failed answer stays failed) — a candidate whose answer at a setting of the range is not the target's is not an account of that question, whoever offers it and however it was changed.
- **moving the goalposts** (goalpost-moving) — changing the frame of a claim without saying so.

## 11. Carriers, histories, pictures and surprise

- **carrier** (occurrence, when it bears content; carrier) — a physical thing or event, at a place and time, that bears content. **Two things:** see **event**.
- **event** (occurrence, as a step in a history; process occurrence) — something that actually happened, at a place and time.
- **content** (content) — a set-up together with what it claims on its range.
- **history** (provenance) — how a link, a range or a content came about: by selection, by construction or by declaration. **Two things:** see **trials** and **course of events**.
- **selection** (selected provenance; selection) — making a link by variation and survival, with nothing in the history picturing the target.
- **pool** (population) — the candidate links selection works on: those the physics and the stated design allow.
- **variation** (variation operator) — the process that makes fresh members of the pool.
- **trials** (the history H of a selected link; "history of encountered changes") — the settings a selected link actually met while it was being shaped.
- **survive** (survives on H) — to be in the pool and match on every trial.
- **untried setting** (unseen change; unseen pair) — a setting of the range that is not among the trials.
- **left open** (underdetermined) — the trials leave a value left open at an untried setting when another survivor in the pool behaves differently there.
- **stated design** (the stated construction, in Part XII's paragraph on selection) — how the members of a pool are built.
- **construction** (constructed provenance; Build) — making a link or content through an episode in which the target is pictured, with evidence of the joins made.
- **declared** (declared provenance; declaration) — written into a model by its author, and nothing more; no claim of creativity may rest on it.
- **modeller** (modeller) — whoever writes a model down.
- **picture** (representation; represent) — a carrier pictures a content when the set-up it embodies has a matching link to the content, with a history of selection or construction; not only an image.
- **pictured** (represented, as in "represented target") — held in a picture by the system.
- **thing layer** (primitive layer) — the layer at which there are lasting things with edges and identity, rather than a blur of sensation.
- **thread** (continuity relation; a thing's continuity cluster) — what keeps a thing in the thing layer the same thing from moment to moment.
- **expectation layer** (simulation layer) — the layer of dependences among things, where predictions are made.
- **moving**, **hiding**, **telling which is which again**, **swapping** (displacement, occlusion, re-identification, swap) — the changes allowed on the thing layer.
- **expectation** (expectation) — what the expectation layer's link gives at a setting that actually happens.
- **miss** (violation) — a link fails to match at a setting that actually happens.
- **surprise** (surprise) — a miss by a selected link at an untried setting.
- **re-tuning** (selection response) — responding to a miss by adding the setting to the trials and letting variation adjust the link within its pool.
- **response by construction** (construction response) — responding to a miss by constructing a fresh set-up or link, with evidence of construction.
- **tracker** (persistence component) — a piece constructed to carry a thing's position and speed through the time it is hidden.
- **inherited history** (inherited provenance) — the history that received content keeps when it is passed on.

## 12. Understanding, construction and newness

- **understand** (Deploy) — a system understands a content when it holds a picture of it, uses it in its work on challenges, and keeps the ability to use it for a stated use task.
- **use task** (declared use task) — the task by which a claim of understanding is judged.
- **lasting** (retained) — kept, not a one-off success.
- **stock** (repertoire) — the contents a system understands, at a moment, for some real use.
- **stretch** (subhistory) — a stretch of a course of events, with a start and an end.
- **join** (binding; binding construction) — a connection the builder makes, such as tying a tracker to a thing's thread.
- **construction evidence** (construction witness) — the record of a construction naming the processes, what came in, the joins made and the resulting picture.
- **relay** (relay) — passing content on without constructing any join in it.
- **reconstruction** (reconstruction by a learner) — working received content out afresh for oneself, which counts as construction.
- **unspoken picture** (inexplicit representation) — a picture that is present and used though not written or said out loud.
- **equivalent** (the equivalence written ≡) — two contents are equivalent at a level of detail when links run both ways between them, each matching on the content's range.
- **new** (New) — a content is new to a system when nothing in its stock before was equivalent to it; the word is used for nothing else.
- **used on the question** (Attempt) — the content was actually put to work on the question.
- **originate** (Origin) — a system originates a content for a question when it used it on the question, it was new to the system, and the system constructed it; the noun is **origination**.
- **finding a question** (question-finding) — originating a range with what is asked.
- **challenge** (problem, in its ordinary sense; problem-directed activity; advanceable challenge) — anything a system is trying to solve or advance; never write "problem" for this.

## 13. Criticism, episodes, repair and credit

- **criticism** (criticism) — a claim that something has a fault, with grounds and a connection from the grounds to the fault.
- **criticized item** (the target of a criticism; critical target) — whatever a criticism is aimed at: a candidate, a question, a range, a link, a method.
- **alleged fault** (alleged defect) — the fault a criticism says the criticized item has.
- **grounds** (grounds) — what a criticism rests on.
- **connection** (connection) — the criticism's argument from its grounds to the alleged fault.
- **fault question** (the question whether the target has the alleged defect) — the question whether the criticized item has the alleged fault, in respect of the original question.
- **bears on** (Bearing) — a criticism bears on its item when its connection, taken as a candidate for the fault question, is an account.
- **warning sign** (adverse signal) — something that tells against an item but is not yet a criticism, because nothing pictures it as grounds for an alleged fault.
- **objection** (conjectural objection) — a guess that something is wrong.
- **using an objection** (reason use) — a response uses an objection when the objection shapes the response in the right way and lies on a route that ran.
- **course of events** (history, in Part IX) — everything that actually happened, as events in an order that never loops back.
- **route that ran** (active route) — a connected cluster of actual events joining a pictured input to an outcome, through which the difference actually passed.
- **episode** (episode) — a stretch of inquiry, from a difficulty to a response.
- **episode of criticism** (complete critical episode) — an episode holding a noticed difficulty, an item there before it was criticized, an objection, and a response that depends on what the objection says.
- **creative episode** (creative critical episode) — an episode of criticism that holds an origination connected to its inquiry.
- **noticed difficulty** (recognized difficulty) — a failed demand, or a conflict in which what the system holds meets a demand only by breaking a safeguard, when the system pictures it.
- **repair** (Repair) — a demand that failed now holds, no safeguard is broken, and the change was produced by the contribution.
- **demand** (claimed obligation) — something stated as what a repair is to achieve.
- **safeguard** (protected obligation; protected condition) — something stated as what must go on holding while the repair is made.
- **occasion** (occasion covered) — a time or case that a demand or safeguard is stated to cover.
- **contribution** (contribution) — a stretch of events together with the changes of content it makes.
- **produced by** (ProducedBy) — a repair is produced by a contribution when a route that ran joins the contribution to the repair.
- **credit** (credit) — which contribution an achievement is attributed to.
- **sharing of credit** (division of credit) — how credit is split among contributions; the theory gives only what the course of events shows.
- **weighting of credit** (a weighting of credit among contributions) — a stated split of credit beyond what the course of events shows; a stated input.
- **outside contribution** (outside contribution) — work supplied from outside a system's boundary.
- **created knowledge** (created explanatory knowledge) — a creative episode that repairs a demand for knowledge through an account the system originated and now understands, whose join lies on the route that did the repair.
- **demand for knowledge** (epistemic obligation) — a demand that a correct account, or the correction of a use through one, be understood.
- **what the contribution prepared** (Result of a contribution) — the contents a contribution made ready by its end.
- **produced through the account** (ProducesVia) — the route that did the repair contains the account's join.

## 14. Worth

- **worth** (worth) — whether something is worth having or doing.
- **standard of worth** (normative relation) — whatever says what is worth having or good, taken from outside the theory and never derived.
- **effect**, **purpose**, **achieved purpose** (effect organization, purpose, achievement) — in the artistic case: what a work does, what it aims at, and all its effects falling inside its aim.
- **artistic value** (aesthetic value) — the worth of a work of art, taken as an input from the standard of worth.
- **reasons of taste** (aesthetic reasons) — reasons given for artistic value.
- **merit score**, **probability of truth**, **ranking of thinkers**, **measure of worth** (merit function, probability of truth, ranking of thinkers, measure of worth) — measures the theory does not supply.

## 15. The physics, ownership and abilities

- **the physics** (physical module; the adopted physics) — the adopted theory of physics, which says what is possible and what set-up a physical carrier embodies at a level of detail.
- **physical thing** (substrate) — a physical system as physics describes it.
- **property** (attribute) — a range of states of a physical thing, such as "hot" or "holding a one".
- **sort of matter** (physical medium) — a sort of material, such as the two sorts of matter in the case "Two sealed sorts of matter"; write "sort", never "kind", because **kind** is reserved.
- **task** (task) — changing a physical thing from a stated input property to a stated output property, with its resources and side effects stated.
- **procedure** (protocol) — a stated way of doing a task.
- **doer** (constructor) — whatever performs a task and is left able to perform it again.
- **carrying-out** (execution) — one carrying-out of a procedure, from start to finish.
- **getting stuck** (deadlock) — a carrying-out that never finishes, which does not count as success.
- **lasting performance** (retained realization) — every carrying-out of the procedure, from any start where the doer is ready, finishes with a right output and leaves the doer ready again.
- **staying able** (retention fixed point) — the widest condition of the doer from which it always comes back to the same condition.
- **making** (construction, in Part XII: "a finite construction of one") — an owned, possible, finite way of making a lasting performance; never write "construction" for this.
- **ability** (capability; owned capability) — an owned lasting performance, or an owned way of making one, under the stated sameness rule and resource terms.
- **able to advance** (achievement) — for each stated starting point, every carrying-out ends with the challenge advanced and the system still in shape.
- **degree of accuracy** (grade; grade of performance and grade of retention) — how exactly a task is done, and how reliably the doer is kept, short of perfect; never write "grade".
- **boundary** (system boundary) — the line stated round a system, saying which processes and resources are its own.
- **sameness rule** (continuity) — what is stated to make a system the same system through change.
- **resource terms** (resource contract) — which resources are stated to count as the system's.
- **own** (ownership; owned) — a process is the system's own when it runs inside the stated boundary; use the word for nothing else.
- **aid** (enabling condition) — help a system is allowed to have in doing a task.
- **fair aid** (Enable: an admitted, non-question-begging enabling condition) — an allowed aid that does not assume what is to be shown.
- **exchange between sorts of matter** (interoperability) — whether contents borne by one sort of matter can pass to another.

## 16. Criticism at any depth, barriers and universality

- **reviewable** (scrutinizable) — an aspect of a system's practice is reviewable when an owned way forward makes it pictured, criticized, and able to change how it is used.
- **criticism at any depth** (recursive capacity; recursive scrutiny) — for every finite chain of items (a criticism of a criticism of a method, and so on), the system has an owned way to go on.
- **barrier** (barrier) — a domain, described on its own terms, that the system cannot reach under any fair aid.
- **way round** (bypass) — a way of reaching what a proposed barrier said could not be reached.
- **well-posed challenge** (coherently posed advanceable challenge) — a challenge stated clearly enough that progress on it can be judged.
- **universal class** (the universal class) — systems with criticism at any depth that can, with a fair aid, understand every explanatory content and advance every well-posed challenge.

## 17. What the theory rests on

- **foundation** (primitive) — one of the two things everything else is defined from: the physics, and the standard of worth.
- **stated input** (declared input) — something a claim must state, which the theory records and does not supply.
- **missing input** (missing declared input) — a stated input that a claim needs and has not stated.
- **unsettled** (unsettled) — a verdict is unsettled when it turns on a missing input.
- **building order** (dependence order) — the order in which the theory's definitions rest on one another.
- **no circles** (well founded) — the building order never loops back on itself.
- **filled-in model** (interpretation) — the theory's blanks filled in for a real or imagined system.
- **class** (class): **base class**, **creative-episode class**, **knowledge-creation class**, **criticism-at-any-depth class**, **universal class** — the groups of filled-in models the theory defines, from the widest to the narrowest.
- **proof by example that the theory hangs together** (relative-consistency witness) — a worked example in which all the definitions hold at once.

## 18. The project's words

- **case** — a short everyday situation from one of the case books.
- **fixed verdict** (thoughtful person's verdict) — what a careful person says about a case, written in advance and never changed.
- **proposed case** (candidate case; "candidate O76") — a case with an agreed verdict that no round has yet used; never write "candidate case", because **candidate** is reserved.
- **round** (a test round such as S81) — one complete trial of a version of the theory on the cases; never write "test" for it.
- **ruling** (determination) — the project's final reading of what the theory says on a case, with reasons.
- **agrees**, **silent**, **split**, **disagrees** (the marks AGREE, SILENT, SPLIT, DISAGREE) — the theory gives the fixed verdict; the theory does not reach it because something it needs is missing; the theory can be read two ways; the theory gives a different verdict.
- **toward**, **away** — a change between versions moves a case toward the fixed verdict, or away from it.
- **outside readers** — two AI systems from outside the project that cross-examine its rulings and drafts.
- **cross-examination** — asking the outside readers to attack a ruling or a draft, then checking every attack that lands.
- **file 10**, **file 11**, **file 13**, **draft 4** — the older version, the version in force, the next version, and the fourth draft of the next version.
- **change of claim** — a change from which a reader can conclude something file 11 left unconcluded.
- **source books** — the two books the owner named as the theory's sources, by David Deutsch and by Chiara Marletto.
- **departure** — a place where the theory differs from its source books on purpose.
