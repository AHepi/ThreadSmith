# Cold read 1

I read "whole v1.md" once, from the first line to the last, as someone who has never seen the theory. I did not open any other file. Below, every place where I got lost is written down in the order I met it. Each one gives the heading, a short quote, what confused me, and the question I would want answered.

After the list come the words that seem to mean the same thing, or one word used for two things. Then a short list of claims that seem to contradict each other, the three hardest parts, whether "The theory in one page" prepared me, and what I would cut.

**Count: 200 confusing places.** Of these, 181 are in the section-by-section list (entries 1 to 181) and 19 are word problems that run through the whole document (entries W1 to W19). The contradictions list points back to entries already counted.

## Word problems that run through the whole document

The owner's rule is one word for each thing. These are the places where I met two words for what seemed one thing, or one word for two things. They are counted in the total.

**W1. "answer"** is used in three ways: what the world gives (section 2's definition), what a candidate gives ("the candidate gives the target's answer", "their answers there differ"), and a candidate itself ("offered as an answer", "failed answers", "a wrong answer stays wrong").

**W2. "input"** is used in five ways: a slot set directly (section 2); a "stated input" a claim must supply; a "missing input"; a "pictured input" (route that ran); an "input property" (tasks). The word list also uses "inputs" for "carriers that came in".

**W3. "verdict", "ruling" and "mark".** Three words for judgements, and "verdict" is also used for the theory's own judgement ("the verdict is unsettled", "a verdict on the limit"), not only the careful person's fixed verdict.

**W4. "record"** is used for the record of changes, the evidence ("the record has not chosen"), a log of failures (Bruno "records every failure"), a written trail ("put on the record", "honesty ... lives in the record"), a derived document (Sam's diary, "a later record"), and the owner's "A record is redundant".

**W5. "route"** means a support, a group of commitments (section 5), and also a cluster of actual events (section 10). "Route" and "support" are then two words for the same thing.

**W6. "rival" and "competitor".** The one page says competitor, the body says rival.

**W7. "problem", "challenge", "noticed difficulty", "difficulty".** "Problem" is promised one meaning, but it appears in its everyday sense in the novelist case, and Deutsch's "problem" is said to be the theory's "noticed difficulty". "Challenge" and "noticed difficulty" overlap.

**W8. "complaint", "objection", "criticism", "warning sign".** Section 1 calls Part 0's objections "complaints". Section 10 then uses "objection" as a separate idea, next to "criticism".

**W9. "system", "thinker", "mind", "creative agent", "judge", "doer", "builder".** It is never said whether these are one thing.

**W10. "link", "tie", "correspondence", "picture".** The one page says tie, the subtitle and section 12 say correspondence, the body says link, and a picture is a kind of link.

**W11. "candidate", "theory", "claim", "explanation", "idea", "rule".** For example: "a system can picture a false theory", "a later theory changes the range", "a theory may hold a correct dependence".

**W12. "episode" and "stretch"** (and "episode of inquiry"). "Episode" is explained in section 7, redefined in section 10, and used in section 2 before either.

**W13. "narrowing", "rescue", "retreat", "restated rule", "limit".**

**W14. "change", "step", "operation", "move", "edit".** "Allowed changes" in section 2, "allowed steps" with the tokens, "operation" with the string.

**W15. "measurement".** It is a defined response pattern in section 4, and an everyday word with the goatherds, straight after. "Measure" appears in section 2 as well.

**W16. "repair".** It is a defined word in section 10, and also used in the everyday sense: "renamed ... to repair it", "File 11 repaired it", "Three more defects ... are repaired in draft 4".

**W17. "proof", "derivation", "theorem".**

**W18. "dimension" and "slot".**

**W19. "group" and "class".** "Group" is a defined word for commitments, and the one page also says "the groups it defines", meaning classes.

Also noticed but not counted separately: "possible" is defined twice (sections 3 and 11); "pins down" is a defined word for readings but is used loosely for spread-out support ("pins it at nought"); "pool" and "population" appear together (the forbidden wire); and "account", "accounted for" and "holds to account" sit side by side.

## The three hardest parts

**1. Section 3: "A candidate" and "The four requirements".** This is the heart of the theory and the hardest place to get in. Seven new words (link, anchor, cluster, slot pairing, carries over, commitment, background) come before any example, and the pole is never walked through them. The third requirement packs two different tests under one name, and the second test, about losing a difference, is the hardest sentence in the document. The fourth requirement's paragraph ends by saying something fails the third. The cases that follow are good (Carla, the domino, salt), but by then I had already lost the definitions they were meant to show.

**2. Section 6: "Rivals" through "Same anchors, one account".** The owner's own decision lives here, which makes it the section that most needs to be clear. It is not. The second half of the meaning of "conflict" has no example. "What is established" brings in about twelve new words in one paragraph. Two results are surprising and not explained: the dog and the turtle are not rivals, and the tilt is easy to vary relative to the myth. The last of these is later called undecided. The Derivation 2 paragraph ends with a "list of defeats" being "triggered", which reads as if the theory has already failed.

**3. Section 11: "Tasks, and what is possible" and "Barriers and the universal class".** This is a pile of abstract words (doer, procedure, carrying-out, lasting performance, getting stuck, staying able, degrees of accuracy, fair aid, domain) with no example at all. "Possible" gets a second, harder meaning. The universal class refers to "both lists" that are never explained.

Close behind: section 5's idle commitments and the smallest-supports theorem, and section 10's single-sentence definition of created knowledge.

## Did "The theory in one page" prepare me for the rest?

Partly.

What worked: the pole and shadow is a clear first example, and "only by what happens when things are changed" gave me the one idea that carried me through. The outline told me the order of topics and which section to look in.

What did not:

- The subtitle is jargon.
- Words are put in bold without an example, and several are never explained on the page: kind, repair, credit, boundary, system, pictures, groups.
- "Four things" comes straight after "four requirements", so I mixed them up.
- The page says "competitor" where the body says "rival", and "tie" where the body says "link".
- It says nothing about cases, fixed verdicts, rulings and files, which fill every section afterwards.
- It does not warn that the document is also a progress report. Several verdicts are open, one example (Tomas) may go against the theory, and one entry of the list of defeats "stands triggered" for the older texts.

After reading it I knew where things were, but not what they meant. Each bullet would work better with the name of one case beside it: the farmer's rule for "easy to vary", the apprentice for "originates", the plumbers for "credit".

## What I would cut, or move out of the main text

- **The "In the theory: Parts ..." line at the end of every section.** These are codes. Move them into one table at the end.
- **The "What draft 4 changed here" paragraphs.** They describe things a newcomer never saw ("explanatory jobs", "reach", "drops 'today' from owning", "absence gains the question of appearance"). Gather them in one closing section, "Changes from the current text".
- **The file numbers.** Use "the old text", "the current text" and "the new draft", and drop "file 13" altogether.
- **The status tags on every example.** Cut them to one short mark each ("tested: agrees", "not yet tested"). Explain in section 1 whose view the untested ones are, once, and do not repeat "the view recorded with the draft" each time.
- **The mathematics** (odd-sized tables, spread-out support, the smallest-supports theorem, the proof on error build-up, "sums of the unknowns with fixed weights"). Move it to an appendix for readers who want it. Keep one plain sentence in the main text for each.
- **Terms explained and never used again:** "tipping edge" and "reviewable".
- **Examples named but never told:** "parallel and priority wiring", "The expert's question", "The same afternoon, three achievements", "delivery logs". Either tell them in two lines or drop the names.
- **The answer to the complaint "So a thing is whatever you can do to it"** (the "content ... still holds every piece" sentence). I could not follow it, and it brings in a key word in the wrong place.
- **"Values may be paths, proofs or histories; pieces may form loops; several consistent states stay several."** Not needed on first reading.
- **Section 13's "Words not defined, or not placed".** It names words the reader never met. Keep it for the project, not the newcomer.
- **The bracketed formal terms in "Words used here"**, such as "(Lipschitz constant)" and "(edit–boundary pair)". Move them to a separate list for the project.
