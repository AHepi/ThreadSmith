# What Avida taught us about the semantics, in plain words

## The main point

Avida's evolved programs hold the kind of knowledge evolution makes; nothing in them builds anything new with a goal in view. What Avida teaches the theory: it cannot yet say whether an evolved program stands for its task. That turns on one choice only you can make, and on one missing word.

## The choice

Two words first. In the theory, a **match** between a program and its task is **selected** when copying and keeping what worked shaped it, with nothing earlier in its history already aiming at the task. It is **declared** when someone simply set it up. A selected match stands for what it matches; a declared one stands for nothing.

Avida pays programs through a **checker**: code, written by people, that tests whether a program's answer is, say, NOT of the number it was given. The choice is whether that checker counts as something earlier in the program's history that already aims at the task.

**One program, three answers.** In one run, a program 110 instructions long, carried by 44 programs, hands back NOT of its number.

- **A: the checker is one of the execution environment's rules, like a law of physics.** The match is selected. It stands for NOT, and taken whole the theory counts it as an explanation of NOT. A bad one (cut into its instructions it fails the theory's test), and never one the program created.
- **B: the checker is people's work that already aims at NOT.** The match is declared. The program stands for nothing and explains nothing. The theory would then say the same of anything bred or found by a search that people scored: dog breeds, crops, a design found by a computer search.
- **C, new here: it depends on where the system's edge is drawn.** The **edge** is the line, stated before any judgement, between what belongs to the system and what comes from outside. Draw it round the whole execution environment, as you did ("The environment is the thing that does selecting"), and the checker's match came in whole from outside, so inside the edge it aims at nothing; the program's match is selected, as in A. Draw the edge round the people who wrote Avida too, and you get B.

Living things, whose adaptations nobody checked, come out selected under all three; the choice decides only whether Avida is a fair model of them.

**My recommendation, yours to decide: C, with your edge.** It treats Avida as you have: the execution environment selects, and what the programs are should not turn on who wrote the checker, which you asked to set aside ("Ignore the fact that they were evolved to solve a problem"). Under it, "knowledge without the explanation kind" holds as the sources mean it: nothing in Avida, or in evolution, creates an explanation. If you meant that an evolved program must not count as an explanation even in the theory's narrow sense, no answer gives that for living things; it would need a change to the theory's central claim, against your answer on the copied pendulum formula ("No, not if just declared").

## The missing words

- **Copying itself.** Useful: it can be defined from words the theory already has.
- **Withstanding change.** Useful as a definition; how much change is withstood is out of scope. It is not repair: nothing in Avida repairs anything.
- **Lasting by being copied faster than the world removes.** Out of scope: the physics of things that copy. Remaining as such can already be said.
- **Being copied more, not just surviving.** Needed. The theory's selection asks that survival require the match; in Avida, and in nature, a match only makes copying more likely. Without a word for that, the theory cannot call most of Avida, or most of evolution, selected. A wording is proposed.
- **How widespread doing a task is.** Out of scope.
- **Pay that falls as more programs use it.** Out of scope.
- **The order in which tasks are posed.** Out of scope; the theory already records order.
- **What a program can become after one change.** Useful: the theory has it, unnamed.
- **A cost borne for another.** Out of scope.

Your three properties (being copied, resisting change, remaining) are the first three. Plainly: the theory has no words for them, and no word "knowledge" at all. It should not invent its own: you said knowledge is already defined in constructor theory, the physics the theory takes as given. One proposed sentence would name them as that physics' knowledge, so the theory can say what selection and explanation add. That brings the word "knowledge" into the theory: your call.
