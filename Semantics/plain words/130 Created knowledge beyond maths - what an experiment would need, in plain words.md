# Created knowledge beyond maths: what an experiment would need, in plain words

*Log S130, 2 October 2026. Your words: "Oh I don't know about explanation. It seems like it counts as knowledge, not explanation. Which is the part we are currently at. Knowledge evolved through natural selection is case closed. And knowledge created is mostly. Actually, can we experiment with created knowledge again. But this time beyond just math?" ## The main point

Your objection is fair by the theory's own rules: as written, the theory calls the evolved NOT program an explanation, so if you hold that it is only knowledge, the theory's central claim fails for you unless "explanation" is kept for what was worked out. The experiment would give a subject (the system under test) a sealed box it has never seen, with hidden parts, and ask it to work out how the box works, then use what it worked out to fix a problem.

## One example: the sealed box

**What the subject sees.** A box with three buttons, a dial, four lamps, a bell and a door. Inside are six hidden parts, wired at random by a program written beforehand, so nobody, us included, knows any box in advance. Some parts hold a state, count or delay, so a first simple guess is always wrong somewhere. A panel lets the subject take a part out a few times.

**What it does.** It presses, turns and reads, up to 60 times, keeping a notebook of its current idea and what it expects before each try. When a try goes against it, we look for it to say which part of its idea was wrong and change that part.

**What we measure.**
- Whether it ever holds two rival ideas and picks a try that would tell them apart.
- Whether its final account (its written description of how the box works) answers 40 sealed questions about changes it never tried ("with part 4 taken out, after these presses, what do the lamps show?").
- Whether the notebook does real work: we rerun the session from a saved point with one line of the notebook altered, and see whether its later choices follow the altered line.
- Whether it can use its account on a changed box to open the door in 12 tries.

**What would count as created knowledge.** In most sessions: a wrong first idea noticed and repaired; rival ideas tested; the sealed questions mostly right, far above its guesses with no box; the altered notebook changes what it does; the door opened through its account.

**What would not.** Answers no better than guessing without the box; right only on what it tried; a notebook that can be altered without changing what it does.

**Checks alongside.** The subject handed the box's rules on a card should come out as copied, not created; a blind search that keeps whatever matches the box, as evolved. Boxes with no trap in them: there should be no wrong first idea to repair. If our test cannot tell these apart, the experiment says nothing.

**Cost.** One job to build it; then about 7,000 calls to the subject and under three hours of computer time.

## Your objection and the theory

The theory has a test for an account: its parts and the whole must match what they are about, under every change it claims to cover. The evolved NOT program passes it, narrowly: the question was written by us, not held by the program or the execution environment; it covers only the two inputs the program was always given; and it passes only taken whole.

The theory then adds one step: anything that passes the test and was not simply written in is an explanation. That step is a guess the theory makes about itself, and it names exactly your kind of objection as the one that would sink it.

The smallest repair is to keep "explanation" for an account that was worked out. Anything that passes the test but was selected by the execution environment stays what the theory already calls a representation, a match with a history behind it, which is your "knowledge". Against it: a proof found by blind search would stop being an explanation, and "worked out" includes learning from a teacher's answers, which you might also call only knowledge.

**Your choice:** keep "explanation" for what was worked out, so the evolved NOT program is evolved knowledge and not an explanation; or keep the theory as it is, so it is a narrow and bad explanation, and your objection stands against the theory's central claim.
