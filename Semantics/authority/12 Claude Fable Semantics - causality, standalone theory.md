# Claude Fable Semantics: Causality

## A structural account of causation as dependence under admitted change

*File 12, 22 September 2026. The theory of file 11 rewritten with causation as its object. It keeps the primitives, the organizations and their admitted edits, kinds as edit-signatures, contracts and queries, transports and the four conditions of an account, support and interference, the transport results, histories and active routes, the physical module, recursion, the collected class and the list of what defeats it, in the same names and notation where the names still fit. Its subject is what it is for one thing to cause another, what a causal claim is held to, which way causation runs, what an actual cause is in a history, and what is settled and what is left open when a cause is claimed. It carries no account of how a correspondence between a model and its target came to hold: a transport is assessed by its fidelity on a contract and by nothing about its history, and everything the earlier file defined from the history of a correspondence is absent here. The parts about the origin of a content in an episode are kept where they can be stated from histories, ownership and repertoires alone. The aesthetic instance of the normative relation and the two-layer episode of persistent things are left out; the parent theory holds them.*

# Part 0 — Read this first

## What this document claims

A cause is a **component of a target whose relation the admitted changes can replace, and on which the answer to a question depends under those changes**. A causal claim is a **question-relevant organization of dependencies**, held to a target by a **transport** that is checked only by **what changes when things are changed**. The definition never asks whether a piece of the model "is a cause" in some sense prior to the changes. That question has no independent content: at any level of detail, a *kind* is nothing over and above how a component responds to the changes that level admits, and "cause", "measurement", "rule" and "constitutive status" are names for families of such responses. Two components no admitted change can separate are one kind at that level, whatever labels anyone attaches to them (Part II, Derivation 1).

Causation has a **direction**, and the direction is derived. An admitted edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port and leaves the other components as they were. A port set by such an edit changes what depends on it and leaves what it depends on unchanged. That asymmetry, and nothing added to it, is the asymmetry between cause and effect (Part II, Derivation 3). No order of time, no transfer of a conserved quantity and no primitive relation of production is assumed; where the physics supplies any of these, it enters through which edits the physical module admits (Part XII).

An **actual cause** of a result in a history is an occurrence joined to the result by an **active route**: a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences whose components satisfy their relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts. A route that started and did no work, or that was already at rest when the result occurred, is not active for that result (Part IX). Pre-emption and overdetermination are read from the history, and the reading is exact (Derivation 11).

The **constitutive conjecture** is this: causation is fully characterized by (i) organizations and the changes they admit, (ii) transports between organizations checked by change-fidelity alone, (iii) histories with their active routes, and (iv) physical realization of the admitted changes. A genuine causal fact that these four cannot represent counts against the conjecture. A candidate that satisfies all four while plainly stating no cause counts against it too. Part XV lists what would count.

## What this document does not claim

It does not reduce causation to probability, to regularity, to counterfactual dependence between two events, or to a transfer between processes. Each of these appears as a pattern that some contracts contain and others do not: a probability is a value a port may take; a regularity is a solution set that holds across a boundary set; a counterfactual dependence is a difference between two admitted edits; a transfer is a component the physical module admits. None is the definition.

It does not say prediction is causation. Fidelity is over **component structure under change**, not over outputs. Two systems with identical outputs and different internal routes are different organizations here, and the semantics says so (Derivation 10).

It does not supply a measure of causal strength, a probability of truth, a merit function, a measure of worth, or a ranking of causes by importance. Where a claim needs one of these, the semantics takes it as a **declared input** and marks the place (Parts XI, XIV). It does not supply a division of credit among contributors beyond what a history establishes (Part XI).

It does not prove that any human, machine, institution or physical system holds the causal claims it makes. It defines what a causal claim is held to.

## What is primitive, what is an index, and what is derived

The semantics has two primitives: the **physical module** \(\Theta\), which says what organization a physical occurrence instantiates at a grain and which controlled actions the physics admits, and the **normative relation** \(\mathcal N\), taken as an input wherever a question invokes worth. Grain, boundary, continuity and the contract of admitted changes are **declared indices**: every claim is relative to them, and none is a predicate that could be true or false. Everything else is derived, in the order Part XIV states. No predicate meaning "is a cause", "really produces" or "is knowledge" appears anywhere (Derivation 7).

## Grievances, anticipated

Each answer points at the part of the document that carries it; the front matter states nothing the body does not state more exactly.

**1. "Without a primitive of causation you cannot tell a cause from a correlation."** You can, and only this way. A correlation has no component that responds to an intervention on its supposed input; a cause does. Two effects of a common cause are correlated; an admitted edit that sets one of them replaces its assigning component and leaves the other effect where it was. That is a difference in edit-response, which is what the semantics checks (Part II, "Kinds are edit-signatures"; Part VII, "Common cause and chain"). A primitive adds no discriminating power: where a separating change exists, fidelity finds it; where none exists, the primitive asserts a distinction the level does not contain.

**2. "So this is interventionism with new words."** It shares with any interventionist account the thought that a cause is what a change on it changes, and it differs in four places. Admitted edits include more than settings of a variable: deletions, replacements of a rule, changes of a dimension, and edits to a reporting relation are edits on an organization, with the same fidelity conditions. Roles are derived from the admitted edits, so no variable is marked as intervention variable, cause or effect before the contract is fixed. Fidelity is required at the component level and the global level at once, so a model that predicts every intervention while decomposing the target wrongly fails (Part V). And actual causation is read from active routes in a history, so a pre-empted route is excluded by what it did rather than by a counterfactual it fails (Part IX).

**3. "Then everything is relative to a contract of admitted changes, and there is no objectivity."** The physical theory fixes which changes are possible at all. A contract is a declared subset of those. A causal claim is scoped to its contract and says so. Within any contract there is a fact of the matter about fidelity. A contract that quietly excludes physically possible changes to protect a claim is not caught by a rule about intentions; it is caught by the requirement that the exclusion be stated (non-vacuity, Part V), and then by any criticism that supplies the excluded change. Objectivity lives in the physics and the fidelity facts; scope-honesty lives in the record.

**4. "Causes precede their effects. Where is time?"** Nowhere in the definition. Direction is the asymmetry between an edit that replaces a component and the components it leaves in place (Derivation 3). Where the adopted physics admits settings of earlier states and no settings of later ones, the derived direction follows time; where it admits both, the organization says which dependencies run which way, and where it admits a cycle, the semantics reports mutual dependence and calls it that. Time enters through the physical module, as a fact about which edits are admitted, and through histories, as an acyclic precedence among occurrences (Part IX).

**5. "Causation is a physical process: a mark transmitted, a conserved quantity exchanged."** The physical module says which processes exist and which controlled actions are admitted. Where it admits a process that carries a quantity from one occurrence to another, that process is a component with a signature, and a causal claim anchored to it is assessed as any other. The semantics does not require a transmitted mark or an exchanged quantity, because absences, preventions and rules all figure in causal claims that have no such carrier (Part VII), and it does not forbid one.

**6. "A finite set of tests cannot establish a cause."** Correct, and the semantics says exactly what a finite set of tests establishes. A model checked on a history \(H\) of admitted changes is constrained on \(H\). At a change outside \(H\), its value is underdetermined by \(H\) wherever an admitted rival that agrees with it on \(H\) differs there; where the physics and the stated construction admit no such rival, the value is fixed by them and the tests were never needed for it (Derivation 4). What a test refutes is a conjunction (Part IX, K3).

**7. "Mathematics has no interventions, yet 'because' is used there."** An admitted change need not be a physical intervention. Removing an axiom, dropping a constraint or changing a dimension are edits on an organization, and fidelity under them is well defined. A dependence under rule edits satisfies the same conditions as a dependence under interventions and has a different signature: its components respond to edits of the rule and are invariant under interventions on the world. The semantics represents both and calls only the second a cause (Part VII, "Dependence that is not causation").

**8. "Absences do not cause anything, and the semantics must say so or admit every absence as a cause."** It does neither. A question of the form "why is there no \(X\)-effect?" or "why did the plant die?" has a target in which the ports and components a rival account would need are absent, and a contract containing the edits that would introduce them. An absent component is a deleted subnetwork with the full relation. Whether an absence is a cause is settled by whether the contract contains the edit that supplies the missing component and whether the answer changes under it; which absences are admitted is the contract's declared scope, and the semantics records it rather than choosing it (Part VII, "Causation by absence").

**9. "Kinds obviously exist. A law is not a cause."** A law and a cause differ in how they respond to changes: a law's application changes when the law is edited and not when the world is intervened on; a cause's assignment changes under intervention. That is a difference in edit-signature (Part II), and the semantics represents it exactly. What it denies is that the difference is available before the admitted changes are fixed, or independently of them.

**10. "Freezing the contract for assessment while letting contracts change across episodes is having it both ways."** It is, deliberately, and the two ways are indexed so they cannot be confused. An assessment is an event with a frozen contract. An episode is a history in which contracts change, and every change is recorded as a new index. Derivation 8 shows the two are consistent.

**11. "Pre-emption defeats every dependence account."** It defeats accounts that read actual causation from a dependence between two events. The semantics reads it from a history: the thrower whose rock broke the bottle is joined to the breaking by an active route, and the thrower whose rock arrived at a bottle already broken is joined by a route that was at rest when the result occurred. No counterfactual about the second rock is consulted (Part IX; Derivation 11).

## Where to attack this

The load-bearing claims, in the order of how much falls if they fail, are stated exactly in Part XV together with what would refute each. In short: (A) sufficiency of the four conditions of Account; (B) their necessity; (C) the eliminability of kinds; (D) the derivation of direction from admitted edits; (E) actual causation as the active route. Anything else is a detail.

# Part I — Commitments

**Causal realism.** Whether a transport is faithful on a contract is independent of whether anyone accepts it. Systems can be wrong about their causal models, their observations, their criticisms and their own capacities.

**Fallibility without falsehood-as-work.** A theory may contain an accurate scoped dependence together with errors elsewhere. What cannot count as a causal claim is an error in the very dependence alleged to do the work.

**Conjecture, criticism, action.** A causal hypothesis may be entertained without justification. A criticism has a target and is itself conjectural. A thinker may act on an appraisal without certifying it.

**Recursive scrutiny with operative return.** Any aspect of a system's practice, a question, a contract, a transport, a method, an attention policy, can become a target, and the result must be able to change how the system proceeds.

**Substrate independence with physical obligations.** Any carrier may bear an organization. Every attribution of an organization to a physical system must be permitted by the adopted physics, and every admitted edit in a contract must be an edit the physics admits.

**Direction is derived, not stipulated.** Which way a dependence runs is a consequence of which edits are admitted and which component each edit replaces. No arrow is entered by hand.

# Part II — Organizations and their changes

## Organizations

An organization is

\[
D=(V,(X_v)_{v\in V},J,B,A,L).
\]

\(V\) is a set of ports, each with a nonempty value domain \(X_v\). A valuation is an element of \(X_D=\prod_v X_v\). \(J\) indexes components; each component \(j\) has a footprint \(V_j\subseteq V\). \(B\) is a set of boundary conditions. \(A\) is a set of admitted edits, closed under a partial associative composition with identity \(1\). For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies

\[
L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v .
\]

The compatible valuations are

\[
\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)=\{z\in X_D:\forall j\in J,\ z|_{V_j}\in L_j(a,b)\}. \tag{O}
\]

A deleted component imposes the full relation on its ports. An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port; it does not add an equation beside an incompatible one. A changed rule is a changed component.

Values of ports may be paths, functions, fields, proofs or histories. Cyclic constraints are admitted. Several solutions remain several.

## Roles are derived

No role assignment is supplied. A port \(v\) is an **input** under \(A\) when \(A\) contains an edit that sets \(v\) directly. A port is an **output** of component \(j\) when its value is determined by \(L_j\) given the other ports of \(V_j\) across \(B\). A port is an **observation** when \(A\) contains an edit that alters the relation reporting it without altering what it reports. The direction of an organization is a consequence of which edits it admits, not a stipulation about which way an equation is read.

## Causal dependence

Fix an organization \(D\), a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\) (Part III) and ports \(u,v\). Say \(v\) **depends causally on \(u\) on \(C\)** when there are \((a,b),(a',b)\in C\) such that \(a\) and \(a'\) set \(u\) to different values and differ in nothing else, and

\[
\operatorname{proj}_v\big[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\big]\neq\operatorname{proj}_v\big[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a',b)\big]. \tag{CD}
\]

A **cause of \(v\) on \(C\)** is a port on which \(v\) depends causally on \(C\), or a component whose replacement by an edit in \(C\) changes the projection to \(v\) with the boundary held. The relation is contract-relative: a coarser contract, containing fewer settings, exhibits fewer dependences, and a dependence absent on \(C\) may be present on a finer contract. (CD) is a pattern in (O), not additional data. The word "cause" is used below as an abbreviation of it and never as a further predicate.

## Kinds are edit-signatures

The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is

\[
\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}. \tag{K}
\]

Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one kind on \(C\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)\) and \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j')\) coincide. A kind is an equivalence class of components under this relation. Kinds are therefore relative to the contract; a coarser contract identifies more components, and two components of one kind on \(C\) may separate on a finer contract.

What ordinary language calls a cause, a measurement, a rule or a constitutive status are families of signatures:

- a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits;
- a **measurement** has a signature invariant under interventions on the measured port and variable under edits to the measuring relation;
- a **rule application** has a signature invariant under interventions on the world and variable under edits to the rule.

These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data. The semantics never asks whether a component "is" a cause. It asks what its signature is. In particular, a part that reads or reports another part has a measurement's signature: change only the reading and the part it reports stays as it was; change the part and the reading follows. Which of the two an account offers as producing an outcome is settled by that signature, not by the account's wording.

# Part III — Questions

## Contracts

A causal question is

\[
p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p).
\]

\(D\) is the target. The **contract** \(C\subseteq A\times B\) is the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs the claim ranges over; it contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\). \(\mathcal Q\) is a specified set-theoretic operation on \(D\), its solutions and its component structure, with codomain \(Y_p\). The answer profile is

\[
\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b). \tag{Q}
\]

\(O_p\) is the set of obligations being addressed or protected.

## Contrasts

Every causal question is contrastive, and its contrasts are the pairs of \(C\). "Why did the bottle break?" asks nothing until \(C\) says which changes are in play: the throw withheld, the throw slowed, the bottle moved. Two questions with the same words and different contrasts are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other. A contrast the question mentions and \(C\) omits is not in the question.

## The respect is the query

What a question asks, production, identification, obstruction, prevention, rule-status or purpose-achievement, is fixed by the type of \(\mathcal Q\) and the shape of \(C\), not by a label. A production question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that reads an output port and a \(C\) containing interventions on upstream ports. An identification question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that computes a fibre and a \(C\) containing edits to the observed value. An obstruction question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that returns a reachability truth value. A prevention question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that reads whether an outcome occurs and a \(C\) containing the edit that removes the preventer. Two questions with the same \(D\) and different \((C,\mathcal Q)\) are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other. A measure that identifies an outcome, with a reliable prediction from it, answers the identification question; whether the measured part also produces the outcome is the production question, and the first answer is not the second.

## Scope, and a question that can be wrong

A contract is a declared subset of the physically admitted changes, and a stated scope is what makes it one. A causal claim at a stated scope answers the question asked at that scope; it does not answer a broader question that failed, and a narrowing adopted after a failure is a new claim at a new index (Part VIII). What makes a restriction appropriate to the question asked is a substantive, criticizable part of the claim; the semantics records the restriction and supplies no rule that certifies it.

A question may fail to pick out its alleged target, assume an incompatible baseline, or combine incompatible requirements. Its formulation is still an event. Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract. What is prohibited is changing \(C\) or \(\mathcal Q\) during an assessment without recording that the claim has changed. Supplying a meaning for a replacement query can make a coherent new question; it does not answer the original one.

# Part IV — Layers, transports, and tested histories

## Occurrences and contents

An **occurrence** is a physically located carrier. A **content** is an organization together with its contract-relative commitments. Occurrences are not identified by carrying the same words; contents are not identified by having the same outputs.

## The primitive layer and the simulation layer

A modelled thinker has at least two organizations in play.

The **primitive layer** \(P\) is an organization whose ports are persistent things with boundaries and identity, whose components are their continuity relations, and whose admitted edits include displacement, occlusion and re-identification. It is the layer at which there are *objects* rather than a field of sensation.

The **simulation layer** \(S\) is an organization over \(P\) whose components are dependencies among things and whose queries are predictions: what a port of \(P\) will take under an admitted edit. \(S\) is where expectation lives, and it is where a thinker's causal model lives.

Neither layer is presupposed to exist in any particular physical system. The semantics describes what it is for them to exist and to be connected.

## Transports

A transport from an organization \(D\) to an organization \(E\) is

\[
t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)
\]

where \(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) on the stated scope, \(\tau\) translates edits, \(\sigma\) translates boundaries, and \(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\) with a port translation. A transport is **faithful on \(C\)** when it satisfies the component and global fidelity conditions of Part V. Nothing about how a transport came to be written enters its assessment.

## Representation is derived

An occurrence \(o\) **represents** content \(c\) at grain \(\ell\) when the organization that \(o\) instantiates under the physical module, at grain \(\ell\), admits a transport to \(c\) that is faithful on \(c\)'s contract:

\[
\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists t\,[\operatorname{Faithful}_C(t:\operatorname{Org}_\ell(o)\to c)]. \tag{R}
\]

The carrier–content relation is thus a fidelity fact. A system can represent a false causal model: the transport from carrier to content is faithful while the content's transport to the world fails. An author who writes a transport down has conjectured that (R) holds; (R) holds or fails by the fidelity fact, and the writing settles nothing. A later record derived from a carrier is a record of the carrier, not a second carrier.

The one thing (R) takes from outside is \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell(o)\): which organization a physical occurrence instantiates at a grain. That is supplied by the physical module, not by the semantics.

## Tested histories, expectation, violation

Let \(t\) be a transport with contract \(C\) whose fidelity has been checked on a finite **tested history** \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered. For an edit–boundary pair \((a,b)\in C\) actually occurring:

- the **expectation** is \(\operatorname{Ans}_S(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\);
- a **violation** occurs when fidelity fails at \((a,b)\);
- **surprise** is a violation at \((a,b)\notin H\).

A system with no transport cannot be surprised. A system whose tested history exhausts its contract cannot be surprised. Surprise requires an incomplete tested history: the world must admit changes the system's model was never checked against (Derivation 5). Whether the model could have been otherwise at such a change is a fact about which admitted rivals agree with it on \(H\) (Derivation 4).

Two responses to a violation are distinguished. A **re-tuning** extends \(H\) and alters values within the model's existing components. A **rebuilding** introduces a new organization or a new transport, with a discovery witness (Part X). Only the second can be originative under Part X.

# Part V — Account

A causal candidate for question \(p\) is an organization \(E\), a transport \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\) from \(D\) to \(E\), and an identified set \(\Gamma\) of active commitments in \(E\). The pair \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\) exactly when the following four conditions hold, each a condition on supplied relations under the changes in \(C\).

**Component fidelity.** For every active component \(k\) of \(E\) with anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\), and every \((a,b)\in C\), the relation obtained by imposing the constraints of \(\lambda(k)\) and projecting away its hidden ports equals the component relation of \(k\) under the translated edit:

\[
\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\!\big[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\big]=L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)). \tag{F1}
\]

And the assembled organization agrees:

\[
\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b)),\qquad \tau(1)=1,\quad \tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1). \tag{F2}
\]

(F1) prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition. (F2) prevents a set of locally correct pieces from hiding a lost shared constraint. Together they are fidelity at every level the contract reaches. By (K), (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor; there is no further condition about kinds to state (Derivation 1). By (CD), every causal dependence in \(E\) on \(\tau[C]\) is a causal dependence in \(D\) on \(C\) and conversely: the model's causes are the target's causes, on that contract.

**Question fidelity.** For every \((a,b)\in C\),

\[
\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b). \tag{A}
\]

The query \(\mathcal Q\) is held fixed; an account of a different query is not an account of this one.

**Non-circular dependence.** The answer follows by evaluating \(E\) under its independent boundary conditions. The target's answer does not appear, at the declared grain, as an unanalysed boundary input or as a component; moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named "law" does not discharge this. There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way.

**Non-vacuity.** \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\). The contract \(C\) is a declared subset of the physically admitted edits, and every physically admitted edit excluded from \(C\) is excluded by a stated scope, not silently. A contract consisting only of relabelings, or excluding every change under which the active commitments could matter to \(\mathcal Q\), does not satisfy non-circular dependence and is therefore not a contract on which an account can be claimed.

Thus

\[
\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}. \tag{E}
\]

Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in \(C\). None inspects a label.

## What (E) excludes, and what it does not

A **table of observed answers** has no component whose relation is replaced by an intervention; it fails (F1) under any contract containing one. A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense: it satisfies (F1) as a decomposition does, and it is an account. The word "table" settles nothing; the response under the admitted changes does.

A **reversed calculation**, identification presented as production, fails (F2) under the production contract: intervening on the upstream port changes the target's downstream value but not the calculation's. It may be faithful under the identification contract, which is a different question (Part III).

A **collateral effect offered as a cause** fails (F1) under a contract that sets it: the component anchored to it changes the model's answer under the setting and the target's answer stays (Part VII, "Common cause and chain"). Under a contract with no such setting the model and the target agree, and the semantics reports that the contract contains no cause–correlation distinction between them (Derivation 2).

"\(p\) because \(p\)" fails non-circular dependence. So does an account whose only substantive component restates the answer it was asked for; packaging a genuine dependence that answers a different question beside it does not repair this (the bell does not explain the tide).

An account whose only substantive contrast is one that no physically admitted edit realizes fails non-vacuity by having a silently narrowed contract.

(E) does not exclude a true mechanism guessed for bad reasons; the reasons for adopting it are assessed elsewhere (Part IX). It does not prefer an elegant account to a less elegant one with the same fidelity. It does not reject a coarse dependence for omitting finer workings or an instrument: a causal claim at a coarse grain is a claim about the coarse question, and its strength is fixed by its contract, not by what a finer contract would add. A proof answering "why does this follow under these rules?" satisfies (E) for that question without answering "what caused this?"; depth is question-relative and (E) measures none of it.

## Why there is no anchoring condition

A separate condition, "each component of \(E\) must be tied to a component of \(D\) *of the same kind*", would be either redundant or unevaluable. Where \(C\) contains a change separating two kinds, (F1) already fails for a component anchored to the wrong one. Where \(C\) contains no such change, the two are one kind on \(C\) by (K), and the condition would be asserting a distinction that \(C\) does not contain. There is no third case. Derivation 1 makes this exact. The same holds of a condition "each component offered as a cause must be a cause": by (CD) and (F1), it already is, on the contract, or the account already fails.

# Part VI — Contribution, support, and interference

Fix \(\mathcal E\) and a declared restriction operation. For \(W\subseteq\Gamma\), let \(E|W\) retain the commitments in \(W\) with the named background fixed. Define

\[
\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}. \tag{S}
\]

No upward closure and no minimal member are assumed. For nonempty \(B\subseteq W\),

\[
\operatorname{CriticalBlock}(B;W,p)\iff W\in\mathsf S_{E,p}\land W\setminus B\notin\mathsf S_{E,p}. \tag{B}
\]

A block may be critical while no singleton in it is. Criticality is relative to the support \(W\) it is assessed in: a commitment critical in one successful support need not be critical in the full candidate, and the supports assessed are the ones actually written, not a support someone could write in their place. For a declared family \(\mathcal V\) of organization edits,

\[
\operatorname{Boundary}_{E,p}=\{(v,w)\in\mathcal V^2:\operatorname{Account}(E_v,p)\neq\operatorname{Account}(E_w,p)\}. \tag{D}
\]

**Contributory and indispensable causes.** A commitment in some support is **contributory** to the answer; a commitment in every support is **indispensable**. A cause may be contributory without being indispensable, and the difference is (S), not a grade of causal strength.

**Finite monotone theorem.** If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\). *Proof.* A member of a minimal support is critical for it. If \(d\) is critical for \(W\), finiteness gives a minimal \(U\subseteq W\); if \(d\notin U\), upward closure makes \(W\setminus\{d\}\) a support. Deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when a minimal support omits \(d\). ∎ The theorem applies only where its assumptions hold; an addition to \(\Gamma\) that destroys a support is the interference case below, and there upward closure fails.

**Redundant routes.** \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\): each is contributory, neither indispensable. This is symmetric overdetermination at the level of commitments: two causes each sufficient, neither necessary. Two systems with the same output table may differ in which routes are active; the semantics represents the difference (Derivation 10). A route already present in the candidate is a route whether or not anyone has described its work; a component reassigned to a new target after a deletion belongs to a new candidate with its own assessment, and the new candidate's success is not the old one's.

**Interference.** \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\}\}\): the full candidate fails although a subset succeeds. This is the shape of a preventer among the commitments: success of a subset does not imply success of the whole, and \(b\) is not made "not a commitment" to repair this.

**Infinitary support.** \(\Gamma=\{d_n:|x|\le 1/n\}\): every unbounded index set determines \(x=0\); no minimal support and no singleton witness exists. (B) records the collective contribution.

**Hard-to-vary.** For causal jobs \(F\subseteq F'\), \(\operatorname{Pres}(F')\subseteq\operatorname{Pres}(F)\), where \(\operatorname{Pres}(F)=\{v\in\mathcal V:\forall f\in F,\operatorname{Account}(E_v,f)\}\). More reach constrains variation; the containment need not be strict; counting jobs is not a warrant.

# Part VII — Exact constructions

## Production and direction

Stipulate \(H:=U_H,\ \theta:=U_\theta,\ L:=H\cot\theta\). Under \(A\) containing interventions on \(H\) and \(\theta\), these ports are inputs and \(L\) is an output, by Part II. An intervention on \(L\) replaces its component and leaves \(H,\theta\) unchanged. The forward organization is faithful under this contract, and by (CD) the shadow's length depends causally on the pole's height and the sun's elevation, while neither depends on the length. The reversed calculation \(H=L\tan\theta\) is not faithful: intervening on \(H\) changes the target's \(L\) but not the calculation's \(H\). It is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed \(L\). Direction is derived from the admitted edits (Derivation 3); the nouns "pole" and "shadow" fix nothing.

## Identification

Let \(Z\) be the admitted states, \(g:Z\to Y\) the measurement, \(f:Z\to F\) the feature. The fibre at \(y\) is \(Z_y=g^{-1}(y)\). The feature is identified at \(y\) exactly when \(Z_y\neq\varnothing\land|f[Z_y]|=1\). (I1) It is identified on every attainable \(y\) exactly when \(f=\bar f\circ g\) for some \(\bar f\). (I2) For linear \(A\) and feature \(c^\top\), identification is \(\ker A\subseteq\ker c^\top\). (I4) Repeating rows changes no kernel; an independent calibration can.

For the two balances \(\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}\), the kernel is spanned by \((1,-1,-1)\); the readings identify \(b_B-b_A\) and not \(x\). This is an account of a limitation. "Set \(b_B=0\) because it gives the mass I favour" is not an inference from the readings; its support is circular though its content might be true. Identification is what a reading settles; production is what an intervention settles; a reading that identifies a cause has not thereby produced anything.

## Obstruction

For allowed steps \(R\) and an invariant \(I\) with \(zRz'\Rightarrow I(z)=I(z')\), no allowed path joins states of different invariant value. (O1) Equal values do not establish reachability. Twenty-three indivisible tokens cannot be split equally three ways; permitting division changes the state space and does not refute the scoped result.

## Common cause and chain

Stipulate ports \(c,e_1,e_2\), a component assigning \(c\), a component assigning \(e_1\) from \(c\), and a component assigning \(e_2\). The **fork** assigns \(e_2\) from \(c\); the **chain** assigns \(e_2\) from \(e_1\). Under every boundary, \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) covary in both. On a contract whose only edits set \(c\) or alter reporting relations, the fork and the chain have the same solution sets at every pair, and their components are pairwise of one kind (Derivation 2): the contract contains no distinction between "\(e_1\) causes \(e_2\)" and "\(c\) causes both", and a claim that one holds rather than the other is a claim about a finer contract. On a contract containing an edit that sets \(e_1\), the two separate: the chain's \(e_2\) follows the setting and the fork's \(e_2\) stays. By (CD), \(e_2\) depends causally on \(e_1\) in the chain and not in the fork, on that contract. This is grievance 1 made exact. The distinction between a cause and a correlated collateral is the presence, in the contract, of an edit that sets the collateral; where the physics admits no such edit, the distinction is absent from the physics, and the semantics says so rather than supplying it.

## Causation by absence

A question of the form "why is there no \(X\)-effect?", or "why did the unwatered plant die?", has a target \(D\) in which the ports and components a rival account would need are absent, and a contract containing the edits that would introduce them. An account is faithful when introducing those components changes the answer in \(E\) as it does in \(D\), and their absence leaves both unchanged. The rival's supposed structure, or the omitted watering, is anchored to a *deleted* subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O). Whether an absence counts as a cause is then settled by (CD): it does on a contract containing the edit that supplies the missing component, and it does not on one that omits it. The contract's declared scope says which absences are in play, and every physically admitted edit it leaves out is stated (non-vacuity). This is the semantics' treatment of absences and of eliminative explanation; it is offered as adequate and is listed under attack (B) in Part XV as a place where it may not be.

## Prevention

A preventer is a component whose presence leaves an outcome absent and whose removal, an edit in \(C\), lets the outcome occur. Its signature on that contract is a causal assignment's, on the port that records the outcome. Double prevention, a component that removes a preventer, is assessed on a contract containing edits that remove each; the outcome depends causally on the second preventer on that contract exactly when the first is present, and the semantics reports the dependence as conditional on the boundary rather than as a lesser kind of causation.

## Dependence that is not causation

Question: why is every odd-order real skew-symmetric matrix singular? Contract: remove skewness; remove oddness; remove both; field arithmetic and determinant–invertibility held fixed. The full Leibniz expansion with skewness substituted satisfies (F1) and (F2): every intermediate product is a determinant suborganization whose hidden ports project away. It satisfies (A): the answer is the absence of invertible matrices in the family, and under each contrast the family contains one. Non-circular dependence is witnessed by \(I_3\) under removal of skewness and by \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\) under removal of oddness. Non-vacuity is witnessed by any nonzero odd skew matrix. The expansion is an account. Every edit in its contract is an edit of a rule, and every component has a rule application's signature under (K): the dependence is faithful and it is not causal. "Because" covers both a causal dependence and this one; the signature, not the word, tells them apart. That a three-line proof is shorter is not a fifth condition.

## Constitutive rules

A rule relation \(C_r\subseteq Z\times S\) answers "which status under this rule?" by its fibre. Its signature under (K) is invariant under interventions on \(Z\) and variable under edits to \(C_r\). Whether the rule governs a practice, whether the practice causes anything, and whether it should, are separate questions with separate contracts.

# Part VIII — Transport results

**Functional transport.** If \(\pi\circ S_a=T_a\circ\pi\) for every generator \(a\), the same holds for every admitted finite composition with matching scopes. *Proof.* \(\pi S_bS_a=T_b\pi S_a=T_bT_a\pi\); induct. ∎

**Relational transport.** For \(R\subseteq Z_D\times Z_E\), forward preservation is

\[
zRy\land z\xrightarrow{a}z'\Rightarrow\exists y'[y\xrightarrow{\tau(a)}y'\land z'Ry'], \tag{T1}
\]

with the backward condition required for equivalence rather than one-sided abstraction. Composition of relations preserves both directions when intermediate scopes agree.

**Approximate transport.** With one-step discrepancy \(\varepsilon\) and an \(L\)-Lipschitz next-step map, \(e_n\le\varepsilon\sum_{k<n}L^k\). (T2) Without a modulus, no accumulated bound follows. An exact question is not silently replaced by an approximate one.

**Recoding.** A declared, invertible recoding of a carrier preserves the content when a reader who applies the declared convention recovers every pairing (Derivation 9). A section of a carrier filled from another source is a record of that source, whatever it happens to match.

**Historical index.** A proposition indexed to a contract remains that proposition when a later theory changes the current contract. A new index is a new claim.

# Part IX — Histories, actual causation, and criticism

**Histories.** A history \(h\) is a set of occurrences with an acyclic causal precedence \(\prec_h\) and a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated. The precedence is supplied by the physical module as part of the history; it is a fact about occurrences, and the direction of a dependence in an organization is derived from admitted edits independently of it (Derivation 3). The two agree where the physics admits settings of earlier occurrences only; where they disagree, the history records which occurrences preceded which, and the organization records which dependencies the admitted edits exhibit, and neither is corrected by the other.

**Active routes.** An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts. A route that started and did no work, or that was already at rest when the result occurred, is not active for that result; whether a route is active is read from the history, not from the result.

**Actual causation.** An occurrence \(x\) is an **actual cause** of a result \(y\) in \(h\), under declared contrasts \(K\), exactly when an active route in \(h\) joins \(x\) to \(y\) under \(K\):

\[
\operatorname{Cause}_h(x,y;K)\iff\exists\,\text{active route }r\subseteq h,\ x\in\operatorname{in}(r)\land y\in\operatorname{out}(r). \tag{AC}
\]

(AC) is read from the history and the contrasts and from nothing else. A pre-empted occurrence, joined to \(y\) by a route at rest when \(y\) occurred, is not an actual cause of \(y\) although a type-level model may make \(y\) depend on it under a different boundary. Two occurrences each joined to \(y\) by an active route are both actual causes, and (AC) supplies no ranking between them; where two sufficient routes both ran, both are credited and the history supplies no division of credit that it does not contain (Part XI). A dependence in a model that no route in the history instantiates is a type-level dependence and not an actual cause in that history.

**Bearing.** A criticism has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), grounds \(g\), and a connection. Let \(p_\delta\) be the question about the defect. Then

\[
\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p)\iff\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E_c,p_\delta). \tag{K1}
\]

A criticism occurrence can exist when (K1) fails. An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents how it bears.

**Reason use.** A response uses a reason when a structural map from the represented objection into the response suborganization preserves role bindings, sends content-preserving recodings to the same transition, sends content changes to the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule, and lands on an active route. Using an invalid objection does not make it valid.

**Standing.** For argument application \(u\) with essential premises \(\operatorname{Prem}(u)\):

\[
\operatorname{Usable}_j(u)\iff\operatorname{Lic}_j(u)\land\operatorname{Scope}_j(u)\land\forall d\in\operatorname{Prem}(u),\operatorname{Live}_j(d;u). \tag{K2}
\]

Withdrawing a premise removes a license; it does not make the conclusion false.

**What a test refutes.** For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\) and nothing narrower. (K3) An intervention whose predicted outcome fails refutes the conjunction of the causal model, the background and the claim that the intervention realized the intended edit; which conjunct fails is a further question.

**Receipts.** An evidence leaf is a reference to an event with an interpreted claim. A receipt is a derivation tree over leaves. For \(\phi\), \(P_j(\phi)\) and \(N_j(\phi)\) are the usable receipts for and against. Negation exchanges them; missing evidence stays missing. A record reconstructed from the claim it is meant to support is not a receipt for that claim.

# Part X — Understanding, discovery, and origin

**Deployment.** \(\operatorname{Deploy}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,\xi;U)\) holds when \(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\), by (R) a faithful transport, integrated into problem-directed activity and supporting the declared use task \(U\) as a retained capability (Part XII). The repertoire \(R_{\beta,\ell}(s,\xi)\) is the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect. A system may understand a false causal model. A narrow retained use is what it is: it establishes neither the wider understanding it falls short of nor a permanent inability to reach it.

**Discovery.** \(\operatorname{Find}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an actual subhistory owned by \(s\) and delimited at \(e\) prepares a represented organization for causal use of \(c\), contains a nontrivial binding relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers. A discovery witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings prepared, and the resulting representation. Reconstruction by a learner is discovery; relay is not. A first representation may be prepared from an available problem without prior observation of what it represents. A small binding newly prepared inside received content is discovery of that binding, and the rest of the content keeps its place in the prior repertoire.

**Newness.** With \(R_{<e}(s,h)=\bigcup_{\xi\text{ before }e}R_{\beta,\ell}(s,\xi)\),

\[
\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c. \tag{N}
\]

**Origin.** With \(\operatorname{Attempt}(s,c,p,h,e)\) the relation that \(c\) is actually used to address \(p\),

\[
\operatorname{Origin}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,p,h,e)\iff\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Find}. \tag{G}
\]

The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a contract. When it is a contract, the originative act is the finding of a causal question: which changes to admit. A dimension of variation mentioned in passing is not thereby a port of the account; it becomes one when the account admits changes to it, and adding it is discovery.

**Ownership.** The subhistory in Find is owned by \(s\) when its processes run inside the system boundary and resource contract declared for \(s\) (Part XII). Work supplied from outside that boundary, a diagnosis, a decisive question, an instruction about what to read, remains an outside contribution however it is executed inside; a process that runs inside the boundary is the system's own today whoever wrote it; and where the boundary is drawn decides, not where the process sits in the casing. Ownership is not defined by the capability it is meant to ground (Part XII).

**Episodes.** A complete critical episode contains a recognized difficulty, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a content-sensitive response. A creative critical episode contains an instance of (G) connected to its inquiry. Closing an episode is a decision, not a proof.

# Part XI — Repair, causal knowledge, and the normative

**Repair.** For claimed obligations \(O\) and protected obligations \(P\), fixed for the comparison,

\[
\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\iff\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\land\forall r\in P[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]\land\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O). \tag{P}
\]

The obligations are declared inputs: \(O\) says what is to be repaired and \(P\) what is to be protected, each as a stated condition over stated occasions, and a protected condition is lost exactly when it fails on an occasion it covers. Their declaration makes no claim that the aims are worth pursuing, and (P) does not rank alternatives. Losses outside \(P\) must be exposed. \(\operatorname{ProducedBy}\) holds when an active route (Part IX) runs from \(\Delta\) to the repair: it is (AC) applied to the repair as result. It credits each contribution the history establishes, and where two sufficient contributions both ran, both are credited and the history supplies no division of credit that it does not contain. A correct causal account that produced nothing, an act that repaired without an account, and a repair produced through use of an account are three different attributions.

**Created causal knowledge.** An epistemic obligation requires a correct account, or the correction of a use through one, to be deployable. With \(O_{\mathrm{ep}}\subseteq O\) the epistemic obligations,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{CreateCK}(s,\Delta,h,e)\iff{}&\operatorname{CreativeCriticalEpisode}(s,\Delta,h,e)\land\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\\
&\land\exists o\in O_{\mathrm{ep}}\,\exists c,p_c,e_c\,[e_c\preceq_h e\land\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')\land\operatorname{Origin}(s,c,p_c,h,e_c)\\
&\quad\land\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\land c\in\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\land\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\xi';U_c)\land\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')].
\end{aligned}\tag{CK}
\]

The scope of \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\) is the contract fixed at \(e_c\). A later narrowing of that contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (CK).

**Worth, and the normative relation.** Repairing an obligation establishes that it was repaired; it establishes nothing about whether the obligation, or the question that led to it, was worth having. Where a claim invokes worth, importance, or the choice of one cause as "the" cause among several actual causes, the semantics takes a **normative relation** \(\mathcal N\) as a declared input and marks the place (Part XIV). Selecting one actual cause for mention is such an invocation: (AC) supplies every actual cause and \(\mathcal N\), or a stated purpose, supplies the choice. The semantics does not derive \(\mathcal N\).

# Part XII — The physical module

**Tasks.** A substrate is a physical system; an attribute a set of its states; a task a permitted input-to-output attribute transformation with explicit resources and side effects. Possibility is the absence of a law-imposed limit short of perfection on performance and retention; it is not one successful trajectory. The physical module adopts a task-based formulation of physics for this purpose.

**Retained realization.** For protocol \(\pi\), task \(T\), constructor attribute \(C\), and enabling conditions \(\chi\),

\[
\operatorname{RetReal}(\pi,T,C;\chi)\iff\forall z\in C\ \forall i\in\operatorname{dom}T\ \forall\eta\in\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi),\ \eta\text{ completes with }o\in T[i]\text{ and }z'\in C. \tag{CT1}
\]

Execution families are nonempty on legitimate inputs; deadlock is not vacuous success.

**Retention fixed point.** \(F(C)\) = states whose executions all complete and return into \(C\). \(F\) is monotone; \(C\subseteq F(C)\) is the invariant form; the union of post-fixed sets is the greatest fixed point. (CT2)

**System boundary and continuity.** A capability is attributed to a system under a declared boundary (which processes and resources are the system's) and a declared continuity \(\Omega\) (what makes it the same system through change). A replaced part that preserves the declared continuity leaves the same system; a process run inside the boundary is the system's whoever wrote it; a process run outside it is not the system's however close it sits. Both are declared before the attribution, not chosen after it.

**Owned capability.** \(\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)\) requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one under the same continuity and resource contract. A theorist's description of a protocol is not the system's possession of it. Ownership is grounded in the processes and resources the boundary includes, never in the capability being attributed: "owned because it can, and can because owned" grounds neither.

**Achievement.** \(\operatorname{CanAdv}(\xi,p;\chi,J_p,C_I)\): for each starting configuration in the independently specified \(J_p\), every maximal execution completes with a history satisfying the achievement predicate and a continuing organization in \(C_I\). A first discovery is not a repeatable task; retention is applied to the inquiry-enabling organization. (CA)

**Grades.** \(\mathsf{Cap}^{q,r}_\Omega(\xi)\subseteq\mathsf{Admit}^{q,r}_\Theta\); \(\mathsf{Cap}^\infty=\bigcap_{q,r}\mathsf{Cap}^{q,r}\subseteq\mathsf{Poss}_\Theta\). (CT3, CT4) Finite-grade capability does not imply possibility at all grades.

**Interventions in the physical module.** An admitted edit that sets a port is **realized** when the physical module admits a controlled action that replaces the component assigning that port and leaves every other component's relation as it was. An action that changes two ports at once realizes a different edit, one that sets both, and a contract that names it as setting one has misdescribed its own edit; the semantics assesses fidelity under the edit realized. An edit that no admitted process realizes is excluded from every contract by the physics, and its exclusion is stated by the physics rather than by the account. The same holds of the precedence in a history: which occurrence preceded which is a claim about the physical module, fallible and checkable as any physical claim is.

# Part XIII — Recursion and universality

**Scrutinizability.** An aspect \(d\) of a system's practice is scrutinizable at \(\xi\) when there is an owned, admitted continuation in which a description of \(d\) becomes a represented target, criticism can be directed at it, and the result can affect its operative use. Contracts, transports and the causal model at the simulation layer are among the scrutinizable aspects.

**Recursive capacity.**

\[
\forall n<\omega\ \forall\text{ admitted target chains of length }n,\ \exists\text{ an owned enabling continuation}. \tag{RC}
\]

**Barriers.** A causal barrier is an independently characterized domain for which every admitted, non-question-begging enabling condition leaves the relevant capability unavailable. A finite list of failures is not a barrier proof; a bypass refutes a proposed barrier.

**Universality.** With \(\mathfrak E_\Theta\) the causal contents and \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\) the coherently posed advanceable causal challenges, both specified independently of the candidate,

\[
\operatorname{UU}\iff\forall c\in\mathfrak E_\Theta\ \exists\chi,\ \operatorname{Enable}(s,U_c,\chi)\land\operatorname{Can}(\xi_0,U_c;\chi), \tag{U1}
\]
\[
\operatorname{UC}\iff\forall p\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\ \exists\chi,\ \operatorname{Enable}(s,A_p,\chi)\land\operatorname{CanAdv}(\xi_0,p;\chi,J_p,C_I), \tag{U2}
\]
\[
\mathsf{UCS}=\{(M,s,\Omega,\beta):M\models\operatorname{RC}\land\operatorname{UU}\land\operatorname{UC}\}. \tag{U3}
\]

Recursion does not entail universality; a historical extension does not certify it; a finite performance record does not establish it.

# Part XIV — The class collected

**Primitives.** The semantics has two.

1. The **physical module** \(\Theta\): substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, controlled-action interpretation, resources, accuracy grades, the precedence among occurrences in a history, and \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\), the organization a physical occurrence instantiates at a grain.
2. The **normative relation** \(\mathcal N\), when a question invokes worth. It is taken as an input and never derived; the selection of one actual cause for mention is one place it enters (Part XI).

Everything else is derived. Roles, from admitted edits (Part II). Causal dependence, from solution sets under settings (CD). Kinds, from signatures (K). Direction, from which component a setting replaces (Derivation 3). The respect of a question, from its query (Part III). Representation, from fidelity (R). Account, from fidelity under change (E). Actual causation, from active routes in a history (AC). Understanding, discovery, newness, origin, repair, knowledge, capability, recursion, universality, from those.

**Declared inputs.** Besides the two primitives, some claims take stated inputs that the semantics records and does not supply: the obligations \(O\) and \(P\) of a repair, with the occasions each covers (Part XI); the scope of a contract and what makes a restriction appropriate (Part III); the contrasts \(K\) of an actual-causation claim (Part IX); the system boundary and continuity of an attribution (Part XII). A verdict that depends on one of these is a verdict given the input; where the input is missing, the verdict is unsettled and the semantics says so rather than choosing the input from the verdict wanted.

**Indices, not primitives.** Grain \(\ell\), boundary \(\beta\), continuity \(\Omega\), and the contract \(C\) are declared indices. Every claim is relative to them; none is a predicate that could be true or false.

**Dependence order.** (O) and (Q) depend on nothing. (CD) and (K) depend on (O) and a contract. (F1), (F2), (A) depend on (O), (Q), (K). (E) depends on those. (S), (B), (D) depend on (E). (R) depends on (F1)–(F2) and \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\). (AC) depends on histories and declared contrasts. (K1) depends on (E). Deploy depends on (R) and (CT1). Find depends on histories and (E). (N), (G) depend on Deploy and Find. (P), (CK) depend on (G), (E), (AC), Deploy. (RC), (U1)–(U3) depend on all of the above. Nothing depends on a predicate meaning "is a cause," "really produces," or "is knowledge." The order is well founded: a representation justified only by the model that asserts it, or an ownership and a capability justified only by each other, has not supplied its place in it, and a separate proof that would supply it counts only when the account uses it.

**Membership.** The base class: interpretations supplying these data with correct typing, satisfying physical realization wherever a physical attribution is made. The creative-episode class: base interpretations with an instance of (G) connected to a critical episode. The knowledge-creation class: an instance of (CK). The recursive class: (RC). The universal class: (U3). A single originative act does not place its author in the universal class.

# Part XV — What defeats this class

The load-bearing claims, in the order of how much falls if they fail, each with what would refute it. None is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label.

**(A) Sufficiency.** A candidate meeting all four conditions of (E) on a physically admitted contract that plainly states no cause. Part V says which of the four each classic attempt fails: a table of observed answers fails (F1); a reversed calculation fails (F2) under the production contract; a collateral effect offered as a cause fails (F1) under a contract that sets it; conclusion-as-premise fails non-circular dependence. A table that encodes the response to every admitted change fails none and is an account, so it is not a counterexample; a new attempt must fail none and still state no cause.

**(B) Necessity.** A genuine causal fact whose organization no transport can preserve under any physically admitted contract. Causation by absence and prevention (Part VII) are the exposed cases.

**(C) Reinstatement of kinds.** A case where a label ("cause", "law", "mere correlate") distinguishes two accounts that no admitted change distinguishes, and the distinction does causal work. This refutes Derivation 1 and reinstates a primitive of causation.

**(D) Direction.** A case in which the direction of a causal dependence is fixed by something other than which component each admitted edit replaces: two organizations with the same admitted edits and the same solution sets at every pair of the contract, differing in causal direction, where the difference does causal work. A case where the physics admits settings on both ends and the derived direction is mutual is the theorem's own report, not a refutation (Derivation 3).

**(E) Actual causation.** A fully stated history and set of contrasts on which (AC) and a thoughtful reading of the history differ: a pre-empted route counted as active, an active route counted as at rest, or an absence the contrasts admit and the history joins to the result that (AC) leaves out. A case whose verdict turns on a contrast the statement omits is a case with a missing declared input, not a refutation.

**A mathematical error.** A counterexample to the finite monotone theorem, (I2), (O1), (T2), (CT2), or Derivations 1–4 under their stated assumptions.

# Part XVI — Derivations

## 1. Kind preservation is a theorem, not a condition

**Claim.** If a transport satisfies (F1) on \(C\), then every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has the same signature on \(\tau[C]\) as its anchor \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation.

*Proof.* By (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\) and \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)=\{(\tau(a),\sigma(b),L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)))\}\). (F1) equates the third coordinates pointwise. ∎

**Corollary.** A condition "each component must anchor to a component of the same kind" adds nothing to (F1) on any contract. Where the contract separates two kinds, (F1) already distinguishes them; where it does not, they are one kind on that contract. The word "kind" is therefore eliminable from the definition of an account, and its elimination loses no case. In particular "each component offered as a cause must anchor to a cause" adds nothing: a causal assignment's signature is a pattern in (K), and (F1) carries it across.

## 2. Indistinguishable is identical

**Claim.** Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide.

*Proof.* Immediate from Derivation 1 and (A). ∎

**Consequence.** Underdetermination of an account by a contract is not a failure of the semantics to decide; it is the semantics reporting that the contract does not contain the distinction. The remedy is a finer contract, which is a new question. A claim that two such candidates "really" differ, that one has the cause and the other the correlate, is a claim that some admitted change separates them, and must supply it (Part VII, "Common cause and chain").

## 3. Direction is derived

**Claim.** Let \(D\) contain a component \(j_v\) assigning \(v\) with \(u\in V_{j_v}\), and a component \(j_u\) assigning \(u\) with \(v\notin V_{j_u}\), and let \(C\) contain edits that set \(u\) and edits that set \(v\), with a boundary on which \(L_{j_v}\) is nonconstant in \(u\). Then \(v\) depends causally on \(u\) on \(C\), and \(u\) does not depend causally on \(v\) on \(C\).

*Proof.* An edit setting \(u\) to \(x\) replaces \(j_u\) by the constant relation \(u=x\) and leaves \(j_v\) as it was (Part II, (O)). Two such edits with different \(x\) give solution sets whose projections to \(v\) differ, because \(L_{j_v}\) is nonconstant in \(u\); so (CD) holds for \((u,v)\). An edit setting \(v\) replaces \(j_v\) and leaves \(j_u\) as it was; \(j_u\) does not mention \(v\), so the projection to \(u\) is unchanged across two such edits; so (CD) fails for \((v,u)\). ∎

**Consequence.** The asymmetry of causation is the asymmetry between the component an edit replaces and the components it leaves in place. No order of time is used. Where \(j_u\) does mention \(v\), a cycle, both dependences may hold, and the semantics reports mutual dependence on that contract; where \(C\) contains no edit setting \(v\), the second half of the claim is vacuous and the direction is a claim about a finer contract. Time, where the physics supplies it, enters as a restriction on which edits are admitted (Part XII), and the derived direction then follows it as a consequence, not an assumption.

## 4. A tested model is underdetermined where an admitted rival agrees on the tests

**Claim.** Let \(t\) be a transport with contract \(C\) whose fidelity has been checked on a finite tested history \(H\subsetneq C\). Let \(\mathcal T\) be the set of transports the physics and the stated construction admit. For every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) at which some \(t'\in\mathcal T\), faithful on \(H\), has a different value from \(t\), the value of \(t\) at \((a,b)\) is underdetermined by \(H\): fidelity on \(H\) does not distinguish \(t\) from \(t'\) there. The presence of an untested pair alone does not establish that such a \(t'\) exists; it must be admitted, realizable, and faithful on \(H\).

*Proof.* The component relations \(L_j(a,b)\) are supplied independently for each \((a,b)\). Fidelity on \(H\) constrains only \(\{L_j(a,b):(a,b)\in H\}\). Where \(\mathcal T\) contains a transport with \(L_j(a,b)\) altered to another admitted relation for one \((a,b)\notin H\), that transport is faithful on \(H\) and differs at \((a,b)\), and fidelity on \(H\) cannot select between the two. Where \(\mathcal T\) contains no such transport, \(H\) is silent on the value at \((a,b)\) and the admitted set fixes it. ∎

**Consequence.** A causal model is established where it was tested and, wherever the physics and the stated construction admit an alternative, unconstrained where it was not. This is why a causal model is fallible and why surprise is possible. A physical relation, a stated construction, or another stated constraint may already fix a value that the tests never touched; the semantics records which, and supplies neither the guarantee that every untested value is open nor the guarantee that it is fixed.

## 5. Surprise requires an incomplete tested history

**Claim.** A system can be surprised only if it holds a transport checked on a tested history \(H\) strictly smaller than the contract \(C\) of changes the world admits.

*Proof.* Surprise is defined as a violation at \((a,b)\notin H\). If there is no transport there is no expectation and hence no violation. If \(H=C\), every occurring \((a,b)\) is in \(H\), so no violation at \((a,b)\notin H\) exists. ∎

**Consequence.** Surprise is not a feeling added to the semantics; it is the signature of a tested model meeting a change outside its tests. The two responses, extend \(H\) and re-tune, or rebuild the model, are the difference between learning and discovering, and the semantics distinguishes them by their witnesses, not by their outcomes.

## 6. Question-finding is representable

**Claim.** A contract \(C\) is an organization (a set of edits with a query), and can be the content \(c\) in (G). Hence an episode whose originative contribution is a new contract satisfies (G) and, where the other conjuncts hold, (CK).

*Proof.* A contract is a subset of \(A\times B\) together with \(\mathcal Q\). Give it ports (the edits and their boundaries), components (the closure conditions), and admitted edits (add or remove a change; alter \(\mathcal Q\)). It is then a \(D\) in the sense of (O). Deploy, Find, and New apply. ∎

**Consequence.** Finding which changes to admit, which is finding the causal question, is a creative act on the same footing as answering one. A semantics that takes contracts as inputs cannot represent this; the present one does, by letting a contract be a content. That a question was found says nothing about its worth (Part XI).

## 7. There are two primitives

**Claim.** Every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from \(\Theta\) (including \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\) and the precedence of a history) and, where invoked, \(\mathcal N\), together with declared indices and declared inputs.

*Proof.* By the dependence order of Part XIV, following each definition to its base. ∎

**Consequence.** There is no residual predicate meaning "is a cause," "produces," "represents," or "is knowledge." The two primitives are a theory of matter and, when a question requires it, a theory of reasons. Neither is a semantic primitive about causation.

## 8. The frozen assessment and the moving question are consistent

**Claim.** An assessment event with contract \(C\) and an episode in which \(C\) is replaced by \(C'\) with a discovery witness are both representable without contradiction.

*Proof.* The assessment is indexed to \(C\) and its truth is fixed at that index (Part VIII, historical index). The episode is a history in which a contract-content \(C'\) is found (Derivation 6) and takes over operative use. The claim "\(\mathcal E\) is an account on \(C\)" and the claim "\(\mathcal E\) is not an account on \(C'\)" are claims at different indices and may both be true. ∎

**Consequence.** The point of contact between a causal model and the world moves across episodes because contracts are found; it does not move within an assessment because assessments are indexed. Goalpost-moving is the act of changing the index without recording the change, and it is a failure of the record, not a licensed operation of the semantics.

## 9. Equivariance under genuine recoding

**Claim.** Transporting all carriers, relations, transports, histories, and contracts along structure-preserving bijections preserves (E), (AC), (G), (P), (CK). *Proof.* Each is a conjunction of equalities and existence claims over the transported data; bijections preserve them. ∎ The result does not apply to coarsenings, changed boundaries, or lost event identities.

## 10. Output descriptions do not determine accounts

**Claim.** If \(M_0,M_1\) have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim or an actual-causation claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both. *Proof.* Equal inputs to a function give equal outputs. ∎ Parallel and priority wiring are an instance: two circuits with the same truth table, one in which both inputs drive the output and one in which the second is read only when the first is silent, differ in their active routes and agree in every output. The same holds for attribution from emitted text and for use inferred from delivery logs.

## 11. A pre-emption episode, in exact form

Stipulate a target \(D\): ports \(s\) (the first throw: made or withheld), \(b\) (the second throw: made or withheld), \(r_s\) and \(r_b\) (each rock's arrival: at an intact bottle, at a broken bottle, or none), and \(y\) (the bottle: intact or broken). Components: \(j_s\) assigning \(r_s\) from \(s\) and from the state of the bottle at the first rock's arrival time; \(j_b\) likewise for \(r_b\), with the second rock arriving later; \(j_y\) assigning \(y\) as broken exactly when some rock arrives at an intact bottle. Admitted edits: withhold or make either throw; slow either rock; set \(y\) directly. Baseline: both throws made, the first rock arriving first.

**Type level.** On the contract \(C_1\) containing only the edits that withhold one throw with the other held, \(y\) depends causally on neither throw by (CD): withhold \(s\), and the second rock breaks the bottle; withhold \(b\), and the first does. On \(C_1\) the block \(\{s,b\}\) is a critical block in the sense of (B) and neither singleton is. On the contract \(C_2\) that adds withholding both, \(y\) depends causally on the pair. On the contract \(C_3\) that adds slowing the first rock so that it arrives second, the dependence of \(y\) on each throw is conditional on the boundary that fixes arrival order, and the semantics reports it as such. Each is a different question, and the answers agree with the thoughtful reading of each.

**Actual causation.** The history \(h\) at the baseline: the first throw, the first rock's flight, its arrival at an intact bottle, the breaking, the second rock's flight, its arrival at a broken bottle. The declared contrasts \(K\): each throw withheld. The route from \(s\) through \(j_s\) to \(y\) is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences, its components satisfy their relations, and it has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under \(K\): withholding \(s\) removes the arrival at an intact bottle that \(j_y\) reads. It is active. The route from \(b\) through \(j_b\) to \(y\) joins actual occurrences, but its arrival occurs at a bottle already broken, and \(j_y\) reads nothing from it: the route was at rest when the result occurred. It is not active for \(y\). By (AC), the first throw is an actual cause of the breaking and the second is not. No counterfactual about what the second rock would have done is consulted; the verdict is read from what the second rock did.

**Symmetric overdetermination.** Alter the baseline so both rocks arrive at the intact bottle at once. Both routes are active, both throws are actual causes, and (AC) supplies no ranking. A claim that one of them is "the" cause is an invocation of \(\mathcal N\) or of a stated purpose (Part XI).

**Prevention.** Add a port \(w\) (a wall raised or absent between the second thrower and the bottle) with a component that sends the second rock to "none" when the wall is raised. On a contract containing the edit that raises the wall, with the first throw withheld, \(y\) depends causally on \(w\): the wall prevents the breaking. In the history with the wall raised and the first throw withheld, the bottle stays intact and the wall is joined to that result by an active route. In the history with both throws made, the first rock arriving first and the wall raised, the wall's route ends at a rock that had nothing to do, and it is not active for the bottle's state.

This episode is a relative-consistency witness for the class: the type-level dependences, the critical block, the active-route verdicts on pre-emption, overdetermination and prevention, and the derived direction all follow from the stipulated organization and the admitted edits without a primitive of causation. It is not a claim that any actual thrower, bottle or physical system has been shown to instantiate it.
