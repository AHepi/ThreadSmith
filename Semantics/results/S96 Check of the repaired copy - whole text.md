## 2. Physical possibility (S25, S26, S27)

**What no longer turns on physics:** the range of a question (l. 159: "a declared subset of the changes its target admits"), non-vacuity (l. 257), l. 275 (a contrast no edit of the target realizes fails non-circular dependence, and a contrast no one could produce is still a contrast), conflict (l. 315: "relations of the target's components at that pair, each a relation on the component's footprint"; "it does not turn on what any physics admits"), (F1), (F2), (A), and Part XV (A) and (B) ("a contract of its question", "any contract on its target"). The remaining mentions of physics are about carriers, occurrences, histories, selection populations, capability and Part XII (instantiation and transformation), or about the content of a claim (l. 75, l. 315). The builder's key matches each line, apart from P-2 and P-3.

**The three targets.** *Worked example, l. 343:* (F1), (F2) and (A) as before. Non-circular dependence holds at the removal of skewness (\(I_3\)) and of oddness. Non-vacuity: \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), and the target's edits outside the contract (field arithmetic, including a change of characteristic, and the link between determinant and invertibility) are left out by the stated scope ("held fixed"). No conjunct asks for a physical edit. *A melody:* ports are note positions, the query is whether the progression resolves, and changing a note or chord is an edit (l. 49). *A philosophical claim:* premises and distinctions are components, and dropping one is an edit (l. 49). Both have a nonempty baseline and contrasts in their contracts. Neither needs \(\Theta\) except where a carrier holds or performs them.

- **P-1. Conflict with a claim, applied at an idealized edit (l. 315).** The definition says a candidate conflicts with \(\chi\) "when \(\chi\) excludes what the candidate's organization and transport give there". It does not say "as the target's answer or relations under that pair's edit". Contracts now range over edits "not only those anyone could carry out" and changes that "could not come about" (l. 43, l. 159, l. 275). *Failure:* a question asks what a pendulum does with its friction component deleted (an edit the target organization admits). The target's own answer there is a motion that never stops. Every account of that question gives perpetual motion at that pair, so by the wording every account conflicts with "perpetual motion is impossible". The next sentence then says the argument "rules out that the candidate meets (E) for any assessor \(j\) who can use it". The argument needs a premise the text leaves out: that \(\chi\) holds of the target under that edit. The owner's example ("if your explanation inadvertently describes something with the exact same properties as a perpetual motion machine") concerns what an explanation implies about its target, which the repair keeps.
- **P-2. The attribution clause can bring physics back into a physical target's range (l. 75, l. 159, with l. 213 and l. 517).** l. 75 says every attribution of an organization to a physical system is a claim that the system instantiates it, and that "an attribution that the adopted physics excludes conflicts with it". \(\Theta\) supplies \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\) and lists "admitted processes" (l. 517). Nothing says that an organization's admitting an edit is not a claim that the edit can be carried out or can come about. *Failure:* the organization attributed to the Earth–Sun system admits the edit "axis upright" (Claude's example 3, context only). If admitting that edit counts as part of what the attribution claims, the attribution conflicts with a physics that excludes the edit. The range of every question about a physical target is then again bounded by physical possibility, through the attribution. l. 159 ("does not by itself put a change in a contract or keep it out") speaks of the contract, not of the target's set \(A\).
- **P-3. \(\mathfrak E_\Theta\), l. 497.** "the explanatory contents" carries the index \(\Theta\), which reads as if the physical module fixes which contents are explanatory. Universality concerns which contents a system can hold and deploy (instantiation), so the index belongs to the holding. Minor.

## 3. Broken definitions, references and arguments; S95 B1 to B15

**S95 breaks.** B1 (l. 8, l. 397, \(X_j\)), B2 (l. 315, l. 369), B3, B4 (widened for conflict with a claim), B5, B6 (with "physically admitted" removed), B7 (l. 393, l. 522, l. 526, l. 598), B8, B9, B10, B11 (l. 31, l. 526, l. 600), B13, B14 and B15 are applied as the results file worded them. That is harmless.

- **B-1. \(\operatorname{Live}_j\) makes every argument usable (l. 393, with l. 315 and l. 522). The most serious item.** "\(\operatorname{Live}_j(d;u)\): \(j\) has not withdrawn \(d\)". A premise \(j\) never took up has not been withdrawn, so it is live. "Ruled out for \(j\)" is existential over arguments usable by \(j\) (l. 315). *Failure:* for any contingent \(\psi\), the premises \(r\) and "if \(r\), not \(\psi\)" (neither of them \(\psi\)'s denial, read structurally) with modus ponens rule out \(\psi\) for every \(j\) who admits that form. The same construction rules out \(\neg\psi\). "Not ruled out", "a problem for \(p\)" and S21's two candidates that "both may survive" then never occur, and any \(\chi\) whatever gives a live conflict. S27's own case ("the agent accepted it as a given") needs tentative acceptance, not mere non-withdrawal, and l. 317 already says "a claim the assessor tentatively accepts". The repair must also keep the conclusions of earlier steps usable as premises of later ones.
- **B-2. "Conflict given \(\chi\)" is defined and used nowhere (l. 315, l. 317, l. 526).** Rivals, problems and the dependence order use outright conflict only. *Failure:* two candidates offered one in place of the other that only a physically excluded arrangement would let both meet (F1), (F2) and (A). For an assessor who tentatively accepts the excluding claim, at most one of them can meet (E), yet they are no one's rivals and pose no problem for \(p\). The builder's open point 5 (outright conflict is narrower) is where this comes from. The narrowing follows S25; the missing link to rivals and problems does not.
- **B-3. The denial block says more than it catches (l. 397).** "What a stated premise cannot do is stand in for the steps." The block reads premises structurally, so a conditional premise gets round it: take "if \(r\), this candidate fails (E)" and \(r\) as given, and one step of modus ponens rules the candidate out. Under S27 that is a premise taken as given, and using it is the owner's "costly gamble", not a circular argument. The text should say that the block catches only the premise that is the denial. Also, "it rules nothing out for anyone" is wider than the definition, which blocks only that claim: an argument with \(\neg\psi\) among its premises can still rule out another claim whose denial is not among them.
- **B-4. Inconsistent premises taken as given (l. 397; PLAUSIBLE).** Once B-1 is repaired, an assessor whose tentatively accepted premises are inconsistent still has every claim and its denial ruled out, through forms such as disjunction introduction and disjunctive syllogism, if \(j\) admits them. S27's premises taken "without any thought whatsoever" make this an expected case, not a rare one. The text is silent on it. The sentence proposed in section 5 names the case and leaves the choice with the person, as S21 requires.
- **B-5. "meets (E) at it", l. 49.** (E) is defined on a contract, not at an edit: (F1), (F2) and (A) are pointwise, while non-circular dependence and non-vacuity are not.
- **B-6. "An account whose only substantive contrast … fails non-circular dependence", l. 275.** An account meets (E) by definition, so the sentence contradicts itself. The subject should be a candidate. The wording is older than S96 ("An account … fails non-vacuity" in the scrubbed copy), but S96 rewrote the sentence.
- **B-7. "outside \(C\) it rules out what the candidate's organization gives there", l. 315.** What \(E\) gives is a fact about \(E\). What the argument rules out is that the target gives it there.
