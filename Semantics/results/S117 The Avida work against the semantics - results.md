## 1. The direct answer to S69: if, and how

**If.** In part, and the part is sharply bounded. Of the 284 units of the semantics, 114 line up exactly with something in the Avida work, 36 in part, 18 do not line up, and 116 have nothing in Avida. Of the 116 with nothing, 31 are the text's own worked examples and 18 are about the text's own wording, so they say nothing about Avida either way; the other 67 are things the semantics needs that Avida does not have.

**How.** The line runs between the semantics' two halves:

- **The structural half lines up, often exactly and with computed numbers**: an Avida program is, exactly, an organization in the semantics' sense; Avida's tasks are questions (of the declared kind); kinds come apart under finer changes as the semantics says (computed, C2); a selected correspondence is left open at changes its population never met (Argument 3, computed on Avida's own populations in 16 of 18 runs, C3); critical blocks with no critical single part, interference, parts that do no work (S112); keeping a capability (CT1), ownership and continuity relative to a declared boundary (S111); tasks in constructor theory's sense.
- **The explanatory half has nothing in Avida**: no problems, arguments, criticism, rivals offered, recognized difficulties, construction, origin, deployment or created explanation; no layer that predicts, so no surprise; no recursion or universality. Every unit of that half either has no counterpart or fails a condition on the nearest one.
- **At the hinge, the semantics' own answer flips on a reading it leaves open** (break B1): whether an evolved program's correspondence to its task is "selected" (and so represents the task, and, as one block, passes as an explanation) or "declared" (represents nothing, no explanation) depends on whether Avida's task-checking code, written by people, counts as part of the history that selected the program. Computed both ways in a copy of the model (MC1).
- **Three specific mismatches** where Avida has the thing but it behaves differently: Avida's instruction ablation is not the semantics' deletion (B2); Avida's survival does not require doing the task (B4); Avida's simulated world performs exactly, which the semantics' physics never does (B8).
- **The Avida work's own answer fails the semantics' test**: S112's circuit, offered as "what that evolved information actually is", gets every single-ablation answer right in only 8.8 in 100 program-task pairs (C1, B3).

So the Avida work lines up with the semantics where the semantics describes structure, change and selection, and not where it describes explanation; and the one place where Avida could have told the two apart for the semantics (is an evolved program an explanation of its task?) is where the semantics' answer depends on a reading not yet made. This is consistent with what the earlier record suspected (S110's change map, FW3/FW4): physical knowledge and explanatory knowledge may be of different types; Avida instantiates the first, and the semantics, a theory of explanation, reaches it only through its selection and representation definitions.

## 2. How the line-up was done

As the plan says (sections 2 to 6): 284 units (118 definitions D0.1 to D18.2, 9 encodings E1 to E9, 142 formal claims after round 4, 15 named terms of the text, T01 to T15); for each, the Avida counterpart was named first, then its conditions checked, computed where the raw output or analyze mode allowed, argued from the text and the Avida records where not; alternatives are reported (column "alternatives"). Units that share one reason share one "profile" (`tools/s117_the_line_up_unit_by_unit.py`), so the reason is written once; a unit adds its own note where needed.

**The two readings** used in section 5 and marked "R":

- **Reading A**: the code in Avida that checks a task (and with it the task list) is not an occurrence, in the history that selected a program, that represents the survival condition or the task; it belongs to the world's rules.
- **Reading B**: that code is such an occurrence: written by people, it computes the task, so it represents the task and the survival condition, earlier than the program that holds the correspondence.

The text does not settle which: D12.1 (formal core line 454) excludes a selection history in which "a member of the history represents t, H, or the survival condition", the reading of "member" as occurrences is an invention (I52), and h(t) is "the history that produced that holding, its preparing episode included" (I167). Units whose verdict turns on this are counted under LINES UP IN PART (the semantics' answer for Avida flips), with both readings' answers given; this is a departure from the plan's wording (section 10).

## 4. The main breaks: what does not work, and why

Each with the semantics' piece, the Avida counterpart, what happens, why, a concrete example and the technical references. The plain versions are in the map page.

**B1. Whether an evolved program "stands for" its task, and counts as an explanation, flips on one unsettled reading.** Semantics: D12.1 Sel (selected: population, variation, finite history, survival requiring fidelity on it, and no earlier occurrence representing t, H, the survival condition or cod t), D12.5 (R), D16.XV (Suff: Acc(ℰ) ∧ ¬Dec(t) and an argument not using (E) that rules out Expl(ℰ) defeats sufficiency; Acc ∧ Dec ⇒ ¬Expl, S41). Avida: the most common NOT program of run low seed 2 (S112; 110 instructions; 44 copies), which hands back nand(x, x). Computed in a copy of the model (MC1, one bit, exact for bitwise NOT): as one block, (E) holds, every conjunct true (F1, F2, (A), Dependence, NonVacuous; no slot). Reading A: the model's `sel` holds, `con` fails, Dec false; for an assessor who takes as given that the program is no explanation, (Suff) is defeated under L536 and under L17 as S41 writes it. Reading B: `sel` fails (the history holds an occurrence representing `surv` and `cod`), Dec true; (Suff) not defeated, and S41's rule gives ¬Expl, which agrees with the owner's reading of Avida as the knowledge kind, with "the explanation kind comes next" (S59, S60), and with S111's observation that nothing in the programs refers to where the numbers come from. Why: in Avida the target is written down in advance by people; whether that counts as "in the history" is not settled by the text. Units: D12.1, D12.3, D12.5, D16.XV, FC30.new1, T10, D11.4, FC75 (the eight units marked R).

**B2. Avida's ablation is not the semantics' deletion.** Semantics: D1.3 (a deleted component imposes the full relation on its ports), D6.4's loss clause, FC01 (deletion never removes a solution), FC06 (a setting edit leaves every reader's relation as it was), L103. Avida: instruction ablation (nop-X in place of the instruction; S111, S112). Computed (MC4): at a fine grain with the read pointer as a port, deleting an extra read leaves the answer undetermined (⊥) at the two input pairs where the two numbers differ, where Avida's ablation gives a determined, different answer; at the coarse grain of S112's circuits (each read takes its own number), deleting it changes nothing, where ablation changes the answer. S112: ablating an unused read at instruction 45 of an EQU program made the reads at 47, 53 and 66 receive other numbers; in 85 of 89 hard cases a second ablation restored the copying a first had stopped (non-monotone, against what FC01 proves of deletion). Why: ablation is an admitted edit that replaces a component with another (L103: "A changed rule is a changed component"), not a deletion. Consequence: the owner's "required instruction" is the semantics' contrast pair (D6.4's first clause: a pair of C at which the answer differs from the baseline), not a dependence block.

## 7. The reverse list: what Avida shows that the semantics has no term for

1. **Copying itself**: a content whose own instructions make copies of it (S111: 15 of 100). The semantics names copying only as a transformation of a content (Part XII).
2. **Withstanding change by arrangement**: 69 to 79 in 100 one-instruction changes leave an evolved program copying; more copying mistakes, less loss (S111). The semantics can compute a boundary over a family of changes (D7.4) but has no term for how much of the family a thing withstands.
3. **Lasting by out-copying removal** (S111 section 5).
4. **A graded advantage** (Avida fitness, a rate); the semantics has no score or ordering (L25).
5. **How widespread a capability is** ("present", "common"); no population-level quantity in the semantics.
6. **Pay that falls as more programs use it** (common sums pay less; S113, S116).
7. **An order in which questions are posed** (the growing list ended with more than the all-77 environment; S113). The semantics records a question's origin (D3.4), not the order of declared questions.
8. **What a program can become next** (S115 reply 4): separated by Argument 9, but unnamed.
9. **A cost borne for another** (S115 reply 2, S116).

Four of these are the owner's three properties and their conditions (1, 2, 3; and 4, how the world chooses): the semantics, a theory of explanation, has no terms for them, which is what S110's change map recorded of the present theory ("What it did not keep: the word 'knowledge'; any relation for constructor theory's knowledge").
