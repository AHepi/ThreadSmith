*Log S127, 2 October 2026 (decisions S80, S81). The same agent, rules and citations as the companion file, `results/S127 The Avida findings turned back on the semantics - the hinge.md` (its header applies here). Writing only: no Avida run, no outside model, no GLM check (S70). Avida in the owner's terms (S61). **Nothing in the theory is changed**: every wording below is marked as a proposal for the owner.*

## 0. What this file does, and the answer in short

S117 (results, section 7) listed nine things the Avida work shows that the semantics has no term for. S81 asks, for each, whether the theory needs one. The test used here is what the semantics is for: a theory of explanation (what makes a candidate an account of a question, where a correspondence comes from, what creating an explanation is), with a physical module that says what carriers instantiate and what transformations they can undergo, and with constructor theory taken as given for that module (Part XII, L461). Three verdicts:

- **NEEDED**: the theory cannot state something it must, given its own definitions;
- **USEFUL**: a name or sentence would sharpen a definition or claim it already makes;
- **OUT OF SCOPE**: it belongs to the physics of replicators or to the study of selection, not to explanation.

S125 added nothing to the list in so many words (its files hold no reverse list); two of its findings are considered as candidates in section 2 and fold into items 2 and 4.

**The owner's three properties** (S59: can cause itself to be copied, to resist change, to remain) are items 1, 2 and 3. Plainly: **the semantics, as it stands, has no terms for them.** It has no word "knowledge" at all (S110's change map: the present theory did not keep it). Should it? Not as new primitives: they are constructor theory's, and the owner's S57 says the definitions of information and knowledge are already there. But two of the three can be *defined* from terms the semantics already has (owned capability, retained realization, continuity, a declared family of changes), and the third's property, as distinct from its mechanism, is already expressible; giving them names, and saying in one place that information whose carriers have all three is what the physical module calls knowledge, would let the theory state what selection, representation and created explanation add to it. That last sentence brings the word "knowledge" into the text, which is the owner's decision (what knowledge is stays open: S125, section 8, item 3).

## 1. Item by item

### 1. Copying itself

**What Avida showed.** Avida's default ancestor, 100 instructions, run alone in the test processor, makes an exact copy of itself, executing 389 instructions per copy; replacing each instruction in turn by one that does nothing, 15 of the 100 replacements stop exact self-copying (the instructions that ask for room, find the ends, copy, check for the end and split, and the labels that steer them); 0 of 10,000 random programs of length 100 copy themselves (S111, C1, C2, control K1). What copies is the program's instructions executed by the world's processor: S111 split "causes itself" between the program and the world, which S117 found is the semantics' ownership relative to a declared boundary (D13.7, EXACT).

**Does the semantics need a term?** USEFUL, not NEEDED. Copying appears in the semantics only as a transformation of a content (L461: "holding, copying, teaching, testing, building or performing it") and as relay, which keeps provenance (D12.3, D12.4). Selection needs a variation operator μ (D12.1), and the semantics does not care who performs it. No claim of Parts V to XV fails for want of the term. But the owner's first property can be stated exactly with what the semantics has: an owned capability (D15.5, L475) for a task whose output is a carrier of the system's own organization. A name defined that way costs no import and gives the property a place.

**Where.** Part XII, after "Owned capability" (L475); formal core D15.5 (core line 598).

**Proposed wording (a proposal).** *"A system **copies itself**, under a declared boundary and continuity, when it has an owned capability (Can) for a task whose output attribute is a carrier of its own organization at the declared grain."*

**What it would change.** Avida's programs copy themselves in the semantics' own terms, relative to the boundary that counts the program's instructions as the system's and the world's processor as an enabling condition (S111's split); a program whose copying the world did for it would not. Nothing in (E), provenance or (EX) changes.

**What could count against it.** Constructor theory's own account of self-reproduction separates the information copied from the machinery that copies it; a definition by ownership may draw the line elsewhere. Every name added is prose, which S40 asks to avoid. S117's proposal 2 (D12.4's "a binding newly built gets Con" read literally would give a part made by a copying mistake constructed provenance; "newly built by a construction trace" keeps it with selection) is the one place where copying already touches a definition, and it stands as S117 left it.

### 2. Withstanding change by arrangement

**What Avida showed.** Of every program differing in one instruction from the most common program of each run at update 50,000, 0.698 (low copy error rate), 0.754 (default) and 0.758 (high) still copy themselves; 0.169, 0.214 and 0.312 are also unchanged in fitness; the ancestor's share is 0.812 (S111, R1, R2). Programs evolved under more copying errors lose less to a single change (neutral share higher at the high rate at all three moments). Instructions needed for copying stay put far more than the rest (0.836 against 0.178 at the low rate; 54 of 54 saves). **Nothing in the program repairs anything** (S111): what buffers change is the arrangement of instructions, and what keeps needed instructions unchanged is the world removing programs that cannot copy. S125 (correction 28, from Astra's reply 03) adds that tolerating variation is not the error correction constructor theory puts at the centre of accurate copying.

**Does the semantics need a term?** USEFUL for the property; OUT OF SCOPE for the share. The semantics can already compute where a family of changes breaks an account (Boundary, D7.4, L302) and which parts do no work (D7.6, L313), and S111's 85 removable instructions are its "commitments that do no work" (S117, FC41). What it has no name for is a capability kept under a family of changes to its carrier. That is definable from retained realization (CT1, D15.2, L463) applied to each changed carrier. The share (69 to 79 in 100) is a count and a grading, and the semantics supplies no measure of that kind (L25; S20 for hard to vary); a share belongs to the study of mutational robustness. Two things worth stating: (a) withstanding change is the reverse of what makes an explanation hard to vary (every part doing work); a program that withstands change has many parts that do no work. (b) Active error correction of copying can be stated with the semantics' Repair (P) (L435; D14.1, D14.2), taking "copies itself exactly" as the claimed aim; Avida shows none.

**Where.** Part XII after L475, with item 1; D15.2, D15.5; a pointer to D7.4.

**Proposed wording (a proposal).** *"A capability **withstands** a declared family 𝒱 of changes to its carrier when, for each member of 𝒱, the changed carrier keeps a retained realization of the task (CT1). Withstanding a family is not repairing: a repair of the capability, if any, is a Repair (Part XI) whose claimed aim is the capability."*

**What it would change.** The owner's second property gets a definition that separates S111's two senses (the program's behaviour kept under changes; the instructions kept unchanged by the world's removal) and keeps tolerance apart from error correction, as S125 asked. No verdict on (E), provenance or (EX) changes.

**What could count against it.** Whether the owner's "resist change" means tolerance or active error correction is open (S125, section 8, item 3); under the second reading the definition names the wrong thing, and Repair carries the owner's sense. A definition relative to a declared family 𝒱 makes "withstands" as relative as the family chosen; one-instruction changes are one choice among many.

### 3. Lasting by out-copying removal

**What Avida showed.** All nine populations lasted 50,000 updates, never fewer than 3,481 programs; every program descends from the one injected ancestor over 6,310 to 15,940 generations; no program identical to the ancestor remained; 65% to 86% (condition means) could still copy themselves exactly (S111, M1 to M3). With death by age switched off (K3) a knocked-out ancestor that cannot copy remained for all 2,000 updates; beside the intact one, with nothing dying of age (K4), it was gone by update 500, its cell taken by the intact one's offspring. So in Avida, what makes remaining depend on copying is the world's removal rule; the program's part is to be copied faster than it is removed (S111, section 4).

**Does the semantics need a term?** OUT OF SCOPE for the mechanism. The balance of copying and removal is population dynamics, the physics of replicators. The property, "it remains", is already expressible: Part XII's declared continuity Ω says what makes a system the same through change (L473; D15.4, core line 594), and the lineage that remained while no program did is a system that remained under the continuity "descends from the injected ancestor". Nothing the semantics claims turns on how a lineage persists.

**Where.** Nothing to add; if item 1's proposal is taken, its last sentence (section 3 below) uses continuity for this property.

### 4. A graded advantage

**What Avida showed.** Avida fitness is a rate of copying that decides which program spreads, not a pass-or-fail test. Doing a task pays more running time; it is not required. In the environment that pays for nothing, run 1: 3,600 programs at update 50,000, 11 doing NOT; in the fixed list, run 1: 3,595 programs, 2,290 doing NOT (S117, B4 and C3). Every population of S113 lasted 50,000 updates in every environment, the one paying nothing included. Only a copying rule that requires solving something (S118's "copy only after solving something") or Avida's required tasks make a task a condition of copying.

**Does the semantics need a term?** **NEEDED**, in one place. Selected provenance asks for "a survival condition requiring fidelity on H" (L195), "enacted by the environment" (L481). As the companion file sets out (section 5), on a strict reading of "requiring", the programs of every graded-pay run in the record are declared under every reading of the hinge, and so is every adaptation of a living species, whose advantage is also graded; on the formal core's reading (surv := fidelity on H and an environmental condition given as none, I177), the 11 programs doing NOT in the run that paid for nothing would count as selected for NOT, though nothing in their history made NOT matter. Neither can be what the text means, and the text cannot say what it means without a word for a graded advantage. Without it, the semantics cannot apply its own definition of selection to nature or to most of Avida (S125, correction 15). The word is not a score on explanations: L25 ("a function that orders explanations or thinkers") is untouched, because the ordering is a physical fact about copying, stated in the physical module.

**Where.** Part IV, Selected (L195); Part XII, Selection in the physical module (L481); formal core D12.1's surv and Env (core line 454; I177), D15.8 (core line 610).

**Proposed wording (a proposal).** L195: "a survival condition requiring fidelity on H" becomes *"a survival condition under which fidelity on H changes which members persist or are copied, at each stage of the history (a requirement is one such condition; a higher rate of copying for members faithful on H than for members not faithful on H is another)"*. L481, at the end: *"An environment that copies some members faster than others enacts such a condition; that is a claim about the physical history, not an ordering of the transports by the semantics."* Formal core: surv(t, H) :⟺ Faithful_H(t) ∧ Env_{h(t)}, with Env_{h(t)} the requirement, read through Θ, that in h(t) members of 𝒯 faithful on H are copied or persist at a higher rate than members that are not, at the stages where the pairs of H occur.

**What it would change.** Under Reading A or C of the hinge, the programs of the graded-pay runs become selected wherever doing the task raised their rate of copying, and the programs doing NOT in the run that paid for nothing do not (nothing made NOT matter there): S117's D12.1 moves from IN PART to EXACT (companion file, section 4). Adaptations of living species become selected in the semantics' sense, as the text's picture of the object layer assumes (Part IV; grievance 6). Argument 3's step is untouched: it holds under any condition on values at H (FC80.new1 (b)), and the comparison above reads only values at H.

**What could count against it.** (a) "At a higher rate" needs a comparison across the population, which no definition of the semantics makes now; it is a claim read through Θ, like Occurs and Attempt (D0.2), and adds one more such primitive. (b) Linked correspondences: in Avida new task circuits share required instructions with earlier ones (S113), so a correspondence can rise because a linked one is paid; the wording would call it selected unless "fidelity on H changes which members are copied" is read as a difference made by that fidelity itself, which needs the semantics' own edit-and-compare machinery applied to the population. (c) It touches FROZEN material if Part A's freeze is read as including L195 (S108); the owner's word decides whether that matters now that Part B is held.

### 5. How widespread a capability is

**What Avida showed.** S113 counts a capability "present" when at least one program performs it and "common" when at least one in ten does: with the growing list, 65, 48 and 55 tasks common at update 50,000 and 74, 67 and 72 present (S113, section 0). S125's refined hypothesis speaks of an arrangement "held across the program population", because a capability done in two places stops only under a pair of ablations.

**Does the semantics need a term?** OUT OF SCOPE. A share of a population is a statistic of selection. What the semantics needs about many carriers it has: provenance is assigned per holding (I167), a copy is relay keeping its source's provenance (D12.3), and redundancy across places is its routes and critical blocks (D7.2, D7.3; S117, MC3). No claim of the semantics turns on how many carriers hold a content.

### 6. Pay that falls as more programs use it

**What Avida showed.** In COMMON TASKS PAY LESS each of the nine two-input tasks is paid from a store that runs down: 17, 9 and 8 tasks common at update 50,000, against 7, 8 and 8 under fixed graded pay (S113, section 0). In the example of plain file 125, about 3,200 programs did NOT and the NOT store stood near 270, against the 10,000 it would refill to if nobody used it (S125).

**Does the semantics need a term?** OUT OF SCOPE. An aim whose worth depends on how many already meet it is frequency-dependent selection, a matter of the environment's economics. The semantics takes aims as declared inputs (D14.1) and worth as the appraisal relation (Part XI), and orders nothing. One consequence for item 4: the survival condition is then not one fixed condition through the history, so item 4's wording says "at each stage of the history". That is folded into item 4, not a term of its own.

### 7. An order in which questions are posed

**What Avida showed.** The growing list, which added harder tasks as easier ones became common, ended with 65, 48 and 55 tasks common, against 32, 41 and 0 (13 by the test processor in seed 3) for the fixed large list with all 77 paid from the start; with EQU alone paid, no program ever did EQU, while with the easier tasks also paid EQU became common in all three seeds (S113, section 0).

**Does the semantics need a term?** OUT OF SCOPE. The effect is one of search dynamics (what a population can reach depends on the order its tests come in). The semantics already records the order: a history has a causal precedence (D11.3, L375), the pairs of H are pairs that occurred in h(t) (D12.1), and an episode records every change of contract with its provenance (D13.8). It makes no claim that would need the order named: H enters Sel as a set, and Argument 3 is about what H leaves open, whatever the order. The questions Avida poses are declared (S113, section 7; D3.4); a system that posed itself questions in an order would be finding questions, which the semantics represents as constructed contracts (Argument 5), and nothing in Avida does that.

### 8. What a program can become next

**What Avida showed.** S115's reply 4: two hand-made founders with the same copying and NOT at the same speed; after the same single instruction change, one does NAND and the other nothing; S115 reran it to the last digit (Experiment A matched byte for byte). S117 found the semantics separates them by Argument 9 (two systems with one output can be different organizations, FC101, EXACT), but names nothing for the difference. S125 corrections 17 and 18 add that success at a case where actual survivors disagree is reach not forced by the history, and that "more often than the survivors allow" needs a sampling model the semantics does not give.

**Does the semantics need a term?** USEFUL. The thing is already in the formalism without a name: the variation operator μ: 𝒯 → P(𝒯) (D12.1), and μ(t) is exactly what t can become next. It is used once, in the selection response (D12.8: t' ∈ μ⁺(t)), and implicitly in Argument 10's "structural, not parametric" failure (L626). Argument 3's Consequence says what H leaves open "the population fixes", where 𝒯 is every transport the physics and the stated construction admit (D15.8); the Avida computations (S117, C3) and Astra's checked readings (S125) used the members actually present. The two differ, and the text does not separate them.

**Where.** Part IV, Selected (L195); Argument 3's Consequence (L576); formal core D12.8 (core line 492), D12.9 (core line 496), D15.8.

**Proposed wording (a proposal).** After Argument 3's Consequence: *"Two transports that agree at every pair of C can differ in their **variants**, μ(t): what one change of the population's variation makes of each (Argument 9, for variation). A selection response from t reaches only μ⁺(t). The members of 𝒯 present at a stage of h(t) are those the history has produced so far; what fixes a value at an unseen pair is 𝒯 for the claim of Argument 3, and the members present for what a given history did."*

**What it would change.** The semantics could state the founders' difference (equal on C, different variants), say of Argument 10 that the failure is structural because no member of μ⁺(t₀) survives the extended history, and keep S125's "reach not forced by the history" apart from what the admitted population allows. No verdict on (E), provenance or (EX) changes.

**What could count against it.** What a program can become depends on μ, which is the physical module's, so the name is relative to the variation the physics admits, and adds nothing to any claim of Parts V to XV beyond D12.8 and Argument 10. Evolvability is a topic of its own in the study of selection, and a name here could be read as taking a side in it.

### 9. A cost borne for another

**What Avida showed.** S115's reply 2 and S116: programs that give running time to a neighbour at a cost to themselves, choosing by cues; after 5,000 updates "reject all" was the most common rule in every run (76% to 88%), 9% to 20% of programs still gave to some cues and not to others, and the founder's rule fell from every program to 2% to 6% (S116, B3a).

**Does the semantics need a term?** OUT OF SCOPE. One system's cost for another's benefit belongs to social evolution and to the resources of a system (Part XII's resource contract). The semantics' nearest terms, contribution from outside a boundary (L427) and the attribution of a repair to contributions (ProducedBy, Part XI), are about who did the work of an explanation, not about cost; nothing in the theory of explanation turns on it.

## 3. The owner's three properties, together

**As the semantics stands, it has no terms for copying itself, resisting change or remaining, and no word "knowledge".** Its nearest relations are representation with a selected history (R) and created explanation (EX) (S110, options O10 and O11).

**Should it?** Not as new definitions of its own, and not as a third import: they belong to constructor theory, which the semantics takes as given for its physical module, and which the owner names as where knowledge is defined (S57). But items 1 and 2 can be defined from owned capability, retained realization and a declared family of changes, and item 3's property from continuity, all already in Part XII. One sentence would then put the owner's definition where the theory can use it (a proposal, and the owner's decision, since it brings in the word):

*"Information whose carriers, in a stated environment, copy themselves, withstand a declared family of changes and remain under a declared continuity is what the physical module calls **knowledge** (Part XII). The semantics does not define knowledge further; a correspondence that such carriers hold may be selected, constructed or declared (Part IV), and only a constructed one can be the originative content of a created explanation (Part XI)."*

**What it would change.** The theory could say, in its own words, what Avida shows (S111: all three properties, in S111's measured sense) and what it does not (S117: no construction, so nothing created), which is the line S80's answer drew between the adaptive and the explanatory kind, and which the companion file's recommendation rests on.

**What could count against it.** S110's two-types result (constructor theory's knowledge as a one-place property of information; explanatory knowledge as a relation with a problem) warns that one sentence may join two things of different type; the sentence keeps them apart by saying only where they meet. Whether "withstand" should require active error correction is open (item 2). The theory's text has had no "knowledge" since the change map S110 recorded, and adding it is a choice about the whole theory, not a repair.
