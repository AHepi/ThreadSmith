| Q21 | \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) is **account-hood at \(C\)**. The stronger grade, **anchored account-hood**, adds four conditions on the anchor map, all stated in the same vocabulary:<br><br>1. **Properness.** Every anchor \(\lambda(k)\) is a proper subnetwork of \(D\), and the anchors of the active components refine a cover of \(D\)'s components: no anchor is the whole target, and no component of \(D\) that some pair in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) touches lies outside every anchor.<br>2. **Resolution.** Whenever two components of \(D\) can be separately varied by pairs in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) with distinct effects on \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\), they lie in distinct anchors. The decomposition of \(E\) is at least as fine as the dependencies the question asks about.<br>3. **No global relation.** No active component's relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it: a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name.<br>4. **World-kind.** Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits. Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.<br><br>Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution. It is not a new primitive and it does not inspect a label.
