## Outside case register

The following fourteen situations are the outside cases used throughout this return; the O4 addition remains the outside author’s stipulated recalibration. No situation has been rewritten to make a proposal pass.

**O1 — Bruno the forecaster**

Written by: an outside author

> Bruno sells a rule for predicting when the village stream floods. It fails in March, and he restates it as a rule for months outside the thaw. It fails on a dry-month Tuesday after a dam release upstream, and he restates it for days with no dam release. It fails again, and he restates it for days when the wind is from the west. Each new limit is described in ordinary terms anyone can check, with no mention of whether his rule succeeds. Each restated rule has so far been right inside its limits. He records every earlier failure openly.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> Bruno has no explanation of the floods. Each limit is honest taken alone, and the series as a whole is a retreat.

**O2 — Carla’s tide almanac and bell**

Written by: an outside author

> Carla explains the harbour's tides with two parts. Part one is the printed almanac of high and low water for every day. Part two is a true local mechanism: when the float in the harbour gauge rises past a mark, it tips a lever and the harbour bell rings. She offers both together as her account of why the water is high at noon today. Take the bell mechanism away and what her account establishes does change, since it no longer says the bell will ring. The bell mechanism is physically real and independently grounded.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> Carla has explained the bell. She has explained nothing about the tide. The almanac is the answer written down.

**O3 — Nadia’s unseen cards**

Written by: an outside author

> Nadia learns a card game only by watching cards being laid on the table. She is never shown a hidden hand and nobody tells her hands exist. For weeks she predicts the next card from the cards just played, and is often surprised. One evening she starts keeping a tally of cards that have yet to appear, and treats them as "waiting somewhere". Her predictions improve sharply, and she can now say why a player with few cards left must play a certain suit.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> Nadia built something new: a part that stands for cards she has never observed. That is construction, and it is her own.

**O4 — Dario’s somnic index**

Written by: an outside author

> Dario explains why a certain syrup puts people to sleep: it has a high somnic index. He defines the somnic index carefully and without mentioning people at all. It is the reading on a standard instrument that times how quickly a mouse stops moving after a measured dose. Syrups with a high index do put people to sleep, and ones with a low index do so less. Lower the index by diluting the syrup, and people stay awake longer.

Round 4 added fact, unchanged:

> a technician can recalibrate the instrument so that every syrup reads ten points higher, and people who take the syrup sleep exactly as before.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> Dario has a useful measure and a reliable prediction. He has said almost nothing about why the syrup causes sleep. The index is the sleepiness itself, observed in a mouse.

**O5 — Greta’s dough**

Written by: an outside author

> Greta's rule says her bread dough doubles in two hours. It fails in January. She restates it for rooms warmer than twenty degrees, and her account says why: the yeast that makes the gas works slowly in the cold. Replace her limit with "on days other than Tuesdays" and her account has nothing to say about why Tuesdays would matter.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> This is a legitimate narrowing. The limit follows from a part of her account.

**O6 — Hana’s thermometer**

Written by: an outside author

> Hana says the child has a fever, since the thermometer reads thirty-nine. Later she says the thermometer reading is what is making the child shiver. Warm the thermometer in her hand and the reading climbs while the child shivers exactly as before. Bring the child's temperature down with a cool bath and the shivering eases.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> Her first statement is a good reason to believe there is a fever. Her second statement mistakes the instrument for the illness.

**O7 — Ines’s salt**

Written by: an outside author

> Ines says the ice on her step melted because she spread salt on it. Spread salt and the ice melts within the hour. Sweep the salt off at once and the ice stays. She has no account of what the salt does at any finer level, and no instrument is involved anywhere.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> Ines has a genuine explanation, a coarse one. The salt is what produced the melting.

**O8 — Petra’s own ovens**

Written by: an outside author

> Petra explains why the fuse trips in her bakery when both ovens and the mixer run together: their combined draw exceeds what the old wiring carries. Her account covers her bakery and its wiring, and that was its scope from the start. It says nothing about other bakeries.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> a good explanation with an honest scope. The limit says what she is asking about.

**O9 — The float that does two jobs**

Written by: an outside author

> A float sits in a fuel tank. A rod from it turns a dial on the dashboard. The same float, when it drops low enough, pulls open a valve to the reserve tank. Milan says the falling float opens the valve. Lena says the dial reaching "low" opens it. Bend the needle to read "full": the float is where it was and the valve opens as before. Hold the float up with a wire: the dial reads "full" and the valve stays shut.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> Milan is right, Lena is wrong. The float is read by the dial and also does the work.

**O10 — Two thermostats**

Written by: an outside author

> Two thermostats of the same make hang in two rooms, one set to eighteen degrees and one to twenty-two. Rosa says they are one kind of thing in two different states. Emil says they are two kinds, since one keeps a warm room and one a cool room.

**Thoughtful person’s verdict, unchanged:**

> Rosa is right about what the devices are. Emil's difference matters only when the question is why this room is warm.

**O11 — The apprentice and the third lock**

Written by: an outside author
