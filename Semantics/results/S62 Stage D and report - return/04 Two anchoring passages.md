# 04 Two anchoring passages

## Revised Part V — quoted in full

The following is the complete “Two grades: account at C, and anchored” passage from the revised source, before R2 changes it. Its numbered conditions are retained inside the quotation because their exact text is the object of comparison.

> \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) is **account-hood at \(C\)**. The stronger grade, **anchored account-hood**, adds four conditions on the anchor map, all stated in the same vocabulary:
>
> 1. **Properness.** Every anchor \(\lambda(k)\) is a proper subnetwork of \(D\), and the anchors of the active components refine a cover of \(D\)'s components: no anchor is the whole target, and no component of \(D\) that some pair in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) touches lies outside every anchor.
> 2. **Resolution.** Whenever two components of \(D\) can be separately varied by pairs in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) with distinct effects on \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\), they lie in distinct anchors. The decomposition of \(E\) is at least as fine as the dependencies the question asks about.
> 3. **No global relation.** No active component's relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it: a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name.
> 4. **World-kind.** Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits. Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.
>
> Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution. It is not a new primitive and it does not inspect a label. It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose, and it is what "the model is about the actual mechanism" means here. The one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3; the reversed calculation of Part VII is an account at the identification contract and fails condition 4 on the production edits; a decoy with the same signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4.

## Earlier Part V — “Why there is no anchoring condition” — quoted in full

> A separate condition — "each component of \(E\) must be tied to a component of \(D\) *of the same kind*" — would be either redundant or unevaluable. Where \(C\) contains a change separating two kinds, (F1) already fails for a component anchored to the wrong one. Where \(C\) contains no such change, the two are one kind on \(C\) by (K), and the condition would be asserting a distinction that \(C\) does not contain. There is no third case. Derivation 1 makes this exact.

## What changes

The revised passage adds a stronger grade assessed against all physically admitted changes, proper and sufficiently resolved target subnetworks, a ban on global-solution relations and their projections, and actual rather than merely available routes; it removes the earlier claim that no further same-kind condition is needed once fidelity on the chosen contract has been assessed, while keeping component-level and assembled fidelity and keeping kinds dependent on changes rather than labels. The extension to a larger change list can separate a decoy that the smaller list cannot; that does not invalidate the earlier argument about the fixed smaller list. R2 C subsequently replaces the blanket properness, coverage and projection rules with relevant-dependence requirements and allows a one-component account at an appropriate coarse grain.

## The conditional presentation contrast at O7

Written by: me

Represent Ines’s salt-to-melting dependence as one active relation anchored to the whole coarse salt-and-ice target, with the two stated salt changes retained. This is an added presentation for comparison, not an extra fact supplied in O7. The earlier Account conditions can accept that faithful, non-circular dependence; the unamended revised stronger grade rejects this particular presentation under properness and the no-global-relation condition; R2 C restores its eligibility when the target dependence is licensed independently and the coarse question genuinely concerns it. This is a CHANGED eligibility for the specified presentation at the stronger grade, not a demonstrated reversal of the unchanged outside case or proof that no other faithful presentation exists. The separate proper-subblock defect is not attributed to anchoring.

None of the fourteen unchanged cases establishes a verdict that the earlier argument against an additional anchoring condition necessarily handles differently; O7 supplies the nearest conditional presentation contrast, and the new stronger grade goes beyond the earlier argument’s fixed-contract conclusion.
