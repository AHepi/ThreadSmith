# S110 When something in a creative agent is knowledge - what the sources offer

*Written 29 September 2026 by one Opus 5.5 agent (decision S56) for the owner's question of S57, before the GLM cross-examination of this file was sent. The owner's words: "We have a definition of information and knowledge, and they are in constructor theory. I suspect the next move is how do we know when this indefinite amorphous thing in a creative agent constitutes knowledge." What follows lays out the ways of telling that the sources offer: constructor theory (Chiara Marletto, *The Science of Can and Can't*, 2021, with David Deutsch, *The Beginning of Infinity*, 2011, where the book leans on it), each of the owner's six earlier frameworks, and the present theory. Each option starts with an everyday example, then says what it would take to check it on a case. **Nothing is settled (S28), and the options are not ranked or graded (S20)**: which, if any, to go on with is the owner's choice (S21). Book quotations are in curly quotation marks and checked by `tools/s110_quote_check.py`; the frameworks' and the owner's words are in straight ones. Marks as in file 1: [book], [implied], [reading].*

## In brief

- **The sources offer eleven ways of telling** (section 2), which fall into four families: *it keeps itself in being* (constructor theory's resilience; the elimination test; the abstract catalyst); *it came from error correction* (Deutsch's condition on how knowledge arises); *it improved a problem situation* (FW0, I2, FW2, FW3/FW4's created knowledge, FW5's (EK), the present theory's (EX)); *it holds a correspondence with a history* (the present theory's representation, with selected or constructed provenance).
- **The first family asks about the future of a piece of information; the third asks about the past of a problem.** That is the two-types result of FW3 and FW4 (section 3): one is said to be "a one-place predicate on information", the other "a relation with a problem argument", and neither implies the other.
- **Examined here, not settled**: each of FW3/FW4's two examples rests on a reading. The spreading mistaken idea counts as knowledge on Deutsch's own account, but knowledge *of the minds it lives in*, not of what it says; the lost explanation depends on reading "capable of remaining instantiated" as "did remain". FW5 had already objected that the examples "mix content, instantiation, current capability, and historical events". And the book's own examples give its knowledge an environment place and an "of" place, so "one-place" holds of the wording, not the use (file 1, I7).
- **A step every option needs before it can be checked on a mind**: fix *which thing*, in which carrier, at which grain. FW3 says the question "Is this string knowledge?" has no answer until an interpretation is fixed; constructor theory needs states that can be told apart and copied; the present theory needs the organization a carrier instantiates at a grain, from its physical module. The "indefinite amorphous thing" of S57 becomes checkable, on every source, only after that step, and none of the sources says how to take it for a mind.
- **Where the present theory sits**: it has no "knowledge". Its nearest relations are (EX), created explanation (the third family), and representation (R) with a selected or constructed history (the fourth), which on one reading lets a correspondence produced by variation and survival, with no represented target, count as holding something about its target, as constructor theory's knowledge does without a knower [reading].

## 1. What the owner asked, and the words used here

The owner's question has three parts: a definition exists (constructor theory's); there is an "indefinite amorphous thing in a creative agent"; how do we know when that thing "constitutes knowledge". "How do we know" is read here as: what would one have to find out about a case to say that the thing is, or is not, knowledge in a given source's sense. Nothing below claims that any finding is final: whatever is accepted is accepted tentatively (S23), and ruling out a claim by a claim taken as given is a choice the person makes (S28).

"The thing" is left open on purpose: a thought, a habit, a draft, a hunch, a rule of thumb, a skill. The words: **carrier** for the physical thing that holds it (a brain state, a page, a habit of the hands); **content** for what it holds; **problem** for a clash between ideas that no argument the person can use has decided (the present theory's Part VI). The book's word is close: it says that “knowledge creation always starts with a problem, which we can think of as a clash between different ideas someone has about reality.” (Marletto, ch. 1)

## 2. The ways of telling

### O1. Constructor theory: it keeps itself in being (resilience)

*Example.* A family bread recipe, first worked out by a grandmother, written on a card, copied by each grandchild who liked the bread, and still baked seventy years later in four countries, while her other notes are lost.

*What the source says* [book]. Knowledge is “information that is capable of keeping itself instantiated in physical systems. It is resilient information.” (Marletto, ch. 1) It need not be known by anyone (ch. 1). Resilience is relative to an environment: “To be resilient in a given environment, an adaptation needs to be useful” (Marletto, ch. 5).

*What it would take to check it on a case.* (1) Fix the information: which pattern, in which carriers (the card, the grandchildren's habits), copied how. (2) Fix the environment. (3) Find whether the pattern *causes* its own copying there: here, the bread is liked, so the recipe is copied; a recipe copied only because a law required every card to be kept would be kept, but not by itself [implied from file 1, I3]. (4) Read "capable": the pattern need not actually persist, only be able to, in that environment [implied]. For a thought in a mind: which thought, copied into what (speech, other minds, later states of the same mind), and does its own content cause the copying?

### O2. Constructor theory: the elimination test

*Example.* A cyclist rides home every day without falling. To stop that happening reliably, what would you have to remove? Not the bicycle (another will do), not today's legs (they recover): the balance the rider learned as a child, which would have to be unlearned everywhere it has been passed on.

*What the source says* [book]. The best way to define this kind of information is that “it is exactly the thing one would ultimately have to eliminate in order to prevent a particular transformation from being performed reliably.” (Marletto, ch. 5) The book's cases are a mole cricket's genome and an aircraft factory's recipe.

*What it would take to check it on a case.* (1) Name a transformation the agent performs reliably (riding, solving a kind of equation, writing in a style). (2) List what is needed for it. (3) Find the part that would have to be removed *everywhere it is copied* to stop the transformation for good; that part is the knowledge [implied]. For a creative agent the difficulty is (1): its most creative acts are not repeated transformations but first discoveries, and a first discovery is not a repeatable task (FW5 and the present theory, Part XII, "A first discovery is not a repeatable task"). So the test applies to the agent's retained abilities more readily than to its single creations [reading].

### O3. Constructor theory: the abstract catalyst

*Example.* A knot that a sailing club teaches its members: it lets each of them tie off a boat again and again (it enables a transformation and keeps the ability), it is passed from sailor to sailor in words and hands (it can be copied), and it has lasted because boats that are tied with it stay put (it keeps itself in being).

*What the source says* [book]. An abstract catalyst is information that enables a transformation and keeps the ability, is copiable, and keeps itself instantiated; its information is knowledge; every catalyst contains one (ch. 5, paraphrase).

*What it would take to check it on a case.* O1 and O2 together, plus the copying: the content must be passable to another carrier without changing what it does [book, ch. 3 and ch. 5]. For a thought, can it be taught, and does the taught version still enable the same transformation?

### O4. Deutsch: it came about through error correction

*Example.* Two people give a train's arrival time. One read it off the timetable after checking yesterday's delays against it; the other guessed and happened to be right. The two answers are the same words; only the first came from a process that corrects errors.

*What the source says* [book: Deutsch]. Of the information in genes and brains: “once they are physically embodied in a suitable environment, they tend to cause themselves to remain so.” (Deutsch, ch. 4) He adds a condition on how such information comes about: “Such information – which I call knowledge – is very unlikely to come into existence other than through the error-correcting processes of evolution or thought.” (Deutsch, ch. 4)

*What it would take to check it on a case.* The history of the content: was it shaped by variation with selection, or by conjecture with criticism? This is a condition on origin, so it needs a record or a reconstruction of how the content arose. Deutsch states it as what is "very unlikely" otherwise, not as part of the definition [reading], so it tells where knowledge comes from rather than what it is.

### O5. FW0: explanatory knowledge creation, with physical knowledge a separate sort

*Example.* A student's essay: the teacher writes "your second paragraph contradicts your first"; the student rewrites the second paragraph so the two agree and the argument now reaches its conclusion; the new version is handed in and can be read by others.

*What the source says.* FW0 §16.5: explanatory knowledge creation is a critical creative process with a result, a success judgment K_E on it, and the result accessible; "Retention is availability, not endorsement. K_E, not retention, supplies success." §16.6: physical knowledge, K_CT, "a different sort", its non-equivalence with K_E "a theorem obligation", deferred.

*What it would take to check it on a case.* An event record: the criticism, the response, the revised version, each an attackable entry; a certificate that the defect was addressed and each merit kept (§16.3); and a separate judgment K_E of success, which FW0 keeps apart from the record: a relation that holds or fails whatever the record says (FW0 Part I, "two things that must never be confused").

### O6. I2: actual progress, and use is not certification

*Example.* A team keeps a note, "check the temperature before trusting the scale's reading", in its working notes. The note has been used every week for a year. Using it that long does not show that the team learned something; what would show it is an account of the defect it answered and what it changed.

*What the source says.* I2 Part I: "Successful explanatory knowledge creation has further requirements, including actual explanatory progress." Part V: a method note's "continued use does not certify it as learned knowledge". I2 also keeps apart holding a passage, using it, and developing it: "Retaining a passage, presenting it for examination, using it as a premise, and allocating another development turn to it are different operations."

*What it would take to check it on a case.* The defect the content answered, the change it made to later work, and whether the change answers the reason; I2 says a host can check that a change reached the next context, while "interpreting whether that change answers the reason remains fallible inquiry".

### O7. FW2: explanatory improvement, not persistence

*Example.* A town council keeps a rule, "never build east of the river", for two hundred years. The rule persists; no one knows why. Then an engineer finds the river floods to the east because of the land's slope, and explains why the rule worked. The rule persisted; the engineer's account improved the town's understanding.

*What the source says.* FW2's K-PROGRESS: "Knowledge growth is explanatory improvement, not persistence." "Successful explanatory knowledge creation requires an actual improvement in the relevant problem situation through explanatory work and error correction. A new conjecture need not achieve it; a critical response need not; retention alone does not." Explanatory knowledge creation: a creative critical contribution, actual progress attributable to it, and the result available for use; "loss does not make creation not to have occurred". Physical knowledge separately: "information with a causal capacity for preserving its instantiation; an inherited adaptation can have that character without representing an explanation, and the merit of an explanatory discovery is not the duration for which one copy survives." And an organism's evolved constraints are "physical knowledge in the organism, deployed as premises, withdrawable, and not warrant".

*What it would take to check it on a case.* The problem situation before and after; which deficiency was removed; what work survives or was superseded; what was lost; and that the contribution is the one that did it (FW2 Part II, "Progress, possession, and historical assessment"). For physical knowledge, O1's checks.

### O8. FW3 and FW4: three relations, and a question that must be indexed first

*Example.* Asked "is Newton's theory of gravity knowledge?", one can mean three things: does it account for the orbits within its scope (yes, and it did not stop doing so when a later theory came); is it the account that withstands the criticism now available among the actual rivals (no, not any more); was it created, by someone, as an improvement of a problem situation, and is it still usable (yes, and yes).

*What the source says.* FW3 I.5 and FW4 Part VI: "knowledge" names adequacy (Account), Merit (withstanding criticism "sufficiently well to merit preference among the actual alternatives"), or created knowledge (progress by a creative critical contribution, attributed, and deployable). T18: "Is this string knowledge?" has no answer "until an interpretation (ℓ, context, attributed system) is fixed, and fixing it is a conjecture." T1: no explanatory relation has a knower argument. T19: a problem, criticism, standard, method or limitation "is knowledge relative to the situation it improved."

*What it would take to check it on a case.* First fix the interpretation (grain, context, attributed system), then say which of the three relations is meant, then: for adequacy, find a working subset of the content's commitments that the feature depends on (FW3) or a "support" (FW4's word) with a critical commitment, or a discharge (FW4 Part IX: a structural claim, an application claim, a relevance claim, each refutable); for merit, the actual rivals and the criticism now applicable; for created knowledge, the progress, the contribution and deployability. FW5 later dropped Merit (change map, e79).

### O9. FW5: created explanatory knowledge, and a conditional bridge to constructor theory

*Example.* A mechanic finds why a car stalls (a cracked fuel line that opens when warm), fixes it, and teaches the apprentice how to look for it. The account repaired the problem (the car no longer stalls, nothing else was broken); it is the mechanic's own; it is now used in the workshop, which keeps it going by teaching it, and it is kept going *because* it keeps working on the cars that come in.

*What the source says.* FW5 (EK): a creative critical episode; a Repair (P) of at least one "epistemic obligation" (a correct structural account deployable in a specified problem-use respect, or the correction of a use through one); an originative, adequate, deployable content that participates in the repair on its active route. "Creation is historical." Beside it, CTK, constructor-theoretic knowledge with its "realization ecology" displayed, and a conditional bridge: if an explanatory organization is instantiated as information, a vehicle uses it in a retained task organization that maintains or reconstructs its instantiations, and "the information's specific organization contributes causally to that maintenance, rather than merely being inert material that is indiscriminately copied", then the realization plays constructor theory's preservation role. "No unconditional converse follows."

*What it would take to check it on a case.* For (EK): the obligations declared before the comparison, their state before and after, the protected obligations kept, the content's origin, its Account, its deployability, and the active route (FW5's "ProducesVia"). For the bridge: the vehicle (the workshop), its retained task (repairing cars), and whether the account's own content, not just its copying, is why it is kept.

### O10. The present theory: created explanation (EX)

*Example.* A choir director hears that the altos come in late at one bar every rehearsal, works out that the conductor's upbeat there is ambiguous, changes the gesture, and the altos come in on time from then on without anything else in the piece going wrong; the director can explain the change and use it again elsewhere.

*What the source says.* Part XI, (EX): a creative critical episode; a Repair of an explanatory aim (claimed and protected aims declared as inputs); an originative content c that meets Account on its question (and is not merely declared, Part V), is deployable in a declared use task, and whose binding lies on the active route of the repair. Part X: newness against the repertoire before the event; construction, not relay. The theory never uses the word "knowledge".

*What it would take to check it on a case.* A history: the recognized difficulty, the target, the objection, the response; the aims; the content's newness and its construction trace; (E) for the content on its question, which the theory says turns on the candidate, its question and its target, "not on anyone's view of them"; the declared use task; the active route. The theory supplies no assessor and no procedure; an argument usable by a person rules claims out for that person (Part IX).

### O11. The present theory: representation with a history (R)

*Example.* A frog snaps at small dark moving dots and catches flies. Nobody taught it; frogs whose ancestors snapped at such dots ate and bred. The frog holds nothing it could state, yet its snapping tracks flies, in the pond where its ancestors lived, and would misfire on a flicked pellet.

*What the source says.* Part IV: an occurrence represents a content at a grain when a transport from the organization it instantiates to the content is faithful on the content's contract and has a selected or constructed history; "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything". A selected transport is produced by variation and survival on a history of encountered changes, "with no represented target in that history"; "Whether that transport is faithful on changes it was never selected against turns on the transport and the target alone" (Grievance 3), and what its history leaves open is what its population leaves open (Argument 3).

*What it would take to check it on a case.* The carrier's organization at a grain (supplied by the physical module, the one thing (R) takes from outside); the content and its contract of admitted changes; fidelity on that contract; and the history: a population, a variation operator and a survival condition (selected), or an episode with a represented target (constructed) [reading: this is not called knowledge anywhere in the theory; it is set here beside O1 because both need no knower and both can come about without design].

## 3. Where they pull apart: the two-types result of FW3 and FW4

### 3.1 What the result says

FW3 T2: "physical knowledge (Marletto) is a one-place predicate on information; the two differ in logical type". FW3 T5 and FW4 Part VI (R2, R5): explanatory and physical knowledge are "extensionally independent": "a false belief spread by imitation preserves its instantiation and accounts for nothing; an explanation that accounted for its feature and was lost preserved nothing." "They meet only in realization." (FW3 T5.) In section 2's families: O1 to O3 on one side; O5 to O10 on the other; O4 and O11 between.

### 3.2 Examining the first example: the spreading mistaken idea

*Example.* A story that a goblin lives in the well is told to every child in a village, so that the children keep away from the well; the children grow up and tell it to theirs.

- **On O1 to O3** [implied]: the story keeps itself in being in that village; its content causes its retelling (fear makes it memorable); so it is resilient information. On the book's literal definition, it counts.
- **On Deutsch's reading** [book: Deutsch]: an idea of this kind is what he calls an anti-rational meme, one that survives by disabling its holders' criticism, and of its example he writes that “its overt content – the idea of the hobgoblin’s existence – need contain no truth.” (Deutsch, ch. 15) Yet he also writes that for it to last “it is essential that its implicit knowledge of these vulnerabilities be true and deep.” (Deutsch, ch. 15) So on Deutsch's reading it *is* knowledge, but knowledge of the minds it lives in, not of the well [reading].
- **On O7 to O10**: the story accounts for nothing about the well, and no problem situation about the well was improved; it is not created knowledge or (EX) about the well. Whether it is (EX) about something else (keeping children safe) turns on the aims declared; FW5 and the present theory take the aims as inputs.
- **On O11** [reading]: a selected transport from the village's story-telling organization to a content about children's fears, faithful on the changes the village met, is conceivable; the theory would then say the practice represents something about its hosts, with no one representing it.
- **What this shows** [reading]: the example separates *what the idea says* from *what the idea keeps in step with*. On the book's and Deutsch's use, constructor theory's knowledge has an "of" (the book: “for adaptations, it is knowledge of some features of the environment”, Marletto, ch. 1), and here the "of" is not the story's subject. So the example does not show preservation without knowledge; it shows that the two relations can have different objects. Whether that is "independence" depends on whether one compares the two on the same object (FW5's "a common domain").

### 3.3 Examining the second example: the lost explanation

*Example.* An engineer works out why a bridge design will fail, writes it in a notebook, and the notebook burns before anyone reads it (S89's case "The burned notebook").

- **On O7 to O10**: the explanation was created (the problem situation of the engineer improved; the content was hers, new, and accounted for the failure); "loss does not make creation not to have occurred" (FW2), "Creation is historical" (FW5). On (EX), the content had to be deployable at the end of the episode; it was, until the fire.
- **On O1 to O3** [implied]: the book's definition says "capable of" remaining instantiated. On one reading, capability belongs to the information in a suitable environment, and a burned notebook was in an unsuitable one; on that reading the explanation was knowledge that happened to be destroyed. On the other reading, capability is shown by persistence, and it was not knowledge. Deutsch's wording leans to the first [reading]: the information in genes and brains, he says, “once they are physically embodied in a suitable environment, they tend to cause themselves to remain so.” (Deutsch, ch. 4) So FW3/FW4's second example holds on the second reading only.
- **What this shows** [reading]: the example turns on the modal word "capable", which FW3 and FW4 read as actual preservation. FW5's objection names the same thing from its side: the examples mix "current capability" with "historical events".

### 3.4 Examining "one-place"

- The book's sentence is one-place: “Knowledge is defined entirely via counterfactuals: it is information that is capable of remaining instantiated in physical systems.” (Marletto, ch. 5)
- Its use has at least an environment place (the same DNA is or is not an abstract catalyst depending on its environment) and an object, knowledge of some features of the environment (ch. 1 and ch. 5, paraphrase; file 1, I7).
- FW5 put the environment in explicitly (CTK indexed by its "realization ecology"), without calling it a correction of Marletto's wording.
- So "different logical type" may be a difference between a one-place wording and a two- or three-place use [reading]. If constructor theory's knowledge is read with an environment and an object, and explanatory knowledge with a problem, the two have the same shape: information held in step with something, relative to a setting. They would still differ in *what* they are held in step with (a feature of the environment that keeps the information going; a feature a problem asks about) and in *how* (survival; criticism).

### 3.5 What is left open

1. Whether constructor theory's knowledge has an "of" place that the definition omits, or only in the book's informal use.
2. Whether "capable of remaining instantiated" is read in a suitable environment or in the actual one.
3. Whether explanatory knowledge is ever resilient *because* it is explanatory: whether a content kept going by criticism (it survives because rivals fail it less) is a kind of resilience, as Deutsch's "rational memes" are: of those memes he says that, being ideas “which are created by rational and critical thought, subsequently also depend on such thought to get themselves replicated faithfully.” (Deutsch, ch. 15) This is the one place where the two families might meet by more than realization; FW5's conditional bridge is a statement of it.
4. Whether the owner's "amorphous thing" is a content (answerable by O5 to O11 once fixed) or a carrier state (answerable by O1 to O3).
5. Whether one needs both, and if so, which question comes first.

## 4. What every option needs first: fixing the thing

*Example.* Someone says "she has a feel for when a soufflé is ready". Before anyone can ask whether that feel is knowledge, one has to say where it is (her eyes, her timing, a habit of tapping the dish), at what grain (a yes-or-no judgment; a sense of how long is left), and what it is about (this oven; any oven).

- **Constructor theory**: information needs states that can be set and copied (file 1, I4); a feel has to be read as some such states, in some carrier, before O1 to O3 apply.
- **FW3, FW4**: "Is this string knowledge?" has no answer until an interpretation (grain, context, attributed system) is fixed, and "fixing it is a conjecture" (T18).
- **FW5, present theory**: which organization a physical occurrence instantiates at a grain is supplied by the physical module, the theory's one import on this side; representation is then defined from it (Part IV). An inexplicit representation "is not an absent one" (Part X): the feel can be a representation if the distinctions and transformations it involves are instantiated and used.
- **I2**: a prose statement "I see a difficulty but cannot yet articulate it" stays available; the engine does not force a form.

None of the sources says how to fix the thing in a mind. Each says only that it must be fixed, and that fixing it is itself open to criticism [reading].

## 5. Where the sources stand on a creative agent

| source | what a creative agent is | what in it can be knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| constructor theory | a system that can create new knowledge by thinking (ch. 7); human brains have the ability | resilient information in it; the book does not say which |
| Deutsch | a universal explainer (not read for this file; S89, M10) | information that keeps itself in being, arisen by error correction |
| FW0 | a system with originative acts and critical creative processes, recorded as events | results of a critical process that meet K_E and are accessible |
| I2 | a composite of participants and a host (a realization) | contributions that make actual progress; use certifies nothing |
| FW2 | an organization with the recursive kernel (conjecture, criticism, return) | adequacy, progress, created knowledge; physical knowledge separately |
| FW3, FW4 | as FW2 | adequacy, merit or created knowledge, each for a problem, after an interpretation is fixed |
| FW5 | an organization with owned retained capability for inquiry | (EK); CTK where its bridge's conditions are met |
| present theory | a system whose episodes contain originative construction (G) connected to criticism | the theory has no knowledge; (EX) and (R) are the nearest |
