# Cross-examination report

## Points

**1. R04 (task b) — declared WORDING in fact weakens a claim.**

The old wording states: *"No predicate meaning 'really explains', 'is a cause' or 'is knowledge' appears anywhere."* The new wording states: *"No predicate meaning 'really explains', 'is a cause' or 'is knowledge' is taken as primitive, and no definition depends on one."*

These are not the same claim "in other words." The old wording rules out every such predicate—derived or underived. The new wording rules out only (i) its being primitive and (ii) its being used in any definition's definiens. A predicate meaning "explains" that some definition *introduces* (as its definiendum) and that no other definition *uses* satisfies both new conditions while violating the old wording. "No definition depends on one" reads naturally as "no definition *uses* one" (a definition of *X* depends on *X*'s definiens, not on *X*). The new wording therefore permits orphan derived predicates the old wording forbade.

The consequences run both ways required by task (b): a reader can now conclude that the theory is compatible with a derived "explains" predicate (the old text precluded this); and a reader can no longer conclude that no such predicate exists anywhere (the old text guaranteed this). Either suffices: this is a claim change in WORDING clothing.

The pointer compounds the problem. Derivation 6's Consequence reads (line 594–595): *"There is no residual predicate meaning 'explains,' 'represents,' 'is a cause,' or 'is knowledge.'"* On the natural reading of "residual" (present in the theory after the derivation), this supports the *old*, stronger wording—not the new, weaker one. On the weaker reading of "residual" (underived), Derivation 6 supports "not taken as primitive" but says nothing about "no definition depends on one"; that claim is supported by Part XIV's dependence order (*"Nothing depends on a predicate meaning 'really explains,' 'is a cause,' or 'is knowledge'"*), which R04 does not cite. Either way, the pointer does not match the new wording cleanly.

**Repair:** Reclassify R04 as CLAIM with declaration: *"Part 0 no longer claims that no such predicate appears anywhere; it now claims only that none is taken as primitive and that no definition depends on one."* Alternatively, keep WORDING and strengthen the wording to match Derivation 6: *"No predicate meaning 'really explains', 'is a cause' or 'is knowledge' exists in the theory (Derivation 6)."*

**2. R39 (task b) — declared WORDING in fact reclassifies 𝒩.**

Old: *"declared as a substantive input when aesthetic value is claimed."* New: *"taken as a substantive input when aesthetic value is claimed."*

The single word carries classificatory weight. Throughout the text, "declared" is a term of art: Part XIV has a section titled **"Declared inputs"** (*"stated inputs that the semantics records and does not supply"*), Part 0 and Part XIV have **"declared indices,"** and Part IV has **"Declared"** as a provenance type. To say 𝒩 is *"declared as a substantive input"* places it in the declared-input category. To say it is *"taken as"* an input matches Part XIV's description of 𝒩 as primitive 2 (*"It is taken as an input and never derived"*). A reader of the old wording can conclude 𝒩 is a declared input; a reader of the new wording can no longer conclude this and can now conclude the opposite. That is a change of claim.

R38 (correctly typed CLAIM) already declares the reclassification: *"no longer calls it a declared input."* R39 performs the same reclassification at the second mention of 𝒩 in the same paragraph but is typed WORDING with no declaration. If R39 is part of R38's change, it should be merged into R38 or retyped. As drafted, it changes a claim it is declared not to change.

**Repair:** Retype R39 as CLAIM with declaration: *"𝒩 is now 'taken as' rather than 'declared as' a substantive input in the aesthetic case, consistent with its status as primitive 2."*

**3. R48 (task a, task c) — the modified passage over-attributes dependencies to (P).**

The new wording reads (line 520): *"(P), (EK) depend on (G), (E), Deploy; (P) also on ProducedBy and on the declared obligations with their occasions, and ProducedBy on histories and their active routes."*

The addition of ProducedBy and the obligations is correct: (P) (Repair, line 438) is defined as *"∃o∈O[¬o(ξ) ∧ o(ξ')] ∧ ∀r∈P[r(ξ) ⇒ r(ξ')] ∧ ProducedBy(Δ,ξ,ξ';O)"*—it uses only the obligations and ProducedBy. But the unchanged clause *"(P), (EK) depend on (G), (E), Deploy"* attributes (G) (Origin), (E) (Account), and Deploy to (P). (P) does not use (G): Origin is defined as *"Attempt ∧ New ∧ Build"* (line 371), and none of these appears in (P)'s definiens. The semicolon structure ("…Deploy; (P) *also* on…") confirms the reading that (P) depends on the first list *and additionally* on ProducedBy and the obligations. The composite claim—(P) depends on five things, three of which it does not use—contradicts (P)'s definition.

R48's declaration says *"(P) on ProducedBy and the declared obligations with their occasions"* and does not mention the (G), (E), Deploy attribution. Task (b) directs attention to parts of the changed passage the declaration omits: the unchanged clause is one such part. It says in both texts what it said before, but in the *combined* new sentence it now reads as the baseline from which (P)'s "also" dependencies are added, giving the error a form it did not have before ("(P) also on…" presupposes the prior list is correct for (P)).

Additionally, (EK) uses Repair (line 443: *"∧ Repair_{O,P}(ξ,ξ';Δ)"*), so (EK) depends on (P). The grouping *"(P), (EK) depend on…"* never says this. The passage R48 is rewriting as erratum carries two structural errors; R48 fixes neither.

This is partly pre-existing, which is why R48 does not fall on it alone. But the repair should accompany the change. **Suggested repair** for the shared clause: *"(EK) depends on (G), (E), Deploy and (P). (P) depends on ProducedBy and on the declared obligations with their occasions, and ProducedBy on histories and their active routes."*

**4. R10 (task c) — "the ground of its restriction" is used without definition.**

Line 159: *"Where the claim states the ground of its restriction, a verdict on the restriction is given with that ground; where it states none and a verdict turns on one, the ground is a missing declared input (Part XIV)."*

"Ground of its restriction" names a new relatum—the thing Part XIV calls *"what makes a restriction appropriate"*—but no sentence equates the two expressions. The pointer "(Part XIV)" lands on a list that uses different words, and Part XIV points back to Part III with "(Part III)", yielding a cross-reference cycle that never states the identity. Meanwhile "grounds" is already in use in Part IX (*"A criticism has target z, alleged defect δ, grounds g"*), for a different relatum. The reader must infer both the definition and the disambiguation. This does not move any verdict (the logic of R10 is sound and its declaration is satisfied), but the term should carry a gloss. **Repair:** *"Where the claim states what makes its restriction appropriate (its ground), a verdict on the restriction is given with that; where it states none and a verdict turns on one, that ground is a missing declared input (Part XIV)."*

**5. R03 (task c) — bolded "declared inputs" appears before definition.**

Line 31 reads: *"Everything else is derived from the two primitives, the declared indices and the **declared inputs**, in the order Part XIV states."* "Declared indices" is introduced and glossed in the immediately preceding sentence (*"every claim is relative to them, and none is a predicate that could be true or false"*). "Declared inputs" receives bold (the typographic signal of a defined term) with no gloss and no prior occurrence in Part 0—R02 has just removed the earlier mention at line 25. The definition arrives 485 lines later at Part XIV. The pointer to Part XIV mitigates this, and the claim R03 makes is correctly declared; the defect is purely one of load-order. **Repair (minimal):** add a gloss matching the indices sentence: *"…and the **declared inputs**: stated data a claim takes as given, which the semantics records and does not supply…"*
