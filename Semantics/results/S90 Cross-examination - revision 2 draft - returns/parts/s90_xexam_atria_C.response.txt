## Report

1. **R12, R13 · (c) — “defined for every transport” is not supported by the section’s own formula.** R13’s added sentence (line 223) reads: “Expectation and violation are defined for every transport, surprise only for a selected one: a constructed transport that fails at a pair of its contract is violated, and the failure is not surprise…”. But the definition it rests on (lines 217–221) fixes the expectation as “\(\operatorname{Ans}_S(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\).” Since \(\tau,\sigma\) are the transport \(t\)’s own translations, that expression is well typed only when \(t\)’s target organization is the simulation layer \(S\). Part IV says explicitly (“Three provenances,” line ~203): “A physical system may hold selected transports at the primitive layer and constructed transports at the simulation layer,” and (R)’s transports run from a carrier to a content. For a transport whose target is not \(S\), \(\operatorname{Ans}_S(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) is undefined, so “defined for every transport” is false as written. The current text did not assert this: it opened “Let \(t\) be selected on history \(H\) with contract \(C\),” and the section was naturally scoped by “\(S\) is where expectation lives” (line ~179). R12’s setup line, “Let \(t\) be a transport with contract \(C\) and, where \(t\) is selected, history \(H\),” generalizes the section; R13’s sentence then turns a latent looseness into an explicit contradiction with unchanged text. *(d) check:* no verdict moves — in N18 both designers’ transports are simulation-layer, so the violation/surprise distinction works and matches the fixed verdict (see point 2). *Repair:* “Expectation and violation are defined for every transport into the simulation layer, surprise only for a selected one,” or replace \(\operatorname{Ans}_S\) with \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\), where \(E\) is the organization \(t\) targets. I hold R13 to fall: its sentence is the false statement. R12 stands, because its declaration is satisfied and its setup line is still contextually scoppable — but the same repair must touch it.

2. **R12, R13, R14 · (d) on N18 — the declared distinction is exactly the fixed verdict.** Rhea holds a constructed transport whose contract covers crowds of the size that walked on it; the swaying is a violation at a pair *inside* her contract, so “the failure is not surprise” (R13) and it contradicts grounds she had. Dov holds a selected transport shaped on a history of small crowds; the large crowd is \((a,b)\notin H\), so his failure is *surprise* (R12), and by Derivation 3 his history left that value open wherever his population admits a differing survivor — the swaying went against his expectation but contradicted nothing he had reason to expect. This is the declared content of both changes, and it is the verdict. R14 (“extends the history \(H\) of a selected transport”) is properly WORDING: once R12 makes \(H\) conditional on selection, “extends \(H\)” would otherwise be ill defined, and a selection response is already fixed by \(\mu\) and the population, so nothing new is concluded.
