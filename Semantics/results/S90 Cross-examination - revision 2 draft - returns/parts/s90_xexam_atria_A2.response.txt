The load-bearing question is whether R55's new conclusion ("so far as (F1), (F2) and (A) reach, the two candidates are one account (Derivation 2)", line 624) is still available after R51 added a shared-anchor premise to Derivation 2 (lines 554–562). It is. Derivation 2(ii) requires "a bijection φ of their active components [that] gives each k and φ(k) one anchor, the same subnetwork of D with port translations onto the same ports of D." In Derivation 10 the exchange e of the two things gives λ′(1) = e(λ(1)) = thing 2's continuity and λ′(2) = thing 1's continuity; taking φ = the exchange, λ(1) and λ′(2) are the same subnetwork (thing 1's continuity) with translations onto the same ports of D, and likewise λ(2) and λ′(1). So the premise of (ii) is satisfied, and the conclusion "k and φ(k) are of one kind on C" follows for each pair. R51's declaration and R55's declaration both describe their wordings exactly, and neither overstates.
