## Report

I tested all eleven changes against the whole revised text and the fixed verdicts. The points below are ordered by what was most at stake; none of them establishes a defect.

**1. R42 (c, d) — the one change that moves a verdict, and it moves it toward the fixed verdict.** Part XII, line 475, now reads "a population of **candidate** transports", and its own next sentence already said: "The population is the set of transports the physics and the stated construction admit; a transport that would need a part every member of the population is built without is not in it." Part IV independently says "There is a population \(\mathcal T\) of **candidate** transports". So the current text's "realized transports" contradicted both Part IV and Part XII's own last sentence, leaving the population's membership split. This matters for **O24**: the differing arrangement is "reachable by hand" but never used, so on the "realized" reading it is outside the population, Derivation 3's condition fails, and the record would have *settled* the question — against the fixed verdict ("the record has not chosen between them"). On the candidate reading the arrangement is in the population, survives on \(H\), and the record has not chosen. The same bears on **N5**: the months-reading and the seasons-reading coincide on the village's history, so as candidates both survive on \(H\) and the rule "cannot tell him which reading to trust"; on a realized-only reading they are not separately realizable and the population would fix one. Both moves are toward the fixed verdicts, and the declaration states exactly the change ("candidate transports, as Part IV does, not realized ones") that produces them. R42 also repairs an internal contradiction in the current text.

**2. R11 and R52 (a, c, d) — the survival definition and Derivation 3's qualification close each other.** R11, line 195: "A transport **survives on \(H\)** when it is a member of \(\mathcal T\) that meets the survival condition on \(H\); fidelity on \(H\) without membership in \(\mathcal T\) is not survival." R52, line 566: "it must be a member of \(\mathcal T\), a transport the physics and the stated construction admit (Part XII), and it must survive on \(H\) (Part IV)." Both pointers are correct: Part XII defines the population as what physics and the stated construction admit; R11 in Part IV supplies the definition of "survives on \(H\)" that Derivation 3's "also surviving on \(H\)" and its proof ("that transport survives on \(H\)") now turn on. Dropping "realizable" from the old list loses nothing, since Part XII makes physics-and-construction admission a condition of membership in \(\mathcal T\). On **O48** ("every device in the stated population is built without that wire") the theory, before and after, gives no differing survivor and so agrees that "the premise is not met": Derivation 3 already required \(t'\in\mathcal T\). No verdict moves.

**3. R31 (a, d) — a repair of an undefined symbol, and it settles the newness cases.** The current text's (N) used \(d\equiv_\ell c\) without defining it; line 407 now defines it: "there are transports from \(d\) to \(c\) and from \(c\) to \(d\), both faithful on \(c\)'s contract at grain \(\ell\)." The definition is well formed for both directions (a transport to a content is assessed on the content's contract, as in (R); a transport from a content is assessed on its domain's contract, as in Part V). This moves the newness cases from unsettled to settled, all toward the fixed verdicts: **O11** Wednesday (the chosen key is mutually faithful with Tuesday's, so not new — "she is reusing what Tuesday gave her"); **O18** and **O31** (the small binding is not equivalent to anything in the received repertoire, and the rest keeps its inherited provenance); **N15** and **N21** (newness is indexed to the system's prior repertoire, so "not new to the world" and "her own construction" are both respected). The declaration states the definition, so the moves are accounted for.

**4. R22 (a, c, d) — two senses of "route", kept apart by design.** Line 307: "a route is a support of the candidate, a member of \(\mathsf S\), and is a route whether or not any history runs it; it is active in a system when occurrences that realize it form an active route (Part IX)". Part IX defines that technical notion ("a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result") and adds "A route that started and did no work … is not active for that result", so the gloss is accurate; Part XI's "ProducedBy holds when an active route (Part IX) runs from \(\Delta\) to the repair; it credits each contribution the history establishes" is what the last clause restates. The cases divide exactly as the verdicts require: **O52** (Ana's bypass jams and takes nothing) is a support that is not active, so Ben is credited; **O39** and **O20** (two sufficient routes both ran) are both credited with "no division of credit that it does not contain"; **O45** (the second spring, connected and sufficient from the start) was already a support, hence already a route — a conclusion the unchanged sentence "A route already present in the candidate is a route whether or not anyone has described its work" already carried, so R22 moves nothing.

**5. R32 and R33 (a, c) — ownership of a process versus credit for content.** Line 421 now reads "a process that runs inside the boundary is the system's own whoever wrote it" (matching Part XII's "a process run inside the boundary is the system's whoever wrote it", so the unindexed "today" is gone) and adds "Credit for content and ownership of a process are different attributions: a routine written outside the boundary and run inside it is the system's own process, and its content remains its writer's contribution." The second clause restates Part X's own "Work supplied from outside that boundary … remains an outside contribution however it is executed inside". This reproduces **O30** (the comparison is the robot's process; the routine's origin is the person's) and, with **O21**/**O50**, lets the theory accept the verdicts' weightings rather than contest them: Part XIV lists "a weighting of credit among several contributions to one achievement" as a declared input, and O50's three separate attributions (joint reinterpretation, robot's discovery, expert-framed inquiry) are exactly what Part X and Part XI distinguish. No verdict moves away.

**6. R35, R36 and R37 (a, c) — typing the three symbols (P) and (EK) use.** Line 429 types \(\Delta\) as "a subhistory together with the changes of content it makes", which is what Part IX's active route can run from and what (P)'s ProducedBy takes; line 435 glosses \(r(\xi')\) as holding "on every occasion it covers from \(\xi\) to \(\xi'\)", which follows from the unchanged "a protected condition is lost exactly when it fails on an occasion it covers"; line 447 defines \(\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\) and \(\operatorname{ProducesVia}\), both of which (EK) uses undefined in the current text. No symbol clashes: \(\Delta\), Result and ProducesVia appear nowhere else. On **O35** the interval reading is stricter, but the protected condition naturally read as availability to users held throughout ("nobody drew from it in those seconds"), and Part XIV says a verdict that turns on a declared input is "a verdict given the input" — the theory agrees given the input the verdict states, and did before the change.

**7. R40 (a, c) — the boundary fallback.** Line 467 adds the two clauses the declaration names. They sit consistently after "Both are declared before the attribution, not chosen after it" (the fallback supplies what counts as a declaration where none was made) and with Part XII's "Ownership is grounded in the processes and resources the boundary includes, never in the capability being attributed". The cases land right: **O17** (the log shows the routine running inside, so it declares the boundary; the manual's "because the robot can diagnose" is the attribution); **O51** (the boundary was declared explicitly, naming the controller and its routines, so the fallback is never reached and the module sits outside it); **O49** (the offered circular definition is the attribution, not a boundary, and the two histories carry the distinction).
