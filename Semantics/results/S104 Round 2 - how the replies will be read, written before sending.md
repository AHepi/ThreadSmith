## How the replies will be read

1. **Nothing is opened before the runs are over.** No reply, receipt, reasoning file, attempt file or standard-output file of a call is opened until every call of both runs has ended its last allowed pass (a reply accepted, or failed with no pass left), or until 24 hours have passed since the earlier of the two launches, whichever comes first. Each launch time is the first line of its run log (`launched <UTC time>`). If the 24 hours pass first, the replies then accepted are read under this rule, the calls still going are left to finish, and a reply that comes later is read under this same rule and recorded as late. The progress logs (the runners' `start`, attempt, `ok`, `failed` and `done` lines) may be watched; they hold no reply text.
2. **What counts as a reply.** A Mimo reply counts only when it finishes "stop" with END OF REPORT on its last line (`s80_call.accept_reader`); a GLM reply only when its last non-blank line carries END OF REPORT (`glm_via_claude_code.py`). A failed call counts neither for nor against any item; its attempt files are kept and are not read for arguments. A call that failed only on the connection (every attempt ended with no HTTP status, a cut stream, a timeout or a proxy refusal) is not an answer from the model and is not counted against it (lessons S21, S24); if such a call is still failed after pass 3, it may be sent again only under a new note written before sending. A call still failed after pass 3 on answers the model did give (length at the ceiling, or no END OF REPORT) is final, and the items of its part are reported as **not examined by that reader**. After any container restart, the runners are looked at and every call not finished is relaunched from a shell started after the restart; a proxy failure is not a reply (lesson S24).
3. **A reply is a set of arguments to rule on, not a result.** No sentence of the text, and no definition, claim or invention of the maths, is kept, changed or dropped on a reader's say-so. Silence is not agreement: an item no point attacks is not thereby shown to hold, and a claim that held on every model tried holds on nothing beyond them.
4. **Tabulation first.** Once the runs are over (rule 1), one fresh Opus 5.5 agent, which built nothing of this round and has read no reply, tabulates both readers' replies to all seventeen parts before any ruling: for each item (a definition, encoding, claim, counterexample, invention, U- or H-entry, NF entry, round-1 matter or change, or a sentence of the text a reader names) and each reader, every point made about it, with the exact wording of every proposal, and the line or lines of the text each proposal would change. It writes `results/S104 Round 2 - tabulation of the replies, before any ruling.md`, created at once and filled as it goes (lesson S28). It rules on nothing.
5. **What goes to a checker.** Two kinds of item go to a checker:
   - **every item a reader challenges**: a point that the maths says other than the sentence, that a counterexample tells against the text, that the text settles an invention or should carry wording that settles it, a counterexample to a claim that held, a proposed change for a round-1 matter or change, or any other proposed change to the text. Where it is in doubt whether a point challenges an item, the item goes to a checker.
   - **every counterexample the second check reproduced and worked by hand**, whether or not a reader raised it: all twelve (FC05; FC18; FC20; FC23 (b); FC25 (b); FC63 (c-i); FC77; FC78, FC81 (d) and FC82, which the check found to be one finding; FC83; FC102 (b), second half).

   Items are grouped by the line or lines of the text they would change (an item that would change no line of the text, only the maths, forms a group with the lines its claim or invention formalizes). **Each group goes to one fresh Opus 5.5 checker of its own**, who built nothing of this round and rules on no other group of it, so that no line of the text has two checkers. The checker is given the part brief or briefs, the text under review, both readers' passages on the group's items whole and any other passage of either reply that names them, the tabulation's rows for them, the maths files and both checks whole (including the check of the formalization's sections 2 and 3, withheld from the readers), and this rule. For each line of the text in its group it rules:
   - **KEEP**: the sentence stands as it is;
   - **FIX**: the exact new wording, whole; the sentence changes; with the reasons why this wording and not the text's, nor a reader's where it differs;
   - **DROP**: the sentence leaves the text; with every sentence that depends on it named, by line and words, and what becomes of each.

   For each item of the group it also says what the item comes to: for a claim or definition read against its sentence, which of the two should stand, the maths or the words; for a counterexample, whether it tells against the text or only against an invention or against the claim's own wording; for an invention or unrecorded choice, whether the text settles it (quoting the words that do), should now settle it, or should leave it open. The checkers run at most five at a time, a limit kept by a count the orchestrator keeps (decisions S12, S17; lesson S22).
6. **Inventions and the text.** A FIX may not write an invention into the text unless the ruling says, in so many words, that the text should now settle that invention, names it (Inn, Un or Hnn), and gives the wording; the ruling and the round's record list every such FIX as "settles Inn". A result that rests only on inventions the text leaves open changes nothing in the text by itself: such a counterexample is recorded as a counterexample to that way of writing the text, with the inventions it rests on, and the line it bears on is KEEP unless the ruling finds that the text should settle the invention (then the first sentence of this rule applies) or that the text says something it should not, for reasons that do not rest on the invention. A choice that no register entry records, found by a reader or a checker, is recorded as an invention (the register's next number, in a later record of the round, by the orchestrator after the critical review), and that record is not a move.
7. **Items that go to no checker.** An item no point of either reader challenges, and that is not one of the twelve counterexamples, goes to no checker and is recorded **"not challenged by either reader; not thereby final"**; where one reader's reply to its part never came back (rule 2), "not challenged by one reader; not examined by the other; not thereby final"; where neither did, "not examined in this round".
8. **Disagreements are ruled on their arguments, not counted.** Where the readers differ on an item, or offer two different wordings, the checker states each side's argument and rules between them or writes a third wording; the number of readers on a side decides nothing, and so do the length and the confidence of a reply.
9. **Parked, and the owner's questions.** A proposal about what hard to vary covers is recorded as parked and not applied (decisions S33, S34). A proposal that would move where values are placed is recorded as a question for the owner and not applied. The item is ruled on the rest of the points made about it.
10. **The owner's words bound every ruling.** A FIX may not say more than the owner's words of S20, S21, S23, S25 to S28, S33, S34 and S36 say, nor less than they require: no list, set, count, grade, ranking or record doing work the explanation's own content should do (S20); nothing about what must happen to candidates, which is the choice of the person choosing (S21); no word or idea S23 forbids, "argument" as reasons why this and not that, and every accepting tentative (S23); physical possibility only where information or knowledge is instantiated or transformed, and as the content of claims a candidate can conflict with (S25 to S27); nothing settled, and a ruling out by a claim taken as given a choice the person made (S28); every invention recorded (S36). A point that turns on a choice the owner has not made is recorded, with both sides' arguments, as a question for the owner, and the checker rules only whether the present wording states the matter as the owner's words leave it.
11. **Quotations are compared with the text.** Each quotation a reply or a ruling relies on is compared with the text under review or the brief; where one is not found, that is recorded and the point is ruled on what the text says. A reply's quotation of a book longer than 25 words, a run of quotations joined only by page numbers, punctuation or a few words counting as one (lesson S29), is not copied into any record. A model a reader gives by hand is checked by the checker, by hand or by the program in `results/S104 Round 2 - maths/model/`, and the ruling says which.
12. **File names** (lesson S25). The replies stay under their tags in `results/S104 Round 2 - returns/`. Each ruling is written to `results/S104 Round 2 - rulings/ruling L<first line>[-L<last line>].md`, one file per group, created at once and filled as it goes (lesson S28), never written over; a second ruling on the same group (rule 13) goes to `ruling L<...> - second.md`.
13. **A critical review before anything is applied.** After every ruling has been written, one critical review by Fable 5.1 (decision S35: "use Fable 5.1 sparingly for critical reviews") reads every ruling, the tabulation and the records of rule 7 against this rule and the owner's words, and writes `results/S104 Round 2 - critical review of the rulings.md`. No ruling is applied before it. Where the review contests a ruling, the orchestrator decides (decision S13), with its reasons why this and not the other: it keeps the ruling, or sends the group to a second fresh checker with the review's point; either way it is recorded.
14. **Applying.** The FIXes and DROPs are applied by program, after the review, to a new copy of the stand-alone text, `tests/104 The semantics, standing alone, after round 2.md`, one line to one line where a DROP does not remove a whole line; `tests/103 The semantics, standing alone, after round 1.md` and every earlier text are never written over, and nothing is written into `authority/`. Each applied change is compared by program with its ruling, byte for byte. The cases the changed sentences bear on (the worked cases in the text, and any case of the project's case book that turns on them) are re-read on the new copy by a fresh agent, the formal claims whose sentences changed are marked in the round's record as to be formalized again, and the records are updated.
15. **Moves, and when the series ends.** The moves of this round are the FIXes and DROPs applied. A round with no moves ends the series: "no moves left" (decision S35). Otherwise the next round goes to the new copy, under its own rule, written and committed before sending.

## How the runs are launched

From the repository root, after this file's commit is pushed, two processes side by side, each detached so that it outlives the agent that starts it, the keys loaded into its own environment from the session's key file and never printed (the other keys unset):

```

setsid nohup bash -c 'cd /home/user/ThreadSmith || exit 1; echo "launched $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)"; set -a; . <key file>; set +a; unset ATRIA_API_KEY MIMO_API_KEY OPENAI_API_KEY; <scratchpad>/venv/bin/python Semantics/tools/s104_glm_loop.py "Semantics/tools/s104_jobs - round 2, GLM.json"; echo "loop ended $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)"' > <scratchpad>/s104_glm_run.log 2>&1 < /dev/null &
```

Each loop's process id (the session leader's) goes in `<scratchpad>/s104_mimo_run.pid` and `<scratchpad>/s104_glm_run.pid`. In the Mimo loop each turn is one pass: a job whose reply has come back is skipped, a failed job goes as the next pass (its earlier files kept under their pass numbers), and the runner refuses a pass above `max_pass`; pass 2 starts only after every call of pass 1 has ended. The GLM loop works in rounds of the same kind (`s104_glm_loop.py`).

**When each run is over.** Mimo: the log's last line reads `loop ended <UTC time>` and the process in the pid file has exited; or each `s104_maths_mimo_<n>` (n = 1 to 17) has a `.response.txt` in the returns folder, or a `.error.txt` and a `.receipt.json` with `"failed": true` from its third pass. GLM: the log holds `s104_glm_loop: every pass ended; …` followed by `loop ended <UTC time>`, and the process in the pid file has exited; or each part has a `.response.txt` under one of its tags (`s104_maths_glm_<n>`, `_pass2`, `_pass3`), or `s104_maths_glm_<n>_pass3.receipt.json` with `"accepted": false`. A watcher outside the agent that launches the runs waits for both (lesson S14); the launching agent hands back as soon as a call of each reader is in flight.
