The earlier standalone theory is the authority. R2 is available only through the pack's quotations, and the four S62 clauses and S64 H64 and I64 are proposed amendments to that authority. Part 2 leaves H64 and I64 unchanged and proposes H65. Every comparison below holds the outside situation and its thoughtful verdict fixed. O4 retains the recalibration fact supplied in round 4. The common baseline is the earlier version with applicable R2 and all four S62 clauses, excluding H64, I64 and H65. Each full-corpus table adds only its named clause to that common baseline. For a S62 table the displayed column still reports that common baseline; I also checked it with only that S62 addition withheld, retaining its inherited R2 text and the other three S62 clauses. The withheld-addition verdicts coincide on all thirteen current cases. Restoring each tested addition therefore yields a substantive preservation check rather than treating its prior inclusion as a reason for SAME.

REVISED ONLY: the stronger anchored grade, the repair-based question-merit gate, the express source exclusion concerning new dimensions, and the separately supplied inadmissible-counterfactual apparatus are absent from the earlier authority. References to them in R2 or S62 are not used as earlier-version conditions. In particular, S62 G's new merit-input sentences have no applicable earlier merit condition to modify; its inherited question-preservation sentences remain relevant to O33 and O34.

SAME preserves the case verdict and its limits, including uncertainty; it does not mean that the whole thoughtful verdict has been derived. CHANGED would require a difference in the verdict, not a restatement, an extra demand for grounds, or a case's placement by a tighter pair. The uncertain comparative judgements in O20, O21 and O27, and O35's unspecified protected condition, are not promoted to successes because the proposed clauses expose what is missing. A clause's silence means it supplies no independent verdict on the identified issue; the rest of the baseline continues to apply.

For O36, using a premise is read in its ordinary local sense of depending on it in the written proof route, rather than merely mentioning it. The deletion verdicts below are conditional on that reading; no complete proofs are supplied for an independent formal check. A merely cited redundant premise would require a different assessment.
