## O35 — H, row 82: which occasion the protected condition covers

This is an underspecified attribution, not a demonstrated false result from a fully specified instance of Repair.

### I65-T — The instructed sensor swap

Written by: me

**What I expect.** Making the physical correction necessary will not make it the recipient's diagnostic discovery when the complete interpretation arrived in the instruction.

**The situation.** Elin diagnoses a pump remotely, identifies a stuck sensor and tells a robot which second sensor to use, how to interpret it and which fault it will reveal. The robot follows each instruction correctly. Without its sensor switch the diagnosis cannot be checked. Without Elin's interpretation the robot has no way to choose the fault.

**The thoughtful person's verdict.** The robot performed a necessary check. Elin supplied the diagnosis and its interpretation. Necessity of the robot's action does not make the diagnosis mostly its discovery.

| Audit condition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Component fidelity | The instruction-to-switch and reading-to-choice connections distinguish executing the supplied interpretation from constructing it; no complete explanatory transport is provided. |
| Question fidelity | The attribution concerns who supplied the diagnostic interpretation, not whose physical action was necessary to carry out the check. |
| Non-circular dependence | The incoming instruction contains the interpretation before the robot acts, so robot origination is not inferred from the correct result. |
| Non-vacuity | The described history permits the contrast between supplied interpretation and recipient reinterpretation; a full Account contract is otherwise empty in this case. |

**The theory's verdict under the earlier version with R2, S62 and I64.** The robot executes the supplied diagnostic work; Elin supplies the interpretation. Earlier X and R2 I do not turn this transfer into robot origination. I64 does not award the robot comparative priority from its necessary action.

**Do they differ.** No for the stated division and the rejected inference.

**Best reply on the theory's behalf.** The clause requires consideration of the work attributed; the work here includes a fully supplied interpretation, unlike O27's recipient-originated reinterpretation.

**Does the difference survive the reply.** No contrary verdict survives, while the clause still supplies no general comparative ordering when two participants each make necessary original contributions.

**How I know each claim.** The situation and verdict are written by me as an attack, not independently supplied evidence. The assessment is worked out from the stipulated message and action, earlier X and R2 I.

**What it gives up.** Retaining I64 leaves mostly as a substantive comparative judgement requiring more than a presence test for reasons. It gives up any claim that the last correction, a novel third answer, sheer necessity or the count of actions always determines dominance. O27's reason can be persuasive without its persuasiveness having been derived by this clause. No numerical rule or blanket priority for either expert or robot is introduced.

**Whether the earlier theory already said this.** Earlier Part X, “Understanding, construction, and origin,” Construction, already says (S64-E01):

> **Construction.** \(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an actual subhistory owned by \(s\) and delimited at \(e\) prepares a represented organization for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers. A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting representation. Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not.

This is a distinction among actual processes and incoming content. It is not a comparative measure of the whole achievement. I64 retains that distinction in assisted diagnosis and makes the missing comparative judgement visible. Earlier XII's ownership condition (S64-E04) supplies no ranking rule either. The earlier theory's express lack of a ranking of thinkers is consistent with leaving this extension unfilled; it is not a proof that every ordinary comparative attribution is meaningless.

## H65 — six-field fix card for the temporal scope of protection

**The proposed or changed clause.** Append this clarification to the applicable protection portion of R2 H and S62 H. It does not impose protection throughout every repair history, alter the earlier Repair formula, or choose an obligation for O35. Each sentence is mine.

> [yours] A protected obligation is assessed as the <u>same stated condition</u> over the <u>same stated occasions</u> in the before-and-after comparison; a claim extending protection across the intervening history must include that <u>temporal extent</u> in the condition.
>
> [yours] An interruption is a loss relative to that obligation exactly when it makes the condition fail on an <u>occasion the condition covers</u>; interruption and lack of use alone do not choose the protected condition.
>
> [yours] Where the case supplies no condition or scope <u>sufficient to settle</u> whether the interruption violates protection, leave that verdict unsettled while recording the interruption; a later choice of a <u>different condition</u> is a new comparison, not a vindication of the earlier one.

**The case that forced it.** O35's four-second interruption does not fit the inherited pair's automatic inference from stopped tap to lost protection while preserving the fixed thoughtful verdict. The missing item is the protected condition and when it must hold. Earlier XI compares fixed obligations at two states; a claim about continuous availability is a further specification, not something supplied by the word protected. The clause exposes that input rather than granting a retrospective exception because nobody used the tap.

**Two cases it must leave alone.** O13, written by an outside author, must retain Ben's actual stopping contribution, Ana's correct unused explanation and the absence of an explanation-guided repair. A condition on protection cannot erase those events or manufacture an epistemic repair. O26, written by an outside author, must retain the two actual feed contributions and their measured division. Neither case supplies an intermediate protected-service conflict for H65 to settle.

**Its weakest sentence, read as another author's.** The second sentence in the box might be misread as permission to make every unused failure harmless. The following attack fixes continuous availability expressly, so lack of demand cannot change the obligation.

### H65-T — Four seconds under a stated continuous condition

Written by: me

**What I expect.** An expressly continuous protected condition will fail during the interruption even if nobody tries to use the water.

**The situation.** Before repairing a washer, Mira states the protected condition: the tap must be able to supply water at every instant of the repair. Switching the mains leaves it unable to supply water for four seconds. Nobody opens it then. At the end it works as before, and Mira records the interruption.

**The thoughtful person's verdict.** The stated protection failed for four seconds. Nobody's actual use was frustrated, but that is a different condition. The final restoration does not make continuous availability true of the repair.

| Audit condition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Component fidelity | The four-second inability is a stipulated supply fact; the case leaves a full transport and the washer's explanatory mechanism empty. |
| Question fidelity | The stated question protects availability at every instant, so replacing it with actual-use protection would change the obligation. |
| Non-circular dependence | The recorded interruption defeats continuous availability without using the desired verdict as a premise. |
| Non-vacuity | The fixed condition distinguishes a repair with an available temporary supply from one with the recorded gap; no complete Account claim is supplied. |

**The theory's verdict under the earlier version with R2, S62 and H65.** This repair does not preserve the expressly continuous protected condition. The endpoint restoration can satisfy a separate endpoint condition but cannot answer this history-wide claim. Earlier XI accepts the fixed obligation as an input; H65 does not replace it with a weaker one.

**Do they differ.** No on the protection claim.

**Best reply on the theory's behalf.** The earlier formula need not be construed as automatically imposing history-wide protection; the case has expressly supplied it. States can carry the relevant history, and the condition can evaluate it, without redefining every protected obligation in the theory.

**Does the difference survive the reply.** No; the fixed condition prevents the no-use escape.

**How I know each claim.** The situation and verdict are written by me. The result is worked out from its explicit condition and interruption, earlier II's allowance for history-valued ports, and earlier XI's fixed obligations. It is not an independently supplied test of H65.

**What it gives up.** H65 does not settle O35 without the missing input. It gives up an automatic equation of interruption with protected loss and an automatic exemption for unused service. It derives neither the worth of uninterrupted availability nor a best temporal scope. An owner-supplied follow-up can fix the obligation; it must be recorded as a new supplied condition, not inserted silently into the frozen O35 situation.

**Whether the earlier theory already said this.** Earlier Part XI, “Progress, knowledge, and the normative,” Repair, states (S64-E03):

> **Repair.** For claimed obligations \(O\) and protected obligations \(P\), fixed for the comparison,
>
> \[
> \operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\iff\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\land\forall r\in P[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]\land\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O). \tag{P}
> \]

Read aloud: some claimed obligation goes from unmet to met, each protected obligation that held before still holds afterwards, and the credited change produced the claimed improvement. This does not, without the content of the obligation, assert that every physical variable stays unchanged between those states.

The immediately following passage says (S62 Q09):

> (P) does not rank alternatives. Losses outside \(P\) must be exposed.

This is a missing declared input in O35, not a demonstrated defect in the formal satisfaction condition. The fixed-obligation comparison and exposure of losses are already present. H65's explicit separation of endpoint, actual-use and continuous conditions is a clarification motivated by O35. It supplies no new ranking and does not derive the selected condition's value. Unlike a clause that simply declares every four-second unused interruption harmless, it remains answerable to H65-T.
