## 2. For the owner: the flagged candidates in plain words

Each: one sentence, an everyday example, and the past decision it seems to go against, in the owner's words.

**PB-C2.** *In one sentence:* a question only counts if every change it asks about is one the world could actually bring about. *Example:* "why would this bridge sag if its steel were twice as heavy?" asks about a change nobody can make to that bridge, so on this reading nothing answers it. On the reading that the changes in your cases cannot be made to the thing, your two-part sign and your weathervane explanations stop counting; if the day and the wind are read as circumstances rather than changes, nothing moves. *Seems to go against* S25: "It has nothing to do with explanation."; and, on that reading, S44: "In either case, it is an explanation." and S41 Q15: "Yes, it can be explained".

**PB-C5.** *In one sentence:* a question must ask about at least one change made to the thing itself, not only about different circumstances around it. *Example:* "why is the lamp on when I flip the switch?" counts; "why is the lamp on at night and off at noon?" alone does not. On the reading that Tuesday and the north wind are circumstances of your sign and your vane, not changes made to them, neither your two-part sign explanation nor the vane's counts; on the reading that they are changes, nothing moves. *Seems to go against*, on that reading, S44: "In either case, it is an explanation." and S41 Q15: "Yes, it can be explained".

**PB-C9.** *In one sentence:* if some background part of a candidate simply fixes the answer, the candidate is not an explanation at all. *Example:* a recipe card that explains why the cake is sweet by listing the steps, with a note tucked in the back saying "result: sweet", stops counting. *Seems to go against* S45: "Yes, take the test out".

**PB-C10.** *In one sentence:* a candidate whose answer is simply written into one of its parts fails the test of answering the question, so it is not an explanation. *Example:* a table listing "Monday: red, Tuesday: blue" as its only part stops counting. Your two-part sign stays one on the reading that a part is "written in" only if it alone gives the answer everywhere asked; on the other readings it stops being one. *Seems to go against* S45: "Yes, take the test out"; and, on those readings, S44: "In either case, it is an explanation."

**PB-C11.** *In one sentence:* a copy of a whole worked-out result counts as worked out too. *Example:* the student who copies the pendulum formula from a textbook and writes "this stands for the pendulum" has an explanation, because the textbook's author worked it out. *Seems to go against* S41 Q2: "No, not if just declared".

**PB-C13.** *In one sentence:* a link picked by trial counts as picked only if it was tried on every case of the question that has come up; otherwise it counts as simply declared. *Example:* a gardener who kept the watering schedule that worked in spring, though summer had already come round once, has only declared it. An explanation of your sign or your vane reached by trying and keeping what worked, where not every day or wind that came up was tried, would stop being an explanation. *Seems to go against* S41 Q2: "No, not if just declared" (Claude's question set a link "simply declared" against one "found by trial or worked out"); and, for such a history, S44: "In either case, it is an explanation." and S41 Q15: "Yes, it can be explained".

**PB-C14.** *In one sentence:* a link counts as found or worked out if anyone who ever held it found or worked it out. *Example:* the student copying the pendulum formula holds the same link the author worked out, so the student has an explanation. *Seems to go against* S41 Q2: "No, not if just declared".

**PB-C15.** *In one sentence:* only what happened inside the student's own reach counts in the story of the student's link. *Example:* the textbook sits outside the student's boundary, so the student who checked the copied formula on one swing counts as having found it by trial (on the reading that the textbook is outside that boundary). *Seems to go against*, on that reading, S41 Q2: "No, not if just declared".
