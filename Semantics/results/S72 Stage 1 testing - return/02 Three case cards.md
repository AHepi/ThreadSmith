## O50 — The same afternoon, three achievements
Written by: an outside author

**What I expect.** Keeping the three credited achievements separate will locate the shared reinterpretation and the robot's finding while leaving I64's comparative judgement about the whole inquiry unresolved.

**Aimed at.** I, row 96 and I64: the capacity or achievement being attributed, and the difference between locating contributions and ranking their importance.

**The situation.** The expert sends two candidate diagnoses and the separating test. The result fits neither. The robot notices the sensor assumption; the expert proposes the swap; the robot does it and finds the third fault. Three things were achieved that afternoon: the reinterpretation of the test, the discovery of the fault, and the completed inquiry from first question to answer.

**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Credit each achievement on its own. The reinterpretation was theirs together. The discovery was the robot's: it ran the test and found the fault. The inquiry was mostly the expert's: the expert framed it, supplied the candidates and the test, and supplied the fix. One word for all three would be wrong for at least two of them.

| Non-vacuity | A failed test and changed treatment of its sensor assumption supply meaningful contrasts, but a complete physically admitted change list and retained-capability evidence are unprovided. [T-E43] [T-E22] |

## O51 — The module in the casing
Written by: an outside author

**What I expect.** The declared controller-and-routines boundary will keep the module's diagnostic work outside the attributed system despite its position inside the shell.

**Aimed at.** I, row 93: spatial position is insufficient to settle membership in a declared continuing system.

**The situation.** A controller runs a diagnostic module bought from a supplier and fitted on a removable processor inside its own shell. The system boundary declared at the start names the controller and its routines and says nothing about the module. The module produces today's diagnosis.

**The thoughtful person's verdict.** The diagnosis was produced inside the shell and outside the declared boundary. Where the boundary was drawn decides it, and the boundary was drawn without the module. Being inside the casing changes nothing.

| Audit condition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Component fidelity | The module's execution produces today's diagnosis, while the declaration names the controller and its routines; the described membership and execution facts are kept separate, and a full diagnostic transport is unprovided. [T-E22] [T-E35] [T-R29] |
| Question fidelity | The claim concerns the declared system boundary, not the shell's physical interior or the commercial owner of the module. [T-E35] [T-R29] |
| Non-circular dependence | The prior boundary and identified executing module fix the attribution without reasoning backwards from success or location to inclusion. [T-E22] [T-R29] |
| Non-vacuity | Including versus excluding the same module in a declared boundary would change the attribution while leaving its location and output fixed; no full Account or future capability witness is supplied. [T-E35] [T-E43] [T-R29] |

**The theory's verdict under the earlier version with the proposals.** The supplied attribution excludes the module. Its diagnosis therefore arises outside that boundary even though the process is physically inside the shell. R2 I explicitly requires inclusion of delegated processes in the stated boundary and rejects spatial enclosure as sufficient; earlier XII and XIV require claims to retain their stated system and continuity indices. Neither buying the module nor the controller's calling it silently includes its diagnostic work. H64, I64 and H65 supply no contrary boundary rule. [T-E22] [T-E35] [T-R29]

**Do they differ?** No on the case's supplied boundary and attribution.

**Best reply on the theory's behalf.** A removable routine may in a different declared system count as part of that system; O30 already allows an included uploaded routine's current execution to belong to the robot. Here the outside verdict expressly fixes the module as excluded. The conclusion follows that supplied declaration, not a general rule that every separately purchased or removable process must be external. [T-E35] [T-R29]

**Does the difference survive the reply?** No. A new declaration including the module would support a new attribution, not change this fixed one. [T-E35] [T-R29]

**How I know each claim.** The module, its execution and the named boundary come from the outside situation; the supplied thoughtful verdict disambiguates the omission as exclusion. The reading applies earlier XII/XIV and R2 I's full ownership paragraph, not an invented casing criterion. [T-E22] [T-E35] [T-R29]

**Stage B row, side, agreement and mark.** I row 93: the inside-casing but outside-declared-system side. The full fixed verdict agrees. **PLACED.** This new coverage does not change what was included in O30 or enlarge O41's robot-only boundary.

## O52 — The route that started and was stopped
Written by: an outside author

**What I expect.** The recorded jam and closure will distinguish a started route from a stopping contribution without requiring a meter or unequal start times.

**Aimed at.** H, row 90 and H64: a historical contribution established by operation, rather than mere start, temporal priority or a divided meter reading.

**The situation.** Two routes to stopping the leak start in the same moment: Ana's bypass begins to open, Ben's valve begins to close. Ana's bypass jams a quarter open and takes nothing; Ben's valve closes and the flow stops. The operating log records both starts, the jam, and the closure, before any meter is read.

**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Ben stopped it. Ana's route started and did no stopping. The log says so without the meter.

| Audit condition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Component fidelity | The bypass's recorded jam and taking nothing distinguish its ineffective start from the valve's completed closure and the stopping flow; no unseen contribution is assigned to the bypass. [T-E20] [T-R35] |
| Question fidelity | The question is which route actually stopped this leak, not which one started, could stop a leak in isolation or later received a meter reading. [T-E03] [T-R35] |
| Non-circular dependence | The stated operating history supplies the stopping distinction independently of the desired division of credit; an aggregate dry-pipe result alone would not supply these facts. [T-E20] [T-R15] |
| Non-vacuity | Removing the jam so that the same bypass actually carries stopping flow would make a substantive contribution contrast; the case itself supplies no full explanatory contract or epistemic deployment claim. [T-E43] [T-R35] |

**The theory's verdict under the earlier version with the proposals.** Ben's completed closure stopped the leak; Ana's bypass started but made no stopping contribution. The independent operating facts, earlier IX's actual history, earlier XI's production requirement, and R2 F/H distinguish these events. H64 accepts this historical argument without requiring a meter, a unique numerical share or one route to start first. Physical stopping does not by itself establish repair through an explanation. **Do they differ?** No on stopping attribution or the sufficiency of the supplied operating record.

**Best reply on the theory's behalf.** An uninterpreted timestamp of a start would be insufficient, but the stipulated log records the operative jam and closure in the case's given route arrangement. No competing stopping event remains in that supplied history. H64 says complete records need not settle every division; it does not require withholding an exclusive attribution that this history establishes. The ordinary interpretation of the operating log is being supplied by the case, not inferred from a bare output alone. [T-E20] [T-R35] [T-R15]

**Does the difference survive the reply?** No. Requiring a meter reading or actual temporal priority would add a condition absent from H64 and would import earlier test examples as mandatory rules.

**How I know each claim.** The starts, jam, no-take bypass, valve closure, stopping flow and available log are outside stipulations. The verdict is worked out from that history and the named earlier passages with R2 F/H; no additional physical experiment or meter observation is claimed. [T-E20] [T-E16] [T-R35] [T-R15]
