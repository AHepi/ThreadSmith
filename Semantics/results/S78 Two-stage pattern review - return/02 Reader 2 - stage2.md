**Verdict:** makes sense with repairs

The split does the thing the owner asked for, and on its one completed round it earned its keep: Stage 1 marked O24 CHANGED, Stage 2 worked the case from the source and called the change a narrowing of a theorem's reach rather than a change to the case's verdict, and S75 accepted that, so the new version was built on one case instead of two. The weak joint is the row sample. Its mandatory part fires on Stage 1's own labels, so the error the round exists to prevent, a row that should be CHANGED and is written SAME, is reachable only by the fixed every-fifth sequence; O37 and O49 make the very judgement the audit had to overturn on O24, in the opposite direction, and neither was audited. The reference standard is the part nobody may touch: the thoughtful person's verdict is Claude's, is fixed against both agents by instruction, and has no mark that could report it wrong. With the sample retargeted, the auditor's reading written before it opens Stage 1's row, and the cases put out of the determiner's hands, the pattern would be considerably harder to vary; without them, a right theory failed by a wrong reference verdict, and a wrong reading on an unsampled row, both pass through.

## Whole-pattern checks

**Flip.** Partly yes, and the partly is the point. Had file 10 been badly wrong, column 6 would have had to fill with disagreements, since it asks, for every one of 52 rows, either what in the bare version gives the wrong verdict, quoted, or SILENT. Every disagreeing row is then mandatory for the audit. So a broad wrongness has a route: it shows as disagreements, they are all audited in full, and the draft list carries them. In S72 that column came back 42 rows of 'No disagreement', 8 SILENT and 2 problems, which is what a mostly-right theory looks like and also what a tired tester looks like; the sample is the only thing that tells those apart, and it covers one row in five. A narrow wrongness, one row deep, has a route only if Stage 1 notices it. The returns would have looked different under a badly wrong theory in one respect I can point at: the eight SILENT rows would not all cluster on the same two judgement groups (credit division and 'mostly'), and the judgement gauge would have moved off seven.

**Direction.** S72 ran forward. The instruction froze the question (does any amendment change a verdict), named the one place already known from S70, asked for every such place, and the round came back with two, of which one was cut by the audit; the determination followed the returns rather than leading them, and it sided against the tester twice. S76 as set up leans backward: the expected result is printed in the tester's pack, the file 10 baseline is copied from the previous round rather than re-derived, and the theory under test was written by the determiner from the previous round's result. The forward shape is recoverable by withholding the prediction and the baseline column from the tester.

**Answer hidden in the starting points.** Three of the four inputs come from one hand. The cases are Claude's (log 28, 'Claude's four outside cases'; log S70, 'all written by Claude with the verdict fixed first'), the thoughtful person's verdicts are Claude's and are fixed against both agents by instruction, the instructions are Claude's, and at S76 the theory under test is Claude's. What is not Claude's: file 10 itself, which is the owner's; R2, which is an earlier agent's; and the two agents' readings. So the answer is not sitting in the starting points in the crude sense, because the theory's own text is the thing being read and the two agents can and did disagree with the reading put to them. It is sitting there in one specific place: the reference verdict. Credit where due, the verdicts are written before the theory's verdict is computed, and the one outside reading in the record (log S73, a judge reading file 10 sentence by sentence with no cases) reached the same Derivation 3 finding independently, which is the strongest evidence in the whole record that the result is not an artefact of the case set. That reading is explicitly not held by the project.

**Where the jobs came from.** Given by the owner and fixed: the split into testing and audit, two agents, Claude determining (S7); fresh agent per stage and zips (S5, S2); push to main (S6); positive wording (decision 20); file 10 as authority (S4). Given by the instructions themselves: find every place an amendment changes a verdict; build only from rows that survive; check every quotation; hold back lines resting on disputed rows or unverified quotations and say so; state how many rows were audited and how many left; keep every situation and verdict as written. Added by me, and each finding that rests on one says so: a wrong theory must have a route to being reported wrong rather than absorbed; the audit must be able to catch Stage 1's error in the direction that costs a claim change; the reference standard must be checkable by someone who could contradict it; a finding must not move when only the sampling rule moves; the agent under audit should not choose what is tested next.

**Pairs that pull.** Fresh agent with no memory against a pack that must carry every earlier round: the pack for S76-2 carries nine returns plus two theory files plus the workflow, and it grows each round. Which gives way is visible already: the parts the instruction names in full got full attention (119 quotations checked, 19 rows worked from source with exact line ranges) and the parts it did not name got a recount (the coverage ledger, 'mechanically recounted rather than independently re-audited row by row'). What would show the line is in the wrong place: a VERBATIM mark on a passage that differs, or a row audit that quotes a passage at a wrong line. I looked for both and found neither. Positive wording against exactness: the sampling clause is positive and self-contradictory ('of the remaining rows, every fifth one counting from O1 (O1, O6, O11 ...)') because a negative ('not counting rows already selected') was unavailable. Audit sampling against cost: 19 rows of 52 is the whole trade, and it gives way on the direction of error that matters. Keeping every verdict as written against finding what is wrong: the reference wins by instruction, every time. The auditor building the fix card, which is right, against nobody checking the auditor's new claim in the same round, which is only partly answered by the next round's test.

**Catch-alls and their gauges.** PARKED: empty in both returns and in the four before them, 'PARKED LIST EMPTY.' Its gauge is whether a later return smuggles in a score or a graph; none did, so it is swallowing nothing visible and is idle rather than dangerous. SILENT: the real catch-all, eight of 52 rows, and it does have a gauge, the judgement-phrase list of heading (4), which names seven groups (H88, H82, C, G80, H90, I96, A), each with a mark and a source, counted at the start and end of the round. What the gauge lacks is a reading that would worry anyone: it has stood at seven since S62 and nothing says at what point a group that four cases cannot close becomes a defect of the theory rather than an input it declares. 'Held back by the audit': a good catch-all, because it has content (the S70 CLAIM line's O24 warrant) and prints its empty category as empty. The every-fifth sample is not a gauge at all; it is a sample, and S75 reads it correctly as 'reason to trust the rest and not proof of it', which is the honest form of a claim that a fixed, announced, one-in-five, label-triggered sample cannot support. Declared inputs: these are the theory's own catch-all rather than the method's, and the gauge tracks them, which is the right place for them.

**What the change list leaves out.** The same 52 cases every round, written by the same author, with the same verdicts, read under the same reading rules, by the same family of agent, against the same sampling offset. What never varies and could: who writes the cases; whether a verdict can be questioned; which rows are sampled; which model runs which stage; whether the tester knows the expected result. The 32 unrecovered Stage B rows have been outstanding since log 41 and are an admitted hole in the change list, stated openly in every return and in S75. The theory's formal apparatus is read 'in ordinary language, as the supplied workflow requires', so a defect reachable only by working the formalism is outside the change list by design; the S73 judge found one by reading the formalism, from outside the method.

**Rivals.** Single stage (S71): told apart by O24. The same agent would have written the row and then the fix card on it, and the change list would have carried two cases where one survives. Two independent testers plus a tiebreak: told apart by a fabricated or mis-sited quotation, which two testers catch only if they happen to cite the same passage, while the audit catches it by construction because Part 3 checks every identifier, not a sample; that full-coverage check is what makes this an audit rather than a duplicate run, and it is the pattern's best part. Cases written blind by an outside author with hidden verdicts: told apart by a case whose thoughtful verdict is wrong, which the present pattern cannot report at all; this is the rival that beats the pattern on the one job it cannot do. Audit by a different model family: told apart by a shared blind spot in reading the theory's formalism, which the present pattern would confirm 19 of 19 rather than catch; no return records which model ran it, so this rival cannot even be told apart from the current arrangement by reading the files. A human adjudicator: no change on the list tells it apart from Claude determining, so at this level it is the same rival; the finer question would be whether the adjudicator wrote the cases. A pre-registered results template: S76 has one, and it is the pattern's strongest single addition since the split; it is undone by being printed in the tester's pack.

**Where the parts came from.** Fixed by the owner, untested here: the split, two agents, Claude determining, fresh agents, zips, push to main, positive wording, file 10 as authority. Built, and each with the thing that forced it in view: the quotation check, built from the failure recorded in the project's traps ('log 57 checked four of its quotations against 10 and two were absent from 10'); the identifier on every verdict, built to make that check reach the verdicts; the two separate marks for row and sentence, built because a genuine sentence can be a false changer, which is exactly what O24 then was; the held-back list, built so a disputed row leaves a trace instead of vanishing. Fitted: the sampling rate, tuned to what one agent can do in a sitting, with no stated reason for a fifth; the six-field fix card and the case card, inherited from workflows 24 and 30 and shaped round earlier rounds. Asserted: that the case author is 'an outside author'; that the thoughtful person's verdicts are right; that the two stages are two agents, which is the owner's fixed practice and leaves no trace in any return.
