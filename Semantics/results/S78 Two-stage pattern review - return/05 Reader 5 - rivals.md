## The question, frozen
Does the two-stage pattern - Stage 1 reads the theory bare against 52 fixed case cards with an identifier on every verdict, Stage 2 audits that return row by row against the sources and builds the fix card and draft list from what survives, and Claude then reads both and determines - make sense as a way of finding what is wrong with the theory? Frozen over the rounds the record shows it used or planned for (S72, run and returned; S76, written and packed), against the theory as it stands in file 10 and as rewritten in file 11, with the owner's standing choices held fixed (push to main only, a fresh agent per step, returns held unchanged, zips, Claude determines). Three things are asked and all three answered: whether each part does inside the run what the instruction says it does, and whether the S72 returns show it doing so; whether the pattern can fail a wrong theory and pass a right one, with a route by which a wrong theory gets reported wrong rather than absorbed by a reading, a PARKED file or a held-back list; and whether each part is held in place by a job, so that swapping a part for its near neighbour breaks something.

## Overall
**Verdict:** makes sense with repairs

The two-stage pattern is a sound checking pattern carrying the name of a finding pattern, and the gap between those two is where its trouble is. As a check it works and the returns show it working: Stage 1 put an identifier on every verdict, Stage 2 checked 119 quotations, and I confirmed by hand that all 43 file-10 quotations resolve verbatim in the authority. The split earned its keep, narrowly: it caught one over-marked row (O24) and one over-marked card (O52), which a single agent building its own fix card would have carried forward. But three parts pull the finding capacity out of it. The instruction names, in the pasted part, the two rows the round exists to find, and Stage 1 returned exactly those two out of 52, which the project's own Lesson 50 predicts. The auditor is instructed to print Stage 1's verdict before writing its own and says plainly it was not blind, so 19-of-19 agreement on both verdict columns is one reading read twice. And the audit's sample and summary lists are both conditioned on Stage 1's flags, so the pattern can subtract findings and has never added one. The one real finding of the round, Derivation 3's claim outrunning its proof, was produced by the single-stage S70 round before the split and was corroborated by an outside reader working from file 10 alone; the two-stage round confirmed it and reduced two findings to one. The repairs are small and nearly all positive in wording: strike the naming sentence, reverse the print order, make the sample unpredictable and shape-based, and give the audit a heading where it can report a change Stage 1 missed.
