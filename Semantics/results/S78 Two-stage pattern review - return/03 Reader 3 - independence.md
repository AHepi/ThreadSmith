## The question, frozen
Whether the two-stage testing-then-audit pattern makes sense as a way of finding what is wrong with Claude Fable Semantics, over the rounds run so far (S72 in full, S76 as set up) and over the theory as it stands in files 10 and 11. Three things are asked and all three answered: (1) internally, does each part do what its instruction says, as the S72 returns show; (2) as a test, can the pattern fail a wrong theory and pass a right one, and is there a route by which a wrong theory gets reported as wrong rather than absorbed by a reading, a PARKED file or a held-back list; (3) as an explanation of why this method finds what is wrong with the theory, is each part held in place by a job, and does swapping a part for its neighbour break something. My lens is independence: where in the chain from theory-author to determination can the pattern agree with its author by construction. The question is held still: I am not asking whether the theory is true, nor whether the agents did careful work, nor whether the owner's fixed choices are good ones.

## Overall
**Verdict:** makes sense with repairs

The two-stage pattern does several things it says it does, and does them well: the identifier on every verdict lets a stranger check a row (all 119 resolved, none absent); the quotation check against file 10 is real, and I confirmed three of its entries by hand at lines 569–573, 210 and 38; the AGREE/DISAGREE and CONFIRMED/DISPUTED marks can fire and did, on O24's label and O52's placement; the "Held back by the audit" list has a gauge and was used; the judgement-phrase gauge is reported at start and end. Where it fails is independence, and it fails in the same place at every link. Claude wrote the theory's revisions, wrote O15 to O52 with the thoughtful person's verdict fixed first, wrote both stage instructions, decides what goes in each pack, reads both returns, and determines the result — and both instructions name the round's answer in their second paragraph ("O24 and O48 against Derivation 3"), while Stage 2's Part 4 pre-titles the fix card, names O48 as the forcing case and assigns O24 the control role that Stage 2 then gives it. Stage 2 is handed Stage 1's whole return and says in writing that its "expectation" was not blind; it returned 19 AGREE and 0 DISAGREE on both verdict columns. Three of the four audit triggers are computed from Stage 1's own output, so Stage 1's misses are reachable only by a fixed one-in-five sample, and the 33 unaudited rows are carried forward verbatim as S76's baseline for file 10. The bare theory disagrees with the thoughtful person on nought of 52 cases — which, against verdicts written by the theory's own reviser and forbidden to be questioned, is the skill's "passing your own test" in plain sight. The reach is exactly the case set: R2 J's second qualification, the missing initial-error term in Part VIII, is quoted verbatim in every citation both agents used, was named unresolved by Stage 2, is absent from S75, and file 11 carries file 10's sentence unchanged at line 361. The repairs are small and specific: take the answer out of the packs, make the deliverables conditional, blind one sample on both sides, and run the pattern once on a theory known to be wrong.

## Whole-pattern checks

**Flip.** Had file 10 been badly wrong in a way its author did not anticipate, the same two-stage run would not have reported it. The route by which a wrong theory gets reported is Stage 1's column 6 — "Where column 3 disagrees with column 2, one sentence: what in the bare earlier version gives the wrong verdict, quoted" — and that branch has never fired in 52 rows; every entry reads "No disagreement" or SILENT.
