## Rulings

**Disagreements (rule 5).** Both closing lines read CHALLENGED, for different reasons and with different wordings: Atria on "established" and internal failures; Mimo on the "nor do" parse, the "as when" illustration and the declaration. On (d), Atria says RIGHT and Mimo RIGHT, WITH A LOSS. On the declaration's silence about "offered for the whole of p", Atria (point 2) finds no error and Mimo (point 3(i)) an omission. On (a) both say FAITHFUL, and on (c2) both say UNSETTLED BY THE TEXT. Each point is ruled below on the texts; the number of replies on a side decides nothing.

### Ruling 1: the parse of "and nor do two candidates" (Mimo, point 1): KEEP

"X conflict at no pair, and nor do Y" is the ordinary English construction in which "nor" carries the negation over to the second subject: Y do not conflict at any pair either, as "cats eat no grass, and nor do lions" says that lions eat none. English does not give the construction the reading on which "nor" negates the already negative phrase ("do not conflict at no pair", hence "conflict at some pair"), literally or otherwise. So the sentence says what the declaration says without help from its last clause, and "neither pair is a pair of rivals" confirms it. No wording is needed. Mimo's rewording in its replacement says the same in more words.

### Ruling 2: the "as when" illustration of conflict (Mimo, point 2): FIX (NEW)

*The challenge is upheld.* L315 defines conflict at a pair by two disjuncts. It attaches "as when two of their active components with one anchor have different relations there" to the second, whose two conjuncts are that each candidate could meet (F1), (F2) and (A) there under some admitted relations of the target, and that no such relations let both. "As when" offers the clause as an instance of that condition, but it guarantees only the second conjunct. Mimo's counterexample is sound (qualified above only as to the query). Where one candidate can never meet (F1) at the pair, because two of its own components share an anchor with different relations, the first conjunct fails, the answers can agree, and neither disjunct holds; yet the clause's description is met. Read as the text offers it, the illustration is then false. It also pulls against the paragraph's own recoding sentence. Take such a candidate and its rewriting by a structure-preserving bijection that takes its transport with it: a component of each has one anchor and a different relation, so by the illustration the two conflict, while the recoding sentence says they "conflict at no pair". Two outside (d) paragraphs read the clause as a condition that can "fire" (Atria F) or that makes candidates "competitors" (Mimo H), so the reading is not strained. The entry's own REASON ("Conflict") excludes exactly this case from the second route: "A candidate that could not meet the conditions at a pair whatever the target does is excluded from the second route: its failure is its own, and it would otherwise conflict with its own relabelling (03a, r2)."

*Mimo's repair is not taken.* A separate disjunct, "when two of their active components have one anchor and different relations there", makes the clash enough for conflict whatever else holds. A candidate whose own components clash then conflicts with its own rewriting (take a component of each). In Mimo's replacement this contradicts the next sentence, "Two candidates that differ only in how they are written, … do not conflict at any pair". The repair also restores the form that the entry's REASON records as refuted (03a, r2), and it would change what the theory claims without the declaration saying so.

*The smallest change* keeps the illustration and makes it illustrate only the conjunct it entails. In NEW (the paragraph "Rivals"), replace

```text
and no such relations let both, as when two of their active components with one anchor have different relations there.
```

with

```text
and no such relations let both, as none do when two of their active components with one anchor have different relations there.
```
