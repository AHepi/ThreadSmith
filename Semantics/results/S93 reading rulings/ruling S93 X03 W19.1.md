## Rulings

### Challenge 1: no index or value identification, and "defines" is too strong (Mimo point 1 against Atria point 1): **KEEP**

- **Index (i).** Atria is right. The wording does not compare \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)\) and \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau'[C]}(k')\) as sets of triples. It compares them "read on \(C\) through \(\tau\) and \(\tau'\)", which indexes both by \((a,b)\in C\). L564 uses the same phrase ("read on \(C\)"), and L554 uses \(\tau[C]\) as the same shorthand, with \(\sigma\) understood. The typing sentence names \(\sigma\) one clause before. Mimo's own wording keeps the phrase as its gloss ("this is their signatures read on \(C\) through \(\tau\) and \(\tau'\)"), which concedes that the phrase names the index. The wording already says what Mimo says it must say.
- **Values (ii).** The unchanged first sentence of L119 already requires "a bijection of their footprints under which \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)\) and \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j')\) coincide". Inside one organization the two footprints are different ports, and each port has its own domain: "\(V\) is a set of ports, each with a nonempty value domain \(X_v\)" (L91). Mimo's contrast ("Inside one organization the shared \(X_v\) can be read in") does not hold. The question of values arises there in the same way, and the text answers it in the same way. Coincidence under a renaming of ports is equality of the relations, which requires corresponding ports to take values in one set. Read that way across two organizations, "coincide" has a truth value. It is false when the domains differ, as with heights and dial labels, and O9's fixed verdict needs those components apart. Derivation 2(ii) is proved on this reading. (F1) at L236 equates a projection of \(D\)'s solutions with \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\), so whatever the port translation does with values in (F1), the composed translation at L564 does too. X03's "footprint bijection" is exactly as precise as Part IV's "port translation" (L189) and L564's "footprint bijection". The open question is whether a port translation may carry a map of values. The change list already carries it forward at l.239, "Port translations and values", as a Part IV question. It bears on L119's first sentence, (F1) and L564 as much as on X03. X03 did not introduce it and cannot settle it alone.
- **"Requires no shared anchor" (iii).** That is true and intended. The definition is general, and Derivation 2's shared anchor is a sufficient condition for it to hold, not part of its meaning. Since (ii) fails, the declaration's "Part II now defines when…" stands.
- **Mimo's replacement wording is not adopted.**
  - "Correspond under one bijection of \(V_k\) onto \(V_{k'}\) that carries the value domain of each port of one onto the value domain of the other" replaces a relation the text defines, "coincide", with one it does not, "correspond". A bijection of ports carries a value domain onto another only together with a map of values, which the wording neither supplies nor restricts.
  - If the wording admits a map of values, the relation across candidates becomes coarser than the one within an organization. Take two components on the same real-valued ports, one with \(y=x\) and one with \(y=2x\). They are not of one kind under L119's first sentence, but a map rescaling \(y\) makes them so. On that reading Mimo's own clause "whose classes are kinds in the same sense" would be false. The relation would also stop reducing to (K)'s when the two candidates are one (Atria, point 3). It would settle Part IV's question of values for kinds only, leaving (F1) and L564 as they are. That is a change of claim that no reply argues for and no declaration states.
  - If the wording admits no map of values, it adds nothing to "coincide".
  - The replacement also narrows the definition to "one question", which is not needed: L113 fixes \(D\) and \(C\), and Derivation 2 states "the same \(p\)" where it uses it. It adds a claim about classes (challenge 3) and rewrites the settled sentence whole. That goes beyond the smallest change for any challenge upheld here.
