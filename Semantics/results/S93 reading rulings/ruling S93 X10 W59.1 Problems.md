**Problems.** Two rivals that both fit what is established for an assessor pose, for that assessor, a **problem for \(p\)**: a conflict between ideas that what the assessor has established has not settled. ## Rulings

**1. Mimo's optional phrase "For that assessor" (Mimo B point 5). KEEP.** A phrase offered as optional is kept out unless the wording without it is false, incoherent or misleading, and it is none of these. The paragraph's first sentence defines "pose … a problem" with the index written in ("pose, for that assessor, a problem for \(p\)"), and "fits", the only way into a problem, is defined only for an assessor (L315). "Easy to vary" is defined as posing "a problem of the second kind", "in the sense used here", so the index and the question come with the defined term. The next sentence ("the term says nothing about which of them is right") and the one after (a criticism "must supply such a rival") leave no room for reading it as an absolute mark on the candidate. The words offered would also be worse where they would stand: "that assessor" has no antecedent in that sentence, since the one assessor named is in the paragraph's first sentence, six sentences back, before cases (i) and (ii). Mimo itself says the text is not incoherent without them. No change; the declaration is unaffected.

**2. (c2): the two readers disagree, UNSETTLED BY THE TEXT (Atria) against ESTABLISHED (Mimo). KEEP on X10.** Under rules 5 and 6 the question here is whether the present wording states the matter truly. Whether the text should settle it, and which way, is the owner's; it is set out below.

- *Atria's side.* "Established" runs through usable receipts, which are derivation trees over evidence leaves, each "a reference to an event with an interpreted claim" (L397). The gloss on (K3) ties a result that tells against a candidate to "the test that yields it" (L315). Part VIII's pattern points at the world (L369). On the other hand, an examination is an occurrence and so could be a leaf. Nothing says either way. It should be settled at L315 or in Part IX, so that such a failure counts, and X10 needs no change.
- *Mimo's side.* The fit clause reaches "a condition of (E)" (L315), and (E) includes non-circular dependence, which no test of the world shows. L315 itself sets "(F1), (F2) and (A)" apart from "the rest of (E) on \(C\)". Receipts are general over propositions ("For \(\phi\)", L397). Part VIII already establishes an exclusion "from the candidate's own answer there" (L369). Were it otherwise, "until what is established leaves at most one of them fitting" would be idle for problems of the second kind.
- *Ruling between them, on what the text states.* No sentence of draft 4 says that a failure found by examining a candidate is a result established for an assessor, and none says it is not; a search of the text for "establish", "examin", "inspect" and "verif" finds nothing further. Mimo's points show that the definitions admit that reading and lean toward it. The fit clause names every condition of (E). "Result" is unrestricted. L369 already lets a candidate's own content serve as a premise of what is established. But they do not show that the text states it. L369's pattern still rests on a receipt for the target's answer. The (K3) gloss at L315 and the evidence-leaf sentence at L397 are worded for tests and events, which leaves a reader the room Atria describes. Mimo's last argument is also overstated. Even on the narrow reading, a problem of the second kind closes when a test inside \(C\) shows one rival's own failure of (F1), (F2) or (A), which L317 itself provides for ("only for a failure of its own"). The closing clause is idle only where a rival's sole failure is one that no test shows. As an account of what the text now states, **Atria's "unsettled by the text" is the more exact**. Mimo's "established" is the reading the definitions best support, not one the text states.
- *Whether L317 states the matter truly.* It does, on either reading, and it takes no side between them. Kind (i)'s claim ("since afterwards at most one of them fits") holds on the narrow reading as well: after the test, the rival that cannot meet the conditions under the established relations is shown failing them from the established result and its own content, the pattern L369 already counts as established. Kind (ii)'s three claims are true on both readings. The closing clause states the condition under which a problem ends and says nothing about how it can be met. Nowhere does L317 say that a test is the only way a problem ends. So O1 (Atria A: the named solver "cannot catch a circularity") is true of the test and is no defect of L317, which names the test as what solves a kind-(i) problem and not as the only way any problem ends. Whatever the owner decides, L317 needs no change: the settling words proposed by Atria A, Atria B, Mimo A, Mimo B and Mimo C all stand at L315 or in Part IX, not in L317, and they are X09's checker's (tabulation §6.2). No FIX, and no change to the declaration.

**(c1): whether "easy to vary" should point at one side (the tilt against the myth on the Greeks' question).** *Does the present wording state the matter truly?* Yes. The exact form differs a little from the brief's paraphrase and from both replies' paragraphs. On the Greeks' question \(p\), suppose the tilt and the myth are rivals (one offered in place of the other, conflicting only at admitted pairs outside \(C\)) and both fit what an assessor has established. A finer contract "makes a new question", on which they pose a problem of the first kind, so the tilt is not easy to vary relative to the myth *there*. On \(p\) itself the problem "stands until what is established leaves at most one of them fitting". Results at southern pairs lie outside \(p\)'s contract and show no failure of (E) on it, because a candidate "outside \(C\) … claims nothing on \(p\)" (L315). So even after the southern seasons are established, on the Greeks' question the tilt stays easy to vary relative to the myth. That lasts until something established shows the myth failing (E) on the Greeks' own contract, for instance its non-circular failure (N3's "written into the terms of the bargain"), *if* such a finding counts as established. That condition is (c2). The entry's LOSS declares the consequence ("the tilt on the Greek question included").
