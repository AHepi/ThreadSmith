## 0. The answer, in short

The owner asked two things (S60): what had to be removed from the Avida programs that did the logic tasks for them to stop, and what that evolved information is, given the Avida execution environment.

**What had to be removed.** For each task, in 98.0% of the sequence-task pairs (each distinct instruction sequence with each task it performs; 98.6% counted by Avida program) [Xb5, Xc1] among the programs that perform it and replicate, **one instruction** is enough: there are sites whose single ablation stops the task performance while program replication goes on. These are the program's **required instructions** for that task: on average 5 (NOT, NAND) to 27 (XOR) per program. They are, by kind, mostly `IO` (the instruction that writes a number out and reads the next number in), `nand` (the one logic instruction), `nop-C` and `nop-A` placed right after an instruction (they choose which register it acts on), `swap`, `push`, `pop` (they move numbers between registers and stacks), and for some tasks `add`, `sub`, `inc`, `dec`. By role on Avida's virtual CPU: the reads of the input numbers, the `nand` steps and arithmetic that combine them, the moves and register choices that wire one step to the next, the output write, and **extra `IO` reads that are not on the path at all but set which input number the later reads receive** (Avida hands the three input numbers out in rotation). In the other 2.0%: 1,573 needed a pair of sites whose single ablations each leave replication (almost always the task has a second route that one ablation leaves standing); 85 more (NOT 41, ORN 44; 91 Avida programs) needed a pair holding one site whose single ablation stops replication, the other site's ablation letting the program replicate again (found by the settling rerun; the search as sent did not try such pairs and counted these 85 as not stopped) [Xb1, Xb2]; two needed a set of three; and 4 (NOT, 4 Avida programs) were stopped by no set found.

**What it is.** In the Avida execution environment, what those instructions make up, when executed, is a **small fixed circuit of the instruction set's operations** from the input reads to the output write: a few `nand` steps, sometimes with `add`, `sub`, `inc`, `dec` steps that on these numbers act bit by bit, applied to the numbers the `IO` channel hands in. For example, the most common circuit of NOT is one `nand` of an input with itself (63% of the NOT programs); of NAND, one `nand` of two inputs (82%); of ORN, `nand(nand(x,y),x)` (69%); of EQU, a five- to eight-step circuit (four circuits cover 81% of the EQU programs). Across all 23,332 distinct instruction sequences of the nine runs, each task has from 35 (XOR) to 404 (OR) distinct circuits (identity steps removed and constant parts folded [Xc6]; 40 to 417 if constants that are neither ZERO nor all ones are told apart by their value [Xc3]), but a few are run by most programs, and each common circuit is realised by many different instruction strings (the top NOT circuit by 352 different strings of route instructions, in 7,959 distinct sequences [Xc6]). Every circuit that could be computed on all eight combinations of input bits (all but those holding an unnamed constant) gives an output Avida's check accepts for its task, save one.

**What it depends on.** It is that circuit only with Avida's meanings for the letters: read the `nand` letter as `nor` and 2% to 84% of the programs still perform a given task, while the rest perform what the circuit then computes (predicted from the trace for 99% of the programs); read it as `and` and 0% to 73% remain; read `IO` as doing nothing and no task is performed by any program (which follows from Avida's rules: numbers enter and leave only through `IO`) [Xa4]; give the 26 letters their meanings in another order (three shufflings tried) and no program replicates or performs anything [Xa3]. With other input numbers of the kind Avida hands out in its worlds, 99% to 100% still perform each task; with fully random 32-bit numbers, 66% to 90%.

The rest of this file gives the numbers, the method, worked examples and what is left over.

**What the programs share across runs.** The same kinds of instruction are required in every run, but their shares differ widely from run to run [Xa7] (in the `.json`, run by run): for NOT, `IO` is 14% to 49% of a run's required sites and `nand` 0% to 42% (in one run the NOT programs make NOT by arithmetic, `sub(ONES, x)`); for XOR, `IO` 15% to 32%; the most frequent role is the gate in some runs, the register choice, the mover or arithmetic in others. On Avida's virtual CPU a gate executes after the reads of the numbers it combines, and the output write last (the data flow requires it); reads, gates and moves can interleave (the XOR, NOR and EQU examples of section 3.1 read an input after a gate has run), and the order of the required instructions outside the route was not checked across programs [Xa8]. The exact strings differ: 458 (XOR) to 1,704 (NOT) distinct strings of required instructions, in the order of the instruction sequence, for 2,673 to 12,732 sequences.

**Redundancy.** Of the 1,664 sequence-task pairs [Xb5] that no single site stops, 1,646 have more than one route in the trace (NOT 712 of 718, NAND 452 of 463, ORN 308 of 309, ANDN 166 of 166, AND 7 of 7, OR 1 of 1); a minimal pair then takes out one site of each route (or of the rotation and one route). Pairs also exist beside single sites: minimal pairs total 876,577 over all tasks and sequences (per task in the `.json`, `minimal_pairs_total`; pairs holding a site whose single ablation stops replication not counted) [Xb3], in addition to the required instructions. In the most common sequence of each run, exhaustive triples found further minimal sets of size 3 (0 to 254 per task: in default seed 1's, 254 for NOT). So the required instructions are the smallest removals, not the only ones.

### 2.1 Worked examples of what had to be removed

**NOT, low seed 2's most common NOT sequence (44 Avida programs, 110 instructions).** Its route (Avida's trace): site 11 `IO` reads B into register BX; site 12 `push` puts it on the stack; site 16 `pop` (with `nop-C` at 17) puts it into CX; site 20 `nand` computes nand(BX, CX) = nand(B, B) = NOT B into BX; site 21 `swap` moves it to CX; site 26 `IO` (with `nop-C` at 27) writes CX out, which Avida's check accepts as NOT. **Required** (single ablation stops NOT, replication goes on): site 12 `push`, 15 `nop-C`, 20 `nand`, 21 `swap`, 27 `nop-C`. **[Xa2]** Site 15 is the register choice of the `dec` at 14, an instruction outside the route: in the program as it is, the `dec` takes one from CX and the `pop` at 16 then overwrites CX; with 15 ablated (rerun, traced), the `dec` takes one from BX, where B sits, so the `nand` at 20 combines B − 1 with B, and no output of the run is NOT (the program still replicates and keeps its other seven tasks). The `pop`'s own register choice at 17 is not required: with it ablated (rerun, traced), the `pop` puts B into BX and CX keeps the all-ones the `dec` made from zero, so the `nand` at 20 computes nand(B, all ones), which is NOT B, and the same output at 26 carries NOT by that other circuit. **Not required although on the route**: the input read at 11 (with it ablated, an earlier `IO` in the rotation supplies a number, and NOT of any input is NOT) and the output write at 26 (with it ablated, the NOT value stays in CX and a later `IO`, at site 34, writes out a three-`nand` NOT circuit instead: rerun in the scratch space). So for NOT, what had to be removed is the `nand` that makes it and the wiring that brings one number to both of its operands and its result to the output register. Nine minimal pairs exist beside these five (for example the two `IO` at sites 10 and 11 together).

**EQU, low seed 3's most common EQU sequence (28 Avida programs, 108 instructions).** Required: 25 sites: `IO` at 27, 41, 47, 53, 66 (input reads), `IO` at 45, 51, 55 (outside the route: rotation), `nand` at 43, 75, 78, 91, `add` 60, `inc` 63, `sub` 65, `push` 44, `pop` 90, `swap-stk` 54 and 88 (they decide which stack the `pop` reads), `nop-C` at 28, 61, 67, 76, 79 (register choices), and the output `IO` at 92. No minimal pair. Ablating the rotation `IO` at 45 (rerun): the later reads at 47, 53, 66 get C, B, A instead of A, C, B, and the output at 92 becomes another function (logic id 139), not EQU.

## 3. What it is: the circuits (b)

**How.** Avida's own `TRACE` of every sequence on the fixed inputs (analysis mode). A replay follows each trace step by step and carries, for every register and stack place, where its number came from (an input read, a constant, or an operation on earlier numbers), using the register and stack effects of each instruction in Avida's source (`cHardwareCPU.cc`); after every step the carried numbers are compared with the trace. **Steps where the replay's number differs from Avida's trace: 0**, in all 23,332 sequences. Each output an `IO` writes is put to Avida's check (the logic id rule, `cTaskLib.cc` 369-448, with the lists each task accepts, 513-575); every accepted output is a **route** for the task; the first is the one Avida credits, and for **every** performing sequence and task it is the step at which the trace's own task count goes from 0 to 1 (83,725 of 83,725). The tasks the trace credits equal the test processor's for all 14,965 replicating sequences.

**The circuit** of a route: the operations from the output back to the input reads, with the movers and register choices left out as wires, constant parts folded to ZERO, ONES (all 32 bits set) or OTHER, identical parts merged, and the three inputs named x0, x1, x2 in the order that gives the smallest written form (so the same circuit on other inputs counts once). The **reduced** form also drops steps that leave a number unchanged (`add` or `sub` of ZERO, `inc` after `dec`, `dec` after `inc`) and writes `nand` with ONES as a `nand` of the number with itself, and `nand` with ZERO as ONES. Each reduced circuit was also computed on the three numbers `0x0f0f0f0f`, `0x33333333`, `0x55555555` (every byte holds all eight combinations of three bits) to read which bit-by-bit function each of its parts is.

### 3.1 One worked example per task, in the instruction set's terms and in plain logic

- **The meaning of the logic instruction.** With `nand` read as `nor`, 98% to 99% of the programs still replicate, and what they then perform is what their circuits compute with `nor` in place of `nand`: the prediction from the trace matches the test processor for 81,567 of 82,347 sequence-task pairs (99.1%), counted over the pairs whose program still replicates under the change [Xa9, Xc2]. - **The input and output channel.** With `IO` doing nothing, no program performs any task; 83% to 89% still replicate (`IO` is not part of their replication). **[Xa4]** That no task is performed follows from Avida's rules, as the plan predicted: Avida credits a task only at an `IO` output, and numbers enter the registers only through `IO`, so with `IO` doing nothing no input reaches any circuit and no output is written. Given Avida's meanings, its `IO` channel and its rotation of inputs, the circuit gives the task for most input numbers of the world's kind.

## 6. Departures from the plan

1. **The trace reader's circuit form.** The first reader wrote each circuit as one nested expression; in programs whose parts are used many times it grew without bound (one worker passed 1.6 GB of memory; an earlier pool broke), and the job was stopped by its exact process numbers. The reader now keeps each circuit as a table of distinct parts (identical parts merged, as the plan asks), works without recursion, folds `nand` with ZERO to ONES in the reduced form, and records each part's logic id. The first run's output from the old reader was set aside unread for results.
2. **The shuffled instruction sets (E4)**: Avida refused a set with the `nop` instructions anywhere ("invalid NOP placement"); the shufflings keep the three `nop` meanings among a to c and shuffle the other 23. A few letters keep their meaning by chance (2 to 4 per shuffling).
3. **Replication and credit.** The plan says a program that never splits is credited nothing; that holds, but Avida's "viable" means forming a colony, and 1,549 sequences split without replicating and are credited. They are counted apart (section 5). The environment tests are counted over the same replicating performers as the ablations.
4. **Added, not planned**: the world's own counts (section 1); the required sites against the credited route; the bit-by-bit check of every reduced circuit on all eight combinations and the grouping by the functions their parts compute; the naming of roles outside the routes (rotation `IO`, stack switch); three reruns in the scratch space for the worked examples (the NOT example's `IO` at 11 and 26, the EQU example's `IO` at 45).
5. **Run times**: the pairs took two workers and a four-hour limit (the plan said about 1.5 hours on three); the last run's traces were read again with three workers after one worker went slowly (its programs write many outputs). Nothing else changed.
6. **[Xa1] Reported after the cross-examination**: the plan's removal-against-route check was, as sent, only in the `.json` (`ablation_against_routes`); it is now in section 2.

## 7. Tested, not tested, unsure

**Tested**: every single ablation of every distinct instruction sequence; every pair among the sites that leave replication, in every replicating performer; triples in the nine most common; Avida's trace of every sequence, replayed with no differing step; the circuits' outputs checked against Avida's task check, on the fixed inputs, on all eight bit combinations, and on 200 other input triples; four kinds of change to the Avida execution environment, with predictions made from the traces.

**Not tested**: removal sets holding a site whose single ablation stops replication, except for the 91 sequence-task pairs of the settling rerun (a further ablation can let the program replicate again, and 85 such pairs were found there) [Xb1]; ablation by deleting an instruction (which shifts every later position) rather than replacing it by `nop-X`; sets of three or more beyond the greedy search (outside the nine most common sequences); task performance outside the test processor (in the world, with neighbours and changing inputs); whether the circuits found are the only ones a program could execute on other inputs (the traces follow one run, on the fixed inputs).

**Unsure**: the canonical form names parts by a hash, so two circuits could in principle share a form by collision (not seen; 20 hexadecimal digits); "the same circuit" here means the same operations on the same inputs after identity steps are dropped, and circuits with the same bit-by-bit result but different arithmetic count as different; the role of a required site outside the route is read from what it was executed as, not tested one by one (three reruns only; five with the settling's two [Xa2]); circuits differing only in the value of a constant folded as OTHER share one form, so the distinct-circuit counts are lower bounds in that respect (told apart by value: section 3) [Xc3]; the step-by-step check compares the numbers in the registers and stacks, not where the replay says they came from, so two readings that give the same numbers on the fixed inputs could not be told apart by it (the agreement of the first accepted output with Avida's own credit, 83,725 of 83,725, and the predictions matching for 99% of the sequence-task pairs under changed meanings, bear on the provenance too) [Xc5].
