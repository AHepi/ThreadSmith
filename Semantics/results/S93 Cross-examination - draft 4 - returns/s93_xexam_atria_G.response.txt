## Report

**1. X04 · T1 — the strongest truth attack: does "expectation" for a *declared* transport say something false?**
Attack: the new lead-in, "Let \(t\) be a transport to the simulation layer \(S\)" (L217), extends expectation and violation to declared transports, but L211 says "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does," and L177 says "\(S\) is where expectation lives." So for a declared transport \(\operatorname{Ans}_S(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) is the modeller's computation, not the system's expectation; the bullet at L219 misuses the term.
Why it fails: in this theory "expectation" is not a feeling but an answer-profile value (L219), and L584 confirms "Surprise is not a feeling added to the semantics." The theory evaluates fidelity for declared transports elsewhere on exactly the same footing — L265: "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in \(C\)," with no provenance test — so computing an answer profile and a fidelity failure for a declared transport is how the theory already treats them. The provenance taxonomy at L193 ("exactly one of three provenances") governs *representation* (R) at L205–209, not the definitions at L217–221. The declaration's "whatever its provenance" makes the intended reading explicit rather than an overreach.

**2. X05 · T1 — the strongest truth attack on the new claim.**
Attack: L223's new clause "a violation the system represents can be a recognized difficulty (Part X)" is unsupported, because L429 defines a recognized difficulty as "a failure of a claimed obligation, or a conflict in which what the system holds meets a claimed obligation only by failing a protected one (Part XI), when the system represents it," and no stated text makes a transport's fidelity a claimed obligation.
Why it fails: "can be" is existential, and the theory contains a model of it. In Derivation 10, \(t_0\) is violated at the occlusion pair while the system holds the obligation "predict displacements correctly" (L628); that obligation's failure *is* the prediction failure, and the system represents it. L201 supplies the general shape: a constructed transport has "a represented target and criticism in its history," so its violation is represented as a failure of something the system stood behind. Unchanged L317 already uses the same device — "A problem that a system represents can be a recognized difficulty (Part X)" — so the bridge from Part IV to Part X follows an established pattern, and the qualifier "the system represents" is what keeps the claim from being universal.

**3. X04 · T2 — is the kind really CLAIM, and is the declaration exact?**
Attack: this is ORDER or WORDING. The old block ran "Let \(t\) be selected on history \(H\) with contract \(C\)," but L225 already spoke of "Two responses to a violation," one of them "A construction response," and Part V states fidelity conditions on supplied relations; so violation for non-selected transports was already available.
Why it fails: the old wording scoped *all three* definitions to a selected \(t\); a careful reader could not conclude from the old text that a constructed or declared transport is "violated" in the technical sense, and L225's construction response is a response *to* a violation, not a definition of violation for constructed transports. Nothing in the current text defines expectation or violation outside the selected case, so the extension concludes something new. The declaration is exact: "expectation and violation ... for every transport to the simulation layer, whatever its provenance" matches L217–220, and "keeps surprise for a violation of a selected one at a pair outside its history" matches L221 ("surprise is a violation of a selected transport at \((a,b)\notin H\)"). Nothing is lost relative to the old text, and nothing extra is claimed.

**4. X05 · T2 — is the kind really CLAIM, given that the declaration itself says "restating its definitions"?**
Attack: the first clause of the new sentence restates L217–221 in prose, so the item is ORDER with one appended clause.
Why it fails: the sentence carries two claims beyond the restatement — that a constructed transport's failure "is violated, and the failure is not surprise," and that a represented violation "can be a recognized difficulty (Part X)" — and the second is not stated anywhere in the current text (L317 covers *problems*, not violations). A CLAIM item may include restatement when it also concludes something new, and the declaration enumerates all three parts accurately, including the ORDER-like first part. The kind should be CLAIM.

**5. X04 · T4 — the verdict that genuinely moves, and whether the declaration accounts for it.**
In N18, Rhea's worked-out theory is a constructed transport (L197; L201 — represented target and criticism in its history); Dov's copy of an eighty-year-old design is selected, with \(H\) containing crowds only up to a few dozen (L195). Under the old text, "expectation" and "violation" were defined only for Dov's transport, so the theory was silent on whether the swaying went against what Rhea expected. Under L217–220 both have expectations and both are violated, so the theory now agrees with the fixed verdict that the swaying went against both expectations. The further verdict — Rhea's grounds contradicted, Dov's not — is then carried by unchanged text: Rhea's violation is violation-but-not-surprise (L223), and her history contains criticism of a represented target (L201), while Dov's is surprise at \((a,b)\notin H\), and by Derivation 3 (L572–576) "survival on \(H\) does not distinguish \(t\) from \(t'\) there," with L576 giving up "the blanket claim that every untested value is unconstrained" — so nothing Dov had reason to expect was contradicted, only more than his evidence supported. The move is from silent to agreeing, i.e. toward the fixed verdict, and X04's declaration accounts for exactly the extension that produces it. No sub-question moves away.

**6. X05 · T3 — coherence of the pair, and the trap the two items avoid together.**
X04 extends violation to declared transports; X05 restricts the new recognized-difficulty link to "a violation *the system represents*." Since L211 says a declared transport "does not make an occurrence represent anything," the declared case cannot manufacture a recognized difficulty. The two changes interlock rather than pull apart. L225's two responses remain consistent: a violation now has a provenance-sorted pair of answers (extend \(H\) and re-tune, or construct), and L429's episode structure (recognized difficulty → target → objection → content-sensitive response) is reached only through the represented-violation route X05 adds. L223's unchanged sentences ("A system with no transport cannot be surprised"; "A system whose history exhausts its contract cannot be surprised") stay true, since surprise remains selected-only.
