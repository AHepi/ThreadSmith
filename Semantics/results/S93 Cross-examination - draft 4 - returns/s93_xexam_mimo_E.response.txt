## Points

**1. X18 · T3 — the note's definition of recognized difficulty does not say what Part X says it says.**

The note reads: "a conflict in which meeting a claimed obligation fails a protected one (Part XI), when the system represents it." Part X, L429 reads: "a conflict in which **what the system holds** meets a claimed obligation **only by** failing a protected one (Part XI), when the system represents it." Two qualifiers are dropped.

*(i) "only by."* The text requires that the claimed obligation cannot be met without failing the protected one. Without it, the note's definition counts as a recognized difficulty any case in which meeting a claimed obligation happens to fail a protected one, even where the system holds an alternative that meets it without. Example: a system holds both a rushed route (meeting a claimed delivery obligation by breaking a protected speed rule) and a legal route. Under the text, no recognized difficulty — what the system holds meets the obligation not only by failing the protected one. Under the note, a recognized difficulty — meeting the obligation fails the protected one.

*(ii) "what the system holds."* The text's definition is about what a system holds (beliefs, resources, commitments) meeting the claimed obligation only by failing the protected one. The note's version drops the subject, making the definition about abstract obligation conflicts rather than a system's situation.

The pointer to Part X and Part XI is correct in location, but the definition given does not match what those Parts state.

Proposed replacement for the whole sentence:

```
A problem in his wider sense can be a recognized difficulty (Part X): a failure of a claimed obligation, or a conflict in which what the system holds meets a claimed obligation only by failing a protected one (Part XI), when the system represents it.
```

## (a) X18

The note states hard-to-vary "through rivals and problems, with no measure or count of variants (Part VI)." Its description of easy-to-vary — "a rival that fits as well and conflicts with it only outside what the question covers" — names a discovered, offered rival, never a listed set. It says the term "prefers neither," matching L317's "the term says nothing about which of them is right" and "Nothing here counts rivals, grades a candidate or ranks candidates." No enumeration, no count of versions, no grade, no ranking, no record or log of rescues appears anywhere in the note. The four departures under *Hard to vary* are consistent with the owner's position: a rival must be discovered and offered ("shown only by offering the rival, which is the criticism"); two discovered rivals that both fit constitute a problem; the theory does not reject a candidate merely because a flexible rival exists; variants conflicting at no admitted change are not rivals. The note's framings of Deutsch's ideas are named as departures, checked against the owner's decisions.

(a) X18: FAITHFUL

## (c1) X18

The text says what the question describes. Part VI, L317 defines easy-to-vary as posing a problem of the second kind: both candidates fit what is established and conflict at no pair of C, only at admitted pairs outside it. On the narrow question (seasons in Greek lands), the tilt and the myth both fit and conflict only outside C (southern lands, the far north). Each is then easy to vary relative to the other, and the tilt stays so until the question is widened to contain a pair at which they conflict. The text explicitly says "in the sense used here" (L317), "the rival is then easy to vary too, and the term says nothing about which of them is right" (L317), and "Nothing here counts rivals, grades a candidate or ranks candidates" (L317). These disclaimers keep the property from functioning as a grade. The consequence — that on a narrow question a good explanation and a myth cannot be distinguished — is what the theory correctly says: the narrow contract does not contain their difference, and a wider question is needed. The text already says this ("A finer contract… makes a new question"). This is a harmless consequence of the theory's core question-relativity, not a defect.

(c1) X18: HARMLESS
