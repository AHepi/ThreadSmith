**(d) X06 — Rivals must conflict at an admitted change.** The condition is right in the theory's own terms. L315 makes rivalry require (i) one candidate offered in place of the other and (ii) conflict "at some admitted edit–boundary pair of the target that both their transports translate, in \(C\) or outside it". The restriction to admitted pairs is forced: only admitted pairs are testable under the adopted physics (Part I, L159), and L317 defines a problem as "a conflict between ideas that what the assessor has established has not settled" — no conflict, nothing to settle. The "or outside it" half is load-bearing, not loose: if conflict had to be inside \(C\), then of two rivals both meeting (E) on \(C\) at most one could be an account, and the problem of the second kind, hence "easy to vary" (L317), could never arise for a pair of accounts — against the owner's position that two discovered variations that both fit constitute a problem. Two candidates that differ only in how they are written correctly fall outside: L315 and Derivation 8 (L612) say a structure-preserving recoding preserves (E) and leaves answers as they are, so they conflict at no pair. Two candidates that differ only by a part that does no work also correctly fall outside *when the part changes no answer at any admitted pair*: X06 is what makes the case expressible at all, since a no-work part can still be in \(\Gamma\) (L313), so the two differ in their commitments; but Derivation 2(i) (L562) gives them one answer profile on \(C\), and if the part is anchored to a deleted subnetwork its relation is full by (O) (L339) and inert everywhere, so no conflict. If instead the part changes answers at admitted pairs outside \(C\), they conflict there, are rivals, and pose the second-kind problem — which is the owner's "two discovered variations that fit". Nothing the theory should keep is lost: a part inert everywhere is already excluded from what explains by L313, which is all the fixed verdicts need (N1), and the only thing given up — "easy to vary" cannot reach an inert appendage with no conflicting rival — follows directly from the owner's rule against grading and counting.
