## Report

### 1. X14 · T1 — attack: "established of every such candidate" uses *established* outside its technical sense

The strongest attack I could build. Part VI (L315) says a result "is established for an assessor who holds a usable receipt for it (Part IX)," and Part IX (L397) says a receipt is "a derivation tree over leaves" where "an evidence leaf is a reference to an event with an interpreted claim." X14 then says the exclusion "is established for that assessor of every such candidate alike, **from the candidate's own answer there**." A candidate's answer at (a,b), `Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b))`, is a value of a set-theoretic operation on the candidate's supplied relations, not an event in the world; so the exclusion of each candidate seems *derivable*, not *receipt-established*, and X14 would be conflating the two.

The attack fails. X14 itself fixes the anchor of "established" on the target side: "Here 'established' is meant as in Part VI: the assessor holds a usable receipt for the target's answer at (a,b) (Part IX)." What is established by receipt is the target's answer; the exclusion of candidates answering y then follows "from the candidate's own answer there." And reading a candidate's own answer is the theory's standard assessment mode, not a covert empirical test: Part V states each conjunct of (E) is "a condition on supplied relations under the changes in C" (L231) and "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in C. None inspects a label" (L265). So one receipt (target side) plus ordinary inspection (candidate side) yields the exclusion, and "with no record of which candidates failed before" is exactly satisfied. The residual indeterminacy concerns *pure* structural failures with no target-side receipt at all — that is question (c2), a gap X14 neither creates nor depends on.

### 2. X14 · T1 — attack: "the exclusion ceases to be established" is too strong

X14: "If a premise about them ceases to be live, the receipt is not usable and the exclusion ceases to be established, for every such candidate alike." If a second usable receipt also establishes the target's answer at (a,b), the exclusion does not cease. The attack fails because "the receipt" is the one "the test that yields it" produced, introduced in the preceding clause; the sentence is about dependence on *that* receipt, and its singular leaves room for another. At most a wording imprecision, not a falsehood; no verdict turns on it.

### 3. X14 · T3 — attack: contradiction with Part VI, "an answer it refutes stays refuted on p (Part VIII)"

L317 says a test "solves the problem whatever it shows, since afterwards at most one of them fits; an answer it refuted stays refuted on p (Part VIII)," while X14 says the exclusion "ceases to be established" when a premise about background or instruments ceases to be live. The attack fails: the two are about different relations. "Stays refuted" is the indexed fact — the candidate answered y at (a,b), the target's answer is not y, so it fails (A) at that index, and "A proposition indexed to a contract remains that proposition when a later theory changes the current contract" (L367). "Ceases to be established" is the assessor's warrant, which L315 already makes (K3)-fragile for *every* established result. X14 marks the distinction itself: the caveat sentence is about the receipt, the final sentence about the standing failure. Coherent; and it supplies the target that L317's pointer "(Part VIII)" previously lacked.

### 4. X14 · T2 — kind and declaration

I checked the declaration part by part against the wording: the (A)-based exclusion (L250), the extension to every question with the same target and query containing the pair, the four candidate descriptions unified under "keeps y there," the receipt-grounded establishment with no record, the (K3)/(K2) fragility, and the final different-question sentence (L151, L159, L367) are all declared, and the wording claims nothing beyond the declaration and nothing less. The core exclusion is already derivable from (E)+(A) in the current text; what is new is the persistence across re-offerings and changes and the receipt-fragility of the assessor's warrant. CLAIM with reason "clarification" is therefore right, not ORDER: the established/ceases-to-be-established machinery is stated here for the first time, and it fulfils L317's existing pointer.

### (a) X14

X14 is faithful to the owner's position and the drafters' rule. The exclusion is carried by the candidate's own content — "from the candidate's own answer there, with no record of which candidates failed before or of how any was changed" — which is the owner's "error correction is carried by the explanation itself" and "a record of rescues is redundant" in the theory's own terms, and it is why a "changed candidate that keeps y there" cannot creep back in on p. Nothing enumerates, lists or counts versions: "every such candidate alike" is a universal conditional over candidates answering y at (a,b), not a set of rivals, and the four-case list ("the candidate that gave y there and failed, that candidate offered again, a rival, or a changed candidate that keeps y there") is four descriptions of one candidate's history, immediately unified under the single condition "whose answer at (a,b) is y." Nothing grades or ranks: "is not an account" is the binary output of (E). No record or log is proposed; the passage expressly declines one. It also does not restate hard-to-vary through a set; it does not restate hard-to-vary at all.
(a) X14: FAITHFUL

### (b) X14

On the same question the correction sticks: any candidate keeping y at (a,b) is excluded by (A), and since y is arbitrary, once the target's answer there is established as z, every wrong value y≠z is excluded, so a "slightly different" wrong answer is covered too. On a narrowed contract or changed query, the text lets the candidate be an account *of the new question* — but that is not the mistake returning, because "an account on it does not answer p, and the failure on p stands" (L151, L159, L367); this is exactly O1's retreat. Through (K3)/(K2) the text lets the *warrant* lapse: a live premise about background or instruments fails, the receipt is not usable, the exclusion "ceases to be established," yet "no candidate is thereby shown to be an account," consistent with L393 ("Withdrawing a premise removes a license; it does not make the conclusion false"). It should let this happen: immunity would make tests infallible. At a neighbouring untested pair the candidate is not an account in fact — it claims (A) "at every pair of C, tested or not" (L315) — but if the target's answer there is not established, the exclusion is not established either; "missing evidence stays missing" (L397). A change of grain or target is a change of index and question (L524), so X14 does not transfer the failure, and the failure on p stands. A genuinely corrected candidate is not excluded by the old failure; if it reverts to y, it is excluded again from its own answer.
(b) X14: HOLDS AS STATED

### (c2) X14

The text does not settle it. It pulls both ways. Part V says every conjunct of (E) is "a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in C" (L231, L265), so checking non-circular dependence, anchoring or internal contradiction is done by inspecting the candidate — which suggests such failures count. But Part VI/IX define "established" as holding a usable receipt, and a receipt is a derivation tree over leaves that are "reference[s] to an event" (L315, L397); a structural failure of a candidate is not an event. Since "fits" is defined as "no result established for that assessor shows it failing a condition of (E)" (L315), the reading is open either way, and X14's phrase "from the candidate's own answer there" gestures at the inspection reading without stating it. It should be settled, in favour of the inspection reading for candidate-side facts, with receipts reserved for target-side and world-side claims. What turns on it: a provably circular or self-contradictory rival would otherwise still "fit" for an assessor who has not tested it, so the pair would pose a problem of one of the two kinds and the assessor would be sent to test the world at a separating pair, when the rival's own content already settles that it is no account at all and no problem exists. Settling it also backs X14's "no record" claim, which needs exactly the principle that a candidate's own answer at a pair is available to an assessor without a receipt.
(c2) X14: UNSETTLED BY THE TEXT
