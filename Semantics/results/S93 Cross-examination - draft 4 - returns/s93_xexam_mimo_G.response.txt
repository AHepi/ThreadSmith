# Cross-examination report — part G (X04, X05)

**1. X05 · T1 and T2 — the restatement drops the block's occurrence scoping.**

The definitions it says it restates are scoped to pairs that occur (L217): "Let \(t\) be a transport to the simulation layer \(S\) … For an edit–boundary pair \((a,b)\in C\) **actually occurring**:" and then (L220) "a **violation** occurs when fidelity fails at \((a,b)\)". The new sentence (L223) restates this as "a constructed one that fails at **a pair of its contract** is violated" — no occurrence condition. Read as written, a fidelity failure at a pair of \(C\) that never occurs is a violation. The definitions do not support that, and the theory elsewhere keeps the two apart: a candidate "claims (F1), (F2) and (A) at every pair of \(C\), tested or not" (L315) — an untested pair can show a candidate is *not an account*; Part VIII speaks of "a pair \((a,b)\in C\)" and an established answer there (L369) with no occurrence; and L584 glosses surprise as "the signature of a selected transport **meeting a change** outside its history". Violation is event language.

The drop matters: O24 (one unused but reachable joint setting) is exactly a case where the two arrangements differ at a pair that never occurs. The fixed verdict is "the record has not chosen between them"; on the loose reading one would say the mismatching arrangement "is violated" at a setting nobody has used, converting a fit-and-rivals fact into an event. The declaration repeats the same unscoped phrase, so it too misstates what is claimed. The third clause also sits ill in the declaration's "restating its definitions": "a violation the system represents can be a recognized difficulty (Part X)" is a link to L429 ("A **recognized difficulty** is a failure of a claimed obligation … when the system represents it"), not one of the definitions at L217–221; relative to the current text it is new (the current text never connects violation to recognized difficulty), which is why the item is rightly CLAIM.

Proposed new wording:

```
Whether the correspondence could have been otherwise at such a change is a fact about its population (Derivation 3). Expectation and violation are defined for every transport to the simulation layer, surprise only for a selected one: a constructed one whose fidelity fails at a pair of its contract that actually occurs is violated, and the failure is not surprise; a violation the system represents can be a recognized difficulty (Part X).
```

Proposed declaration:

```
Part IV now says, restating its definitions, that expectation and violation are defined for every transport to the simulation layer and surprise only for a selected one; that a constructed transport to the simulation layer whose fidelity fails at a pair of its contract that actually occurs is violated, and the failure is not surprise; and, a link to Part X rather than one of those definitions, that a violation the system represents can be a recognized difficulty.
```
