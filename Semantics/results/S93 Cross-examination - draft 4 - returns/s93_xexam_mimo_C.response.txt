# Report — X14 (Part VIII, "A failed answer stays failed")

## Points

**1. X14 · (c2) — What "established" reaches: failures found in the candidate itself.**
X14 builds its whole establishment clause on world-directed testing: "the assessor holds a usable receipt for the target's answer at \((a,b)\) (Part IX), and by (K3) the test that yields it tells against a candidate only together with the background and instruments it relies on" (L369). Part VI matches: "A result is **established** for an assessor who holds a usable receipt for it (Part IX)" and a **test** is "establishing it, the target's relations as well as its answer where their answers there agree" (L315, L317). Part IX defines a receipt as "a derivation tree over leaves", an "evidence leaf" being "a reference to an event with an interpreted claim" (L397). Nothing says whether a leaf may be the candidate's *own* occurrence and content. So a failure of non-circular dependence, a component anchored to nothing, or an internal contradiction is unclassified: it is neither licensed nor forbidden as a "result" with a receipt. Keep apart: the text forces nothing either way. What turns on it is large, because "fits" is evidence-relative ("no result established for that assessor shows it failing a condition of (E)", L315). If internal failures are never results, a candidate that fails (E) outright still fits; then it and a sound account can "pose a problem of the second kind" and the sound account is labelled **easy to vary** (L317) — the theory would condemn a good account on the strength of a twin that is not an account at all. Part VI's own gloss, "where both meet (E) on C both are accounts of p" (L317), shows (E)-failure is meant to matter, but not who establishes it. It should be settled *for* establishment, by a receipt whose leaf is the candidate's own content, with (K3) reading background as the definitions, the declared indices and the reading convention. If the drafters wish to settle it, words of this kind belong in Part IX (Receipts): "A leaf may reference a candidate's own content; where a derivation over that content shows a condition of (E) unmet, the result is established on a usable receipt, and (K3) takes the definitions, the declared indices and the reading convention as background and instruments." X14 states nothing contrary; it stands.

**2. X14 · T1 — The closing sentence read generically.**
Attack: "A contract that omits \((a,b)\), or a changed query, makes a different question (Part III): an account on it does not answer \(p\)" is false if read generically. Counterexample: let \(p\) have \(C=\{(1,b_0),(a_1,b_0)\}\) and \(Q\); let \(p'\) have the same \(D\) and \(Q\) and \(C'=\{(1,b_0)\}\) (it omits \((a_1,b_0)\)); let \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) satisfy (E) on \(C\). Then \((E,p',t,\Gamma)\) satisfies (E) on \(C'\) *and* \(\mathcal E\) is an account of \(p\). The same with a changed query: take \(Q'\neq Q\) with \(Y_{p'}=Y_p\) and \(Q'(D,a,b)=Q(D,a,b)\) for all \((a,b)\in C\); an account of \(p'\) is an account of \(p\) too. The attack fails to defeat the item, because the paragraph is framed by "Fix a question \(p\), a pair \((a,b)\in C\) and a value \(y\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)" and its subject is the failing answer; in that frame the claim is that the retreat is not *thereby* an answer to \(p\) — which is what Part III L151 states ("an answer to one is not an answer to the other") and what the declaration says ("an account on which does not answer the original"). The frame fixes the true reading; the hazard is a wording hazard only. (If the drafters want it closed: "being an account of it is not thereby an account of \(p\)".)

**3. X14 · T2 — Kind and declaration.**
Every part of the declaration is true of the wording, and the wording claims no more. The declaration's general form ("a candidate whose answer at a pair of the contract differs from the target's is not an account") is the universal closure of the wording's single-value schema ("Fix … a value \(y\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)"), and the closure is licensed by Part IX: once the target's answer at \((a,b)\) is established as a value \(z\), "Negation exchanges them" (L397) yields "not \(y\)" for every \(y\neq z\). The kind should be **CLAIM**. What the current text left to derivation is now stated: the cross-question clause ("the same holds on every question with the same target and query whose contract contains \((a,b)\)"), the record-freedom clause, and the (K2)/(K3) lapse. Had the drafters held derivability sufficient, the item would be ORDER with these places named: (A) L247–250; Part III L151, L159; Part VI L315–317; Part VIII L367–368; (K2) L389–393; (K3) L395. But Part VI already points at it — "an answer it refutes stays refuted on \(p\) (Part VIII)" (L317) — and the insertion stands exactly where that pointer lands. CLAIM with "clarification" is right.

**4. X14 · T3 — Terms, pointers, one apparent clash.**
Three wording nits, none fatal. (i) "the exclusion" (L369, twice) is a coinage the theory never defines; the referent is fixed by the preceding clause ("this is established … of every such candidate alike") and by (E)'s conjuncts, but "the result" would carry the meaning the text already fixes. (ii) "established … of every such candidate alike" is loose for "established of each that it is not an account". (iii) "whether it is … a rival …" (L369) is governed by "whose answer at \((a,b)\) is \(y\)", but "a rival" alone could be misread as *any* rival; the declaration's "whatever candidate it is" removes it. Pointers all resolve: (Part III) L151/L159; (Part VI) L315; (Part IX) L397; (K3) L395; (K2) L389–393 with its supporting sentence L393 ("Withdrawing a premise removes a license; it does not make the conclusion false"); (Historical index) L367–368. The apparent clash with Part VI L317, "an answer it refutes stays refuted on \(p\)", is dissolved inside Part VI itself: L315 qualifies every established result with "the background and instruments of the test that yields it", so "stays refuted" never meant "undone by no lapse of premises"; and L317's "(Part VIII)" is the passage X14 supplies. No clash remains.

**5. X14 · T1 — Slightly changed answer; neighbouring pair.**
Attack: a candidate that changes \(y\) to a different wrong \(y'\) escapes the stated exclusion, and one that repeats the mistake at an untested neighbouring pair is untouched. The first is covered: the schema fixes an arbitrary \(y\), and once the target's answer is established as \(z\), Part IX gives "not \(y'\)" by negation of receipts, so every candidate answering \(\neq z\) at \((a,b)\) is excluded. The second is *rightly* untouched: nothing is established there, and Part VI's machinery ("Two rivals that both fit what is established … pose … a problem", L317) is the designed route. Attack fails.

**6. X14 · T4 — The named situations.**
Comparing the current text ((A), Part III, Part VI, Historical index) with those plus X14, no verdict moves. **O1**: both texts give "no explanation of the floods" — Part III L159 already says a narrowing after a failure "does not answer a broader question that failed"; X14 adds only that the failed candidate and any revision keeping \(y\) stay out. **D3-T**: X14 says the discarded design is not an account and its failure stands; the verdict's question (whether passing leaves the untried setting open) is Derivation 3's ("a population with no such survivor is the theorem's own qualification"), untouched. **O27**: X14's K3/K2 clause is the episode's own shape — the first exclusion lapses when the sensor premise dies, "for every such candidate alike", and "no candidate is thereby shown to be an account (K2)"; the second run re-establishes. Credit is Part XI's. **O8**: the paragraph presupposes a failure at a pair of \(C\); Petra has none, and her scope is her scope from the start (Part III L159). **N7**: Maya's contract is 18–35°C; no \((a,b)\in C\) has a wrong answer established, so both stay accounts at their contracts (L277: "an account at a coarse grain is an account of the coarse question"). **N2**: the changed myth *changes* its answer at the failing pair, so X14's exclusion (which reaches only "a changed candidate that keeps \(y\) there") does not touch it; the fixed verdict must come from Part VI (easy to vary), and X14 neither supplies it nor blocks it. **O24**: neither arrangement is refuted at the joint setting, so both fit and pose a problem of the first kind — "One reachable setting is enough" (L317), and once one is refuted there, X14 keeps it refuted. No move in any direction.

**(a) X14 — Faithfulness to the owner's position and the drafters' rule.** Nothing here brings back a listed set of rivals or versions, an enumeration, a count, a grade, or a record of failures or rescues doing content-work. The small list "the candidate that gave \(y\) there and failed, that candidate offered again, a rival, or a changed candidate that keeps \(y\) there" (L369) names *roles of one failing answer* under a universal quantifier — "a candidate … whose answer at \((a,b)\) is \(y\)", the declaration's "whatever candidate it is", and "every such candidate alike" — not a set \(\mathcal V\) to enumerate or count; no \(\operatorname{Pres}\), no grade: "is not an account of \(p\)" is the pass/fail of (E) applied identically to all. The record clause is the owner's own point, in the text's words: "with no record of which candidates failed before or of how any was changed", the exclusion carried "from the candidate's own answer there" — the content does the work, exactly "A record is redundant" and the rule "do not propose a record or log where the explanation's own content does the work". The receipts invoked are receipts for the *target's* answer, leaves of evidence (Part IX L397), not logs of rescues; and L397's "A record reconstructed from the claim it is meant to support is not a receipt for that claim" keeps them from becoming such.
(a) X14: FAITHFUL

**(b) X14 — Does a correction stick?** On the same question it sticks: a candidate keeping \(y\) at \((a,b)\) is never an account of \(p\), and when the exclusion lapses it comes back only as unrefuted — "no candidate is thereby shown to be an account (K2)". A narrowed contract or a changed query is permitted, but only as "a different question … an account on it does not answer \(p\), and the failure on \(p\) stands (Historical index)" (L369; Part III L159). Through (K3)/(K2) the mistake *can* return to live consideration — "If a premise about them ceases to be live, the receipt is not usable and the exclusion ceases to be established" — and it should: (K3) yields \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\) and nothing narrower (L395); what returns is unsettled status, never an account. A slightly changed answer at the pair is covered by the schema and by negation of receipts once the target's answer is a value. The same mistake at a neighbouring untested pair is untouched and rightly so: nothing is established there, and Part VI's problems and tests are the route. A change of grain or of target is a new index ("A new index is a new claim", L368) or a different question (\(D\) is part of \(p\), L137–141); a recoding preserves content (Derivation 8) and a coarsening is a different claim at a different grain. Every avenue the text lets through is one it names and one the surrounding text requires.
(b) X14: HOLDS AS STATED

**(c2) X14 — What counts as established.** The text does not settle whether a failure found by examining the candidate counts as established. "Established" runs through usable receipts over evidence leaves (Part IX L397) and Part VI speaks of tests that establish "the target's relations as well as its answer" (L317); X14 likewise speaks of "the test that yields it". Whether a leaf may reference the candidate's own content, so that a failure of non-circular dependence, an anchor to nothing or an internal contradiction becomes a result showing failure of a condition of (E), is neither stated nor excluded. It should be settled *for* establishment: otherwise a candidate that fails (E) still "fits", and with a sound twin can pose a problem of the second kind and brand the sound account "easy to vary" (L315, L317) — a verdict the theory should never reach. The turn for the two-rival problems is decisive: whether a defective rival is a rival that "both fits" depends entirely on this.
