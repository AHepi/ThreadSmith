**1. `X06 · T1` — the two halves of the new sentence cannot both be true.**
The new wording says (L231) that "The commitments \(\Gamma\) are components of \(E\), those the candidate offers as doing the work", and in the same breath that "the components of \(E\) outside \(\Gamma\), **including any that assigns an input**, belong to the named background of Part VI". Read strictly, the second half says every input-assigning component is outside \(\Gamma\); the first half says \(\Gamma\) is *exactly* what the candidate offers as doing the work. For a candidate that offers an input-assigning component as doing the work the two halves contradict: the component is both in \(\Gamma\) and outside it. The text supplies such a candidate. Part II gives the ingredients: "A port \(v\) is an **input** under \(A\) when \(A\) contains an edit that sets \(v\) directly" (L109), and "An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port" (L103). Part VII then writes (L331): "'Set \(b_B=0\) because it gives the mass I favour' is not an inference from the readings; **its support is circular** though its content might be true." The stipulation "Set \(b_B=0\)" is offered as doing the work — it is the support of the claim, which is exactly why L331 calls that support circular — and it assigns the bias port, which calibration edits set (L330: "an independent calibration can" change the kernel). So by the first half of L231 it is a commitment; by the second half it is background and can never be one. Worse, if it is background it is in every \(E|W\) (L287: "let \(E|W\) retain the commitments in \(W\) with the named background fixed"), no \(G\subseteq\Gamma\) can delete it (L255), and "its support is circular" has no object, since \(\mathsf S_{E,p}\) ranges over \(W\subseteq\Gamma\) (L290). The defect is in the item, not in a reading of it: an unstated exception is doing load-bearing work. Proposed replacement, whole:

````text
and an identified set \(\Gamma\) of active commitments in \(E\). The commitments \(\Gamma\) are components of \(E\), those the candidate offers as doing the work, whether or not anyone has described their work, except that a component that assigns an input is never one of them; the boundary conditions of \(E\) (its \(B\), Part II) and the components of \(E\) outside \(\Gamma\) belong to the named background that Part VI holds fixed in \(E|W\).
````

**2. `X06 · T3` — the partition makes two standing sentences false or stale.**

**3. `X06 · T3` — "boundary values" is not a defined term, and the pointer does not deliver.**
At L231 the theory's defined datum is "\(B\) is a set of boundary conditions" (L91); Part IV calls the things \(\sigma\) carries "boundaries" ("\(\sigma\) translates boundaries", L189); Part V speaks of "an unanalysed boundary input" (L255) and "its independent boundary conditions" (L255). "Boundary values" occurs only in this item and its declaration, with no definition and no pointer; at best it is a fourth name for \(B\), at worst it silently widens the background beyond the organization's declared data. Second, "belong to the named background **of Part VI**" sends the reader to Part VI for the naming, but Part VI never names it: L287 only says "with the named background fixed". The naming is this sentence; Part VI merely holds it fixed. Both are repaired by the wording proposed in point 1 ("the boundary conditions of \(E\) (its \(B\), Part II) … the named background that Part VI holds fixed in \(E|W\)").

**(d) Rivals must conflict at an admitted change.**
The condition is right on the theory's own terms: every distinction the semantics can state is a distinction some admitted change realizes ("Two components no admitted change can separate are one kind at that level", L11; Derivation 2's Consequence, L568), and the second clause of "conflict" keeps real mechanism rivals: "two of their active components with one anchor have different relations there" (L315) is what makes phlogiston and oxygen competitors although their answer profiles coincide on \(C\) (both must match \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) by (A), L250). Recodings fall out correctly: a structure-preserving bijection "conflict[s] at no pair (Part VIII, Recoding; Derivation 8)", and Derivation 8 preserves (E) (L612). Candidates differing only by a part that does no work also fall out correctly: their answers agree at every pair (by (A)), and target relations exist that let both hold, so neither clause of "conflict" fires (L315) — they do not compete, which is the fixed verdict on N1. But the condition drops two things. (i) Variations that differ only where no admitted change reaches are never rivals: "the sun god, who approves" and "the sun god, who is indifferent" both fit, yet no edit–boundary pair of the Earth–sun target separates them, so no problem is posed, although the owner's rule is "If two discovered variations fit, that constitutes a problem". (ii) "a candidate that nobody has offered is no one's rival" (L315) requires an offer "in place of the other", where the owner makes any variation a competitor and mere discovery sufficient; and a candidate whose transport does not translate a pair can never conflict there, so silence is never a conflict.

`(d) X06: RIGHT, WITH A LOSS — two discovered variations that both fit but differ only in what no admitted change reaches (and a variation nobody has offered in place of another) pose no problem, though the owner counts both as competitors.`
