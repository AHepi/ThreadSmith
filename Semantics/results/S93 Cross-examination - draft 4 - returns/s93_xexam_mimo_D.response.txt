# Cross-examination report — Part D (X02, X07, X08)

**1. X08 · T1 (with X02 · T1) — the add-clause against N1.** The sharpest attack on X08: its label is won only if the added commitment's relation survives (F1). Take N1, where \(\Gamma\) = physics \(\cup\{g\}\), \(g\) = "the sun god … keeps the tilt steady". Two anchorings are imaginable. (i) \(\lambda(g)\) is the spinning subnetwork; then \(g\)'s relation on \(C\) coincides with the anchor's, (F1) holds, every support survives \(g\)'s addition and removal, and \(g\) "does no work by itself". (ii) \(\lambda(g)\) is a "god's approval" subnetwork with no counterpart in \(D\); (F1) fails for \(g\), no support contains it, so the add-clause ("every support stays a support after \(d\) is added to it", L313) fails and \(g\) is *not* labelled work-free — while N1's fixed verdict says it is ("Strike it out and the account works exactly as before … not a part of what explains them") and that Tomas explains, which (E) would deny on (ii), since (F1) binds every active component (L233–236). The attack fails on the text's own terms. (F1) reads \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)]=L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\): the projection is onto \(V_k\), so every port of \(k\) must be named in the anchor's port translation. A "god's approval" port with no counterpart in \(D\) leaves no transport at all, hence no candidate (L231); a port with a counterpart must match the anchor's relation. L120 then removes the label: "two components that differ only in those values are of one kind on \(C\), whatever the difference is called", and L127: "Which of the two an account offers as producing an outcome is settled by that signature, not by the account's wording." So (ii) is not a modelling the semantics distinguishes from (i) on \(C\); on any finer contract the case is a new question (L317). N1 is delivered: the god sentence is one kind with the spinning mechanism on \(C\), is work-free, and is not part of what explains.

**2. X02 · T1/T4 — "Explanation" = account (N1, N3, N25).** Attack: with "explanation" equated to \(\operatorname{Account}\), a candidate carrying an inert extra sentence that fails (F1) is not an explanation, turning N1's "Yes, Tomas explains" into "No"; and the first sentence of L69 then under-states the disqualifiers — failing (A), non-circularity or non-vacuity also disqualifies, not only "an error in the very dependence alleged to do the work". The second is not a falsehood: "What cannot count as explanation is an error in the very dependence" states a necessary condition, not an exhaustive one, and the gloss says what the word means, not what the only disqualifier is. A third attack, that L69 now defines "explanation" differently from L11 ("An explanation is a question-relevant organization of dependencies, held to a target by a transport that is checked only by what changes when things are changed"), fails because L69 scopes its equation ("in this commitment") and L11's gloss is the same notion — organization plus transport checked by change — with (E) supplying the conditions. T4: the move on N3 is *toward* the fixed verdict and is declared: "a false theory offered as an answer is an explanatory candidate, which ordinary usage may still call an explanation" is exactly N3's "Yes, it is an explanation of winter, but only in form … and a false one", while "explanation = account" delivers its "No". N25's "as explanations they make the same empty claim" is ordinary usage, which the same clause keeps open; the theory's own verdict there is that neither meets (E) (L273: an account whose only substantive component restates the answer fails non-circular dependence).

**3. X08 · T1 — the biconditional, criticality, the infinitary clause.** I tried to break the three consequences. (a) The biconditional: from \(\forall W\in\mathsf S: W\cup\{d\}\in\mathsf S \wedge W\setminus\{d\}\in\mathsf S\) (L290 defines \(\mathsf S\)), \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\Leftrightarrow\Gamma\setminus\{d\}\in\mathsf S\) follows both ways; and the family \(\mathsf S\) is invariant under toggling \(d\). No counterexample: interference (L309, \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\}\}\)) correctly refuses the label to \(b\) (adding \(b\) destroys \(\{a\}\)) and to \(a\) (removing \(a\) leaves \(\varnothing\), which can never be a support, since non-circular dependence needs a nonempty \(G\), L255). O45's second spring is correctly *not* work-free: removing it from the support \(\{b\}\) leaves \(\varnothing\) — it is contributory, matching "It was already a route" (L307). (b) "\(\{d\}\) is critical in no support": CriticalBlock is \(W\in\mathsf S\wedge W\setminus B\notin\mathsf S\) (L296), and the removal clause kills it for \(B=\{d\}\). (c) The infinitary clause: in L311's example \(\mathsf S=\{W: W\text{ unbounded}\}\); each \(d_k\) is work-free (adding or deleting one index leaves an unbounded set unbounded), yet \(B=\Gamma\) is critical in \(\Gamma\) (\(\varnothing\notin\mathsf S\)). True, and it matches "(B) records the collective contribution". Also checked: "does no work by itself" is not misread against L299 ("a commitment critical in one successful support need not be critical in the full candidate") or L305's warning that upward closure fails in the interference case.
