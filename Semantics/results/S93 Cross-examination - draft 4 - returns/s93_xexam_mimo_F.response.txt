**1. X03 · T1 — the relation the declaration calls a definition is not evaluable across two organizations.**
(K) fixes a signature only inside one organization, on a contract of that organization's own edits and boundaries (L113–117): \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}\), with \(L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\). The insertion asks that two signatures "read on \(C\) through \(\tau\) and \(\tau'\), coincide under a footprint bijection". Three identifications are needed and the wording supplies only the footprint map.

(i) *Index elements.* Derivation 1's own form is \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)=\{(\tau(a),\sigma(b),L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)))\}\) (L556) — elements indexed by pairs of \(E\)'s edits and boundaries; for \(k'\) the index is \((\tau'(a),\sigma'(b))\), pairs of a different organization. As sets of triples these coincide only where \(\tau(a)=\tau'(a)\) and \(\sigma(b)=\sigma'(b)\) as objects, which is not what is meant. Derivation 2's proof pulls both back instead ("the two signatures, read on \(C\), coincide", L564). The wording must say so.

(ii) *Values.* \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) lives in \(\prod_{v\in V_k}X_v\), \(L_{k'}(\tau'(a),\sigma'(b))\) in \(\prod_{v'\in V_{k'}}X'_{v'}\). A footprint bijection maps ports, not values. Inside one organization the shared \(X_v\) can be read in; across two, nothing can. O9's float port (heights) and dial port (labels) are a case where "coincide" has no truth value until a value map is chosen, and the wording chooses none.

(iii) Where the identification *does* come from is visible in the worked case: each \(k,\varphi(k)\) carries "one anchor, the same subnetwork of \(D\) with port translations onto the same ports of \(D\)" (L562), and "composing the one translation with the inverse of the other gives a footprint bijection" (L564). The insertion's general relation requires no shared anchor, so it is left without the identification its own proof uses. The declaration's "Part II now defines when…" is therefore too strong.
