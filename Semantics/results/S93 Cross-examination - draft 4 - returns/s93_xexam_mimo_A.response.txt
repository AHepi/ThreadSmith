# Report — Part A of eleven: rivals (item X09)

## Points

**1. `X09 · T1/T3` — the exclusion sentence reverses its own claim on a literal parse.**
Line 315 reads: "...one carried onto the other by a structure-preserving bijection that takes its transport with it and leaves its answers as they are, **conflict at no pair** (Part VIII, Recoding; Derivation 8), **and nor do two candidates**, however differently they are cut, that some such relations of the target would let both meet (F1), (F2) and (A) at each admitted pair both translate; neither pair is a pair of rivals." The ellipsis site is the verb phrase "conflict at no pair"; "nor do these candidates [conflict at no pair]" parses as "these candidates conflict at some pair" — the opposite of the declaration ("...are not rivals") and in flat contradiction of the clause immediately after the semicolon ("neither pair is a pair of rivals"). In a CLAIM item the wording must carry the declaration; as written it supports two opposite readings and is settled only by the last clause. Repair in the fenced wording below: "do not conflict at any pair ... ; nor do two candidates conflict at any pair, however differently they are cut, when ... both translate. Neither pair is a pair of rivals."

**2. `X09 · T1` — the illustration of conflict is not a case of the defined conflict.**
Line 315 defines conflict at \((a,b)\) as "their answers there differ, **or** when each of them could meet (F1), (F2) and (A) there under some relations of the target that the adopted physics admits (Part I) and no such relations let both, **as when two of their active components with one anchor have different relations there**." The "as when" clause is presented as an instance; it is not entailed. Counterexample. Let \(D\) have a subnetwork \(\lambda\) over ports \(x,y\); fix a pair \((a,b)\). Let \(E\) carry two active components \(k_1,k_2\), both anchored to \(\lambda\), with \(L_{k_1}=\{y\ge x\}\), \(L_{k_2}=\{y\ge x+1\}\); let \(E'\) carry one component \(k'\) anchored to \(\lambda\) with \(L_{k'}=\{y\ge x+1\}\). Then \(\operatorname{Sol}_E=\operatorname{Sol}_{E'}=\{y\ge x+1\}\), so every query gives equal answers: the first disjunct fails. And \(E\) can never meet (F1) at \((a,b)\) under any target relations, since (F1) would demand \(\operatorname{proj}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda}(a,b)\) equal both \(\{y\ge x\}\) and \(\{y\ge x+1\}\): the "each of them could meet" condition fails, so the second disjunct fails. Yet \(k_1\) and \(k'\) are exactly "two of their active components with one anchor [having] different relations there". The illustration therefore asserts what the definition denies. \(E\) is a legitimate explanatory candidate (Part V imposes no requirement that a candidate meet (E)), and its \(k_1/k_2\) clash is precisely the "contradiction inside the candidate" named in question (c2). Repair: make the anchor-clash a disjunct in its own right (fenced below).

**3. `X09 · T2` — the declaration omits one claim and compresses another.**
(i) The sentence "A candidate offered for \(p\) is offered for the whole of \(p\): it claims (F1), (F2) and (A) at every pair of \(C\), tested or not, and the rest of (E) on \(C\)" asserts what offering commits a candidate to, including at *untested* pairs; the current text leaves this unconcluded (it has no notion of being "offered for the whole of \(p\)"), the declaration nowhere states it, and it is load-bearing for O24 and for the "Problems" paragraph. (ii) The declaration's "Two candidates that differ only in how they are written ... are not rivals" drops the wording's conditions ("one carried onto the other by a structure-preserving bijection that takes its transport with it and leaves its answers as they are") and "both translate". Exact additions:

````text
It says that a candidate offered for p is offered for the whole of p: it claims (F1), (F2) and (A) at every pair of C, tested or not, and the rest of (E) on C, while outside C it claims nothing on p. It says that a failure which inspecting the candidate itself shows — a component anchored to nothing, a failure of non-circular dependence, a contradiction inside the candidate — is a result established for an assessor who holds a usable receipt for the inspection, and needs no test of the target. Where the declaration says of the two non-rival pairs, "two candidates that differ only in how they are written", it means differ only in how they are written and be carried one onto the other by a structure-preserving bijection that takes its transport with it and leaves its answers as they are; and "at each admitted pair" means at each admitted pair both translate.
````

## Question (a)

The wording keeps to the owner's decisions and to the drafters' rule. It hard-codes the owner's correction twice: "No list of all rivals is supposed: a candidate's rivals are among the candidates someone has offered, and a candidate that nobody has offered is no one's rival", and "Nor are rivals a selection population: whether a selected transport is underdetermined at an unseen pair is fixed by its population (Derivation 3), whatever rivals anyone offers" — the second is the owner's "Once the explanation is rescued, the mistake shouldn't be able to creep back in" built into the explanation's own content, with no record of rescues doing the work (cf. Part VIII, L369: "with no record of which candidates failed before or of how any was changed"). Nothing counts, grades or ranks: "fits" is a binary predicate relative to an assessor's receipts, not the owner's "harder to fit". The one residual worry is historical: "one of them has been offered as an answer to \(p\) in place of the other" is narrow if "in place of" means "as a replacement of that particular other" — two sibling variations of one original could then escape rivalry. On the natural reading of "variation is a competitor", which the drafters' reading records, "offered ... in place of" is the rendering of "discovered competitor", and the passage needs no list to be produced.

## Question (c2)

The text does not settle it. "A result is established for an assessor who holds a usable receipt for it (Part IX)"; Part IX makes a receipt "a derivation tree over leaves", an "evidence leaf" being "a reference to an event with an interpreted claim" — which can carry an inspection of the written candidate (an occurrence), and (K3) speaks of any established \(\neg O\). But "established" is introduced beside tests, and Part VIII (L369) ties it to "the target's answer at \((a,b)\)". So a failure of non-circular dependence, a component anchored to nothing, or a contradiction inside the candidate is not clearly established, and hence not clearly a bar to "fits". It should be settled, because X10 makes a problem turn on "both fit": if inspection failures do not count, a candidate broken on inspection fits for ever, pairs with any rival, and holds a problem open that reading alone closes; if they count, such a candidate never enters a problem pair, and "easy to vary" can only be charged against candidates that survive inspection (this pulls on the companion item X10). Recommended wording, added after the "fits" sentence (with the declaration sentence fenced in point 3):

````text
a failure that inspecting the candidate itself shows — a component anchored to nothing, a failure of non-circular dependence, a contradiction inside the candidate — is such a result once the assessor holds a usable receipt for the inspection, and needs no test of the target.
````

## Question (d)

Requiring conflict at some admitted edit–boundary pair is right: it is the rivals-side of Part II's rule that a difference no admitted change separates is idle at that level, and it correctly excludes the two named classes — candidates differing only by a part that does no work (N1's sun god: equal answers, relations would let both meet the conditions), and candidates differing only in writing (Derivation 8). The per-pair form loses nothing to *joint* incompatibility, since the component relations "are supplied independently for each \((a,b)\)" (Derivation 3's proof), so pairwise co-satisfiability is joint co-satisfiability. The loss is one of reach: the definition counts only pairs "that both their transports translate", while the same paragraph says "outside \(C\) it claims nothing on \(p\), but what its organization and transport give there can conflict with what another's give" — a disagreement at a pair one transport translates and the other does not is therefore dropped, although both organizations give relations there. Small, but the paragraph invites the case it excludes.

(d) X09: RIGHT, WITH A LOSS — a conflict at a pair only one of the two transports translates is not counted.

## Replacement wording

````text
**Rivals.** Two explanatory candidates for one question \(p\) are **rivals** when one of them has been offered as an answer to \(p\) in place of the other and they conflict at some admitted edit–boundary pair of the target that both their transports translate, in \(C\) or outside it. Two candidates **conflict** at such a pair \((a,b)\) when their answers there differ, when two of their active components have one anchor and different relations there, or when each of them could meet (F1), (F2) and (A) there under some relations of the target that the adopted physics admits (Part I) and no such relations let both. A candidate offered for \(p\) is offered for the whole of \(p\): it claims (F1), (F2) and (A) at every pair of \(C\), tested or not, and the rest of (E) on \(C\); outside \(C\) it claims nothing on \(p\), but what its organization and transport give there can conflict with what another's give. Two candidates that differ only in how they are written, one carried onto the other by a structure-preserving bijection that takes its transport with it and leaves its answers as they are, do not conflict at any pair (Part VIII, Recoding; Derivation 8); nor do two candidates conflict at any pair, however differently they are cut, when some such relations of the target would let both meet (F1), (F2) and (A) at each admitted pair both translate. Neither pair is a pair of rivals. A result is **established** for an assessor who holds a usable receipt for it (Part IX); by (K3), a result that tells against a candidate tells against it only together with the background and instruments of the test that yields it. A candidate **fits** what is established for an assessor when no result established for that assessor shows it failing a condition of (E); a failure that inspecting the candidate itself shows — a component anchored to nothing, a failure of non-circular dependence, a contradiction inside the candidate — is such a result once the assessor holds a usable receipt for the inspection, and needs no test of the target. No list of all rivals is supposed: a candidate's rivals are among the candidates someone has offered, and a candidate that nobody has offered is no one's rival. Nor are rivals a selection population: whether a selected transport is underdetermined at an unseen pair is fixed by its population (Derivation 3), whatever rivals anyone offers.
````
