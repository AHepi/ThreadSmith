## 0. In short

- **What V5 is, in plain words.** A machine that follows two things in a flat world (one it pushes, one that stays put) from one moment to the next, keeping every place they might be and every speed they might have under each of 648 force laws its builders wrote, without keeping the past. It can save and reload that state, forecast where a push would take them, answer a few fixed English questions ("finally, A was left of B"), and pick a plan from a short supplied list, but only when every surviving law agrees. When its laws stop fitting it says so, and says whether only its preferred laws failed or all 648 did. **Then it stops answering. It learns no new law and does not recover** (VR L246; RM L94).
- **The ladder (S90), rung by rung.** Recognise objects: **no**; it is handed two named slots, A and B, with their outlines and their matching from frame to frame supplied by inherited parts (V17, V18). Displacement: **yes, its strongest rung**, as an exact law of motion with speed and force; the family of laws handed in, the right ones found by elimination. Object permanence: **only by premise**; the two slots cannot vanish, but nothing ever goes out of sight, there is no screen, and losing sight of a thing stops the machine. Amounts: **no**; always exactly two things, no counting. Addition: **no**.
- **Construct first (S88).** V5 has two of the four steps of the theory's episode (L429): a **recognized difficulty** (its "learned_model_mismatch" and "full_family_contradiction" states) and a **target held before its criticism** (the carried state of its failed laws). It has **no objection** (it never says which part of a failed law is wrong) and **no response** (it abstains and latches). None of created explanation's further parts (D14.7: an origin with a build, a repair of a claimed aim, an account deployed, the repair produced through it) is present. And V5 has deliberately thrown away the record a repair would be checked against: by design it keeps no past observations (RM L90).
- **V5 against the owner's machine (S135).** V5 has what S135 lacks in the **care of its uncertainty** (every joint alternative kept, never smeared into a range), in **honest refusal**, in a **richer displacement** (speed, force, mass), in **acting** (it issues pushes and plans them) and in an **independent second route of checking**. S135 has what V5 lacks in **everything the owner asked for above displacement**: many objects recognised from the picture, a screen and permanence tested the infant-study way, amounts, and **addition formed by the machine itself from its own failures**, with the found-way control, the order shuffle and the same-final-frame check. Worth carrying into S135: joint alternatives for hidden objects, the "my rule failed / my whole menu failed" distinction, certificate-gated use of a built rule (a way to show the rule deployed), a second scorer. Not worth carrying for the aims: the history-free memory (it removes the ledger S135 builds from), the 648-law menu, the inherited grammar, the save-and-restore engineering.
- **Missing, ranked** (section D): **six BLOCKING** (no rule formed from its own failures; no record of failures to repair against; recognition fixed to two supplied slots; no permanence through going out of sight; no amounts; no addition), **eight IMPORTANT**, **four MINOR**. Five items were removed on re-reading because the material covers them (section E).
- **The plain conclusion for the owner.** V5 is a careful instrument for one rung (displacement) with an honest "I don't know". It is not a step up the owner's ladder and not a constructor: nothing in it is worked out by it. Its best use toward the owner's aims is as a source of three or four parts to carry into the owner's own machine, not as a base to build the ladder on.

### 1.1 The words

- The jobs table's **"given / fixed / added"** (VR L47 to L55) are origins of the **jobs** (what the owner or the agent asked for), not of the parts.

### B.3 The smallest addition that turns its recognised failure into a held target and a built repair

What the owner would need, in order, kept as small as the theory allows. The first item is a precondition V5's design removed.

1. **Keep the evidence a repair is checked against.** From the first `learned_model_mismatch` on, keep the public packets (or their covers and the pushes issued) in a **failure ledger**. V5 keeps none by design (RM L90: "It does not store a current-episode packet or force journal"); V4 had such a journal (VR L39), so the parts exist. Without it, a new law outside the 648 cannot be checked against anything the machine has seen: the carried state holds evidence only *conditional on each law already in the family*. (Cost: small, by the report's own account of V4; exact cost not knowable without the code.)
2. **Hold the failed laws as the target.** At mismatch, keep the two preferred laws and their last predictions as the thing to be repaired (the claimed aim), and the rows they got right as protected (S135's built way, step 1).
3. **Compute the failure's content and blame a part.** For each ledger row, where the failed law's predicted rectangle missed the observed cover, and which single parameter (gain, retention, bias, beta), changed, could close the miss. That is the missing **objection**.
4. **Build the repair from that content.** Solve exactly (V5 already uses exact rational arithmetic, VR L29) for the parameter values that make the law fit every ledger row; keep every solution as a rival ("undetermined" where they disagree, which V5 already knows how to say); commit when one is left. If the parameter language allows values off the 648-law grid (a retention of 3/5, say), the result can be a law no builder listed. This is S135's built way carried over; its verdict would be the same as S135's: **constructed within a class its builders named**, on the present reading, if the five witnesses hold; and whether that is "worked out" in the owner's sense is S134's question, still open.
5. **Run the controls S135 ran.** The found way (the first law in a fixed list of the 648 that fits the ledger: V5's survivors are this already); the filter-all way; the order shuffle; the same ledger without its failed rows; a no-difficulty schedule; the knock-out of the repaired law with sham and restore, once with learning paused and once with it on.
6. **For a change V5's laws cannot express** (the hidden stop and the hidden kick are changes *in* the law at an unknown time), the class must allow a change point: "the law holds until t, then another holds". Written in by us, that class is handed in; the machine would build which t and which second law. A restart after `full_family_contradiction` also needs a start from motion with unknown speed, which V5 excludes ("general initial-state inference", RM L94).

**Rough cost.** S135's whole build step is 287 lines and its run 15 CPU-seconds. V5's exact machinery is far heavier (63 seconds for 1.5 seconds of simulated stationary scene, VR L244; states of millions of bytes, VR L242), and every repair candidate would have to be checked against the ledger by that machinery. On V5, the addition is likely a few hundred lines plus a frozen experiment of the kind of its added case, with run times of minutes to hours per scene; this is a guess from the report's figures, not from the code, which is not here. On S135, the same idea is already built.

### B.4 The active force probe the report recommends: working out, or identification?

The report's next step (VR L306): "one prospectively frozen **active force probe** using the existing observation interface", chosen before execution to separate the three surviving laws from the changed world.

- **It is identification within the supplied family, not working out.** The rivals it can tell apart are the three surviving laws. The changed world (a stop) is not among V5's hypotheses, so the best the probe can do is make all three surviving laws fail sooner, which moves V5 to `full_family_contradiction`, which latches. Nothing is built. The report itself calls it "a new identification question" (VR L306) and lists the probe beside "Richer observations ... or an enlarged changing-law family" as changes to "a named part of the question" (VR L192).
- **It is handed in.** The probe is to be specified by the builders before execution. If V5 instead **chose** its own probe (a push at which its surviving laws' forecasts disagree most), it would hold a problem and act to split it (D10.1; the test at L317; S134's critique test 7, "a designed fork"). That is a real step of the episode (a test the machine chooses), still not construction. It is cheap to add to V5's planner (it already enumerates up to eight candidate pushes; the goal would be "split the survivors" instead of "make A left of B").
- **What it would add for the owner.** Faster recognition of a difficulty. What the owner needs after that recognition (an objection, a built repair) is not in it.

### C.3 Which of V5's parts to carry into the owner's machine

**Worth carrying, for the aims:**

1. **Joint alternatives for hidden things.** When two same-coloured things go behind the screen, keep every pairing of which is which and where, and drop pairings only when the world rules them out (V5's correlated rectangles with correspondence nodes, without hulls). This lifts two of S135's declared limits (identity by nearest; touching things counted as one) and makes permanence honest under uncertainty, which addition rests on (S135's permanence-off knock-out). Rough cost in S135: a few hundred lines; the grid is small enough that exact sets stay small.
2. **The two-level failure.** "My held rule failed" against "no rule in my whole menu fits". S135 already logs the second; making it a state the machine acts on marks the moment when **the menu itself is the problem**, which is the place where the naming trap (S118, S134) could first be faced by a machine rather than by us.
3. **Certificate-gated use of the built rule.** Let the machine plan with its built addition rule (for example, "make there be five behind the screen": choose how many to send in), acting only when every surviving rival agrees. That would give S135's witness 4 (explanatory use, "partly") and D14.7's Deploy and ProducesVia something concrete: a declared aim met through the built rule, and lost when the rule is knocked out.
4. **A second, independent scorer** for the key numbers (V5's attack ledger idea), cheap in S135.
5. **Acting to split rivals**: when S135's built way holds several rival rules, let it choose the next scene's amounts so that they disagree (S135 waits for the world to supply a deciding row). This is V5's planner pointed at a problem, the active observation of S134's bounded constructor.

**Not worth carrying, for the aims:**

- **History-free continuation.** It deletes exactly the ledger S135 builds addition from; it buys forward handoff, which the ladder does not need.
- **The 648-law family and a preferred subset fitted elsewhere.** That is a large menu with found members: the naming trap at full size, and nothing the machine forms.
- **The inherited grammar and English questions.** Not on the ladder; handed in whole (RM L38).
- **Save/restore, source binding, the CLI and the exact work-reduction machinery.** Engineering for V5's scale and trust contract, not for the ladder.

## D. What is missing, ranked

### BLOCKING

**D1. No rule formed from its own failures.**
1. When its laws fail, V5 forms no new law or rule; it abstains and latches.
2. VR L246, "it does not learn a replacement from the mismatch"; RM L94, "No recovery after a latched stop, new-law learning ... is implemented here"; VR L131, L133 (the hidden kick and excited mass2 scenes end in "unknown").
3. **Yes** (VR L246, RM L94, VR L9, VR L302: "not qualified as ... a replacement physical-law learner").
4. S88's goal is a machine that constructs first. In the theory, construction begins at a recognized difficulty and goes through an objection and a response to a built repair (L429, D14.7). V5 stops at the difficulty.
5. Section B.3, steps 2 to 5 (hold the failed law as target; compute the miss; solve for a repaired law; controls). On V5: a few hundred lines plus a frozen experiment, minutes to hours per scene (a guess from VR L242, L244; the code is not here). On S135: already done for addition.
6. **BLOCKING.**

**D2. No record of failures to check a repair against.**
1. V5 keeps no past observations, so a law outside its supplied family cannot be checked against what it has seen.
2. RM L90, "It does not store a current-episode packet or force journal"; VR L240, "The summary is sufficient only for the admitted future questions. It is not a general episodic record."
3. **In part.** It admits the loss of *audit* ("Retain public evidence separately when historical audit is needed", RM L92; "Discarding history ... removes self-contained retrospective audit", VR L286), not that this loss blocks learning a new law.
4. A built repair is checked against the machine's own record (S135's ledger; S134's bounded constructor's ledger). Without one, the only repairs V5 can check are laws already in its supplied menu, and choosing among those is finding.
5. Keep a failure ledger from the first mismatch onward (V4's journal already existed, VR L39). Small.
6. **BLOCKING** (for construct-first in V5 as designed; cheap to lift).

**D3. Recognition fixed to two supplied slots.**
1. V5 has no rule that finds the things in a scene; it is handed two named references with supplied outlines and matching.
2. RM L94, "two persistent conditional references ... Conditional reference correspondence is not established general object identity"; VR L72, "V18 supplies conditional reference-slot correspondence".
3. **Yes** for identity and perception (RM L94; VR L72; VR L59); not as "only two things".
4. S90's first rung, and amounts rest on it: with exactly two things, "how many" cannot be asked.
5. Not small in V5: its exact joint state grows with every pairing (21,024 states for two references, VR L105); a third reference multiplies it. In S135 rule 1 already does this for any number of blobs.
6. **BLOCKING** (because amounts sit on it; that the rule is handed in is not itself a problem, S91 approved handed-in lower rungs).

**D4. No object permanence through going out of sight.**
1. Nothing in V5 ever goes behind anything; losing sight of a thing latches "unavailable" and stops answering.
2. Searched "occlu", "screen", "behind", "out of sight": 0 hits; RM L52, L88; VR L74; VR L59 ("missing-observation recovery, object birth/death, re-identification" outside).
