## 2. The reply's argument

Closing line: "R39: FALLS — declared WORDING, but it removes the normative relation's description as "declared," the very claim change R38 declares." Point 1 (task b), its core:

> "R38 is declared CLAIM precisely to remove that description ("no longer calls it a declared input"). R39 removes the second occurrence of it: the new wording, "taken as a substantive input" (line 449), no longer assigns N to the declared-input category."

> "The WORDING definition requires the new wording to say what the old said. "Taken as a substantive input" and "declared as a substantive input" are not paraphrases in a text where "declared input" is a technical category and the surrounding paragraph used it technically. A reader who could conclude from the current text that N is a declared input (Part XI, second sentence) can no longer conclude it from that sentence. That is a claim change, and R39's declaration says there is none. The reason given ("erratum") confirms the old wording was wrong, not merely loose."

> "The substance of R39 is required: without it, Part XI would still describe N as "declared," contradicting R38's new wording, Part 0 line 25 ("takes the normative relation as an input") and Part XIV line 514 ("taken as an input and never derived"). Repair: re-declare R39 as CLAIM with R38's declaration, or fold R39 into R38."

It names no case. Its point 3 holds that after R02, R38, R45 and R49 the normative relation "is never called a declared input".

## 3. The texts

- **Quotations (S90 rule 8).** Old s2 "takes a normative relation N as a declared input" and old s3 "N … declared as a substantive input when aesthetic value is claimed": found, file-11 L447. "Besides the two primitives": found, file-11 L514. Part 0 line 25 "takes the normative relation as an input": found, revised L25. "taken as an input and never derived": found, but at revised L512 (primitive 2; file-11 L510), not line 514, which reads "Everything else is derived from the two primitives, the declared indices and the declared inputs (below)." The line number is wrong; the quotation and the point stand.
- **"Declared input" is a technical category in both texts.** File 11 L514 heads a paragraph "**Declared inputs.** Besides the two primitives, some claims take stated inputs that the semantics records and does not supply"; revised L518 adds "a declared input is something a claim takes as stated, which the semantics records and does not supply". Old s3 predicates exactly that of \(\mathcal N\): an input that is declared. "Declared as a substantive input" cannot be read as the ordinary verb one sentence after s2 used "a declared input" technically. The drafter's own REASON reads it the same way: left in place, the clause "would bring back ... the category that W6.2 removes".
- **So the entry changes what a reader can conclude.** From old s3 a reader concludes that the aesthetic \(\mathcal N\) is a declared input; from new s3 a reader cannot. That is the brief's CLAIM test ("can no longer conclude something the current text allowed"), and not the WORDING test ("the new wording says what the old wording said"). It is the same conclusion whose removal from s2 W6.2 declares as a claim change, and one removal cannot be a claim change in s2 and a paraphrase in s3.
- **The drafted condition for WORDING is met, but it is the wrong test.** W6.4's clause is in place (revised L516: "A verdict that depends on one of these, or on the normative relation where a claim invokes worth, is a verdict given the input"). So old and new do send \(\mathcal N\) to one missing-input treatment. But the texts reach it differently. In file 11 the rule at L514 covers only "one of these", which excludes the primitives, so it reaches \(\mathcal N\) only through L447's calling \(\mathcal N\) a declared input (in tension with L510/L514). In the revised text it reaches \(\mathcal N\) through W6.4's explicit clause, with \(\mathcal N\) kept out of the category. Having the same consequence does not make the category claim the same claim.
- **The change itself is needed, and it cannot be dropped as REASON says.** Without it, revised L449 would read "(primitive 2, Part XIV) as an input" in s2 and "declared as a substantive input" in s3. It would then pull against revised L512 ("taken as an input and never derived; the aesthetic relation of Part XI is one instance") and L516 ("Besides the two primitives"). W6.2's declaration ("no longer calls it a declared input") would also be false of Part XI as a whole, since that declaration is true of Part XI only with W6.3 applied. So the REASON's "it can be dropped without harm to any other entry" is false.
- **"Fold into R38" is refused.** Merging the two entries would renumber R2-39 onward in the record and in the S90 id maps. It would also turn two disjoint OLDs into one overlapping OLD, for no gain. R38's declaration cannot be copied either: it says Part XI "names the normative relation as primitive 2", which R39's NEW does not do. The drafter's ready declaration, with its positive half, is exact.
- **Related text.** Revised L25 (R02): a claim "of aesthetic value takes the normative relation as an input". It agrees. Revised L31: \(\mathcal N\) "taken as an input wherever a question invokes worth". It agrees. Revised L528 (R49): "one of the declared inputs Part XIV lists or the normative relation". It agrees. Revised L590: "\(\mathcal N\), together with declared indices and declared inputs". It agrees. Not ruled here: revised L51 (file-11 L53, untouched by any entry) still says of aesthetics "In Part XI, as a declared normative relation." It does not say "declared input", so the declaration below and W6.3's GAIN ("No sentence calls \(\mathcal N\) a declared input") remain literally true. But it is the one remaining place that calls \(\mathcal N\) "declared". It is passed to the orchestrator as a loose end.
- **Cases.** The reply names none. The S81 and S89 case books contain no "aesthet", "beaut" or "artistic" (searched). No O- or N-case claims aesthetic value, so reclassifying the entry moves no verdict. CASES AT RISK "None" stands.
