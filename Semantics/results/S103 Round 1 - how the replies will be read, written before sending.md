## How the replies will be read

1. **Nothing is opened before the runs are over.** No reply, receipt, reasoning file, attempt file or standard-output file of a call is opened until every call of both runs has ended its last allowed pass (a reply accepted, or failed with no pass left), or until 24 hours have passed since the earlier of the two launches, whichever comes first. Each launch time is the first line of its run log (`launched <UTC time>`). If the 24 hours pass first, the replies then accepted are read under this rule, the calls still going are left to finish, and a reply that comes later is read under this same rule and recorded as late. The progress logs (the runners' `start`, attempt, `ok`, `failed` and `done` lines) may be watched; they hold no reply text.
2. **What counts as a reply.** A Mimo reply counts only when it finishes "stop" with END OF REPORT on its last line (`s80_call.accept_reader`); a GLM reply only when its last non-blank line carries END OF REPORT (`glm_via_claude_code.py`). A failed call counts neither for nor against any candidate; its attempt files are kept and are not read for arguments. A call that failed only on the connection (every attempt ended with no HTTP status, a cut stream, a timeout or a proxy refusal) is not an answer from the model and is not counted against it (lessons S21, S24); if such a call is still failed after pass 3, it may be sent again only under a new note written before sending. A call still failed after pass 3 on answers the model did give (length at the ceiling, or no END OF REPORT) is final, and the candidates of its part are reported as **not examined by that reader**. After any container restart, the runners are looked at and every call not finished is relaunched from a shell started after the restart; a proxy failure is not a reply (lesson S24).
3. **A reply is a set of arguments to rule on, not a result.** No sentence of the text is kept, changed or dropped on a reader's say-so, and no closing line settles anything by itself. Silence is not agreement: a candidate that no point attacks is not thereby shown to hold.
4. **Tabulation first.** Once the runs are over (rule 1), one fresh Opus 5.5 agent, which built nothing of this round and has read no reply, tabulates both readers' replies to all three parts before any ruling: for each candidate and each reader, the closing line as written (or "no closing line"), every rival wording and variation with its exact wording, and every point that alleges a defect in the candidate itself or a break elsewhere. It writes `results/S103 Round 1 - tabulation of the replies, before any ruling.md`, created at once and filled as it goes (lesson S28). It rules on nothing.
5. **What goes to a checker.** Every candidate that either reader closes VARIES or FAILS, and every candidate on which a point of either reader shows a defect, whatever that reader's closing line says, goes to one fresh Opus 5.5 checker of its own, who built nothing of this round and rules on no other candidate of it. Where it is in doubt whether a point shows a defect, the candidate goes to a checker. The checker is given the part brief, file 99, both readers' sections on the candidate whole and any other passage of either reply that names it, the tabulation's rows for it, and this rule. It rules:
   - **KEEP**: the sentence stands as it is;
   - **FIX**: the exact new wording, whole; the sentence changes; with the reasons why this wording and not the text's, nor the reader's where it differs;
   - **DROP**: the sentence leaves the text; with every sentence that depends on it named, by line and words, and what becomes of each.
   For a VARIES, the checker also says whether the rival wording is **a rewording that changes nothing the theory needs** (then the sentence is easy to vary on this reading: that is recorded, and the ruling is FIX only if the rival is clearer, KEEP otherwise) or **a different claim** (then it is ruled like any other proposal). For a FAILS, the checker says whether the failure is shown, and rules the repair. The checkers run at most five at a time, a limit kept by a count the orchestrator keeps (decisions S12, S17; lesson S22).
6. **Candidates that go to no checker.** A candidate both readers close HOLDS, with no point of either showing a defect, goes to no checker and is recorded **"held under both readers' attempts; not thereby final"**. A candidate one reader closes HOLDS, with no point showing a defect, where the other reader's reply to its part never came back (rule 2), is recorded "held under one reader's attempts; not examined by the other; not thereby final". A candidate neither reader examined is recorded "not examined in this round".
7. **Disagreements are ruled on their arguments, not counted.** Where the readers differ on a candidate (HOLDS against VARIES or FAILS, or two different repairs), the checker states each side's argument and rules between them or writes a third wording; the number of readers on a side decides nothing, and so do the length and the confidence of a reply.
8. **Parked.** A proposal about what hard to vary covers is recorded as parked and not applied (decision S34), whatever the closing line it comes with; the candidate is ruled on the rest of the points made about it.
9. **The owner's words bound every ruling.** A FIX may not say more than the owner's words of S20, S21, S23, S25 to S28, S33 and S34 say, nor less than they require: no list, set, count, grade, ranking or record doing work the explanation's own content should do (S20); nothing about what must happen to candidates, which is the choice of the person choosing (S21); no word or idea S23 forbids, "argument" as reasons why this and not that, and every accepting tentative (S23); physical possibility only where information or knowledge is instantiated or transformed, and as the content of claims a candidate can conflict with (S25 to S27); nothing settled, and a ruling out by a claim taken as given a choice the person made (S28). A point that turns on a choice the owner has not made is recorded, with both sides' arguments, as a question for the owner, and the checker rules only whether the present wording states the matter as the owner's words leave it.
10. **Quotations are compared with the text.** Each quotation a reply or a ruling relies on is compared with file 99 or the brief; where one is not found, that is recorded and the point is ruled on what the text says. A reply's quotation of a book longer than 25 words, a run of quotations joined only by page numbers, punctuation or a few words counting as one (lesson S29), is not copied into any record.
11. **File names** (lesson S25). The replies stay under their tags in `results/S103 Round 1 - returns/`. Each ruling is written to `results/S103 Round 1 - rulings/ruling C<nn> L<line>.md`, one file per candidate, created at once and filled as it goes (lesson S28), never written over; a second ruling on the same candidate (rule 12) goes to `ruling C<nn> L<line> - second.md`.
12. **A critical review before anything is applied.** After every ruling has been written, one critical review by Fable 5.1 (decision S35: "use Fable 5.1 sparingly for critical reviews") reads every ruling, the tabulation and the held records against this rule and the owner's words, and writes `results/S103 Round 1 - critical review of the rulings.md`. No ruling is applied before it. Where the review contests a ruling, the orchestrator decides (decision S13), with its reasons why this and not the other: it keeps the ruling, or sends the candidate to a second fresh checker with the review's point; either way it is recorded.
13. **Applying.** The FIXes and DROPs are applied by program, after the review, to a new copy of the stand-alone text (`tests/103 The semantics, standing alone, after round 1.md`, or the name the next log number gives it), one line to one line where a DROP does not remove a whole line; file 99 is never written over, and nothing is written into `authority/`. Each applied change is compared by program with its ruling, byte for byte. The cases the changed sentences bear on (the worked cases in the text, and any case of the project's case book that turns on them) are re-read on the new copy by a fresh agent, and the records are updated.
14. **When the series ends.** A round in which no ruling is FIX or DROP ends the series: "no moves left" (decision S35). Otherwise the next round goes to the new copy, under its own rule, written and committed before sending.

## How the runs are launched

From the repository root, after this file's commit is pushed, two processes side by side, each detached so that it outlives the agent that starts it, the keys loaded into its own environment from the session's key file and never printed (the other keys unset):

```
setsid nohup bash -c 'cd /home/user/ThreadSmith || exit 1; echo "launched $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)"; R="Semantics/results/S103 Round 1 - how the replies will be read, written before sending.md"; { git ls-files --error-unmatch -- "$R" >/dev/null && git diff --quiet HEAD -- "$R"; } || { echo "the reading rule is not committed or differs from HEAD; nothing sent"; exit 1; }; set -a; . <key file>; set +a; unset ATRIA_API_KEY GLM_API_KEY OPENAI_API_KEY; for pass in 1 2 3; do echo "pass $pass $(date -u +%H:%M:%S)"; <scratchpad>/venv/bin/python Semantics/tools/s87_run.py "Semantics/tools/s103_jobs - round 1, Mimo.json" || break; done; echo "loop ended $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)"' > <scratchpad>/s103_mimo_run.log 2>&1 < /dev/null &

setsid nohup bash -c 'cd /home/user/ThreadSmith || exit 1; echo "launched $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)"; set -a; . <key file>; set +a; unset ATRIA_API_KEY MIMO_API_KEY OPENAI_API_KEY; <scratchpad>/venv/bin/python Semantics/tools/s103_glm_loop.py "Semantics/tools/s103_jobs - round 1, GLM.json"; echo "loop ended $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)"' > <scratchpad>/s103_glm_run.log 2>&1 < /dev/null &
```

Each loop's process id (the session leader's) goes in `<scratchpad>/s103_mimo_run.pid` and `<scratchpad>/s103_glm_run.pid`. In the Mimo loop each turn is one pass: a job whose reply has come back is skipped, a failed job goes as the next pass (its earlier files kept under their pass numbers), and the runner refuses a pass above `max_pass`; pass 2 starts only after every call of pass 1 has ended. The GLM loop works in rounds of the same kind (`s103_glm_loop.py`).

**When each run is over.** Mimo: the log's last line reads `loop ended <UTC time>` and the process in the pid file has exited; or each `s103_vary_mimo_<n>` has a `.response.txt` in the returns folder, or a `.error.txt` and a `.receipt.json` with `"failed": true` from its third pass. GLM: the log holds `s103_glm_loop: every pass ended; …` followed by `loop ended <UTC time>`, and the process in the pid file has exited; or each part has a `.response.txt` under one of its tags (`s103_vary_glm_<n>`, `_pass2`, `_pass3`), or `s103_vary_glm_<n>_pass3.receipt.json` with `"accepted": false`. A watcher outside the agent that launches the runs waits for both (lesson S14); the launching agent hands back as soon as a call of each reader is in flight.
