## How the replies will be read

**Declared departure from the S93 rule, for the owner's budget.** Decision S24 allows 15 Claude agents for this task, and this workflow is agents 10 to 14; one agent is left. So where S93 sent every challenged item to its own fresh checker, here **one fresh Claude reader** (agent 15), who did not build, repair, read or test the repaired copy and did not write these briefs, reads every reply and rules every challenge. Nothing else in the S93 rule is loosened on that account.

1. **Nothing is opened before the run is over.** No reply, receipt, reasoning file or attempt file is opened until every one of the 6 calls has ended its last allowed pass (a reply returned, or failed with no pass left), or until 36 hours have passed since the loop was launched, whichever comes first. The launch follows this rule's commit and push; its time is the first line of the run log (`launched <UTC time>`), and log S96 records it. If the time limit passes first, the replies then returned are read under these rules, the calls still going are left to finish, and a reply that comes later is read under these same rules and recorded as late. The progress log (the runner's `start`, `ok` and `failed` lines, and each `done:` line) may be watched; it holds no reply text.
2. **A reply is a set of arguments to rule on, not a result.** No sentence of the text is kept, changed or dropped on a model's say-so, and no verdict line settles anything by itself. Silence is not agreement: a line that no reply attacks is not thereby shown to hold.
3. **What counts as a challenge.** A numbered finding that asks for a change of the text; a verdict line other than `Q1: NOTHING LEFT`, `Q2: KEPT OUT OF WHAT DEFINES EXPLANATION`, `Q2 targets: ALL THREE CAN BE TARGETS`, `Q3: SAID`, `Q4: ALLOWED, AND NOT REQUIRED`, `Q5: NOTHING BREAKS` or `Q6: FAITHFUL`, for the places it names; or a paragraph that shows a fault under any question, whatever its verdict line says.
4. **The reader's order of work.** The reader creates its file at once and fills it as it goes (lesson S28). First it tabulates every reply: model, part, each finding with its question, lines, what is at fault and proposed wording, each verdict line, and the OVERALL line. Then it groups the findings by the lines they concern, so that an Atria finding and a Mimo finding on the same lines stand together. Then, for each challenge, it states each reply's argument separately, and rules **KEEP** (the challenge is not taken up; the text stays as it is), **FIX** (the challenge is taken up: the exact new wording, with the reasons why this wording and not the text's, nor the reply's if it differs) or **DROP** (the challenge is taken up and the words leave the text), with its reasons why this ruling and not another.
5. **Disagreements are ruled on their arguments, not counted.** Where the two models' findings or verdict words on the same lines differ, the reader states each side's argument and rules between them or writes a third wording; the number of replies on a side decides nothing, and so does the size or confidence of a reply.
6. **The owner's words bound every ruling.** A FIX may not say more than the owner's words of S20 to S27 say, nor less than they require (the brief's Q6), and may not bring back a list, set, count, grade, ranking or record (S20) or say what must happen to candidates (S21); the reader's own text obeys S23. A challenge that turns on a choice the owner has not made (for example hard case 1, whether a candidate meets the requirements with no one assessing, which the S96 results leave open in section 10) is recorded with both sides' arguments as a question for the owner, and the reader rules only whether the present wording states the matter faithfully.
7. **A finding outside its part.** A finding on lines a part gives only for their definitions is ruled like any other, marked "raised outside its part"; it does not count those lines as examined by that model.
8. **File names** (lesson S25). The reader writes one file, `results/S96 Reading of the cross-examination of the repaired copy.md`, holding the table and the rulings; each ruling carries an id naming the model, the part and the finding (for example `atria A 3`, `mimo 2 5`; a ruling that joins findings names each), so no two rulings share a name. It never writes over a file that exists.
9. **A failed call counts for nothing either way, and failed calls go again up to pass 3.** A reply counts as returned only when it finishes "stop" with END OF REPORT on its last line (`s80_call.accept_reader`). A call that failed counts neither for nor against any line; its attempt files are kept and are not read for arguments. The job list allows three passes (`max_pass` 3), and this rule is the note written before sending for all three: when a pass ends, every call with no reply returned goes again as the next pass, with a byte-identical brief, until pass 3. A call that failed only on the connection (every attempt ended with no HTTP status, a cut stream or a proxy refusal) is not an answer from the model and is not counted against it (lessons S21 and S24); if such a call is still failed after pass 3, it may be sent again only under a new note written before sending. A call still failed after pass 3 on answers the model did give (length at the ceiling, or no END OF REPORT) is final: its lines are reported as **not examined by that model**. After any container restart, the runner is looked at and relaunched from a shell started after the restart, and a proxy failure is not a reply (lesson S24).
10. **Quotations are compared with the text.** Each quotation a reply relies on is compared with the repaired copy or the brief; where one is not found, that is recorded and the point is ruled on what the text says. A reply's quotation of a book longer than 25 words, a run of quotations joined only by page numbers, punctuation or a few words counting as one (lesson S29), is not copied into the reading.
11. **Edits come after all rulings.** No ruling is applied until every challenge is ruled. The FIXes and DROPs are then applied by program to a new copy beside the repaired copy, never to draft 5, the scrubbed copy, the repaired copy or the change list, and nothing is written into `authority/`. The reading ends with what it could not rule and the questions for the owner.

## Where the reading given to this agent goes beyond the owner's words

The orchestrator gave this agent a five-point reading of what S25 to S27 require, and asked every agent to report any place where it goes beyond the owner's words. The S96 results already list several (their section 9). Compared with the verbatim words, these points go beyond them; the briefs carry none of the five points as the owner's.

- **Point 1**, "Whether a candidate meets the requirements is settled by the thing explained itself, whether or not anyone knows it": no words of the owner say this. It is the assessor-free relation that the S96 results mark as the owner's open question (hard case 1), and next to S21's "But “matches the problem” is always tentative and may be wrong." it reads as a fixed answer with no one choosing. The list of targets ("a garden, a proof, a melody, a philosophical claim") comes from the drafters' examples.
- **Point 2**, "Physical possibility enters in two places only": the owner answered "This is correct." to a sentence about instantiation and transformation only. The second place, claims about what is possible as content, is the drafters' joining of S26's "Well not strictly nothing. But" with S27's perpetual-motion example; the owner did not say that this is where physical possibility enters. The list of ways (held, copied, taught, tested, built, performed) and "a test changes the target" are the drafters' examples, which the owner answered with footnotes, not with "This is correct".
- **Point 3**, "shown by argument": the owner wrote "If it can be shown"; "by argument" is the drafters' reading, drawn from S23's definition of an argument. "Responding needs more": the owner wrote only that the bare claim "is not enough for a creative agent to do anything about it"; what more a response needs is not said.
- **Point 4**, "the theory must not require it": the owner wrote that "nothing is stopping one from designing a system that must contain the entire contents of other explanations before they do the work of error correction" and "It's also a detail that exists outside the process." So the theory should neither require such a system nor rule it out; a reading that excluded it would say more than the owner.
- **Point 5**, "a failure found by argument, not by looking at the world, counts": the owner's first footnote speaks of explanations that "contradict each other" and of a claim that is "enough to trigger a conflict", and adds that it "is not enough for a creative agent to do anything about it". Nothing in it says that a conflict shown by argument makes either explanation fail, and S21 leaves what happens to candidates to the person choosing. The step from a conflict to a failure that "counts" is the drafters'; the S96 results mark as the drafters' reading only its extension to a candidate that assumes its own answer.

These go to the reader with the replies, as the drafters' reading and not the owner's words, and to the owner.

## How the run is launched

From the repository root, after this commit is pushed, with the keys loaded into the runner's environment from the session's key file and never printed:

```
setsid nohup bash -c 'echo "launched $(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)"; set -a; . <scratchpad>/cross_examiner_keys.env; set +a; for pass in 1 2 3; do <scratchpad>/venv/bin/python Semantics/tools/s87_run.py "Semantics/tools/s96_jobs - repaired copy.json" || break; done' > <scratchpad>/s96_run.log 2>&1 < /dev/null &
```

The loop's process id (the session leader's) goes in `<scratchpad>/s96_run.pid`. Each turn of the loop is one pass: a job whose reply has come back is skipped, a failed job goes as the next pass (its earlier files kept under their pass numbers), and the runner refuses a pass above `max_pass`. The loop runs pass 2 only after every call of pass 1 has ended. The run is over when the loop's process has exited and the log's last `done:` line is followed by no further `start` line, or when every tag has a `.response.txt` in the returns folder, or a `.error.txt` and a receipt with `"failed": true` from its third pass. A watcher outside the agent that launched it waits for that (lesson S14); the launching agent hands back as soon as the first calls are in flight.
