1. **Part VI — Finite monotone theorem**
   - (a) Part VI, heading “Finite monotone theorem.”
   - (b) “If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\).”
   - (c) The statement reverses the two notions. Let \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\) and \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\). Then \(\min\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\}\}\), so \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S=\varnothing\) and \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S=\{a,b\}\). But \(a\) and \(b\) are each critical for a minimal support, while neither is globally indispensable, since no element belongs to every support. The proof after the theorem in effect proves the correct, reversed version.

2. **Part VII — Identification**
   - (a) Part VII, heading “Identification,” final paragraph.
   - (b) “For the two balances \(\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}\), the kernel is spanned by \((1,-1,-1)\); the readings identify \(b_B-b_A\) and not \(x\).”
   - (c) This is mathematically false as stated. The row space of the matrix is spanned by \((1,1,0)\) and \((1,0,1)\), so a linear functional \(c^T\) is identified exactly when \(c_1=c_2+c_3\). The functional for \(b_B-b_A\), whether written as \((1,-1,0)\) or \((-1,1,0)\), does not satisfy that condition. Thus \(b_B-b_A\) is not identified from those readings.

3. **Part V — Account, Non-circular dependence**
   - (a) Part V, heading “Non-circular dependence.”
   - (b) “There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was.”
   - (c) As written, the third disjunct makes the condition vacuous: if the answer profile is left unchanged after removing or replacing the block, the condition is satisfied. Therefore any edit that removes a nonempty block trivially witnesses non-circular dependence, even when the removed block does no work. The phrase “or is left as it was” should not be a disjunct if the condition is meant to require genuine dependence.

4. **Part XVI — Derivation 2**
   - (a) Part XVI, heading “2. Indistinguishable is identical.”
   - (b) “Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide.”
   - (c) The claim is not entailed by (F1), (F2), and (A). Take \(D\) with two independent components; let \(E\) have two components anchored to those two components, while \(E'\) has a single component anchored to the whole of \(D\) and reproducing \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) on \(C\). Both can satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) for a query that reads only the output profile, but their components cannot be pairwise of one kind because one organization has a single component and the other has two.

5. **Part XI — Created explanatory knowledge**
   - (a) Part XI, heading “Created explanatory knowledge.”
   - (b) The formula includes “\(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\)” inside \(\operatorname{CreateEK}\).
   - (c) \(\operatorname{Account}\) is defined in Part V only for a candidate tuple \((E,p,t,\Gamma)\), not for a pair \((c,p_c)\). Meanwhile Part X says the content \(c\) in \(\operatorname{Origin}\) may be an organization, a transport, or a contract. An organization by itself is not an explanatory candidate. So the \(\operatorname{CreateEK}\) definition is not well-typed as written.

6. **Part IV and Part X — Constructed provenance**
   - (a) Part IV, heading “Three provenances,” and Part X, heading “Construction.”
   - (b) Part IV: “or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness.” Part X: “A selected transport has no represented target in its history; a constructed one does.”
   - (c) These two claims contradict each other. The Part IV disjunct allows a constructed transport to be the survivor of a variation-and-survival history, with no mention of a represented target in that history. But Part X asserts that every constructed transport has a represented target in its history. If the Part IV disjunct is taken literally, selection and construction are collapsed at least partly, contrary to the stated distinction.

7. **Part VII — Production and direction**
   - (a) Part VII, heading “Production and direction.”
   - (b) “The reversed calculation \(H=L\tan\theta\) is not: intervening on \(H\) changes the target's \(L\) but not the calculation's \(H\).”
   - (c) Under the semantics’ own Part II definition, an edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port. So an intervention on \(H\) in the reversed organization \(H=L\tan\theta\) directly changes \(H\). The sentence is therefore false as written. The intended contrast is presumably that the calculation’s \(L\) is unchanged, but that is not what the sentence says.

8. **Part V — Account, final claim about the conjuncts**
   - (a) Part V, heading “Account,” sentence after the displayed equivalence.
   - (b) “Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in \(C\). None inspects a label.”
   - (c) This is false because the non-vacuity conjunct includes the requirement that “every physically admitted edit excluded from \(C\) is excluded by a stated scope, not silently.” That is a condition on the modeller’s declaration or stated scope, not a condition on how supplied relations behave under edits in \(C\). So at least one conjunct does inspect a declarative/stated feature rather than relation behavior.
