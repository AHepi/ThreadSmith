1. **Part IV, "Three provenances," and Part X, "Construction"**
   - (b) "or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness." Also: "Construction is not selection. A selected transport has no represented target in its history; a constructed one does."
   - (c) The second disjunct of **Constructed** is exactly the definition of **Selected**: any selected transport is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population containing it. Therefore \(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Con}(t)\), even though the same section says a transport has "exactly one" provenance, and Part X says construction is not selection. A history with no represented target cannot also serve as a construction witness requiring a represented target.

2. **Part XVI, Derivation 2, "Indistinguishable is identical"**
   - (b) "Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide."
   - (c) This does not follow from Derivation 1 and (A). Let \(D\) have ports \(x,u,y\), components \(u=x+1\) and \(y=u^2\), with \(C\) containing edits to \(x\) and query \(y\). Let \(E\) have one component with relation \(y=(x+1)^2\) anchored to all of \(D\), while \(E'\) has two components \(u=x+1\) and \(y=u^2\). Both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A), but the single component of \(E\) is not of one kind with either component of \(E'\). Derivation 1 only shows each candidate matches its own anchors, not that different decompositions have pairwise-kind components.

3. **Part VI, "Finite monotone theorem"**
   - (b) "then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\)."
   - (c) The theorem reverses the two characterizations. Let \(\Gamma=\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\mathsf S=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:a\in W\text{ or }b\in W\}\). This is finite, upward closed, and contains \(\Gamma\), with \(\min\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\}\}\). The elements present in every support are none, so the globally indispensable ones are \(\emptyset=\bigcap\min\mathsf S\), not \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\). The proof's own last sentence, "Deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when a minimal support omits \(d\)," gives the intersection, not the union.

4. **Part IX, "What a test refutes," (K3)**
   - (b) "For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg T\), since \(B\) and \(I\) are background."
   - (c) Modus tollens on \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\) with \(\neg O\) yields only \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\), not \(\neg T\). To infer \(\neg T\), one must also establish \(B\) and \(I\). A false background or setup assumption gives the K3 premises while \(T\) remains true: take \(T\) true, \(B\) false, \(I\) true, and \(O\) false. Then \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\) is true, \(\neg O\) is true, but \(\neg T\) is false.

5. **Part V, "Baseline coherence"**
   - (b) "\(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\neq\varnothing\): the components of \(E\) are jointly satisfiable at the baseline. This is an independent requirement; it is not implied by the conditions above."
   - (c) It is implied by (F2) and Non-vacuity. The baseline \((1,b_0)\) is in \(C\), so (F2) gives \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\). Non-vacuity gives \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\). Since \(\pi\) is a function on the stated scope, the image of a nonempty set is nonempty. Thus \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\) must be nonempty.

6. **Part V, "Non-circular dependence"**
   - (b) "There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was."
   - (c) The final disjunct, "or is left as it was," permits the condition to be satisfied by an edit that removes or replaces a nonempty block of active commitments while changing nothing about the answer. That is the signature of an idle commitment, not a working one. Non-circular dependence should require the answer to change or cease to be determined when the commitments alleged to produce it are altered. As written, the condition fails to rule out circular or idle dependence.

7. **Part XV, "What defeats this class"**
   - (b) "A candidate under any of these headings counts as a defeat only once an episode satisfying (EK) has accepted it as one; until then the class stands."
   - (c) A mathematical counterexample or proof error refutes a formal claim whether or not any episode accepts it. Conditioning defeat on acceptance makes the class self-protective and confuses the truth of a criticism with its social or constructive acceptance. It also uses (EK), which is built downstream of the very conditions under attack, to decide whether an attack counts.

8. **Part V, "Account"**
   - (b) "The pair \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\) exactly when the following four conditions hold..."
   - (c) The subsequent definition gives six conjuncts in the final formula: (F1), (F2), (A), NonCircular, NonVacuous, and Coherent. Even if (F1) and (F2) are counted as one "component fidelity" condition, that yields five conditions, not four. The count is inconsistent with the definition itself.
