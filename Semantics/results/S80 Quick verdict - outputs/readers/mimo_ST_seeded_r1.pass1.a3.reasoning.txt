**1.** (a) Part VI — "Finite monotone theorem." (b) "If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\)." (c) The two characterizations are interchanged: a singleton that is critical for some support (definition (B)) is an element of some minimal support, i.e. of \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\), while an element whose deletion from \(\Gamma\) destroys support lies in every minimal support, i.e. in \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\). The proof itself establishes exactly these swapped forms ("A member of a minimal support is critical for it... Deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when a minimal support omits \(d\)"), so the statement contradicts its own proof. The document's own redundant-routes example, \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\), satisfies the hypotheses and refutes the statement: \(a\) is a critical singleton but \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S=\varnothing\), and \(a\) is not globally indispensable though \(a\in\bigcup\min\mathsf S\). Part XV lists a counterexample to this theorem as a defeat of the class.

**2.** (a) Part IX — "What a test refutes." (b) "For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg T\), since \(B\) and \(I\) are background." (c) Modus tollens on the implication yields \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\), i.e. \(\neg T\lor\neg B\lor\neg I\); singling out \(\neg T\) requires \(B\) and \(I\) to be independently established, which "being background" (assumed and held fixed) does not give. Counterexample: \(T\) and \(B\) true, the rig mis-set so \(I\) false and \(O\) fails; then \(\neg O\) is established while \(T\) is true. The sentence therefore asserts a false logical implication at the point where the semantics says what a failed test licenses.
