1. **(a)** Part VI, “Finite monotone theorem.”  
**(b)** “If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\).”  
**(c)** The second assertion is false. In an upward-closed support family, an element is globally indispensable—present in every support—iff it belongs to every minimal support, i.e. to \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\), not \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\). The document’s own “Redundant routes” example, \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\), gives \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S=\{a,b\}\), yet the text says “neither indispensable.” The proof’s deletion criterion also yields intersection, not union.

2. **(a)** Part XVI, Derivation 3, “Selected transports are underdetermined on unseen changes.”  
**(b)** “Let \(t\) be selected on a finite history \(H\subsetneq C\). For every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) there exists a transport \(t'\), also surviving on \(H\), with a different value at \((a,b)\).”  
**(c)** The proof ignores the population. “Selected” is defined in Part IV relative to a population \(\mathcal T\) and variation operator \(\mu\), but the proof constructs an arbitrary transport by altering an unseen component relation and never shows \(t'\) is in \(\mathcal T\) or reachable by \(\mu\). Counterexample: if \(\mathcal T=\{t\}\), then \(t\) is selected on \(H\), but no alternative surviving transport exists in the actual population. The proof shows only that a different mathematical object can be defined, not that the selection outcome is underdetermined.

3. **(a)** Part XVI, Derivation 4, “Surprise requires an incomplete history.”  
**(b)** “A system can be surprised if and only if it holds a transport selected on a history \(H\) strictly smaller than the contract \(C\) of changes the world admits.”  
**(c)** The right-to-left direction fails. \(H\subsetneq C\) only says there are unseen edit-boundary pairs; surprise is defined as a violation at such a pair, meaning actual fidelity failure. A selected transport may be faithful on all of \(C\setminus H\) even though it was never selected on those changes. Then unseen changes occur without violation and hence without surprise. The proof establishes only necessity, not sufficiency.

4. **(a)** Part XVI, Derivation 2, “Indistinguishable is identical.”  
**(b)** “Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide.”  
**(c)** The answer-profile clause follows from (A), but the pairwise-kind clause does not follow. Derivation 1 compares each candidate’s components with its own anchors, not the components of the two candidates with each other. Counterexample: a target with components \(u=x^2\) and \(y=u+x\), a question reading \(y\), and a contract editing only \(x\). A one-component candidate \(y=x^2+x\) and a two-component candidate \(u=x^2\), \(y=u+x\) can both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A), but there is no component bijection making their components pairwise of one kind.

5. **(a)** Part V, “Baseline coherence,” together with “Non-vacuity” and (F2).  
**(b)** “Baseline coherence. \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\neq\varnothing\): the components of \(E\) are jointly satisfiable at the baseline. This is an independent requirement; it is not implied by the conditions above.”  
**(c)** It is implied. Non-vacuity gives \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\). Since \(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) is a function, its image under a nonempty set is nonempty. By (F2), \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\), so the latter is nonempty. Thus baseline coherence is redundant, not independent as claimed.

6. **(a)** Part IV, “Three provenances,” under “Constructed,” and the later contrast of the two provenances.  
**(b)** “A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has exactly one of three provenances…” ; “Constructed. … or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-selection history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness.” ; “a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both.”  
**(c)** The second disjunct of “Constructed” describes exactly a selected-type history. If such a history can serve as a construction witness, then a transport can be both selected and constructed, contradicting “exactly one” provenance and the claimed historical distinction between the two. If only some such histories are meant to count, the extra condition is not stated. The three quoted sentences are therefore inconsistent as written.

7. **(a)** Part V, “Non-circular dependence.”  
**(b)** “There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was.”  
**(c)** The third disjunct, “or is left as it was,” makes the condition nearly vacuous: any admissible edit that removes or replaces a nonempty block while leaving the answer unchanged satisfies it. But an unchanged answer after removing a commitment is evidence that the commitment was not doing work, not evidence of non-circular dependence. This prevents the condition from doing the work claimed later, e.g. that “\(p\) because \(p\)” fails it.

8. **(a)** Part XVI, Derivation 1, “Kind preservation is a theorem, not a condition.”  
**(b)** “By (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\) …”  
**(c)** (K) defines a signature only for a component \(j\), as \(\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}\). It does not define a signature for a subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\), nor identify it with a projected solution set. The proof silently extends the symbol \(\operatorname{sig}_C\) to subnetworks while claiming “By (K).” Since the whole kind-preservation conclusion rests on this extension, the corollary is not proved from the stated definitions.

9. **(a)** Part XI, “Created explanatory knowledge,” formula (EK).  
**(b)** “\(\operatorname{Origin}(s,c,p_c,h,e_c)\land\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\land c\in\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\)”  
**(c)** This is a type mismatch. In Part V, \(\operatorname{Account}\) applies to a full candidate \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\), not to a bare content \(c\). In Part X, the \(c\) of \(\operatorname{Origin}\) may be an organization, a transport, or a contract. Thus \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\) is undefined unless \(c\) already includes a transport and active commitments; if \(c\) is a contract, it is nonsensical.

10. **(a)** Part III, “Contracts have provenance.”  
**(b)** “A selected contract is the surviving member of a population under a variation-and-survival history.”  
**(c)** No such notion is defined. Part IV defines selected provenance only for transports, with a population of candidate transports, a variation operator, finite history, and survival condition. A contract is a subset of admitted edit-boundary pairs together with a query, not a transport. Since question-finding and \(\rho_p=\text{selected}\) rely on this definition, the absence is a formal gap, not a harmless shorthand.

11. **(a)** Part XV, final paragraph of “What defeats this class.”  
**(b)** “A candidate under any of these headings counts as a defeat only once an episode satisfying (EK) has accepted it as one; until then the class stands.”  
**(c)** A mathematical counterexample refutes a theorem directly; it does not need to be accepted by an episode satisfying (EK). The sentence conflicts with the immediately preceding heading “A mathematical error” as a defeat. It makes truth and mathematical validity depend on acceptance, which is not consistent with the rest of the document’s realism claims.

12. **(a)** Part VII, “Identification.”  
**(b)** “It is identified on every attainable \(y\) exactly when \(f=\bar f\circ g\) for some \(\bar f\). (I2) For linear \(A\) and feature \(c^\top\), identification is \(\ker A\subseteq\ker c^\top\). (I4)”  
**(c)** There is no (I3) anywhere. The numbering jumps from (I2) to (I4), and any later reference to (I4) relies on a gap. Either an equation was removed or the labels were not renumbered.
