Here is the audit of "Claude Fable Semantics". The defects are listed most serious first.

**1. Part V, "Baseline coherence"**
(a) Part V, Account, Baseline coherence.
(b) "Baseline coherence. \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\neq\varnothing\): the components of \(E\) are jointly satisfiable at the baseline. This is an independent requirement; it is not implied by the conditions above."
(c) This is mathematically false. (F2) states \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) and \(\tau(1)=1\). Non-vacuity states \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\). Applying (F2) at the baseline yields \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\). Since the image of a nonempty set under a function is nonempty, baseline coherence follows directly from (F2) and non-vacuity. It is not independent.

**2. Part VI, "Finite monotone theorem"**
(a) Part VI, Work, support, and interference, Finite monotone theorem.
(b) "the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\)."
(c) The union is incorrect. The proof correctly shows that deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when *some* minimal support omits \(d\). Therefore the elements whose deletion destroys support (the genuinely indispensable ones) are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\), not \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\). The error is confirmed by the document's own "Redundant routes" example below it: for \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\), \(\min\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\}\}\), so \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S=\{a,b\}\), yet the text explicitly calls both \(a\) and \(b\) "neither indispensable".

**3. Part IV, "Three provenances"**
(a) Part IV, Layers, transports, and provenance, Three provenances.
(b) "**Constructed.** There is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target; or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness."
(c) The second disjunct exactly matches the definition of **Selected** given just above it. This makes a selected transport also constructed, contradicting the claim that a transport has "exactly one of three provenances," and contradicting the later assertion that "a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both."

**4. Part VII, "Obstruction"**
(a) Part VII, Exact constructions, Obstruction.
(b) "Twenty-four indivisible tokens cannot be split equally three ways; permitting division changes the state space and does not refute the scoped result."
(c) This is an arithmetic error. 24 is divisible by 3 (\(24 = 3 \times 8\)), so 24 indivisible tokens *can* be split equally into three piles of 8. The invariant argument requires a number not divisible by 3 (such as the 23 coins used in the skill document).

**5. Part IX, "What a test refutes"**
(a) Part IX, Criticism, use, and standing, What a test refutes.
(b) "For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg T\), since \(B\) and \(I\) are background. (K3)"
(c) This is a logical fallacy. From \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\) and \(\neg O\), one can only infer \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\), i.e., \(\neg T\lor\neg B\lor\neg I\). Concluding \(\neg T\) requires assuming \(B\) and \(I\) are true, not merely that they are "background." This treats background assumptions as immune to refutation, contradicting the document's own fallibilist commitments.

**6. Part V, "Non-circular dependence"**
(a) Part V, Account, Non-circular dependence.
(b) "There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was."
(c) The final disjunct is logically flawed. If the answer profile "is left as it was" after removing a block, the block did no work, which is the opposite of witnessing non-circular dependence. Including this disjunct makes the condition satisfiable by idle parts, defeating its purpose.

**7. Part VII, "Identification"**
(a) Part VII, Exact constructions, Identification.
(b) "The feature is identified at \(y\) exactly when \(Z_y\neq\varnothing\land|f[Z_y]|=1\). (I1) It is identified on every attainable \(y\) exactly when \(f=\bar f\circ g\) for some \(\bar f\). (I2) For linear \(A\) and feature \(c^\top\), identification is \(\ker A\subseteq\ker c^\top\). (I4) Repeating rows changes no kernel; an independent calibration can."
(c) The labels skip (I3). This is a numbering error.
