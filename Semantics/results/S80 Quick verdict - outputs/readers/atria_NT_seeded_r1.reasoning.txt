Let's quickly verify the counterexample for the Finite Monotone Theorem once more.
$\Gamma = \{a,b,c\}$.
$\mathsf S = \{\{a,b\}, \{a,c\}, \{a,b,c\}\}$.
Is $\mathsf S$ upward closed? A set is upward closed if $W \in \mathsf S$ and $W \subseteq W' \subseteq \Gamma$ implies $W' \in \mathsf S$.
Subsets of $\Gamma$: $\emptyset, \{a\}, \{b\}, \{c\}, \{a,b\}, \{a,c\}, \{b,c\}, \{a,b,c\}$.
$\mathsf S$ contains $\{a,b\}, \{a,c\}, \{a,b,c\}$.
Supersets of $\{a,b\}$ in $\Gamma$: $\{a,b\}, \{a,b,c\}$. Both in $\mathsf S$.
Supersets of $\{a,c\}$ in $\Gamma$: $\{a,c\}, \{a,b,c\}$. Both in $\mathsf S$.
Supersets of $\{a,b,c\}$: itself. In $\mathsf S$.
So $\mathsf S$ is upward closed.
$\Gamma \in \mathsf S$. Yes.
Minimal members of $\mathsf S$: $\{a,b\}$ and $\{a,c\}$. So $\min \mathsf S = \{\{a,b\}, \{a,c\}\}$.
$\bigcap \min \mathsf S = \{a,b\} \cap \{a,c\} = \{a\}$.
$\bigcup \min \mathsf S = \{a,b\} \cup \{a,c\} = \{a,b,c\}$.
Critical singletons: $d$ is critical if $\exists W \in \mathsf S$ such that $W \setminus \{d\} \notin \mathsf S$.
For $d=a$: $W=\{a,b\}$, $W\setminus\{a\}=\{b\}\notin\mathsf S$. So $a$ is critical.
For $d=b$: $W=\{a,b\}$, $W\setminus\{b\}=\{a\}\notin\mathsf S$. So $b$ is critical.
For $d=c$: $W=\{a,c\}$, $W\setminus\{c\}=\{a\}\notin\mathsf S$. So $c$ is critical.
So critical singletons = $\{a,b,c\}$.
Theorem says critical singletons are exactly $\bigcap\min\mathsf S = \{a\}$. FALSE.
