**Frozen question for this audit:** does each claim and proof in "Claude Fable Semantics" hold up — are its parts held in place by what it says, or do some contradict others, overreach, or fail to prove what they claim? The document's own tests (F1, F2, (A), non-circularity, non-vacuity) are applied to the document itself. Defects are ordered by how much of the theory falls if they stand.

---

**1. (a) Part IX, "What a test refutes" (K3).**
(b) "For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg T\), since \(B\) and \(I\) are background."
(c) The inference is invalid. From \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\) and \(\neg O\) you get \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\), i.e. \(\neg T\lor\neg B\lor\neg I\); calling \(B\) and \(I\) "background" does not establish them, so \(\neg T\) does not follow. The document's own glossary states the correct result — "from theory and background and test set-up together predicting wrongly, only 'not all three' follows" — so (K3) contradicts the skill's own entry for K3. A test whose rig was faulty refutes the rig, not the theory.

**2. (a) Part IV, "Three provenances"; contradicted by Part X, "Construction".**
(b) "A transport \(t\) ... has **exactly one** of three provenances" ... "**Constructed.** ... or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness."
(c) Read naturally, the second disjunct is satisfied by *every* selected transport: the selection history is precisely "a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it". So Selected ⊆ Constructed, contradicting "exactly one" and the same section's "a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both," as well as Part X's "Construction is not selection." If the intent was "a population containing a *representation* of \(t\)," the text does not say it, and the condition as written collapses the distinction the theory locates creativity in.

**3. (a) Part VI, "Finite monotone theorem."**
(b) "... the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\)."
(c) The second half is false and contradicts the proof's own last line. Let \(\Gamma=\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\mathsf S=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\{a\}\subseteq W\lor\{b,c\}\subseteq W\}\); \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), \(\min\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b,c\}\}\), so \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S=\{a,b,c\}\) while \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S=\varnothing\). No element is in every support (\(a\notin\{b,c\}\), \(b,c\notin\{a\}\)), so the globally indispensable ones are \(\varnothing=\bigcap\min\mathsf S\), not \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\). The proof's final sentence ("Deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when a minimal support omits \(d\)") establishes the \(\bigcap\) characterization, not the one stated.

**4. (a) Part VII, "Obstruction."**
(b) "Twenty-four indivisible tokens cannot be split equally three ways; permitting division changes the state space and does not refute the scoped result."
(c) Arithmetically false: \(24=3\times8\), so an equal split exists with indivisible tokens. The obstruction example requires a number not divisible by 3 (the companion skill file uses twenty-three coins, which works). As written the illustration does not illustrate an obstruction at all.

**5. (a) Part XVI, Derivation 10 (final two paragraphs).**
(b) "On any contract containing it, the two persistence components are of one kind (Derivation 2). A claim that 'component 1 is *really* thing 1' is a claim that some admitted change distinguishes them, and on this contract none does."
(c) The contract must contain the per-thing displacement edits (\(H_0\) contains displacements and fidelity is assessed over the extended contract), and those edits distinguish the components: displacing thing 1 moves the persistence component anchored to thing 1's continuity subnetwork and leaves the other fixed. Under (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_C(1)\neq\operatorname{sig}_C(2)\) under any footprint bijection, so they are *not* of one kind. Derivation 2 is also mis-cited: it concerns two candidate accounts, not two components within one; the relabelled candidate \(E'\) with \(\lambda\) swapped fails (F1), since \(\lambda(2)\) does not move when thing 1 is displaced.

**6. (a) Part V, "Non-circular dependence."**
(b) "There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, **or is left as it was**."
(c) If the final disjunct describes the answer profile, the existential becomes trivially satisfiable — any block-removing edit qualifies regardless of its effect — and "“\(p\) because \(p\)” fails non-circular dependence" (stated in the same Part) no longer follows. If it describes the block, it contradicts "removes or replaces." Either way the condition as written does not state what it is meant to state: that removing a working part must change the answer.

**7. (a) Part XVI, Derivation 6, read with Part XIV, "Primitives" and "Dependence order."**
(b) Derivation 6: "Every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from \(\Theta\) (including \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\)) and, where invoked, \(\mathcal N\), together with declared indices." Part XIV: "The semantics has two primitives." Yet the dependence order begins: "(O) and (Q) depend on nothing."
(c) The claim of exactly two primitives is contradicted by the document's own dependence order: (Q) — the answer-profile operation \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b)\) — depends on nothing and is not \(\Theta\) or \(\mathcal N\). Moreover the query \(\mathcal Q\), the candidate organization \(E\), the active commitments \(\Gamma\), and the obligations \(O_p\) are supplied data, not derived from the physical module; the "declared indices" list (\(\ell,\beta,\Omega,C\)) does not cover them.

**8. (a) Part XV, "What defeats this class" (final sentence).**
(b) "A candidate under any of these headings counts as a defeat only once an episode satisfying (EK) has accepted it as one; until then the class stands."
(c) This makes the class self-insulating: no counterexample counts until the theory's own positive predicate certifies the refutation. It sits in direct tension with the same Part's heading "A mathematical error. A counterexample to the finite monotone theorem, (I2), (O1), (T2), (CT2), or Derivations 1–3" — a genuine counterexample to (I2) is a mathematical fact regardless of who accepts it — and with Part 0's invitation to attack the conjecture.

**9. (a) Part XVI, Derivation 3, "Consequence"; contradicts Part 0, Grievance 3.**
(b) "A correspondence produced by selection is faithful where it was tested and **unconstrained** where it was not." Grievance 3 says fidelity off the history "is a fact about the transport and the world, entirely independent of whether it survived."
(c) The proof shows only underdetermination *by the selection history*: altering \(L_j(a,b)\) for \((a,b)\notin H\) yields another structure surviving on \(H\). It does not show the transport is unconstrained — physics may fix its behaviour off \(H\), which is Grievance 3's own point. The proof also silently assumes relations at distinct contract points are independent and that \(\tau,\sigma\) separate \(H\) from \(C\setminus H\); \(\tau\) is not required to be injective.

**10. (a) Part XVI, Derivation 2.**
(b) "Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide." Proof: "Immediate from Derivation 1 and (A)."
(c) Derivation 1 equates each component's signature with *its own anchor's* signature; it establishes no bijection between the components of \(E\) and \(E'\). The two candidates may differ in number of components or anchor to different subnetworks of \(D\), so "pairwise of one kind" is not derived. "One account at grain \(C\)" is then a stipulation presented as a conclusion.

**11. (a) Part XVI, Derivation 4.**
(b) "A system can be surprised if and only if it holds a transport selected on a history \(H\) strictly smaller than the contract \(C\) of changes the world admits."
(c) The phrase "the contract \(C\) of changes the world admits" conflates the system's declared contract with the physically admitted changes (Part V defines a contract as a declared *subset* of the latter). A change the world admits but the system's contract excludes is neither in \(H\) nor in \(C\setminus H\), so the biconditional has no case for it. Also, "only if a selected transport" is a definitional artifact: a constructed transport that fails fidelity at an unexpected change is excluded from being surprised by definition, not by argument.

**12. (a) Part V, "Account" (introductory sentence vs. the list and (E)).**
(b) "... satisfies \(\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\) exactly when the following **four** conditions hold" — followed by Component fidelity, Question fidelity, Non-circular dependence, Non-vacuity, and Baseline coherence, while (E) reads "(F1)\(\land\)(F2)\(\land\)(A)\(\land\)NonCircular\(\land\)NonVacuous\(\land\)Coherent."
(c) The count is wrong under either reading: five conditions are listed and six conjuncts appear in (E). Minor in itself, but it is the statement of the theory's central predicate.

**13. (a) Part XVI, Derivation 5.**
(b) "Give it ports (the edits and their boundaries), components (the closure conditions), and admitted edits (add or remove a change; alter \(\mathcal Q\)). It is then a \(D\) in the sense of (O)."
(c) This is a sketch, not a proof. An organization in the sense of (O) requires nonempty value domains \(X_v\) for every port, component relations \(L_j(a,b)\) for each edit–boundary pair, a boundary set, and a partial associative composition with identity on the admitted edits; none is specified, and "the closure conditions" is not a defined relation. The claim that question-finding is representable rests on an unexecuted construction (and raises a regress: the contract of the contract-organization).

**14. (a) Part VI, "Hard-to-vary" (typing of \(\operatorname{Pres}\)).**
(b) "\(\operatorname{Pres}(F)=\{v\in\mathcal V:\forall f\in F,\operatorname{Account}(E_v,f)\}\)."
(c) \(\operatorname{Account}\) is defined for a question \(p\), but \(f\) ranges over "explanatory jobs" \(F\). Unless jobs are identified with questions (which the document does not state), the expression is ill-typed; the surrounding claim that "more reach constrains variation" then has no well-formed statement behind it.
