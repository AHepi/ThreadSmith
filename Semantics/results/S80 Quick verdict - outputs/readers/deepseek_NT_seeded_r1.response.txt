1. **Where:** Part VI, “Finite monotone theorem.”  
   **Exact sentence:** “If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\).”  
   **What is wrong:** The theorem swaps union and intersection. Take \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\) and \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\), which is upward closed with \(\min\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\}\}\). The singletons \(\{a\}\) and \(\{b\}\) are each critical for the supports \(\{a\}\) and \(\{b\}\), so the critical singletons are \(\{a,b\}=\bigcup\min\mathsf S\), while \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S=\varnothing\). Conversely, neither element is globally indispensable, since deleting either leaves a support, so the globally indispensable set is \(\varnothing=\bigcap\min\mathsf S\), not \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\). The proof itself supports the opposite matching: members of minimal supports are critical, and deletion of \(d\) preserves support exactly when some minimal support omits \(d\).

2. **Where:** Part IV, “Three provenances,” under “Constructed.”  
   **Exact sentence:** “or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness.”  
   **What is wrong:** This second disjunct is just selection with the selection history relabeled as a construction witness. It does not require a represented target or a critical episode, so any selected transport can be counted as constructed by fiat. That contradicts the later statement in Part X that “Construction is not selection. A selected transport has no represented target in its history; a constructed one does.” It collapses the central two-provenance distinction the document claims to maintain.

3. **Where:** Part V, “Question fidelity,” and Part III, “Contracts.”  
   **Exact formula:** \[
   \operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b). \tag{A}
   \]  
   **What is wrong:** \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) is defined in Part III as \(\mathcal Q(D,a,b)\), but \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) is never defined. The query \(\mathcal Q\) is specified as an operation on the target organization \(D\), and no rule is given for applying it to the explaining organization \(E\), whose ports and components may be quite different. Thus the core Account condition (A) is not well-defined.

4. **Where:** Part V, “Baseline coherence.”  
   **Exact sentence:** “\(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\neq\varnothing\): the components of \(E\) are jointly satisfiable at the baseline. This is an independent requirement; it is not implied by the conditions above.”  
   **What is wrong:** It is implied by Non-vacuity together with (F2). Non-vacuity gives \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), and the baseline \((1,b_0)\) is in \(C\). Since (F2) gives \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(1),\sigma(b_0))=\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\), and \(\pi\) is a function, the image of a nonempty set is nonempty. Therefore \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\neq\varnothing\) already follows.

5. **Where:** Part XVI, Derivation 3, proof.  
   **Exact sentence:** “The component relations \(L_j(a,b)\) are supplied independently for each \((a,b)\). Survival on \(H\) constrains only \(\{L_j(a,b):(a,b)\in H\}\). Alter \(L_{j}(a,b)\) for one \((a,b)\notin H\) to any other admitted relation; the result survives on \(H\) and differs at \((a,b)\).”  
   **What is wrong:** The definitions do not guarantee that component relations for unseen edits are independently alterable while remaining physically admitted or produced by the variation operator. A lawlike component may fix its response across all edits, so no alternative admitted relation at the unseen pair exists. The proof also changes a component relation in the interpreted organization but does not show the alternative transport is in the selecting population \(\mathcal T\). Thus the claimed universal underdetermination is not proved.

6. **Where:** Part V, “Non-circular dependence.”  
   **Exact sentence:** “There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was.”  
   **What is wrong:** The disjunct “or is left as it was” makes the condition too weak: an account can satisfy it by removing a redundant block whose removal changes nothing. That provides no evidence that the active commitments are doing explanatory work. It also conflicts with the following claim that a contract excluding every change under which the commitments matter fails Non-circular dependence.

7. **Where:** Part XVI, Derivation 2.  
   **Exact sentence:** “Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide.”  
   **What is wrong:** Fidelity and equal answer profiles make the candidates indistinguishable with respect to \(C\), but they do not make them one account or force their components to be pairwise of one kind. For example, a target with two components and a hidden intermediate can be accounted for by one composite component or by two components matching the target. Both can satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on a contract that hides the intermediate, yet the component counts and signatures differ. The correct conclusion is indistinguishability at grain \(C\), not identity.

8. **Where:** Part XVI, Derivation 1, proof.  
   **Exact sentence:** “By (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\) and \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)=\{(\tau(a),\sigma(b),L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)))\}\).”  
   **What is wrong:** Part II (K) defines a signature only for a component \(j\) as the set of triples \((a,b,L_j(a,b))\). Here \(\lambda(k)\) is an anchor subnetwork of \(D\), not necessarily a single component, so \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))\) is not defined by (K). The proof silently redefines “signature” for subnetworks using the projected solution relation, so the claimed kind-preservation theorem is not actually derived from the stated definitions.

9. **Where:** Part XV, “What defeats this class.”  
   **Exact sentence:** “A candidate under any of these headings counts as a defeat only once an episode satisfying (EK) has accepted it as one; until then the class stands.”  
   **What is wrong:** This makes even mathematical refutations acceptance-dependent. A false theorem, such as the finite monotone theorem above, defeats the class regardless of whether any episode satisfying (EK) accepts it. The sentence shields the theory from criticism in a way that conflicts with its own fallibilism and with Part 0’s statement that a candidate satisfying the conditions but plainly explaining nothing “counts against” the conjecture.
