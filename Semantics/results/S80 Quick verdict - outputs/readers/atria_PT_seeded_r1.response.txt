# Review of "Claude Fable Semantics"

**Brief.** Correctness audit of a theory document: false statements, proofs that fail, claims wider than their support, internal contradictions. I read all sixteen Parts and checked the definitions, theorems, proofs, and examples in Part VI, Part VII, Part VIII, Part XII, and Part XVI.

**Summary of the document.** It defines explanation as a question-relative, change-fidelity-checked transport between organizations, with three provenances (selected / constructed / declared) for transports and contracts, and derives consequences for kinds, surprise, question-finding, criticism, creativity, and physical realization.

**Overall assessment.** The architecture is internally coherent in most places, and several derivations (1, 2, 5, 8, 9) check out. But there are at least six defects, including a stated theorem whose conclusion is the reverse of its own proof, a provenance clause that collapses the selection/construction distinction the whole theory rests on, and a central non-circularity condition that does not exclude the cases it claims to.

---

**1. Part VI, "Finite monotone theorem."**

(a) Part VI, heading "Work, support, and interference," the Finite monotone theorem.

(b) "If Γ is finite, 𝖲 is upward closed, and Γ∈𝖲, then the critical singletons are exactly ⋂min𝖲 and the globally indispensable ones are exactly ⋃min𝖲."

(c) Both sets are swapped. Take the document's own "Redundant routes" example immediately below: Γ={a,b}, 𝖲={{a},{b},{a,b}}. Then min𝖲={{a},{b}}, so ⋂min𝖲=∅ and ⋃min𝖲={a,b}. The theorem as stated would make the critical singletons ∅ and the indispensable ones {a,b}, but the example says "each is contributory, neither indispensable." The attached proof in fact establishes the opposite of the claim: "A member of a minimal support is critical for it" gives critical = ⋃min𝖲, and "Deletion of d from Γ preserves support exactly when a minimal support omits d" gives indispensable = ⋂min𝖲. Correction: swap the two right-hand sides.

**2. Part IV, "Three provenances."**

(a) Part IV, heading "Three provenances," second paragraph of the "Constructed" clause.

(b) "…or t is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness."

(c) Every selected transport satisfies this clause, so Selected ⟹ Constructed, contradicting the opening "A transport t … has exactly one of three provenances." It also contradicts the later sentence in the same Part, "a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both," since the clause licenses Con(t) for a history with no represented target. Since the document locates creativity *only* in construction (Part 0, grievance 6; Part X), this clause collapses the distinction the theory is built on. Correction: delete the clause, or restrict it to variation-and-survival histories that themselves contain a represented target.

**3. Part V, "Non-circular dependence."**

(a) Part V, heading "Non-circular dependence," final sentence of the item.

(b) "There exists (a,b)∈C that removes or replaces a nonempty block of Γ while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was."

(c) As formalized, the condition is satisfied by the very cases it is meant to exclude. For "p because p," the answer sits in Γ as an unanalysed input or component; the edit that removes that block makes the answer "cease to be determined in the claimed way," satisfying the existential — yet "What (E) excludes" says "'p because p' fails non-circular dependence." Likewise, a contract of pure relabelings that removes a block and "is left as it was" satisfies the third disjunct, yet the text says such a contract "does not satisfy non-circular dependence." The third disjunct makes the condition near-vacuous; the informal prose ("the target's answer does not appear … as an unanalysed boundary input") is doing all the work. Correction: require that removing the block *changes* the answer profile or breaks its determination in a specified way, and drop "or is left as it was."

**4. Part XVI, "Derivation 3."**

(a) Part XVI, heading "3. Selected transports are underdetermined on unseen changes."

(b) "Alter L_{j}(a,b) for one (a,b)∉H to any other admitted relation; the result survives on H and differs at (a,b)."

(c) The proof never shows the altered object is a member of the population 𝒯 (survival is defined relative to 𝒯), nor that it is still a transport at all: changing one component relation can break (F2)'s global equality π[Sol_D]=Sol_E or the homomorphism conditions on τ. A counterexample to the claim under its stated assumptions is a singleton population 𝒯={t}: then no t′∈𝒯 differs at any point, while H⊊C is finite. The intended underdetermination thesis needs an added assumption that 𝒯 is rich enough (e.g., closed under component-relation variation that preserves (F2)), or the claim must be weakened to "unless the population constrains the unseen point."

**5. Part V, "Baseline coherence."**

(a) Part V, heading "Baseline coherence."

(b) "Sol_E(1,σ(b₀))≠∅: the components of E are jointly satisfiable at the baseline. This is an independent requirement; it is not implied by the conditions above."

(c) It is implied. Part III states the contract contains the baseline (1,b₀). Applying (F2) there gives π[Sol_D(1,b₀)]=Sol_E(1,σ(b₀)); non-vacuity gives Sol_D(1,b₀)≠∅; and π is a function X_D→X_E, whose image of a nonempty set is nonempty. Hence Sol_E(1,σ(b₀))≠∅ follows from (F2) ∧ non-vacuity. The conjunct is harmless but the independence claim is false.

**6. Part VII, "Obstruction."**

(a) Part VII, heading "Obstruction," second sentence.

(b) "Twenty-four indivisible tokens cannot be split equally three ways; permitting division changes the state space and does not refute the scoped result."

(c) Arithmetically false: 24 = 3 × 8, so an equal three-way split exists with no token divided. If the intended point is that some totals are indivisible by the number of recipients, a total not divisible by three (e.g., 25) is needed; as written the example states something a reader can check to be wrong, which weakens the otherwise correct scoped-invariant point it illustrates.

I did not check Part XI's (EK) or Part XIII's (U1)–(U3) against any external literature, since these are internal definitions; no external sources are cited. Derivations 1, 2, 5, 8, 9 and the Part VII identification results (I2), (I4) and the skew-symmetric example were checked and found correct.
