1. **(a)** Part VI, "Finite monotone theorem"  
**(b)** “If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\).”  
**(c)** The second equality is false. The proof itself argues that deleting \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when some minimal support omits \(d\), so the globally indispensable elements are those in every minimal support, namely \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\), not \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\). Counterexample: \(\Gamma=\{a,b,c\}\), minimal supports \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{a,c\}\). Then \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S=\{a\}\), but \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S=\{a,b,c\}\). Deleting \(b\) or \(c\) preserves support, so \(b\) and \(c\) are not globally indispensable.

2. **(a)** Part XVI, Derivation 2, "Indistinguishable is identical"  
**(b)** “Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide.”  
**(c)** Derivation 1 and condition (A) give only that each component matches its own anchor and that the answer profiles coincide. They do not give a footprint-preserving bijection between the components of \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\). Counterexample: let \(D\) have serial components \(u=f(x)\) and \(y=g(u)\). Let \(\mathcal E\) have one aggregate component with relation \(y=g(f(x))\), while \(\mathcal E'\) has two serial components \(u=f(x)\) and \(y=g(u)\). Both can satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A), but the component sets have different cardinalities, so no pairwise kind bijection exists.

3. **(a)** Part IX, "What a test refutes"  
**(b)** “For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg T\), since \(B\) and \(I\) are background. (K3)”  
**(c)** This is not a valid inference. From \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\) and \(\neg O\), the valid conclusion is \(\neg T\lor\neg B\lor\neg I\). To obtain \(\neg T\), one must know that \(B\) and \(I\) are true, not merely that they are “background.” Counterexample: \(T=\top\), \(B=\bot\), \(I=\top\), \(O=\bot\). The implication is true because its antecedent is false, and \(\neg O\) is true, but \(\neg T\) is false.

4. **(a)** Part V, "Non-vacuity"  
**(b)** “The contract \(C\) is a declared subset of the physically admitted edits…”  
**(c)** This contradicts Part III, “Any assessment may use any of the three,” where contracts can be declared, selected, or constructed. It also contradicts Derivation 5, which makes constructed contracts central to question-finding. If only declared contracts can occur in Account, then selected and constructed contracts are not usable for explanations, despite being introduced as first-class provenances.

5. **(a)** Part IV, "Three provenances"  
**(b)** “A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has exactly one of three provenances…” Later: “or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness.” And later still: “a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both.”  
**(c)** The second clause makes a selected-type history capable of serving as a construction witness, so a transport could be both selected and constructed. That violates the “exactly one” claim. It also contradicts the stated criterion that constructed transports have represented targets and criticism, since the survivor clause imposes neither.

6. **(a)** Part V, "Baseline coherence"  
**(b)** “This is an independent requirement; it is not implied by the conditions above.”  
**(c)** It is implied by (F2) together with Non-vacuity. The contract \(C\) contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\), Non-vacuity gives \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), and (F2) gives \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\). Since \(\tau(1)=1\), the image of a nonempty set under \(\pi\) is nonempty. Thus baseline coherence follows, unless \(\pi\) is undefined on the baseline solutions, in which case (F2) is not well-formed as written.

7. **(a)** Part V, "Non-circular dependence"  
**(b)** “There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was.”  
**(c)** The three alternatives are exhaustive: if the answer profile is determined after the edit, it either changes or remains the same; otherwise it ceases to be determined. So the clause contributes no constraint beyond the existence of some edit that removes or replaces a block. As written, it cannot distinguish a block whose removal matters from one whose removal is irrelevant.

8. **(a)** Part XVI, Derivation 3, "Selected transports are underdetermined on unseen changes"  
**(b)** “For every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) there exists a transport \(t'\), also surviving on \(H\), with a different value at \((a,b)\).”  
**(c)** The proof assumes there is always some other admitted relation at the unseen pair and that it can be altered independently of \(H\). The definitions do not guarantee this. Counterexample: take \(D=E\) with one port whose domain is \(\{0\}\) and one component relation \(L(a,b)=\{0\}\) for every \((a,b)\). Let \(H\subsetneq C\). A selected transport exists, but no transport \(t'\) can have a different value at an unseen pair, since only one value is admitted anywhere.

9. **(a)** Part XVI, Derivation 1 proof  
**(b)** “By (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\)”  
**(c)** (K) defines the signature of a component \(j\), not of a subnetwork. But \(\lambda(k)\) is an anchor subnetwork, which may contain several components and hidden ports. No definition of \(\operatorname{sig}_C\) for subnetworks is supplied. The projected-solution expression may be intended, but the proof as written uses an undefined extension of (K).

**(c)** The label jumps from (I2) to (I4); no formula labelled (I3) appears in the document. This is a numbering/cross-reference defect rather than a substantive mathematical error, but it violates the document’s own numbered-item consistency.
