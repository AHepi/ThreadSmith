**Defect 15** — Part II "Kinds are edit-signatures" (K).
Quote: "Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one kind on \(C\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)\) and \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j')\) coincide."
What's wrong: the signatures are sets of triples whose third coordinates lie in different ambient sets, \(\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\) vs \(\prod_{v'\in V_{j'}}X_{v'}\); a bijection of footprints transports relations only given bijections of value domains \(X_v\to X_{\phi(v)}\), which are not part of the data. As written, two components whose ports carry differently named or differently sized domains can never "coincide", so e.g. Part X's "the persistence components have the signature of things on that contract" and the general claim that kind captures role across substrates fail; and transitivity (needed for "A kind is an equivalence class") is unestablished.

**Defect 16** — Part II "Kinds are edit-signatures" causal clause vs Part 0 Grievances 1 and 11.
Quote: "a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits" vs "A correlation has no component that responds to an intervention on its supposed input; a cause does."
What's wrong: the two passages name different discriminators. As the document's own edit mechanics have it ("An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port"), the Part II clause is satisfied by a purely correlational table as much as by a causal equation: both relations are replaced when the output port is set, both are invariant under observation edits. The cause/correlation difference the Part 0 grievances rely on shows up in responses to interventions on the *input* port (in the target, setting x moves y in the causal case and not in the correlational one), which the stated signature family does not mention.

**Defect 19** — Part IX "What a test refutes", (K3).
Quote: "For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg T\), since \(B\) and \(I\) are background."
What's wrong: from the conditional and \(\neg O\) only \(\neg T\vee\neg B\vee\neg I\) follows; \(\neg T\) requires the additional premise \(B\wedge I\). "Background" names their status, not their truth, and Part I explicitly allows background to be wrong ("Systems can be wrong about their transports, their observations, their criticisms"). Counterexample: T true, B false, O false — the conditional holds and \(\neg O\) is established, but \(\neg T\) is false.

**Defect 20** — Part VIII "Approximate transport", (T2).
Quote: "With one-step discrepancy \(\varepsilon\) and an \(L\)-Lipschitz next-step map, \(e_n\le\varepsilon\sum_{k<n}L^k\)."
