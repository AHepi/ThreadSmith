Let's double check the **Finite monotone theorem** counterexample.
Theorem statement: "If $\Gamma$ is finite, $\mathsf S$ is upward closed, and $\Gamma\in\mathsf S$, then the critical singletons are exactly $\bigcap\min\mathsf S$ and the globally indispensable ones are exactly $\bigcup\min\mathsf S$."
My counterexample: $\Gamma = \{a,b,c\}$, $\mathsf S = \{\{a,b\}, \{a,c\}, \{a,b,c\}\}$.
$\min \mathsf S = \{\{a,b\}, \{a,c\}\}$.
$\bigcap \min \mathsf S = \{a\}$.
$\bigcup \min \mathsf S = \{a,b,c\}$.
Critical singletons: $d$ is a critical singleton if $\exists W \in \mathsf S$ such that $W \setminus \{d\} \notin \mathsf S$.
For $a$: $W=\{a,b\}$, $\{a,b\}\setminus\{a\} = \{b\} \notin \mathsf S$. So $a$ is critical.
For $b$: $W=\{a,b\}$, $\{a,b\}\setminus\{b\} = \{a\} \notin \mathsf S$. So $b$ is critical.
For $c$: $W=\{a,c\}$, $\{a,c\}\setminus\{c\} = \{a\} \notin \mathsf S$. So $c$ is critical.
So critical singletons $= \{a,b,c\} = \bigcup \min \mathsf S$.
The theorem says critical singletons are exactly $\bigcap \min \mathsf S = \{a\}$. This is false.
What are "globally indispensable ones"? Not defined. Presumably elements in every support, i.e., $\bigcap \mathsf S$. Since $\mathsf S$ is upward closed and contains $\Gamma$, $\bigcap \mathsf S = \bigcap \min \mathsf S = \{a\}$.
So the theorem has the two sets swapped. The proof given in the text actually derives $\bigcup \min \mathsf S$ for critical singletons, confirming the statement is a typo. This is a definite, provable defect.
