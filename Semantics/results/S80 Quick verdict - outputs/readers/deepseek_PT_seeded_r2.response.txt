1.  
(a) Part V, Non-circular dependence.  
(b) “There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was.”  
(c) This condition is not well-defined, because no operation is supplied by which a target edit–boundary pair \((a,b)\in C\) removes or replaces a block of commitments \(\Gamma\) in the explanatory organization \(E\). Separately, the final disjunction — “changes, ceases to be determined…, or is left as it was” — covers every possible outcome. A candidate whose active commitments are irrelevant to the answer therefore satisfies the third disjunct, even though that is exactly the situation the condition is supposed to rule out. As written, Non-circular dependence imposes almost no constraint.

2.  
(a) Part IV, Three provenances, “Constructed.”  
(b) “**Constructed.** … or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness.”  
(c) This makes a selected survivor also constructed, since “Selected” is defined as surviving a variation-and-survival history on a population of candidate transports. That contradicts the immediately preceding statement that a transport has exactly one of the three provenances, and contradicts Part 0 and Part X, which insist that construction is not selection and that the two provenances are distinguished by their histories. If any selected survivor can serve as its own construction witness, the selected/constructed distinction collapses.

3.  
(a) Part V, Baseline coherence.  
(b) “This is an independent requirement; it is not implied by the conditions above.”  
(c) It is implied. Since \((1,b_0)\in C\), (F2) gives
\[
\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0)).
\]
Non-vacuity gives \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\). The image of a nonempty set under a function is nonempty, so \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\neq\varnothing\). Thus baseline coherence follows from (F2) and Non-vacuity, and the statement that it is independent is false.

4.  
(a) Part VI, Finite monotone theorem.  
(b) “If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\).”  
(c) The two set operations are reversed. Every member of a minimal support is critical for that minimal support, so the critical singletons are the elements of \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\), not \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\). And deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when some minimal support omits \(d\), so the globally indispensable elements are those contained in every minimal support, i.e. \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\), not \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\). The document’s own next example, “Redundant routes” \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\), says each is contributory and neither is indispensable; there \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S=\{a,b\}\) and \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S=\varnothing\).

5.  
(a) Part XVI, Derivation 2.  
(b) “Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide.”  
(c) This is false. Let the target \(D\) have ports \(x,u,y\) and two components \(u=x^2\) and \(y=u+1\). Let \(C\) contain the baseline and edits that set \(x\), and let \(\mathcal Q\) read \(y\). Candidate \(\mathcal E_1\) has one component with relation \(y=x^2+1\), anchored to the whole \(D\)-subnetwork with \(u\) hidden. Candidate \(\mathcal E_2\) mirrors the two \(D\)-components separately. Both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A). But the components are not pairwise of one kind: one has signature \(y=x^2+1\), while the others have \(u=x^2\) and \(y=u+1\). Thus Derivation 2 is false.

6.  
(a) Part XVI, Derivation 3.  
(b) “Let \(t\) be selected on a finite history \(H\subsetneq C\). For every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) there exists a transport \(t'\), also surviving on \(H\), with a different value at \((a,b)\).”  
(c) The proof assumes that for every unseen \((a,b)\) there is another admissible component relation to substitute. The theorem does not state that assumption. Part II allows nonempty value domains of size one; if \(D\) and \(E\) each have a single port with domain \(\{0\}\), all component relations and valuations are unique, and no second transport can have a different value on an unseen edit. The conclusion therefore fails under the stated assumptions.

7.  
(a) Part XV, “What defeats this class,” final paragraph.  
(b) “A candidate under any of these headings counts as a defeat only once an episode satisfying (EK) has accepted it as one; until then the class stands.”  
(c) This makes whether something counts as a defeat depend on an acceptance event governed by the very account-construction relation under attack. A counterexample to the sufficiency or necessity of Account could be rejected simply by not having an episode satisfying (EK) accept it. This is self-sealing and conflicts with Part 0, where the document says a suitable candidate “counts against the conjecture.”

8.  
(a) Part XVI, Derivation 5.  
(b) “A contract \(C\) is an organization (a set of edits with a query), and can be the content \(c\) in (G).”  
(c) This conflates “contract” with “question.” In Part III a contract is defined as \(C\subseteq A\times B\), the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs, while \(\mathcal Q\) is a separate component of the question \(p\). Derivation 5’s proof also says “a contract is a subset of \(A\times B\) together with \(\mathcal Q\),” which is not the definition given earlier. If the intended content is a question or a contract-plus-query pair, the derivation should say so rather than redefining contract.

9.  
(a) Part VII, Identification.  
(b) “It is identified on every attainable \(y\) exactly when \(f=\bar f\circ g\) for some \(\bar f\). (I2) For linear \(A\) and feature \(c^\top\), identification is \(\ker A\subseteq\ker c^\top\). (I4)”  
(c) The labels jump from (I2) to (I4); there is no (I3). This is a numbering inconsistency.
