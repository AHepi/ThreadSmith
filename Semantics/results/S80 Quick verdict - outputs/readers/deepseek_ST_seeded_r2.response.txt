1. **Part XVI, Derivation 3 — “Selected transports are underdetermined on unseen changes”**

> “Let \(t\) be selected on a finite history \(H\subsetneq C\). For every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) there exists a transport \(t'\), also surviving on \(H\), with a different value at \((a,b)\).”

The proof only alters an unseen component relation and observes that the result still agrees on \(H\). But “selected” in Part IV is defined relative to a particular population \(\mathcal T\) and variation operator \(\mu\): a selected transport is a member of \(\mathcal T\) that survived. The proof does not show the altered transport \(t'\) is in \(\mathcal T\), reachable by \(\mu\), or even physically admitted. For example, if \(\mathcal T=\{t\}\), then no such alternative selected survivor exists, even though \(H\subsetneq C\). The theorem therefore proves only that some formal extension can be imagined, not that the selected transport is underdetermined in the semantics’ own sense.

2. **Part IV, “Three provenances” — the Constructed disjunct collapses the selected/constructed distinction**

> “Constructed. There is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target; or \(t\) is the survivor of a variation-and-survival history on a population that contains it, which serves as its construction witness. Write \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\).”

The second disjunct says a transport can be Constructed merely by being the survivor of a variation-and-survival history in a population containing it. But that is exactly the definitional shape of a Selected provenance. This contradicts the immediately preceding claim that a transport “has exactly one of three provenances” and the later statement that “a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both.” Unless the second disjunct is removed or tightly restricted, every ordinary selected survivor also counts as constructed.

3. **Part V, “Account” — “Baseline coherence” is not independent**

> “**Baseline coherence.** \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\neq\varnothing\): the components of \(E\) are jointly satisfiable at the baseline. This is an independent requirement; it is not implied by the conditions above.”

It is implied by the conditions above. Non-vacuity gives \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), and global fidelity (F2) gives \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\). Since \(\pi\) is a function, the image of a nonempty set is nonempty. Therefore \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\neq\varnothing\) follows from Non-vacuity plus (F2), and the claim that baseline coherence is independent is false.

4. **Part VI, “Work, support, and interference” — the finite monotone theorem reverses its own terms**

> “If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\).”

The proof itself shows that deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when some minimal support omits \(d\). Thus the globally indispensable elements—those contained in every support—are the intersection of the minimal supports, not the union. Conversely, elements that are critical for some minimal support are exactly those in the union. Example: with minimal supports \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{a,c\}\), the indispensable element is \(a\), while \(b\) and \(c\) are each critical for their own minimal support. The theorem’s labels are swapped.

5. **Part XI, “Created explanatory knowledge” — \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\) is ill-typed when \(c\) is a contract**

> “The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a contract. When it is a contract, the originative act is the finding of a question.”  
> “\(\operatorname{Origin}(s,c,p_c,h,e_c)\land\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\)”

\(\operatorname{Account}\) is defined in Part V only for a candidate organization \(E\): \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\). But Part X explicitly allows the originative content \(c\) to be a contract. The EK formula then passes such a \(c\) to \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\), for which no definition is supplied. Either the formula must restrict \(c\) to organizations, or a separate account-like predicate for contracts must be defined. As written, the EK definition is not well-typed for the case it explicitly claims to cover.

6. **Part XVI, Derivation 2 — “Indistinguishable is identical” is false as stated**

> “Two candidates \(\mathcal E,\mathcal E'\) for the same \(p\) that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on \(C\) are one account at grain \(C\): their components are pairwise of one kind on \(C\) and their answer profiles coincide.”

Answer-profile coincidence follows from (A), but the conditions do not force the two candidates to have matching internal components. One candidate may realize \(L=H\cot\theta\) with a single component, while another realizes the same global behavior through two components with an internal hidden port. Both can satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A), but there need be no bijection of components under which their signatures coincide. Derivation 1 only shows each component matches its own anchor; it says nothing about a pairwise matching between the components of \(\mathcal E\) and those of \(\mathcal E'\).

7. **Part V, “Account” — the Non-circular dependence condition is vacuous as written**

> “There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes, ceases to be determined in the claimed way, or is left as it was.”

The third disjunct, “or is left as it was,” lets the condition be satisfied by an edit that removes or replaces a nonempty block while the answer does not change. That is the mark of an idle part, not evidence of non-circular dependence. Non-circular dependence should require that some such edit changes or disrupts the answer. As written, an explanation with an idle active commitment can satisfy the clause by leaving the answer the same.

8. **Part XIV, “The class collected” — the claimed dependence order is circular or incomplete for Build**

> “Build depends on histories and (E).”  
> “\(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an actual subhistory owned by \(s\) and delimited at \(e\) prepares a represented organization for explanatory use of \(c\)…”

Build is defined in Part X in terms of a “represented organization,” which is the \(\operatorname{Rep}\) relation from Part IV. But \(\operatorname{Rep}\) depends on \(\operatorname{Con}\), and \(\operatorname{Con}\) is defined via Build/Part X episodes. Thus Build and Rep are mutually dependent, or at minimum Build depends on Rep. The dependency order in Part XIV omits this, and Derivation 6’s proof-by-dependence-order is thereby unsupported.

9. **Part IX, “Criticism, use, and standing” — (K3) states an invalid inference**

> “For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg T\), since \(B\) and \(I\) are background. (K3)”

From \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\) and \(\neg O\), the valid conclusion is \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\), i.e. \(\neg T\lor\neg B\lor\neg I\). One may infer \(\neg T\) only under the additional assumption that \(B\) and \(I\) are themselves established. Calling them “background” does not make them true, and the same document insists systems can be wrong about their background assumptions. As a logical assertion, K3 is false.

10. **Part V, “Account” — the stated number of conditions does not match the definition**

> “The pair \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\) exactly when the following four conditions hold, each a condition on supplied relations under the changes in \(C\).”

The list that follows contains five named headings: Component fidelity, Question fidelity, Non-circular dependence, Non-vacuity, and Baseline coherence. The final equivalence then has six conjuncts:
\(\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Coherent}\). The “four conditions” statement is internally inconsistent with the definition, and Part 0 similarly refers to “all four conditions of Account.”
