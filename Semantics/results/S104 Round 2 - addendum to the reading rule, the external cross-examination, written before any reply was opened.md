## How it is read

1. **One more reader's reply.** Its findings are read as the reply of a third reader, called **the external reader** in every record of the round, under the same rule as Mimo's and GLM's replies, with the differences stated here. It is not a call of this round: it was not sent the seventeen parts, it examined the text and not the maths, and rule 1 (nothing opened before the runs are over) and rule 2 (what counts as a reply) concern the calls of the round only. The document counts whole.

2. **Its findings as items.** Its findings are extracted as items E01 to E22 in `results/S104 Round 2 - external cross-examination - items.md` and `.json`: for each, the place in the text (line numbers, with the sentences quoted and each quotation compared by program with the line it names), the finding in one or two sentences, its evidence, its proposed repair in its own words, whether it challenges the text, and the S104 formal claims, counterexamples and inventions that touch the same place. The extraction is Claude's, not the external reader's; where the two differ, the document's words govern, and the tabulator and every checker read the document's passage whole. The items file and the reproductions are written before any round-2 reply is opened; a later change to either goes into a separate, later record, and the files of that commit are not written over.

3. **Tabulation (rule 4).** The tabulating agent tabulates the external reader's findings beside Mimo's and GLM's, from the document itself, and may use the items file as an index to it.

4. **What goes to a checker (rule 5).** Each finding that challenges the text goes to one fresh Opus 5.5 checker, with any reader's points on the same place: the checker of the group that holds the lines it would change, or, where no point of Mimo or GLM falls on those lines, a checker of its own for a group of its own, under rule 5's conditions (a fresh agent that built nothing of this round and rules on no other group, so that no line of the text has two checkers). Where it is in doubt whether a finding challenges the text, it goes to a checker, as rule 5 says. Besides what rule 5 lists, that checker is given the document's passage whole, the item's entry in the items file, the claims and inventions addenda named in point 9, and the program that runs them. An item recorded as a finding about scope, or as challenging no line, is still given as context to the checker of any group whose lines it names.

5. **Weighed by their arguments, not by their source (rules 3 and 8).** A finding counts for what its argument does and for nothing else. No sentence of the text, and no definition, claim or invention of the maths, is kept, changed or dropped because the external reader says so, because it agrees or disagrees with Mimo or GLM, or because of its length, tone or confidence; the number of readers on a side decides nothing. What it says stands (its section 1, and "a real success for the framework" in 3.1) counts for nothing either way: an item it does not attack, or praises, is not thereby shown to hold (rule 3). The attacks it sets aside itself (section 5) are items like any other; where one of them names an obligation the text leaves open, that is a challenge.

6. **Its verdicts and repairs are proposals.** Its "Verdict" and "Repair" paragraphs, its revision priorities (section 7) and its benchmark (section 6) are proposals, not rulings. Where a repair has no exact wording and the checker upholds the finding, the checker gives it exact wording: a FIX under rule 5, whole, with the reasons why this wording and not the text's, within the owner's words (rule 10); or a KEEP with the reasons why the finding asks for no change of wording. A repair that would write an invention into the text follows rule 6.
