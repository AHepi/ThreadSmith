## O40 — I, row 96: the source of the correction

**What I expect.** Holding the failed test and successful retest fixed while changing who supplies the second-sensor proposal will change the division of reinterpretation without establishing a ranking of the whole achievement.

**First situation.** Written by: me. The expert supplies the two candidate diagnoses and their test. The test fits neither. The robot identifies the assumption that the first sensor reads true, proposes using the second sensor, performs the swap and discovers the third fault.

**Second situation.** Written by: me. The expert supplies the same two diagnoses and test. The test fits neither. The robot identifies the same assumption, the expert proposes using the second sensor, and the robot performs the same swap and discovers the same third fault.

The sole changed feature is who supplies the second-sensor proposal. O40 occupies the second side: the robot supplies the exposed assumption and the expert supplies the correction. The pair therefore earns joint reinterpretation and rejects a story in which either supplied that entire reinterpretation alone. It does not turn either participant’s necessary contribution into a measure of comparative importance. Under the first side the robot supplies both steps of the reinterpretation; that still would not, without a further argument, turn every other contribution into negligible work.

The best local YES reading of I64 is that neither mostly claim has the required comparative reason in this record, so both claims should be withheld. The literal full fixed verdict is stronger if it says no participant can justifiably claim predominance: I64 tells the reader to examine reasons tied to the work but does not say which would suffice. Shared work alone cannot settle that question. The pair leaves that missing contrast visible, so the full verdict remains **BORROWED: JUDGEMENT**. The outside verdict is neither rewritten nor contradicted.

**POKE.** Keep hold of the same second-sensor proposal through both situations: its source moves from robot to expert, the resulting third-fault finding stays, and attribution of that proposal moves. A change only to whether that same expert proposal is spoken or written would leave its source, its content and the stipulated diagnostic result unchanged. The work changes with the source; the medium does not supply a comparison of importance.

## O49 — J, row 110: what supplies the foothold

**What I expect.** Holding the circular wording fixed while varying whether the contrasted histories are supplied will locate the meaning in the histories, and removing only the circular wording will then test its contribution.

**First situation.** Written by: me. A page says that the routine is the robot’s own when it can run it, and that it can run it when it is its own. Alongside are two independently described histories: the robot retained and used a learned routine, whereas in the other history it only relayed an externally completed answer.

**Second situation.** Written by: me. The page contains the same two circular sentences. The two independently described histories are absent, and no other interpretation is supplied.

The sole feature changed in the pair is the presence of the contrasted histories. The first presentation has a meaningful historical distinction despite the circle; the second has no such foothold. O49 occupies the first side, and its definition remains circular on that side. The local positive verdict belongs to the histories’ interpretation, not to a definition rescued by its neighbours. This tightens the original pair by keeping the defective definition present on both sides, rather than changing the definition and removing the histories together.

Now remove only the circular wording from the first presentation. The learned-versus-relayed histories and their distinction remain. That additional removal establishes the definition’s idleness for this contrast; it does not establish that every definition or every account of ownership is idle. **PLACED BY TIGHTER PAIR.** The meaningful contrast remains, the circle adds no basis, and no reduction to the advertised primitives has thereby been proved.

**POKE.** Keep hold of the circular two-sentence definition: removing the independent histories leaves that part unchanged while the presentation loses its supplied contrast. Removing the definition instead leaves the histories and their distinction unchanged. The contrast follows the histories, not the definitional circle. The earlier theory’s reduction claim therefore receives no extra support merely from the meaningful examples.

### I70-T — The address found last
Written by: me

**What I expect.** Naming a real necessary final contribution will locate that contribution without automatically making the larger diagnostic achievement mostly its author’s.

**Aimed at.** I64’s requirement for a reason tied to the work and the risk of treating any such reason as sufficient.

**The situation.** An expert diagnoses a pump’s stuck pressure sensor, explains how to interpret a second sensor and names the fault the retest will reveal. The robot finds which cabinet holds that second sensor, follows the supplied interpretation and confirms the fault. The cabinet search was necessary because the expert did not know its location. The robot says the diagnosis was mostly its work because without that search the retest could not run.

**The thoughtful person’s verdict.** The robot found the cabinet and made the test possible. The expert supplied the diagnostic reinterpretation. The necessity of the cabinet search does not by itself establish that the diagnosis was mostly the robot’s achievement.

| Audit condition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Component fidelity | The supplied interpretation and the robot’s cabinet search have separate stated histories; a complete explanatory match-up is empty. |
| Question fidelity | The comparative claim concerns the diagnostic achievement, so necessity of locating equipment is not silently substituted for supplying the diagnosis. |
| Non-circular dependence | The message already contains the reinterpretation before the robot searches; the robot’s necessity argument does not establish a comparison of contributions. |
| Non-vacuity | The supplied and self-found contributions give a substantive contrast, but a complete Account contract and a general capability assessment are empty. |

**The theory’s verdict under the earlier version with R2, S62 and I64.** Earlier X and R2 I record the expert’s supplied interpretation and the robot’s locating and execution work separately. I64 does not infer mostly from necessary last steps; it requires an assessment of the reason in relation to the work claimed. The robot has supplied a reason, but its stated necessity alone does not provide a comparative ordering. No clause supplies a numerical or automatic priority rule.

**Do they differ?** No on the identified contributions or the failure of the necessity inference; a general rule deciding comparative importance remains absent.

**Best reply on the theory’s behalf.** I64 states a necessary requirement, not that any sentence mentioning the work is sufficient. The expert’s already supplied interpretation is a concrete reason to reject treating the search as the source of that interpretation, even while crediting the search’s actual contribution.

**Does the difference survive the reply?** No opposite attribution is forced. The attack leaves the weakness exposed: evaluating comparative reasons still requires judgement beyond checking that a reason was stated.

**How I know each claim.** This situation and its verdict are written by me after the outside cases, as an attack on the retained wording. Its stipulated histories are not an independent empirical result. The reading uses earlier X Construction, R2 I and I64, with no revised-only condition.

**POKE.** Keep hold of the cabinet-location contribution. If the expert supplies the cabinet location before the robot acts, the robot’s search contribution disappears while the diagnostic interpretation stays the expert’s. If the same robot-found location is communicated by a screen instead of speech, that contribution and the diagnosis stay as they were. Neither change turns execution into authorship of the supplied interpretation.

**What it gives up.** Retention of I64 gives up a claimed derivation of the whole comparative verdict from the mere existence, novelty, necessity or final position of one contribution. It leaves O40’s joint history usable and its no-mostly comparison borrowed. It neither invents equal shares nor says all comparative attributions are meaningless. The sentence remains a demand to expose a reason, not a machine for ranking reasons.

**Whether the earlier version already said this.** Earlier Part X, “Understanding, construction, and origin,” Construction, states the following passage, already registered as S64-E01:

> **Construction.** \(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an actual subhistory owned by \(s\) and delimited at \(e\) prepares a represented organization for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers. A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting representation. Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not.

The source already calls for the actual history and incoming carriers, which can distinguish the assumption-finding from the supplied remedy. It does not state a rule for comparing their importance. Earlier Part XII’s Owned capability condition, S64-E04, fixes ownership and continuity obligations without supplying such a comparison. I64 is a clarification of attribution and an explicit marker of this remaining judgement, not a result derived from a ranking already in the earlier text.
