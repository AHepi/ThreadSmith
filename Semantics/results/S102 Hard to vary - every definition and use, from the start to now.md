## 2. The senses, one by one

Ten senses, in the order in which each first appears in the record.

### 2.1 A count that orders explanations for preference (FW3, as FW5 reports it; row 1)

The earliest record is a report of something already given up. FW3, which the repository does not hold, said that "an explanation reaching more is thereby harder to vary", and gave a count of features "special permission" to "order explanations for preference" (F00 L1300, L1302, quoted in row 1). FW5 withdraws both on 8 September: the comparison holds only when "the organization, interpretation, and variation family stay fixed while preservation requirements are added", and a count "alone does not supply those reasons". This is the first place in the record where hard to vary meets the word "preference", and it meets it in order to be taken away from it.

### 2.2 The counting lemma over a fixed family of variations (FW5 to draft 3; rows 2 to 7, 9, 10)

FW5 fixes "an organization, interpretation, variation family \(\mathcal V\), and a family \(F\) of explanatory jobs", defines Pres, and states (H): adding jobs can only shrink Pres. It then says what hard to vary is, in the one sentence of definition any theory text gives it: "“Hard to vary” is therefore an articulated pattern of constrained changes, relative to an explanatory job." It is "not the requirement that every detail of a complete theory be individually indispensable", nor "a numerical warrant" (F00 L381). Reach is defined beside it (F00 L383).

Files 10, 11 and 12 keep the lemma as one paragraph headed "**Hard-to-vary.**" (F10 L330, F11 L315, F12 L317, over "causal jobs" in file 12), with the family now "declared" (F10 L316) and the closing sentence "More reach constrains variation; the containment need not be strict; counting jobs is not a warrant." FW5's definition of reach is not carried, so "reach" is used once and defined nowhere (S89 observation 5). Nothing depends on the lemma: file 10's dependence order (L525) places (S), (B) and (D) and not Pres, and no condition of (E) mentions it. The S89 audit put it plainly: "hard-to-vary is a lemma and not a condition, while Deutsch makes it central." Drafts 1 to 3 keep the lemma. Draft 4 removes it (row 24). Its family \(\mathcal V\) survives in the latest text only as the domain of (D), "For a declared family \(\mathcal V\) of organization edits," (LT L299), which no other sentence uses.

### 2.3 Variation that runs with no one aware: selection (file 10 to now; rows 8, 9, 10, 25)

From file 10 on, one kind of variation is part of how a correspondence comes to hold: "A correspondence can be *selected* — produced by blind variation and survival on a history of predictions —" (F10 L13); "Selection is a physical history: a population, a variation operator, a survival condition, and a sequence of events." (F10 L56); in the physical module, "a physically admitted variation operator, and a survival condition enacted by the environment." (F10 L482). File 11 adds that the population fixes what a history leaves open (F11 L197), and file 12 calls a member of that population which agrees on the tests "an admitted rival" (F12 L586), whether or not anyone offers it. This is the only variation in any version that drives which variations survive with nothing and no one aiming at it. No version calls it hard to vary. Draft 4 keeps rivals apart from it: "Nor are rivals a selection population" (D4 L315, still in LT L315), and the latest text keeps construction apart from it: "Construction is not selection. A selected transport has no represented target in its history; a constructed one does." (LT L411).

### 2.4 The skill: a critic's method, and an audit aid (before 21 September to 23 September; rows 11 to 14)

The skill defines hard to vary for the person using it: "A good explanation is hard to vary: you cannot change its parts and still have it explain what it is meant to explain." (SKILL.md L8), and, with its three kinds of change kept apart, "an explanation is hard to vary when few changes of kind 1 survive" (L30). It names itself a critic's instrument: "This skill is a way of criticising, not a truth-meter." (L12). It refuses totals ("Do not count confirmations, jobs or parts.", L72) and hands the test to the person questioned: "Give me a different version of this detail that would work just as well." (L129). Its word list maps "hard to vary; reach" onto file 10's lemma: "More jobs can only shrink the surviving versions." (word list L36), and its module on the idea repeats it: "That is why an explanation that reaches further is harder to vary." (`references/the-idea-in-depth.md` L68). The owner set how it may change (decision 22): "If it doesn't fit in the spirit of how to measure \"how to measure hard to vary\" it stays out."

In this project the skill was a method for auditing the theory: the other model's workflow "works with the hard to vary workflow" (decision 20) and its report asks "(5) What would make the theory harder to vary." (file 24, L52); the rebuilt skill was "the test" (decision 23); five reviewers were armed with it (decision S10); then the owner asked "whether the hard to vary skill adds anything or should be abandoned" (decision S12), and, on fifteen unmarked reports, "The skill isn't worth it." (decision S14). From then on "The skill is not handed to readers in this project's audits from here" (S80 Results, L22). In the Language project the skill was used to build a theory of a language's properties (Language decision 2) and to review methods (Language log L76); the HV Skill project goes on testing it on outside papers.

### 2.5 "How hard an account is to vary": a separate property that orders nothing (drafts 1 to 3; rows 16 to 19)

On 23 September the worklist and plan asked for a sentence saying that hard-to-vary is "a separate, non-grading measure" (worklist L737). Drafts 1 to 3 wrote it into Part VI: "How hard an account is to vary is a separate matter, shown by \(\operatorname{Pres}\), and it grades nothing." (D1–D3 L313), with reach defined for the first time since FW5, as "the set of jobs \(f\) with \(\operatorname{Account}(E,f)\); it is fixed by \(E\) and the world, not by which jobs anyone has checked." Part I gained the matching clause, "how hard an account is to vary is a separate matter (Part VI)." (L69), and the note of sources said the same (row 19). Here hard to vary is a degree a single account has, read off the lemma, fixed by the account and the world, and set apart both from what an explanation is and from any order of explanations.

### 2.6 The source's sense (read into the project on 23 and 24 September; rows 15, 19, 29; section 3)

Deutsch's glossary makes hard to vary the mark of a good explanation, by variation alone; his chapter 1 argues that it matters because it makes error correction work; his chapter 4 finds the same trait in adaptations produced by evolution; and Marletto has correction produce a product that is "hard to change further". The project read the theory against this in the S89 audit and in the source reading of 24 September, and recorded its departures in the note of sources and departures, a block cut from every theory text. Section 3 gives the source's words.

### 2.7 An order or a witness over a declared family: the proposals of 24 September (rows 20, 21)

Asked, in the words of log S91, "whether the theory should also grade explanations, as Deutsch does", the owner answered "Well that depends entirely on how error correction is handled" (decision S20). The first analysis offered three options, among them "(c) Full grading from Pres": "order them by inclusion of Pres(F), the variants that stay accounts on every job in F: smaller means harder to vary". It recommended not this but (b), a record of "absorbed failures", with a pointer from the lemma: "What the measure bears on is correction:". The second analysis proposed a lemma (H\(^*\)), "Witness in a common family", in which adding a failed job strictly shrinks Pres when a variation does the old jobs and fails the new one; it says of itself that it "orders no candidates, counts nothing", and it concluded "A record of rescues is not redundant." All three proposals depend on a family of variations listed in advance, and none was applied. The owner's correction (row 22) and lesson S26 record why: "Claude framed hard to vary as a count of versions over a declared or listed set, and built on the framing."

### 2.8 The owner's sense (decision S20, 24 and 25 September; row 22)

In the owner's words: "A variation is a competitor. Whether anyone can list all variations that still fit is beside the point. If two discovered variations fit, that constitutes a problem." And, on correction: "A record is redundant. Once the explanation is rescued, the mistake shouldn't be able to creep back in. Good explanations make bad ones harder to fit by definition." Here hard to vary is not a set, a count, a record or an order: it is what a good explanation does to its bad variations, by its own content, and a problem is what two discovered variations make when both still stand. The change list recorded three refinements agreed in conversation (CL L1320–1323), among them that "the criticism is the producing of the rival". Lesson S26 turns the correction into a rule for Claude (row 23).

### 2.9 "Easy to vary" through offered rivals and problems (draft 4 to now; rows 25 to 29, 31, 33 to 36)

Draft 4 writes the owner's sense into two paragraphs at the end of Part VI and one in Part VIII. Rivals are candidates "offered as an answer to \(p\) in place of the other" that conflict somewhere the target admits a change; "a candidate that nobody has offered is no one's rival" (D4 L315). Two rivals that both still stand for an assessor pose a problem, of the first kind if they conflict inside the question (a test there decides it) and of the second kind if only outside it. "A candidate is **easy to vary**, in the sense used here, when it and a rival pose a problem of the second kind; the rival is then easy to vary too, and the term says nothing about which of them is right." (D4 L317). "A criticism that a candidate is easy to vary must supply such a rival (Part IX)." And: "Nothing here counts rivals, grades a candidate or ranks candidates." Part I now reads "whether an account is easy to vary is a separate matter (Part VI)." (row 27). The owner's "creep back in" becomes "A failed answer stays failed": a candidate that repeats a failed answer is excluded "from the candidate's own answer there, with no record of which candidates failed before or of how any was changed." (D4 L369).

The later copies keep the definition and change what surrounds it. Draft 5 places it in the dependence order: "and easy to vary on a problem for \(p\)." (D5 L526). The scrubbed copy removes "fit" and "established": a problem is two rivals "neither of them ruled out for an assessor", "a conflict between ideas that no argument usable by that assessor has decided", and the definition's last clause becomes "the term says nothing about which of them, and not the other, is an account on a finer contract." (SC L317). The repaired copy and the latest text find conflict "by argument, with no test", say that ruling out by a claim taken as given "is a choice the assessor made", and turn Argument 2's "The remedy is a finer contract" into "whether anyone asks it is that person's choice (Part 0)." (LT L568). The sentence defining easy to vary has not changed since the scrubbed copy. On the S101 graph it is a leaf: it depends on a problem for \(p\), which depends on the assessor's declared forms, scope and premises and on (K2), and no node depends on it. "The offer of one in place of the other", on which rivals depend, is not defined (S101, L514). One question on it is still the owner's: whether easy to vary should point at one side (file 93, L106).

### 2.10 "Hard to vary" left undefined (draft 4 to now; rows 24, 30)

From draft 4 on, no theory text contains the words "hard to vary". The note of 25 September records the removal by grep: "no "Pres", no "job", no "Hard-to-vary", no "grades nothing"" (L188). ## 3. The source's words

Each quotation is at most 25 words and is taken from the source reading of 24 September (appendix B of the error-correction analysis) or from the S89 audit, where each was checked against its page.

- The definition, in Deutsch's glossary, is by variation alone: "An explanation that is hard/easy to vary while still accounting for what it purports to account for." (D p.31)
- The good explanation is one whose details all do work. Of the axis tilt: "That is a good explanation – hard to vary, because all its details play a functional role." (D p.24)
- Why it matters, in chapter 1, is error correction. Of easily varied theories he writes: "when theories are easily variable … experimental testing is almost useless for correcting their errors. I call such theories bad explanations." (D p.22)
- It is also a criterion for choosing. Of policies: "we should choose between them not on the basis of their origin, but according to how good they are as explanations: how hard to vary." (D p.209)
- And a refusal to choose among equals. Where many variants account equally well: "advocating a particular one in preference to the others is irrational." (D p.21)
- It constrains the person who holds the explanation, whatever that person wants. Of the tilt theory: "because it is a good explanation – hard to vary – it is not yours to modify." (D p.28)
- It is a trait of what evolution produces, with no one aware: "good adaptations, like good explanations, are distinguished by being hard to vary while still fulfilling their functions." (D p.78)
- In Marletto, correction produces the trait. The finished work "is hard to change further, while still meeting the criteria, because it has been obtained by tentatively removing flaws in previous versions" (M p.15)

The source reading sums up (appendix B, 5a): "The *definition* of good and bad is structural (p.31). The *argument* that the structural property matters is mainly about error correction (pp.21–25, 32)." It adds that in Marletto the arrow runs the other way, from correction to the trait. The note of sources records a departure that bears on section 4: "Deutsch calls criticism and experiment a selection (chapter 4, p.78). Here selection is blind: its history holds no represented target (Parts 0 and IV)." (CL, the *Selection* line of W38.1).

## 4. Preference and use

*This whole section is Claude's reading of the texts, not a finding of any test.* For each sense it asks one thing: does the text treat hard to vary as something an assessor prefers or chooses by (a criterion, a comparison, an order someone makes, a record someone keeps), or as something that operates in the process whether or not anyone is aware of it (what drives or constrains which variations occur or survive)? A third answer turns out to be needed: some senses are neither.

The owner's words of 27 September, the reason for the question: "My silly mistake was confusing "preferred" with "use". The agent need not be aware, at all, that they use a method. Preference never enters the integration of "hard to vary"."

**What the readings add up to**, again as Claude's reading. From the earliest record the texts took hard to vary away from preference: FW5 withdraws FW3's count "to order explanations for preference" and refuses a predicate of "merit preference" (F00 L849); files 10 to 12 say "counting jobs is not a warrant"; drafts 1 to 3 say it "grades nothing"; draft 4 says nothing here "counts rivals, grades a candidate or ranks candidates"; the note of sources says easy to vary "prefers neither". What no version did was give hard to vary a place in the process instead. In every version, what hard to vary names is either a relation someone can point out (the lemma, the property, easy to vary) or a method someone applies (the skill). The only variation that runs whether or not anyone is aware of it is blind selection, which is never called hard to vary, and which draft 4 keeps apart from rivals and the latest text keeps apart from construction. The note of sources records the split as a departure from Deutsch, who calls criticism and experiment a selection. The one place where the owner's words describe hard to vary as working by itself, decision S20, went into the theory twice: as easy to vary, which works only as a criticism someone offers, and as "A failed answer stays failed", which works from the candidate's own answer, for an assessor.
