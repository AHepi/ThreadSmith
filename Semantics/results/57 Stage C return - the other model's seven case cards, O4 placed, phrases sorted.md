## O4 — The theory’s own distinction

The requested passage is in the **revised standalone theory, Part III, “The respect is the query”**:

> What a question asks — production, identification, obstruction, rule-status, purpose-achievement — is fixed by the type of \(\mathcal Q\) and the shape of \(C\), not by a label. A production question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that reads an output port and a \(C\) containing interventions on upstream ports. An identification question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that computes a fibre and a \(C\) containing edits to the observed value. An obstruction question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that returns a reachability truth value. Two questions with the same \(D\) and different \((C,\mathcal Q)\) are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other.

In ordinary language, what changing the syrup produces and what its assay reading lets us infer are different questions.

**Original situation — Written by: an outside author.**

Dario explains why a certain syrup puts people to sleep: it has a high somnic index. He defines the somnic index carefully and without mentioning people at all. It is the reading on a standard instrument that times how quickly a mouse stops moving after a measured dose. Syrups with a high index do put people to sleep, and ones with a low index do so less. Lower the index by diluting the syrup, and people stay awake longer.

**Added fact from Round 4:** a technician can recalibrate the instrument so that every syrup reads ten points higher, and people who take the syrup sleep exactly as before.

**The pair — Written by: me.**

**What I expect:** Keeping the numerical index fixed as the part under examination will preserve its evidential usefulness while separating it from what produces human sleep.

| Change                                                             | Same part: the index reading | Human sleep                     | Contract and question                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dilute the syrup while retaining the standard assay.               | The index falls.             | People stay awake longer.       | This is an upstream intervention in a production contract asking what changing the syrup does to human sleep.                                 |
| Recalibrate the index alone, leaving the syrup and dose unchanged. | Every reading rises by ten.  | People sleep exactly as before. | This is a measurement-side edit relevant to an identification contract asking what the reported value lets us tell about the syrup’s effects. |

Under the first contract, Dario correctly predicts the effect of dilution. That success does not establish that the index reading produces sleep: dilution changes the syrup as well as its assay result.

Under the second, the index remains a source of information whose interpretation depends on calibration. Recalibration changes the measurement mapping; a higher reported number is therefore not, by itself, evidence that the syrup’s effect increased. The case does not supply a numerical prediction that must become false after a ten-point shift, and I do not invent one.

**The thoughtful person’s verdict:** Dario has a useful measure and a reliable prediction. He has said almost nothing about why the syrup causes sleep. The index is the sleepiness itself, observed in a mouse.

**Does the pair give that verdict?** Yes: the prediction and dilution effect remain, while the purported explanatory part is exposed as an assay reading rather than the part producing human sleep.

**O4: PLACED BY THE THEORY’S OWN DISTINCTION.** The same index moves under recalibration while human sleep does not, placing its contribution on the evidential side without denying the stipulated prediction.

This placement comes from the distinction the new instruction directed me to retrieve. The previous pass left O4 open; it did not independently earn this placement. C’s broader grounding row remains unresolved through O7.
