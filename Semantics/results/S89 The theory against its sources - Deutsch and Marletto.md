## 2. Hard-to-vary and reach (Part VI)

**Book side.**
- Wording: D p.24 calls the axis-tilt account a good explanation, hard to vary because every one of its details does a job (paraphrased). D p.31 (glossary) defines good and bad explanations as hard or easy to vary (paraphrased). D p.28 defines reach as "the ability of some of them to solve problems beyond those that they were created to solve". Confirmed.
- D p.22 "The quest for good explanations is, I believe, the basic regulating principle not only of science, but of the Enlightenment generally." Here "good" means hard to vary (the p.31 glossary; p.22 "I call such theories bad explanations" of easily variable ones). The paraphrase is fair.
- The direction of the claim. D p.29 does say "the harder it is to vary an explanation, the harder it is in particular to construct a variant with a different reach". **But Deutsch also asserts the theory's direction.** D p.306: "The explanation of those experiments in isolation isn't as hard to vary as the full theory." That sentence comes straight after one calling it another example of reach (paraphrased). D p.26: explanations are best when existing knowledge constrains them most (paraphrased). D p.24: the tilt also figures in explaining where the sun is seen against the horizon (paraphrased), an extra job that pins it down. So "more jobs, fewer surviving variants" is Deutsch's view. What is not his is the *word*: his reach is problems solved *beyond* those the explanation was made for, found only afterwards (p.28), not jobs laid on it as constraints.
- A sharper difference the first reader missed. D p.25: "a common way in which an explanation can be bad is by containing superfluous features or arbitrariness".

**Theory side.** The lemma is at 10:330, 11:315 and 12:317 ("More reach constrains variation; the containment need not be strict; counting jobs is not a warrant"). (E) has four conditions (10:277) and "(E) measures none" of depth (10:294). The lemma's lineage is 00:356–384 and 00:1298–1302: "The FW3 statement that an explanation reaching more is thereby harder to vary is not a valid comparison between arbitrary contents". FW5 kept only the containment. So the wording came through FW3/FW4 and was restricted on purpose. Consequence: an account may carry an idle commitment and still satisfy (E). A commitment that constrains nothing, anchored to a deleted subnetwork (whose relation is full by (O)), passes (F1). Part VI marks it as non-critical and does not reject it. The skill gives it the separate mark "idle".

**Verdict: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** Confirmed: the wording originates in Deutsch; hard-to-vary is a lemma and not a condition, while Deutsch makes it central. Refuted: that "more reach constrains variation" is not Deutsch's claim (D p.306, p.26, p.24). What remains of it: "reach" is used for enlarged job sets, which is not Deutsch's sense.

**Gain and loss.** Stating plainly that (E) is fidelity and hard-to-vary is a separate, non-grading measure would cost nothing and prevent the misreading. Making hard-to-vary a fifth condition would bring back grading, which 00 and 10 reject on purpose ("counting jobs is not a warrant"; no `GoodExplanation` primitive, 00:37), and could change verdicts on existing cases. **Best handling: (b)** clarification: (E) tolerates idle parts, and Part VI reports them; "reach" means what observation 5 would define.

**Candidate cases.**
- *Seasons with a flourish.* An explanation of the seasons by the tilt of the Earth's axis, with an added sentence: "and the sun god, who approves of the arrangement, keeps the tilt steady." Nothing else changes. Question: is this an explanation of the seasons, and is the added sentence part of what explains them?
- *The myth amended.* After hearing that southern seasons run opposite to northern ones, a teller changes the Persephone myth so that when Demeter is sad she sends the warmth to the south. Question: does the amended myth now explain the seasons in both hemispheres?

## 4. "Explanation" means different things

**Book side.** D p.19: the myth, though wholly false, is still an explanation of the seasons (paraphrased). Also D p.26: "When a formerly good explanation has been falsified by new observations, it is no longer a good explanation". So Deutsch's "good" is also relative to the current problem, which fits the theory's index-relativity (Derivation 7).

**Theory side.** 10:86 (and 11:71) "What cannot count as explanation is an error in the very dependence alleged to do the work." That is the clash in terms. But the theory has a name for Deutsch's broad sense: "An explanatory candidate for question p is …" (10:246). It also allows false theories as contents: "A system can represent a false theory" (Part IV (R)); "A system may understand a false theory" (Part X Deploy). There is no "good explanation" predicate, and that is deliberate: "There is no residual primitive named `Because`, `ExplanatoryWork`, `GoodExplanation`, or `Creative`" (00:37); Derivation 6.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** as a difference of terms. The correction: the theory's *explanatory candidate* is Deutsch's *explanation*, and its *Account* is closer to "accounts for what it purports to account for" on a contract. Deutsch's "good" (hard to vary) is Part VI, not (E).

**Handling: (b).** One sentence in Part I would do it. No verdict risk.

**Candidate cases.**
- *Persephone at home.* The ancient Greek myth of the seasons (Demeter's grief while Persephone is away), considered only against what the Greeks could observe or change: the time of year, and places within the Mediterranean world. Question: does the myth explain why winter comes each year? Note for the case author: D p.24 says there is no way of knowing that Demeter is sad "other than the onset of winter itself". That bears on non-circular dependence.

## 5. Reach is never defined; only its negative side is formalised

**Book side.** D p.29: reach is settled by what the explanation says (paraphrased). D p.28: we discover it only after the explanation exists (paraphrased). D p.73 "Non-explanatory systems cannot cross the conceptual gap that an explanatory conjecture crosses, to engage with unexperienced evidence". Two points limit the first reader's mapping. D p.29 "It also makes sense to speak of the reach of non-explanatory forms of knowledge", and D p.78 "biological knowledge is non-explanatory, and therefore has limited reach". Limited, not none.

**Theory side.** The word appears only in the lemma (10:330, 11:315, 12:317) and is never defined. File 00 defined it (00:383): "Reach occurs when an unchanged organizational core participates in an account of another question through a stated anchor and additional background." File 10 dropped the definition. The negative side: file 10's Derivation 3 (10:567–573) makes a selected transport underdetermined at *every* unseen pair, which is stronger than Deutsch's "limited reach". File 11's qualified Derivation 3 (11:560–566: "a population restriction, a physical relation, or another stated constraint may already fix a value that the history never tested") is closer to Deutsch. The positive side is not formalised, but it is present in two places:

"Determined by the content itself" can be defined from Part VI without new primitives: the set of jobs f for which Account(E, f) holds is fixed by E and the world, not by anyone's declaration.

**Verdict: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** "Once, never defined" holds for 10, 11 and 12. "Only the negative side" overstates it: 11:401 and the contract's range are positive, though not formalised. File 10's negative side is stronger than the book's.

**Gain and loss.** Gain: the lemma's key word gets a meaning; Deutsch's p.29 claim becomes statable (as survivors shrink, their reach converges). Loss: none; nothing is added to the primitives. **Best handling: (b)** restore a definition such as 00:383.

## 6. Scope: a stated narrowing against a limit that must be explained

**Book side.**
- D p.28: "You cannot confine its predictions to a region of your choosing." Context matters. The modified theory at D p.27 is not a silent limit of scope: it *asserts* something different elsewhere (that everywhere else on Earth the seasons come at those same times, paraphrased), and so "The modified theory is no longer an explanation of seasons, just a (purported) rule of thumb" (pp.27–28).
- M p.24: theories that apply "only to some scales, or domains, are by themselves problematic", since it would still need explaining why they hold at that scale and at no other (paraphrased). "Problematic" here means a problem to be solved, not a disqualification.
- A further passage the first reader did not use: D pp.118–119, number theories confined to small numbers would be "so full of arbitrary qualifiers, workarounds and unanswered questions that they would be very bad explanations".

**Theory side.** File 10: an exclusion must be "excluded by a stated scope, not silently" (10:272). Grievance 4 says a quietly narrowed contract "is caught by the requirement that the exclusion be stated … and then by any criticism that supplies the excluded change" (10:41). Narrowing *after* a failure is caught (10:456; Derivation 7), which answers the prophet and gambler of D p.22. File 11 addresses the tension head on and chooses not to settle it: "What makes a restriction appropriate to the question asked is a substantive, criticizable part of the claim; the semantics records the restriction and supplies no rule that certifies it" (11:161). The appropriateness of a restriction is listed as a declared input (11:514). File 12 has the same (12:171, 12:532). The owner's skill takes the books' side: "A limit that is held comes with its reason inside the explanation … A limit that only says what is being asked about is free; say so" (SKILL.md, "Check the patches"). Its word-list attributes this to "hard-to-vary over scope: Pres(F) under edits to the contract" in Parts III and VI, but file 10's Part VI varies organizations (𝒱), not contracts. **The skill therefore credits file 10 with a notion file 10 does not contain.**

**Verdict: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** File 10 does accept any stated, unexplained narrowing, and both books would object. The tension is narrower than claimed: the theory limits the *claim* (what is assessed), while Deutsch's example limits the *content* (it asserts something else elsewhere). File 11 records the point as open on purpose.

**Gain and loss.** Gain: the theory would agree with both books and with the owner's skill, and the skill's word-list would point at something real. Loss if done as a fifth condition: a declared input would become a verdict, and existing cases with honest narrow scopes could flip. **Best handling: (b)** clarify that (E) on a scoped contract does not certify the limit, plus an optional **(c)** Part VI-style remark that variants of the contract's limits form a variation family, so a limit held by nothing is loose. That is a measure, not a condition.

**Candidate cases.**
- *Seasons, known world only.* An astronomer states that the tilt of the Earth's axis causes the seasons "in the Mediterranean and nearby lands; I make no claim about anywhere else." Question: is this an explanation of the seasons in the Mediterranean?
- *Bread between two temperatures.* A baker explains why her dough rises (yeast producing gas) and adds "this holds for kitchens between 18 and 25 degrees". A second baker gives the same explanation with the same limits and adds why: the yeast is sluggish below and dies well above. Question: does each baker explain why the dough rises?

## 7. Two kinds of counterfactual; kinds as edit-signatures and M pp.207–209

**Book side.** M p.1 defines counterfactuals as statements about which transformations are possible and which are impossible (paraphrased). M p.207: "one expresses the counterfactual property that is required of a physical system for it to embody the entity in question". M p.209: details that leave the counterfactual property unaffected can be set aside (paraphrased). Deutsch uses "counterfactual" 0 times. Neither book uses "intervention" in the modelling sense (D 4 hits and M 3, all ordinary usage). Confirmed.

**Theory side.** Parts II–V are interventionist (edits, signatures). 00:31 names both kinds ("counterfactual interventions, and modal transition semantics"), and 00 R6 cites Halpern–Pearl. The link between them already exists: admitted edits must be *physically admitted* (Grievance 4, 10:41; non-vacuity, 10:272), and Part XII supplies possibility. File 12 makes the link explicit: an edit is admitted when "the physical module admits a controlled action that replaces the component" (12:491). File 12 also calls itself interventionist openly (12:41) and denies that it reduces causation to counterfactual dependence (12:23). The parallel with M pp.207–209 is real. Marletto adds an *interoperability law* to explain why details become irrelevant; the theory simply identifies components with equal signatures.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** as a description. It is not a hidden inconsistency: the theory ties interventionist edits to modal possibility through "physically admitted".

**Handling: (b)** one clarifying paragraph (file 12's Part XII paragraph could be carried back). **Candidate case:** see observation 15, *No machine that runs forever*, which tests whether the theory tells "impossible" apart from "never happened".

## 8. Knowledge as self-preserving information is missing

**Book side.** M p.13: knowledge in this sense need not be known by anyone (paraphrased). M p.13: natural selection, selecting biological adaptations with no purpose, can create knowledge by accident (paraphrased). M p.155 "Knowledge is defined entirely via counterfactuals: it is information that is capable of remaining instantiated in physical systems." D p.78 and D p.130: once embodied, such information tends to keep itself embodied (paraphrased). All confirmed.

**Theory side.** File 10's only knowledge notion is (EK) (10:446–456), and Derivation 6 excludes any "is knowledge" predicate. "Information" occurs 0 times in 10, 11 and 12, and 15 times in 00. File 00 had the CTK attribution and a conditional bridge (00:995–1011; 00:1330–1334: "Constructor-theoretic knowledge is not accidental longevity"). File 10 dropped both. There is a partial overlap: CT1 and CT2 retention (a constructor stays in its attribute) is Marletto's catalyst retention (M p.139, p.149). What the theory lacks is *information that preserves itself*.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**Gain and loss.** Gain: moth-wing and gene knowledge, central in both books, becomes expressible; the books' two routes to knowledge get a common frame. Loss: a new predicate that needs realization-ecology data from the physical module, and tension with Derivation 6. File 00 already showed the bridge is only conditional in either direction. **Best handling: (d)** leave it out, with a stated reason ("the class is about explanatory creativity; knowledge in the constructor-theoretic sense is a physical attribution outside it"); or **(c)** restore 00's conditional bridge as a derived relation of the physical module.

**Candidate cases.**
- *Dark moths.* Over generations, moths on soot-darkened trees come to have dark wings, and pale moths are eaten. No moth has any idea of bark colour. Question: do the moths, as a population, hold knowledge of the bark?
- *The burned notebook.* An engineer works out why a bridge design will fail and writes it down. The notebook burns before anyone reads it, and she dies soon after. Question: was knowledge created, and does it still exist?

## 9. Selection against construction: a clash of vocabulary

**Book side.** D p.78: "In the case of human knowledge, the variation is by conjecture, and the selection is by criticism and experiment." D p.79: mutations are random, but conjectures can be made on purpose, for an end (paraphrased). M p.15 treats criticism as the counterpart of natural selection (paraphrased). M p.17: natural selection cannot make jumps, while conjecture and criticism can (paraphrased). D p.73 is the conceptual-gap passage quoted under observation 5. All confirmed.

**Theory side.** 10:216 ("a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both"); 10:416; Part XV "Construction reduced to selection" (10:541). Two things the first reader missed:
- **File 11 dropped "blind" from Part 0.** 10:13 says "produced by blind variation and survival"; 11:15 says "produced by variation and survival on a history of encountered changes". The front matter now reads like Deutsch's broad sense, where criticism counts as selection. The body still carries the blindness ("No member of the history represents t, H, or the survival condition", 11:197).
- **File 12 has no provenances at all** (its preamble, 12:5), so neither the defeater nor the clash arises there.

D p.78 is not a Part XV defeater. Part XV asks that every construction witness be rewritten "as a selection history without loss", where a selection history has no represented target. Deutsch's broad sense keeps the target (conjectures are made "for a purpose").

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** **Handling: (b)** define "selection" in the theory as selection without a represented target, note D p.78's wider usage, and put "blind" back in 11's Part 0. **Partly testable:**
- *Two essay writers.* Two students each write ten drafts of an essay. The first keeps whichever draft her teacher marks highest, without knowing why. The second keeps whichever draft survives her own criticisms, each aimed at a stated fault. Question: did each student create what is good in her final draft?

Knowledge packed into a variation language by a programmer sits in the system's prior repertoire, so (N) denies it is new. - *Popper's parrot.* A parrot at Popper's lectures learns several of his sentences and repeats them to visitors. Question: has the parrot acquired Popper's ideas?

## 11. Question fidelity agrees with the books; the right level is not addressed

**Book side.** D p.116: the domino-by-domino answer "is addressing a different question – predictive rather than explanatory", and it poses the question at the wrong level of emergence (paraphrased). D p.108: emergence is his name for high-level phenomena becoming explicable at their own, nearly self-contained level (paraphrased). These are confirmed. The first reader's "agreement" needs two corrections:
- M pp.28–29 treats the transistor as **one** question that "has at least two answers", and says "Both explanations are essential to understand what is happening". The theory would count two questions, with different targets or contracts. The difference is in how a question is counted, not a contradiction.
- D p.117 "the physics-based explanation is true as well" and is "essential to explaining why prime numbers are relevant".

**Theory side.** (A) and Part III: "an answer to one is not an answer to the other" (10:168). Grain is a declared index (10:523). 11:279: "an account at a coarse grain is an account of the coarse question". The theory can say an answer is "at the wrong level" (it answers a question with a different target), but not which level a question *should* have. Deutsch's quasi-autonomy is definable in the theory's terms (there is an Account for the question whose anchors all lie at grain ℓ), but it is not defined.

**Verdict: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** **Handling: (b)**, with an optional **(c)** derived notion of a quasi-autonomous level. It must not become a predicate on grain, because "indices, not primitives" would break.

**Candidate cases.**
- *The domino that never falls* (D pp.115–118). An observer asks why one domino in a large domino computer never falls. Answer A traces, domino by domino, that none of its neighbours ever falls, back to the start switch. Answer B: "because 641 is prime, and the network tests the input for primality." Question: does each answer explain why that domino never falls?
- *The transistor* (M p.28). Why is this transistor on at the end of the run? A: the electrons started in such-and-such a state. B: the computer was factoring 15, and this transistor holds part of the answer, 3 × 5. Question: are A and B two answers to one question, or answers to two questions?

## 12. Regress and unexplained starting conditions

**Book side.** The cited pages do not support the claim that unexplained starting conditions are failures in general:
- D p.174, on turtles all the way down: a bad explanation "not because it fails to explain everything (no theory does)", but because the part it leaves unexplained is in effect the very thing it set out to explain (paraphrased).
- M p.54: laws plus initial conditions are "excellent for a special purpose—i.e., to make predictions about what happens on a subpart of the universe". The regress arises only when they are asked to explain everything (paraphrased).
- D p.116: passing the buck from domino to domino "is correct as far as it goes, but it doesn't go very far". It is faulted as an answer to a different question, not as circular.

The books' further objection to arbitrary starting points is that they are easy to vary: D p.64 "It must be easily variable because it cannot answer the question: why that foundation and not another?"; M p.48 (dog or turtle, an arbitrary choice); M p.59: an arbitrary choice of starting snapshot is unsatisfactory (paraphrased).

**Theory side.**
- Non-circular dependence forbids the target's answer "as an unanalysed boundary input or as a component" (10:270). That is Deutsch's p.174 criterion. 11:275 adds "an account whose only substantive component restates the answer". 00:210 adds "Identity of that assertion is structural at the declared grain." With kinds as edit-signatures, "another turtle" is the same kind as the turtle to be explained whenever no admitted change separates them, so a same-form regress falls under this clause.
- Starting values chosen to fit are caught in Part VII: "Set b_B=0 because it gives the mass I favour … its support is circular" (10:344). Compare M p.56, cosmologies "designed to agree that the universe should look exactly as we see it now". File 11 adds "A record reconstructed from the claim it is meant to support is not a receipt".
- Arbitrariness belongs to Part VI (easy to vary), which by design is not a condition.
- M p.59's arbitrary direction (reversible laws) is answered by derived direction (Part II, 10:126; 12 Derivation 3, 12:580–584).

"Regress" does occur 0 times in 10, 11 and 12, as claimed.

**Verdict: REFUTED as a gap.** At most NOT ESTABLISHED for same-form regress, which depends on reading "the target's answer" structurally at the grain (00:210 said so; 10 kept only "at the declared grain").

**Gain and loss.** A condition forbidding unexplained starting points would contradict both books (D p.174; M p.54). A one-line clarification is safe: the answer's identity is structural at the grain, so an input of the same kind as the thing to be explained counts. **Handling: (b).**

**Candidate cases.**
- *Turtles.* "The Earth rests on a turtle; that turtle rests on another; and so on down." Question: does this explain what holds up the Earth?
- *A starting state chosen to fit.* A cosmologist picks the universe's starting state so that, run forward under known laws, it yields exactly the sky we see, and offers this as the reason the sky looks as it does. Question: does the chosen starting state explain why the sky looks as it does?

## 13. Surprise is narrower than Deutsch's "problem"

**Book side.** D p.17: "there is a problem when a theory makes a prediction that we did not expect. Expectations are theories too." Also D p.17: a problem can arise from theory alone, with no observation involved (paraphrased). Confirmed.

**Theory side.** Surprise is defined only for a selected transport (10:232–238; 11:219–225). Derivation 4: "can be surprised only if it holds a transport selected on a history H" (10:577; 11:570). A constructed transport has no H, so the refutation of a constructed theory is not "surprise" in file 10 or 11. It is handled instead by K1 and K3 (criticism; "what a test refutes") and by the "recognized difficulty" of a critical episode (10:432). That term is never defined. **File 12 has already widened surprise** to any transport checked on a "tested history" (12:217–225; Derivation 5 at 12:596). Deutsch's "problem" as a conflict between ideas has no direct counterpart, but Repair_{O,P} (claimed against protected obligations, 10:441) is close; see missed relation M2.

**Verdict: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** True of 10 and 11; answered in 12.

**Gain and loss.** Widening surprise (12's way) lets refutation of a constructed theory count. The cost: Derivation 4's consequence ("the difference between learning and creating") and Derivation 10 rest on surprise being specific to selection, and would need rewording. **Handling: (b)** state that "surprise" is a technical term for selected transports, and map Deutsch's "problem" to recognized difficulty and Repair; or **(c)** adopt 12's version in the next revision of 11.

**Candidate cases.**
- *The engineer's bridge.* An engineer designs a footbridge from a worked-out theory of loads. It is never tested at crowd loads. On opening day it sways badly under a crowd. Question: did the engineer's theory meet a problem, and was she surprised?
- *Two theories at odds.* A physicist holds two well-tested theories that predict different results for an experiment nobody has yet done. Question: does she have a problem before any experiment is run?

## 14. Other gaps: error-correction, inexplicit knowledge, objective aesthetics

**Book side.**
- D p.140: "Without error-correction all information processing, and hence all knowledge-creation, is necessarily bounded." D p.31 defines "Rational" as "actively pursuing error-correction by creating criticisms of both existing ideas and new proposals".
- D p.405: we know language rules inexplicitly "yet we pass its rules on with remarkable fidelity to the next generation". D p.412: "for no idea can be represented entirely explicitly". D p.367 glossary (explicit, inexplicit, implicit).
- D p.368: "There are objective truths in aesthetics."

All confirmed.

**Theory side.**
- *Error-correction.* Present at the level of ideas: criticism and bearing (K1), Repair (P), what a test refutes (K3). That is Deutsch's p.31 sense. What is missing is the computational claim of D pp.140–142. The theory has the accumulation half, (T2) "Without a modulus, no accumulated bound follows" (10:376), and unbounded recursion (RC), but nothing about correction. Deutsch's conclusion (unbounded processing at a fixed accuracy needs a correction step) looks derivable as a lemma from T2 together with RC and the accuracy grades of CT3/CT4.
- *Inexplicit knowledge.* Absent from 10, 11 and 12. File 00 had "Inexplicit representation is not absent representation" (00:855–861), which file 10 dropped. File 10's representation (R) is structural, so it does not exclude inexplicit knowledge. But Build asks a witness to identify "the bindings constructed" (10:414), which is hard to do for inexplicit construction.
- *Aesthetics.* Left empty on purpose: "marks those places as empty" (10:25); "The semantics does not derive N" (10:458; 11:447). File 00 had an "objective interpretation" of N (00:898) and cited the *Nature* interview (R7), not Ch.14.

**Verdict: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** Error-correction is present for ideas and missing for computation. Inexplicit knowledge is confirmed missing (dropped from 00). Aesthetics is confirmed, and deliberate.

**Handling.** Error-correction: **(c)** a derived lemma. It adds nothing to the primitives, and its risk is low. Inexplicit: **(b)** restore 00's paragraph. Aesthetics: **(d)** keep empty, with the reason already given.

**Candidate cases.**
- *String tally.* Goatherds count a flock by reeling out a length of string per goat and reeling it back as goats return. Others cut the string into whole inches and round after every step (D pp.140–141). Question: can each method keep count of a flock of any size?
- *A rule she cannot state.* A native speaker correctly applies a grammar rule to sentences she has never heard, cannot state the rule, and learned it as a child without being told it. Question: does she know the rule, and did learning it involve creating anything?

## 15. Ready-made cases for explanations of absence (Attack B)

**Book side.**
- M p.65: the chess draw "is about the impossibility of transformations on the pieces on the board".
- M p.61: "that a perpetual motion machine is impossible does not mean it does not happen under a particular initial condition!"
- D p.117: "The second type of answer would be, 'Because 641 is prime.'"

All confirmed.

**Theory side.** These cases mainly test Part VII **Obstruction**: reachability and invariants (10:346–348, "no allowed path joins states of different invariant value"). The chess draw is a reachability question under rule edits, and perpetual motion is an invariant (energy) preserved by every allowed step. The 641 domino is a production question with an abstract input port. The theory itself calls Obstruction exact. The exposed case in Attack B is "Explanations that remove structure" (10:350–352): the rival's posited structure is absent. File 12 extends the exposed case to absences and prevention (12:341–347, 12:548). For Attack B, the more pointed source is D p.154, on denying qualia: "simply to deny their existence is a bad explanation: anything at all could be denied by that method". The denial must itself be backed by a good explanation of why the mistaken beliefs appear so different in kind (paraphrased). That adds a job to eliminative explanation, **explain the appearance of what is eliminated**, which Part VII does not ask for (missed relation M6).

**Verdict: PARTLY CONFIRMED.** The cases are good and ready, but they aim at Obstruction more than at Attack B.

**Candidate cases** (the first two are the observation's own; the others are added).
- *A king and a knight against a king* (M pp.64–65). The game ends with a lone king against a king and a knight. Question: why can this position never become checkmate?
- *No machine that runs forever.* No one has built a machine that keeps running without fuel. Answer A: nobody has found the right design yet. Answer B: energy cannot be changed without some side effect. Question: does each answer explain why there is no such machine?
- *The dismissed feeling* (D p.154). A theorist says people do not really have inner experiences; they only have false memories of having had them. Question: has the theorist explained why people seem to have inner experiences?

## Relations the first reader missed

These came from searching the books for the theory's own terms (transport, fidelity, contract, repertoire, invariant, persistence, catalyst, recipe, same kind, witness, table, interference, criterion, rephrase, level of explanation, universal explainer, and others), and from reading the context around each cited page. Where the concordance (a list of 161 book passages set against the theory's terms, made in the same session and not committed, because it quotes the books at length) already listed a candidate that the 15 observations left out, that is noted.

- **M1. Derived direction answers the books' problem of reversibility.** M p.59: "the laws are reversible: one may well explain everything occurring in the sequence in terms of the final snapshot". D p.118: at the level of the laws of motion, cause and effect can trade places, and the very notion of a cause is emergent and abstract (paraphrased). The theory derives direction from admitted edits (10:126; the pole and shadow, 10:338). File 12 makes it a theorem, with "mutual dependence" where both ends can be set, and time entering "as a restriction on which edits are admitted" (12:45, 12:580–584). This is the theory's clearest agreement with D p.118, and it offers a resolution of M p.59. (The concordance had a weaker form, B11.) Testable: a clock-pendulum or pole-and-shadow case with edits allowed at both ends.
- **M2. A problem as clashing criteria is close to Repair.** D p.17: "Solving a problem means creating an explanation that does not have the conflict." M pp.14–15: the author "has to find a way of meeting both these criteria". Repair_{O,P} (10:441) is exactly "meet o without losing any protected r". File 00 notes "their reconciliation is a further problem about purposes and obligations" (00:804) and "A newly discovered problem may supply a new obligation" (00:785). The concordance's C10 ("no counterpart") is too strong. The counterpart is Repair; what is missing is a definition of "recognized difficulty".
- **M3. Where a population's fixing comes from: D pp.159–161 and file 11's Derivation 3.** File 11's revision says a population can fix a selected transport's value on unseen changes (11:197, 11:473, 11:566). Deutsch says the apparent reach of an artificially evolved robot may be the programmer's: "some of the knowledge that you packed into that language … will have reach" (p.161). The theory does not say that the population itself has a provenance. If the population was constructed or declared, the selected transport's fidelity on unseen changes is inherited from that construction. This matters because file 11's Derivation 3 is the claim under test. Deutsch's inspiration against perspiration (p.160, "ever less inspiration and ever more perspiration") also matches Part IV's two responses to violation (re-tune or construct). Candidate case: *the evolved walking robot*. A walking program is evolved by trial and selection inside a movement language its programmer spent months designing. Question: whose knowledge makes it walk on terrain it was never tested on?
- **M4. Interoperability against "Substrate independence".** Part I: "Any carrier may bear an organization" (10:92). Marletto treats this as a contingent physical law: "A universe where the interoperability property is violated would not have universal computers" (M p.95; also p.87 and pp.208–209). File 00 cited it ([R3], 00:912); file 10 dropped it. This bears on Part XIII: a Dust sector (M pp.93–95) is a physical barrier in the theory's sense ("independently characterized domain … every admitted … enabling condition leaves the relevant capability unavailable"). Candidate case: *two sealed sectors*. A universe has two kinds of matter that cannot exchange signals, each with its own computers. Question: can any computer in that universe perform every computation its physics allows?
- **M5. Testability is a counterfactual, and labels do no work.** M p.47: "'testable' means that it must be possible to set up an experiment to disprove the prediction, if it is false". That is non-vacuity's demand that the substantive contrast be a *physically admitted* edit, not one actually performed (10:290). M p.48, on the dog that supports the universe: "how can one even check whether it is a dog or something else?" That is Derivation 2 and the kinds clause: where no admitted change separates dog from turtle, they are one kind and the label does nothing.
- **M6. Eliminative explanation must explain the appearance (D p.154).** Quoted under observation 15. This is the most on-target book passage for Attack B and Part XV's "exposed case", and neither the concordance nor the 15 observations used it.
- **M7. Idle parts.** D p.25: superfluous features make an explanation bad. (E) accepts an idle commitment, and Part VI reports it. Folded into observation 2 above; the 15 observations did not state it.
- **M8. The theory's positive side of reach already exists in file 11.** 11:401 "A first representation may be constructed from an available problem without prior observation of what it represents" corresponds to D p.73, where the SETI project "can conjecture the existence of something, calculate what some of its observable attributes would be". Folded into observation 5.
- **M9. Hard to vary as error-correction in transmission.** D p.94: "random errors in the transmission of a good explanation are easier for the receiver to detect and correct". This ties hard-to-vary to fidelity of transmission and to reconstruction by the learner (Part X; file 11's Recoding, 11:363). No theory file connects these.
- **M10. People as universal explainers.** D p.56 ("everything that is not forbidden by laws of nature is achievable, given the right knowledge") and D p.146 ("universal explainers"). The theory deliberately abstains: it "does not prove that any human … belongs" (10:27), and "Recursion does not entail universality" (10:510). This is a deliberate abstention of the same kind as aesthetics. The concordance had it (A9); the 15 observations did not.
- **M11. The skill's attribution.** The owner's skill word-list maps the "held limit" test to a Part III/VI construction that file 10 does not contain (observation 6). Where the skill runs ahead of file 10, it runs toward the books.

## The observations most worth acting on

1. **Observation 6 (scope).** It touches a condition of Account (non-vacuity). Both books agree against file 10, the owner's skill already implements the books' view (and credits file 10 with it), and file 11 names the question but defers it. Cases are easy to write and would show whether the thoughtful person treats an unexplained stated limit as still explaining. A non-condition remark in Part VI ("hard to vary over scope") would close the gap without turning a declared input into a verdict.
2. **Observation 2, sharpened (idle parts and "reach").** Deutsch's central criterion is a lemma in the theory, and (E) accepts accounts with idle parts, which D p.25 calls bad. This is the widest real divergence from the main source, and one case (*Seasons with a flourish*) would show whether the theory's split between fidelity (E) and hard-to-vary (Part VI) matches thoughtful judgement. The wording fix ("reach") is cheap.
3. **Observation 13 with M2 (surprise, problem, Repair).** Files 10 and 11 differ from file 12 here, so a choice is due at the next revision anyway. The two bridge and physicist cases would test whether "surprise" should stay specific to selection. Mapping Deutsch's "problem" to Repair and recognized difficulty costs nothing.
4. **Observation 5 (reach undefined).** Restoring file 00's definition (00:383), or defining reach as the set of jobs a content does, is cheap and risks no verdicts. It also gives the lemma in observation 2 its proper meaning.
5. **Observations 1, 3 and 10 together (attribution), plus M3.** The attribution fix is free and has no verdict risk. M3 is the one missed relation that bears directly on the claim under test in round S76: where file 11's revised Derivation 3 lets a population fix untested values, Deutsch pp.160–161 says whose knowledge that is.

**Limits of this check.** File 00 was read by section, not line by line.
