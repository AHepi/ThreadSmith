## The sentence in its place (L161, whole)

> A question may fail to pick out its alleged target, assume an incompatible baseline, or combine incompatible requirements. Its formulation is still an event. Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract. An assessment is an event with a frozen contract (Part 0, grievance 10): a change to \(C\) or \(\mathcal Q\) during it, left unrecorded, makes the record name a claim other than the one assessed. Supplying a meaning for a replacement query can make a coherent new question; it does not answer the original one.

"event" is not defined in file 99. Its other uses are: L53 "a sequence of events (Parts IV, XII)"; L55 "An assessment is an event with a frozen contract."; L161 (the assessment sentence above); L397 "A record leaf is a reference to an event with an interpreted claim."; L604 "An assessment event with contract \(C\) and an episode in which \(C\) is replaced by \(C'\) with a construction trace are both representable without contradiction."; L612 "The result does not apply to coarsenings, changed boundaries, or lost event identities." Across these, an event is a particular with an identity, something a record leaf can refer to, of the same kind as an assessment. "occurrence" is defined at L169: "An **occurrence** is a physically located carrier. A **content** is an organization together with its contract-relative commitments."

## What the sentence does

The first sentence of L161 lists ways a question can be defective. That invites a doubt: if a question fails to pick out its alleged target, is there anything there at all, anything to record, criticize or fail to answer? The sentence answers the doubt. Each of its three parts has work:

- **"Its formulation"** separates the event from the question. A question is the structure \(p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\) (L138), and questions are told apart by that structure, not by when or by whom they were put: L151, "Two questions with the same \(D\) and different \((C,\mathcal Q)\) are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other." Where the structure is defective (no target, an incompatible baseline), what is still there is the formulating of it. Every question has one: L155 says every contract, "whatever its provenance, is a declared index of the claims made on it (Part XIV)", and L526 says the declared indices "are stated, not defined". So "its formulation" presupposes nothing the text does not already give for every question, declared, selected or constructed.
- **"an event"** puts that formulation in the class the text uses for what records refer to and what assessments are (L55, L397, L604, L612). This is what the next sentence needs. "Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract": by L377 that other question is a criticism's question \(p_\delta\), and "A criticism has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), premise \(g\), and a connection." The defective question's formulation is the target, and a record leaf about it is "a reference to an event with an interpreted claim" (L397). It is also what the last sentence of L161 needs: "it does not answer the original one" requires the original one to stay there to be not answered.
- **"still"** marks the sentence as the answer to the doubt the first sentence raises: the defect does not cancel the event.

## The readers' points, weighed

Both readers closed HOLDS; neither proposes a change. The points are weighed on their arguments (rule 7), not on the number of readers.

**Mimo, "occurrence" (reply line 32: `Its formulation is still an occurrence.`).** Mimo says this breaks L169 and L397: "a carrier alone carries no interpreted claim, and the next sentence's record names a claim". The record that names a claim is in the assessment sentence, two sentences on, not the next one; the point stands without that. By L169 an occurrence is the carrier side of the carrier–content line. The defects the first sentence lists (no target, an incompatible baseline, incompatible requirements) are defects in what is asked, not in the carrier; the formulation that "exposing the defect" takes as its target has to hold what was asked, and L397's record leaf refers to "an event with an interpreted claim", not to a carrier. "occurrence" is therefore a different, narrower claim, one that moves the formulation to the carrier side of L169's line. GLM's version of the same point (reply line 39) is the same in substance: it "leaves it open whether a formulation can be a record leaf". The point holds.

**Mimo, "input" (reply line 37: `Its formulation is still an input.`).** Breaks L592: "A semantics that takes questions as inputs cannot represent this; the present one does, by giving contracts provenance." Calling the formulation an input puts back what L592 sets aside. The point holds.

**Mimo, "fact" (reply line 42: `Its formulation is still a fact.`).** Mimo says this breaks S23 and L397. "fact" is not in S23's list, but S23 also takes out "anything that could imply some sort of foundational truth or authority", and "fact" reads that way; file 99 does not use the word anywhere. And a fact is not what L397's record leaf refers to. The point holds.

**Mimo, dropping the concession (described, no wording written out).** Weighed with GLM's (c) below.

**Mimo, step 3.** "A reader might ask whether a formulation whose query has no meaning is an event with an interpreted claim (L397); the sentence claims event-status only." This checker reads it as Mimo does. The sentence claims only that the formulation is an event; it does not say that it is a record leaf. And where a record leaf refers to the formulation of a question whose query has no meaning, the interpreted claim is a claim about the event (what was asked, with what alleged target), which can be stated when the query has no meaning. That is the case L161's last sentence and case O34 turn on (below). No defect.

**GLM, (a) (reply line 33: `Asking it still happens.`).** Drops "event" and with it the tie to L55, L397 and L604 that GLM names: what merely happens is not named as the kind of thing a record leaf refers to or an assessment is. Also, "asking" is narrower than "formulation": by L155 a contract may be declared, selected or constructed, and a selected contract is "the surviving member of a population under a variation-and-survival history", which no one need have asked. The point holds, and this adds to it.

**GLM, (c) and Mimo on the concession (GLM reply line 43: `Its formulation is an event.`).** Without "still" the sentence claims the same thing: every formulation is an event. What goes is the mark that the sentence answers the doubt the first sentence raises. That leaves the claim as it was and makes its place in the passage less plain. The readers call this dropping the concession. Read so, the point holds: the wording without "still" is not clearer, so no FIX.

**GLM, fault sought** (reply line 45):

> "Its formulation" could be misread as the formulated text rather than the act; but "event" forces the act reading (events happen), so the ambiguity is not live.

File 99 does use "formulation" in the other sense elsewhere: L461, "The physical module adopts a task-based formulation of physics for this purpose." There it is a way of stating something, a content. At L161 the predicate "is still an event" allows only the act reading, as GLM says. The two uses stand in different Parts and bear on different things, and neither sentence can be read with the other's sense. No defect.

## This checker's own attempts to vary it

- `It is still an event.` A different claim: it makes the question itself, the structure of L138, an event. That breaks L151 (questions are told apart by \(D\), \(C\) and \(\mathcal Q\), not by when they were put) and blurs L169's carrier–content line from the other side. "Its formulation" is doing work.
- `Its statement is still an event.` File 99 uses "statement" for something stated, a content: L159, "that statement is a missing declared input (Part XIV)"; L409, "rather than by a statement of it"; L473, "a statement that names the system". The word would pull against those uses. Not a rewording that changes nothing.
- `Formulating it is still an event.` This takes out the act-or-product reading GLM names. On its most natural reading it claims what the sentence claims. But a gerund names the doing, which can be read as a kind of doing and not as one particular with an identity, and records refer to particulars (L397; L612, "lost event identities"). The ambiguity it removes is not live (GLM, and above). It is not clearer, so no FIX.
- `Its formulation is nonetheless an event.` (Mimo's "nonetheless", reply line 46.) A re-spelling of the concession. It changes nothing the theory needs, and it is not clearer than "still".
- `Its formulation is still an event, which a criticism can take as its target.` Adds a clause that the next sentence and L377 already carry. It says more than the sentence needs to and repeats them.

None of these keeps what the theory needs from the sentence and reads more plainly than it does.
