## Each side's argument (rule 7)

**Mimo, VARIES** (`s103_vary_mimo_1.response.txt`, lines 52–74). Rival wording:

Mimo's reasons: L13 puts provenance on correspondence and L193–L201 on transports; (G) at L421–L422 puts Build in every creative attribution, and L425 lets the content be "an organization, a transport, or a contract", so what every attribution requires is construction, "not a transport carrying that provenance". **GLM, HOLDS** (`s103_vary_glm_1.response.txt`, lines 49–73). GLM's rivals and what each breaks: widening to "every explanation" breaks L199 and L13 (declared transports serve explanations; only a claim about creativity cannot depend on declaration); narrowing to "creativity" loses the theory's unit, since "creative attribution" is what (G) (L422), the creative-episode class (L528) and the episode (L429) attribute; and "Construction has a separate provenance" is a category error: "Construction *is* one of the three provenances (L193, L197 …); it does not have one." Faults sought: none; "separate trace" is stated at L405 and L411, and the requirement is what (G) states.

**Between them.** The readers differ on one point that decides the rest: whether "Construction is a separate provenance" is loose. GLM's argument stands and Mimo's does not. Mimo reads L193 as putting provenance only on a transport, so that "construction" could name only the process and not the provenance. But the body uses the noun exactly as L47 does, at L201 ("Neither provenance is reducible to the other", said of selection and construction) and at L542 ("the two provenances"); Mimo itself grants that L13 allows the shorthand. So the first clause is not loose against the body; it is the body's own way of speaking. GLM's third rival shows the other side of the same point: construction does not *have* a provenance, it *is* one.

## The rival wording: a rewording or a different claim

Mimo's rival changes two things: the subject ("Construction" becomes "Constructed correspondence") and the pronoun ("it" becomes "construction").

- **On Mimo's intended reading it is a rewording.** Read as shorthand for the constructed provenance of a correspondence, its first clause says what the sentence says; its second clause spells out the pronoun the sentence already resolves to. It keeps every term the sentence uses and every tie the sentence makes (to L193–L201, L405, L411, (G) at L422, L528). It changes nothing the theory needs from the sentence. As rule 5 asks, that is recorded: **on this reading the sentence is easy to vary in its wording** (the brief's plain sense, not the text's own term at L317); what varies is the phrasing, and no tie of the sentence to the body varies with it.
- **It is not clearer, so the ruling is KEEP, not FIX.**
  - Its subject is less exact than the sentence's. L13 says a correspondence "is a **relation with a provenance**". A correspondence has a provenance; read as written, "Constructed correspondence is a separate provenance" says that a relation is a provenance, which is the category shift GLM's third rival shows in the other direction. The sentence's "Construction is a separate provenance" is the body's own usage (L201, L542).
  - Its second change is needed only because of its first. With "Constructed correspondence" as subject, "it" would point to a correspondence and ask a transport of every creative attribution, the very reading Mimo feared; so the rival has to write "construction" out. With "Construction" as subject, as in the sentence, the pronoun points to construction, which is what (G) asks (L422, L405). "every creative attribution requires construction" after a clause whose subject is "Construction" repeats the noun where the pronoun has one natural antecedent; the repetition removes no doubt the body leaves.
  - Read literally rather than as shorthand, the rival would be a different and less exact claim (a correspondence as a provenance, against L13), and would not be adopted as a challenge either.

## This checker's own search for a failure of the sentence

- **The pronoun.** "It" has the clause's subject, "Construction", as its natural antecedent. The other grammatical candidate, "a separate provenance", is indefinite ("separate" from what?) and gives no definite requirement; a reader who took it would still land on the constructed provenance, which is construction. Every reading resolves to what (G) asks (L422). No failure.
- **"Every creative attribution".** The attributions the text calls creative are (G) and those that contain it: the creative critical episode (L429), the creative-episode class (L528), (EX) (L447–L448), question-finding (L592). Each contains Build. The capability attributions of Parts XII and XIII (Can, CanAdv, (RC), (U1)–(U3)) attribute what a system can do, not a creative act, and the text never calls them creative; even so, owned capability "requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one" (L475). Repair (P) (L438) is not called creative, and "an act that repaired without an account" is its own attribution (L441). No attribution the text calls creative lacks construction. No failure.
- **"A separate trace".** Selection's history (L195) and construction's trace (L405) are told apart in the body (L411, L584). No failure.
- **Conflict with a case.** The worked case at L626–L628 attributes (G) only "If \(S_1\) was built by an owned subhistory containing a nontrivial binding construction" (L628), and Argument 5 attributes the creative act of question-finding "by an owned construction (G)" (L592).
