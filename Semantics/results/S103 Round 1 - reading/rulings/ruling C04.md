## The sentence and what it does

L113, first sentence (unit L113.s1):

> Fix an organization \(D\) and a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\) (Part III).

It opens the section "Kinds are edit-signatures" (L111). What the theory needs from it:

- **\(D\) as an organization of (O).** L88: "D=(V,(X_v)_{v\in V},J,B,A,L)." The components \(j\), their footprints and relations \(L_j\), and the sets \(A\) and \(B\) that (K) and L119 use are \(D\)'s.
- **\(C\) typed as edit–boundary pairs of \(D\).** (K) at L116, "\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}. \tag{K}", can be read only if each \((a,b)\in C\) is an edit and a boundary of \(D\): L91, "For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies", with L94, "L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v ."
- **The pointer to where a contract is defined.** L141: "The **contract** \(C\subseteq A\times B\) is the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs the claim ranges over; it contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)." And L159: "A contract is a stated subset of the changes its target admits (Part II), and a stated scope is what makes it one." L113 is the first use of "contract" in Part II; before it the word stands in Part 0 and Part I (L17, L31, L43, L49, L55, L67, L69, L73), named but not typed (L31 already calls it one of the "**declared indices**").
- **\(D\) and \(C\) held as the parameters of what follows.** The next sentence, "The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is" (L113.s2), and (K) index the signature by \(C\); L119 then draws the index out: "Kinds are therefore relative to the contract; a coarser contract identifies more components, and two components of one kind on \(C\) may separate on a finer contract." ## Weighing the wordings

Each wording is weighed on what it keeps and what it breaks; none is weighed by who offered it or how many were offered.

- **"Take an organization \(D\) and a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\) (Part III)."** (Mimo, the closing rival.) It keeps every load listed above: \(D\), \(C\), the typing, the pointer, and \(D\) and \(C\) held for (K), L119 and L121–L127. So Mimo's point holds: on what the theory needs, this wording works as well, and the sentence is easy to vary on this reading, in the brief's plain sense; that is recorded here. It is not clearer. In file 99, "take" never introduces a parameter; it is the text's verb for adopting and for premises used to rule out: L75, "here and throughout, to adopt something is to take it tentatively, open to replacement", and L397, "A premise may be a claim taken as given: tentatively accepted, for whatever reason". Six lines below, L119 uses it for port values: "not from the values its ports take in a solution" (and L177, "what a port of \(P\) will take under an admitted edit"). "Take an organization \(D\) and a contract" would bring a third sense of the word into a section that already uses one, and it invites reading \(D\) and \(C\) as claims taken up, which they are not: they are the organization and the declared index (L524) a definition is stated on. "Take" also says less plainly than "Fix" that \(D\) and \(C\) stay put while the components \(j, j'\) range.
- **"Let \(D\) be an organization and \(C\subseteq A\times B\) a contract (Part III)."** (GLM, the closing rival.) It keeps every load as well; GLM's point holds, and it too is a rewording that changes nothing the theory needs, so the sentence is easy to vary on this reading as well; recorded. "Let" is the text's commonest verb for introducing objects (L217, L329, L353, L363, L377, L562, L572), and it has no second sense a reader might bring. Against that, "Fix" is the verb the text itself uses where named objects are held while something else in the passage ranges: L287, "Fix \(\mathcal E\) and a declared restriction operation.", where \(W\) then ranges over subsets and the background stays "fixed"; L369, "Fix a question \(p\), a pair \((a,b)\in C\) and a value \(y\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\).", where the candidates then range ("every such candidate alike"). L113 is a passage of that shape: \(D\) and \(C\) are held and components are compared. The text's wording also sets its two objects in parallel, noun then symbol ("an organization \(D\) and a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\)"), where the rival reverses the order and leaves a verb to be supplied ("\(C\subseteq A\times B\) a contract"). One consideration favours the rival: a reader new to the register might take "Fix an organization" as "repair an organization" in a text whose Part XI is on repair. The text does not force that reading: every use of "fix" and its forms in file 99 means hold or determine (L57, L113, L127, L151, L253, L269, L277, L287, L315, L325, L343, L369, L413, L435, L453, L471, L497, L574, L576), mending is always "repair", and the symbol after "an organization" shuts the reading off. Changing L113 alone would leave L287 and L369 on "Fix" and make the text's own idiom less uniform. On balance the rival is not clearer.
- **"Fix an organization \(D\) and a set \(C\) of changes (Part III)."** (Mimo, line 86, offered as breaking.) Mimo's point holds, with one qualification. The variation does not contradict L141: L159 itself calls a contract "a stated subset of the changes its target admits (Part II)", so "changes" is the text's own word for \(C\)'s members. What it breaks is by loss. It drops the word "contract", so that L119's "Kinds are therefore relative to the contract", "a coarser contract" and "a finer contract", and L526's "(K) depends on (O) and a contract", no longer have \(C\) as their contract, and \(C\) loses its standing as a declared index (L524); the pointer "(Part III)" then points to no term the sentence names; and without the typing, \((a,b)\in C\) in (K) is not an edit–boundary pair in place (L159's "changes" are made pairs only at L141).
- **"Fix an organization \(D\) and a contract \(C\subseteq A\) (Part III)."** (Mimo, line 90, offered as breaking.) Mimo's point holds: it contradicts L141's \(C\subseteq A\times B\), and (K)'s \((a,b)\in C\) and \(L_j(a,b)\), supplied "For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\)" (L91), have no place for the boundary.
- **Dropping the typing** (Mimo, described, not written out; written out here only for weighing: "Fix an organization \(D\) and a contract \(C\) (Part III)."). Mimo is right that nothing becomes false: L141 gives \(C\subseteq A\times B\) through the pointer. But (K) at L116 could no longer be read where it stands: the reader would have to go ahead to Part III to learn that \((a,b)\in C\) ranges over \(D\)'s edits and boundaries, for which alone \(L_j(a,b)\) is supplied (L91). A rewording that changes nothing the theory needs, and less clear.
- **Dropping "(Part III)"** (GLM, described, not written out). GLM's point holds in this sense: the typing would still let (K) be read, but "contract" would stand in Part II with nothing saying where it is defined, and with it what the typing leaves out: that a contract "contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)" and is "the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs the claim ranges over" (L141), and that it is "a stated subset" (L159). Nothing would contradict anything; the loss is the pointer the text gives for terms defined later (L119: "(Part V)", "(Part IV)").
- **\(\subset\) for \(\subseteq\)** (GLM, described, not written out). GLM's point holds: file 99 writes inclusion \(\subseteq\) (L91, L94, L141) and strict inclusion \(\subsetneq\) (L572: "on a finite history \(H\subsetneq C\)"). Read as strict, \(\subset\) would exclude \(C=A\times B\), which L141 admits; read as mere inclusion, it would change nothing but break the text's notation.

## The sentence itself

- **Does every part do work?** Mimo's point that "the typing clause is the sentence's only strict content", and that dropping it breaks nothing, does not show an idle part. The typing restates L141 at the first use of "contract" in Part II, where (K) needs it to be read in place; the text restates it at L141 in the same words and names a contract again at its uses (L217: "Let \(t\) be a transport to the simulation layer \(S\), with contract \(C\)"). And the sentence carries more than the typing: it names \(D\) and \(C\) as the parameters that the next sentence, (K), L119 and L121–L127 are indexed to. Without the sentence, "on \(C\)" in L113.s2 and "the contract" in L119 would have no antecedent in Part II. Both readers' C12 sections rely on exactly this: Mimo, the index "already fixed at C04" (reply line 272); GLM, "\(C\) is in scope from L113" (reply line 263). The verb "Fix" is the one part that carries nothing the theory needs, which is what both rivals exhibit; that is register, not a fault. Mimo itself says "Nothing fails".
- **Does "Fix" conflict with L119's varying of \(C\)?** No. The sentence holds \(D\) and \(C\) for the definition; L119 compares what the definition gives at different contracts, which is what an index is for (L524). Holding a declared index for a definition is not settling any claim (S28).
- **Circularity.** L113 points ahead to Part III for "contract", and L159 points back to Part II for "the changes its target admits". That is a pair of cross-references, not a cycle in the order of definition: \(A\) and \(B\) are given with \(D\) (L91) without contracts; a contract is a subset of \(A\times B\) (L141) and a declared index, which L526's order puts beside (O) and (Q) as depending on nothing ("(O) and (Q) depend on nothing; nor do the declared indices and the declared inputs, which are stated, not defined."); (K) "depends on (O) and a contract" (L526).
- **Scope of the setup.** The sentence fixes an organization \(D\) and a contract of \(D\). Argument 1 reads (K) on the other side of a transport as well (L556: "\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)=\{(\tau(a),\sigma(b),L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)))\}"). That reading is supplied by L119 ("A component of \(E\) and a component of \(E'\) are of one kind on \(C\) when their signatures, read on \(C\) through \(\tau\) and \(\tau'\), coincide under a footprint bijection") and by L245, not by L113; no reader raised it, and it is no failure of this sentence (see the note at the end).
- **The owner's decisions.** "Fix", "organization" and "contract" are no words S23 forbids, and the sentence carries no idea S23 forbids; fixing a parameter is not taking up a claim at all. It says nothing about what must happen to a candidate (S21). It holds no list, count or grade (S20). It brings in no physical possibility: \(C\) ranges over edits the organization admits, and L75 says "That an organization admits an edit is not a claim that the edit can be carried out or can come about" (S25–S27). It settles nothing (S28).

**Easy to vary on this reading.** Both readers' rivals, "Take an organization \(D\) and a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\) (Part III)." and "Let \(D\) be an organization and \(C\subseteq A\times B\) a contract (Part III).", are rewordings that change nothing the theory needs from the sentence: the sentence is easy to vary on this reading, in the brief's plain sense, and that is recorded. Neither is a different claim. Under rule 5 the ruling is FIX only if a rival is clearer; neither is. "Take" brings in a sense of a word the text keeps for adopting, for premises taken as given and for port values (L75, L397, L119), and appears nowhere else as a setup verb. "Let" is as plain as "Fix" but leaves the text's own idiom for holding named objects while others range (L287, L369), reverses the parallel of noun and symbol, and gains only against a reading of "fix" as "repair", a sense file 99 never uses.

**Between the readers.** They agree on every point of substance and on the verdict; each rival works as well as the other and as the text's wording. The variations each offered as breaking do break what they name, with one qualification to Mimo's: "a set \(C\) of changes" breaks by losing the word "contract", its index and the pair typing, not by contradicting L141, since L159 itself calls a contract's members changes. Mimo's point that the typing is the sentence's only strict content does not hold: the sentence also names the parameters (K), L119 and L121–L127 are indexed to, and both readers' C12 sections rely on that.

**What depends on the sentence.** L113.s2 ("The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is") and (K) at L116 take \(D\) and \(C\) from it; L119 (its "on \(C\)" and "Kinds are therefore relative to the contract"), L121–L127 (the families of signatures, "patterns in (K)"), L245 ("By (K), no component of \(E\) whose signature on \(C\) differs from its counterpart's meets (F1)"), L520 ("Kinds, from signatures (K)."), L526 ("(K) depends on (O) and a contract.") and Argument 1 (L552–L558, "By (K)") go through (K) as set up here.
