## The sentence and what it does

L161 s1 names three ways a question, in the sense of L135–L147, can be in error: at \(D\) ("fail to pick out its alleged target"), at \(b_0\) ("assume an incompatible baseline"), and among what the question asks of an answer, its contract, query and aims ("combine incompatible requirements"). The two sentences after it lean on it: "Its formulation is still an event." (s2; "Its" is such a question) and "Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract." (s3; "the defect" is one of s1's). The sentence says "may": it names ways a question can be in error, not that any question is, and not every way (both readers agree on this: Mimo's step 3; GLM does not contest it).

The word "compatible" is the text's own. L97–L100: "The compatible valuations are" \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)=\{z\in X_D:\forall j\in J,\ z|_{V_j}\in L_j(a,b)\}\) (O). L103 uses the contrary the same way: an edit "does not add an equation beside an incompatible one". So "an incompatible baseline" has, in the text's own sense, its first reading ready: a baseline at which no valuation is compatible, which is what L257's "\(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\)" rules out for an account. For the requirements, "combine" gives "incompatible" its relatum: requirements incompatible with one another.

## Weighing Mimo's rival

**1. "a baseline incompatible with its remaining terms".** Why the text's wording and not this one:

- *Where the relatum is stated.* The text gives the defect a place where what it is incompatible with gets said: L161 s3, "Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract", and L377, "A criticism has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), premise \(g\), and a connection", with \(p_\delta\) "the question whether \(z\) has \(\delta\) in respect of \(p\)". What an alleged incompatibility is an incompatibility with is carried by that criticism's premise and connection, in that further question. The rival settles it in advance inside s1.
- *What the rival closes.* The premise of such a criticism need not be one of the question's own terms. It can be a claim taken as given: S27's example is a conflict triggered by "perpetual motion is impossible", without testing against the world, and S28 has it that "That "ruling out" is a choice that was made." A question whose baseline has a device running without end can be exposed, by a person who takes that claim as given, as assuming an incompatible baseline. Under the rival that is not "an incompatible baseline", since the incompatibility is not with the question's remaining terms. The owner's words leave the grounds open: S21, "The problem may, for whatever reason, be ill posed." The rival narrows what the owner leaves open; nothing in the owner's words asks for the narrowing.
- *Mimo's reason for it.* "Nothing makes a question defective by failing the world" guards against a question's defect being settled by the world. The text as it stands already guards against that: the sentence says "may"; the defect is exposed in another question (s3); a criticism's defect is "alleged" (L377); and a ruling out on a claim taken as given is a choice (S28). S28 and S21, as Mimo quotes them, say that nothing is settled and that matching is tentative; neither says that a question can be in error only against its own terms. So the narrowing is not needed for what Mimo wants it for.
- *"Terms" is not the text's word.* File 99 never calls the parts of \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) its "terms" ("terms" stands only in "in terms of", L21, L520, L596). "Its remaining terms" would take in \(\rho_p\) and \(O_p\), and the text gives no sense to a baseline's being incompatible with a provenance or with an aim. The rival is not clearer.

**2. "combine requirements that cannot be met together".** Taken alone, this keeps the claim ("combine" already makes the requirements incompatible with one another), so it changes nothing the theory needs. It is not clearer: it replaces the text's structural word, "incompatible", used in the sense of (O) and L103, by a modal, "cannot be met", whose kind the sentence does not state. A reader can take it as whether anyone could meet them, which is the reading L159 keeps out of a question ("admitting a change is not a claim that it can be carried out or come about") and L75 keeps out of the question's definition ("It does not define a question, its range of changes, an account or a conflict between candidates (Parts III, V, VI)."), after S25 and S26. So this half gives no ground for a FIX.

**Easy to vary?** The rival taken whole is a different claim (half 1), and half 2 is a rewording that is not clearer. So the sentence is not shown easy to vary on this reading. The two changes that keep the claim (half 2 alone, and "may" for "can") were looked at for clarity only. Neither is clearer.
