## The sentence in its place

> L375| **Histories.** A history \(h\) is a set of occurrences with an acyclic causal precedence \(\prec_h\) (write \(\preceq_h\) for its reflexive closure) and a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated. An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components meet the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts. A route that started and did no work, or that was already at rest when the result occurred, is not active for that result; whether a route is active is read from the history, not from the result.

What it uses, and where it is used:

- **occurrence**, L169: "An **occurrence** is a physically located carrier."
- **represents (R)**, L205: "An occurrence \(o\) **represents** content \(c\) at grain \(\ell\) when there is a transport from the organization that \(o\) instantiates under the physical module, at grain \(\ell\), to \(c\), faithful on \(c\)'s contract, whose provenance is selected or constructed".
- **input**, L109: "A port \(v\) is an **input** under \(A\) when \(A\) contains an edit that sets \(v\) directly."
- **components and their relations**, L91: "For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies"; and L375's first sentence: "a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated".
- **route of a candidate, kept apart**, L299: "(not an active route of a history, Part IX)"; L307: "A route of the candidate is a route whether or not any history runs it; it is active in a system when occurrences that realize it form an active route (Part IX), a subnetwork of actual occurrences in the system's history."
- **what the text says it represents**, L307: "Two systems with the same output table may differ in which routes are active; the semantics represents the difference (Argument 9)." L23: "Two systems with identical outputs and different internal routes are different organizations here, and the semantics says so (Argument 9)." L616: "If \(M_0,M_1\) have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both." and "Parallel and priority wiring are an instance."
- **its uses**: reason use, L385: "and lands on an active route"; construction by use, L409: "and lie on an active route, as reason use asks of an objection (Part IX)"; ProducedBy, L441: "\(\operatorname{ProducedBy}\) is met when an active route (Part IX) runs from \(\Delta\) to the repair; it attributes the repair to each contribution whose active route ran to it in the history, and where two sufficient contributions both ran, the repair is attributed to both and the history supplies no division of the attribution that it does not contain."; ProducesVia, L453: "\(\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')\) is met when an active route that runs from \(\Delta\) to the repair of \(o\) contains the relevant binding of \(c\)."; the dependence order, L526: "and ProducedBy on histories and their active routes".

## What the sentence does

1. **It defines the history-side route, apart from a candidate's route.** Part VI's route is a member of \(\mathsf S_{E,p}\), a set of commitments (L299); this sentence's route is made of "actual occurrences" in a history. L307 joins the two ("it is active in a system when occurrences that realize it form an active route"), and says the join is made in ProducedBy, "not to the routes a candidate contains".
2. **Its four clauses each carry a part of what the uses need.** "Connected" and "joining a represented input to an operative result": the route runs, by actual occurrences, from something represented to a result in effect, so that "runs from \(\Delta\) to the repair" (L441) and "contains the relevant binding of \(c\)" (L453) have something to be met by. "Whose components meet the applicable relations": the occurrences behave as the interpretation's relations for them say (L91; L375's first sentence). "Which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts": the route, taken as a route from the input to the result, carries the distinction: under the declared contrasts, what passes along it is not the same whatever the distinction is.
3. **The dependence clause is a property of the route, not of the result.** The grammar puts it there: "a connected subnetwork …, whose components meet … and which has nonconstant dependence …": "whose" and "which" both hang on "subnetwork". That is what lets two routes to one result differ in being active, which the text says it represents (L307, L23, L616), and it is what the next clause of L375 says: "whether a route is active is read from the history, not from the result."
4. **"Under the declared contrasts" gives "nonconstant" its range.** The dependence is across the alternatives the declared contrasts set out, as L255's contrast is across pairs of \(C\) ("There exist \((a,b)\in C\) and a nonempty block \(G\subseteq\Gamma\) such that"), and not, say, change over time within the one history, which almost any running route shows.

## The readers, weighed

The number of readers on a side decides nothing; each point is weighed on its argument.

### Mimo's rival (reply line 72; closing line, reply line 95)

> An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components meet the applicable relations and whose result varies with the represented distinction under the declared contrasts.

Mimo's ground (reply line 75): "\"has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction\" becomes \"whose result varies with the represented distinction\": the same property, now bound to the operative result the definition names."

The ground itself names the change: the property is "now bound to the operative result". It is not the same property. In the text, what depends nonconstantly on the distinction is the subnetwork (point 3); in the rival, it is the result. The result is one occurrence, and every route that joins the input to it shares it. So the rival's clause is met or unmet for every route to one result alike, and whether it is met is a matter of which result goes with which value of the distinction, that is, of the input–result pairing. That is where the rival breaks what the text says:

- **Priority wiring (L616, L307).** Two systems with the same output table: in one, two routes run in parallel from the input to the result; in the other, the second route is gated off while the first has priority. The gated route is connected, made of actual occurrences (the gate and its connections are there, "the connections actually instantiated"), joins the input to the result, and its components meet their relations (a closed gate passes nothing, as its relation says). Only the dependence clause can mark it not active. On the text's wording it is not: what passes along it is the same whatever the distinction. On the rival's wording it is: the shared result varies with the distinction (through the first route). The rival would then count the same routes active in both systems, against L307: "Two systems with the same output table may differ in which routes are active; the semantics represents the difference (Argument 9)." It would also read the dependence clause from the result, against L375's own last clause: "whether a route is active is read from the history, not from the result."
- **Preemption and two sufficient routes (L441; cases O25, O20, O39).** In O25, "Ana closes valve A at ten o'clock exactly. Ben closes valve B one second later." Fixed verdict: "Ana stopped it. Ben's act would have stopped it and did not, because there was nothing left to stop when it came." On a contract whose contrasts vary Ana's act while Ben's stays as it was, the result at the declared grain (the leak stops) does not vary with Ana's distinction, since Ben's valve would have stopped it; so the rival does not count Ana's route active, Ben's is excluded by the history (L375's third sentence), and ProducedBy (L441) attributes the repair to no one, against the verdict. On the text's wording, Ana's route, from her act through valve A to the flow, varies with her act under that very contrast, so it is active and the verdict stands. In O39 ("Either alone would have stopped all of the flow. Both operated fully."; fixed verdict: "the leak was stopped by both, and neither can claim it alone") and in O20 ("either valve alone would have stopped it"; fixed verdict: "Both did something that would have stopped it. Neither can claim it alone.") the same kind of contract, holding the other valve as it was, gives neither route active on the rival's wording, so that ProducedBy attributes the repair to neither: against O39's "stopped by both", against O20's "Both did something", and against L441's "where two sufficient contributions both ran, the repair is attributed to both". On the rival's wording, whether either route is active turns on whether the declared contrasts happen to take the other route away; on the text's, it does not.

So the rival is **not a rewording that changes nothing the theory needs**. It is a different claim: a condition on the result where the text has a condition on the route. Weighed as a challenge, it shows no defect in the text's wording; taken, it would drop the difference L307 says the semantics represents and could move the verdicts of O25, O20 and O39. Not taken. What is plainer in the rival, "varies with" for "has nonconstant dependence on", does not come apart from the change of subject in Mimo's wording; this checker's attempts to keep the plain verb with the route as subject are below.

### Mimo's step 3 (reply line 93)

Mimo: "What a reader might take it to mean: that the \"is\" is exhaustive, which would clash with the exclusion of a route \"already at rest when the result occurred\". The text forces no exhaustiveness — compare L231's \"meets \(\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\) exactly when\" — so the exclusion is read inside the definition."

Mimo's conclusion (no failure) holds; its ground does not. A sentence of the form "An **active route** is …", with the term in bold, is a definition, and a definition gives the conditions exactly; that L231 says "exactly when" in words does not make other definitions of the text looser (L109's "A port \(v\) is an **input** under \(A\) when" is of the same form). The clash does not arise for another reason: the two routes the third sentence excludes fail the definition's own clauses when these are read in the history, which is what "read from the history" asks.

- **A route that started and did no work** (case O52: "Ana's bypass jams a quarter open and takes nothing"): past the jam nothing passes, so what the route delivers to the result is the same whatever the distinction; the dependence clause is not met, and the jammed route does not join the input to the result by actual occurrences.
- **A route already at rest when the result occurred**: none of its occurrences is going on when the result occurs, so it does not join the input to that result by occurrences in the history, and the result's occurrence does not depend on the distinction through it; "joining … to an operative result" and the dependence clause are read for that result. The relativity to a result is in the definition already ("joining a represented input to an operative result"); "not active for that result" uses it. A route that ran earlier and whose later occurrences carry on up to the result (a fire lit by a match that has since gone out) is not at rest when the result occurs, taken as a route from the input to the result.

So the third sentence reads as the definition applied to two kinds of history, which is how the S81 determination records the place (M31: "CLAIM (application and new)"). Neither reader shows a route that meets every clause and is yet excluded by the third sentence. Were one shown, the fault would lie in the pair of sentences, and the third is not under review. Recorded; nothing proposed.

## This checker's own attempts

- `… and which varies with the represented distinction under the declared contrasts.` Keeps the route as subject with Mimo's plain verb. It reads as though the subnetwork itself changed (its membership) with the distinction. Less plain, not more. Not taken.
- `… and whose occurrences vary with the represented distinction under the declared contrasts.` A different claim. Read as "some occurrence", it is met by the represented input itself, which is an occurrence of the route and varies with what it represents, so the clause would do no work; read as "every occurrence", it is too strong, since a route may run through occurrences that are the same under every contrast (a fixed pipe). The text's "which has nonconstant dependence" is said of the route taken as a route from the input to the result. Not taken.
- `… and which carries the represented distinction to the result under the declared contrasts.` Close to what the clause means (point 2), but "carries" is the text's word for what an occurrence is ("a physically located carrier", L169) and invites a reading of content carried with fidelity, as in (R), which the clause does not ask. Not clearer. Not taken.
- `… and which does not have constant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts.` A rewording that changes nothing the theory needs. Longer, not clearer. Recorded, not taken.
- `… joining a represented input to a result, …` (dropping "operative"). A different claim: any occurrence downstream of the input, an idle by-product included, would count as the route's result, and reason use ("lands on an active route", L385) and construction by use ("lie on an active route", L409) could be met by a map landing on a route that ends in nothing in effect. Not taken.
- `… joining an input to an operative result, … on the distinction under the declared contrasts.` (dropping "represented" twice). Every wording that changes the claim either moves the dependence off the route (Mimo's rival), lets the clause do no work (some occurrence varies), or widens where a route may start or end; the rewordings that change nothing it needs are not clearer. So, on this reading, the sentence is not easy to vary; the rewording is recorded.
