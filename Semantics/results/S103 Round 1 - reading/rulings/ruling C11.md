## What the sentence says, in the text's terms

**Where it stands.** It is the second of five sentences on L127, closing the section "Kinds are edit-signatures" (L111–L127). The section defines a component's signature on a contract (K), kinds as equivalence classes of signatures (L119), and then:

> What ordinary language calls a cause, a measurement, a rule or a constitutive status are families of signatures: (L121)

> - a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits; (L123)

L127 then reads, sentence by sentence: "These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data. The semantics never asks whether a component "is" a cause. It asks what its signature is." and goes on to the measurement's signature and "Which of the two an account offers as producing an outcome is fixed by that signature, not by the account's wording."

**"component".** The defined term: "\(J\) indexes components; each component \(j\) has a footprint \(V_j\subseteq V\)." (L91). Signatures are signatures of components: "The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is" (L113). The sentence's "component" is what C12's "its signature" needs.

**"never asks".** The text says what the semantics or its definition asks, in three places, and this sentence is one of them:

- L11: "The definition never asks whether a piece of the explanation "is the same kind of thing" as a piece of the world."
- L37: "That is a difference in edit-response, which is what the semantics asks about (Part II, "Kinds are edit-signatures")."
- L127.s2–s3: this sentence and "It asks what its signature is."

L37 points at this section by its heading for what the semantics asks about; C11 and C12 are the place in the section that says it in so many words.

**"is", in quotation marks, and "cause", without.** The text does tell a cause apart. L37: "A correlation has no component that responds to an intervention on its supposed input; a cause does." L57: "a cause's assignment changes under intervention." L121–L123 make the ordinary word a family of signatures. So the semantics does, in effect, find which components have a causal assignment's signature. What it never asks is a further question: whether a component *is* a cause over and above that signature. The quotation marks on "is" mark that sense of "is", the one L11 disowns for kinds ("a *kind* is nothing over and above how a component responds to the changes that level admits"). "Cause" is left as the ordinary word L121 maps to a family, and the text uses it that way at L37, L57 and L121. L57 says what is denied: "What it denies is that the difference is available before the admitted changes are fixed, or independently of them."

**What it does in the text.**

- With C12 it states, of causes, what L11 states of kinds, and what L31, L526 and L600 state of the vocabulary:
  - L31, excerpt: "No undefined predicate that says "explains" without a question and a contract, or "is a cause", is taken as an import, and no definition depends on one (Argument 6)"
  - L526, excerpt: "Nothing depends on an undefined predicate that says "explains" without a question and a contract, or "is a cause""
  - L600, excerpt: "There is no residual, undefined predicate meaning "explains," "represents," or "is a cause""
  
  Those lines say the semantics has no such predicate. This sentence says it asks no such question, and C12 says what it asks instead. The three are not one claim: a text could lack the predicate and still pose the question in words; C11 says it does not.
- It gives C12 its antecedents: "It" is the semantics, "its" the component.
- It answers grievances 1, 9 and 11 at the place L37 and L57 point to: "(Part II, "Kinds are edit-signatures")" (L37), "(Part II)" (L57).
- It carries L127.s5 ("fixed by that signature, not by the account's wording"): what an account calls a component fixes nothing about which one produces the outcome, because the question the semantics asks is the signature's.

```

```

- "never asks" is kept.
- "an "is a cause" question of a component" names the same question as "whether a component "is" a cause": the question whether a component is a cause, set apart by quotation marks from what the text says of causes at L37, L57 and L121–L123.

**So the rival is a rewording that changes nothing the theory needs from the sentence,** and the sentence is easy to vary on this reading. That is recorded.

**It is not clearer, so the ruling is KEEP.** Why the text's wording and not the rival:

- **The L11 frame.** L11 reads "The definition never asks whether a piece of the explanation "is the same kind of thing" as a piece of the world." The text's sentence repeats the frame "never asks whether … "is …"" word for word; the rival keeps "never asks" and drops "whether". Both readers treat the echo of L11 as something the sentence carries (Mimo line 250, GLM line 243). The rival buys an echo of L31/L526/L600 at the price of the echo of L11, and L31, L526 and L600 are about the vocabulary, while L11 and this sentence are about what is asked.
- **The contrast with C12.** In the text, the scare-quoted "is" of "whether a component "is" a cause" stands against the plain "is" of "what its signature is". The one question it disowns and the one it asks are both "is" questions, and the marks say which "is" is disowned. The rival's marks enclose the predicate, and that contrast goes.
- **Plainness.** "Asks whether a component "is" a cause" is plain English. "Asks an "is a cause" question of a component" puts a quoted predicate in front of a noun and needs "of" to attach it. It says the same thing less plainly.
- **"Cause" at arm's length.** Quoting the whole predicate also puts "cause" in quotation marks, and the text uses "cause" plainly as the ordinary word L121 maps to a family (L37, L57, L121). The text's wording marks only the "is" it disowns and leaves "cause" as it uses it elsewhere. This is a small point, and a reader could take the rival's marks as mention of L600's predicate, which is harmless.

None of these makes the rival fail. Each is a reason not to change a wording that the rival does not improve on.

## Variations tried here

Each stands or falls on what it breaks (S20).

- **"The semantics never asks whether a component "is a cause"."** This keeps "never asks whether" and moves only the closing mark. It is another rewording that changes nothing the theory needs, and the sentence is easy to vary on this reading too. It keeps the L11 frame and quotes the predicate as L31, L526 and L600 do. It is not clearer: it loses the "is"/"is" contrast with C12 and puts "cause" at arm's length (both as above). No FIX.
- **"The semantics never asks what a component is."** This breaks L119 and C12. The semantics does say what a component is on a contract, namely of which kind: "Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one kind on \(C\)** when …" (L119), and C12 asks "what its signature is". The denial is of one question, not of every "is".
- **"The semantics never asks whether a component "is" a causal assignment."** This breaks L123. A causal assignment is a signature pattern ("a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port …", L123); asking whether a component has it is asking about its signature, which is what C12 says the semantics asks.
- **"… whether a part "is" a cause."** This loosens the tie to (K). Signatures are defined for components (L113), and C12's "its signature" needs a component. L127.s4's "a part that reads or reports another part" is a gloss on a signature pattern, not a replacement for the defined term.
- **Dropping the sentence.** C12 would lose its antecedents ("It asks what its signature is." would follow "These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data.", so "It" would have no singular antecedent, and "its" none). L37's pointer "what the semantics asks about (Part II, "Kinds are edit-signatures")" would still be met by C12 alone, once repaired, but the section would no longer say what the semantics does not ask, which grievances 1 and 11 turn on (L37, L57). The sentence is not idle.

"Never" carries the denial across every contract and grain; the marks on "is" keep it from contradicting L37 and L123; "component" ties it to (K). Each part does work.
