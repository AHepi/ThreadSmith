## Weighing the wordings

Each wording is weighed on what it keeps and what it breaks; none is weighed by who offered it or how many were offered.

- **"It does define the classes."** (Mimo, the closing rival.) It makes the same claim: the auxiliary "does" adds emphasis and nothing else. It keeps the contrast, the pointer to L528 and the agreement with L632. So Mimo's point holds: on what the theory needs from the sentence, this wording works as well, and the sentence is easy to vary on this reading, in the brief's plain sense; that is recorded here. It is not clearer. The emphatic auxiliary reads as answering a doubt that nothing in the line raises (L27's first sentence already speaks of "the classes defined"), and it can be read as a concession, "it does not decide that, but it does at least define these". It also breaks the plain pattern of its own section, where L21 answers "It does not define reference in terms of uninterpreted matter" with the plain "It defines reference in terms of matter **plus a selection or construction history**". Rule 5 then gives KEEP.
- **"It defines the classes and their membership conditions."** (Mimo, line 11.) It says nothing the theory needs that the text's wording does not: at L528 the membership statements are the definitions of the classes, so defining a class is stating what belonging to it takes. As written, the conjunction presents the conditions as a second thing defined beside the classes, which misdescribes L528 slightly, and it lengthens the line for no gain. A rewording that changes nothing the theory needs; not clearer.
- **"It defines the class."** (Mimo, line 16, offered as breaking.) Mimo's point holds within L27: the antecedent in the same line is plural ("the classes defined"), and L528 defines five classes. The singular is not idle in the text (L3, L513, L532 and L632 speak of "the class" the semantics is), but in L27 the singular would no longer answer the plural of the first sentence, and it would leave out the five classes of L528 whose membership is what the first sentence declines to decide.
- **"It decides the classes' membership."** (Mimo, line 20, offered as breaking.) Mimo's point holds: it contradicts the first sentence of the same line ("It does not decide whether any human, machine, institution or lineage belongs to the classes defined") and L632 ("It is not a claim that any actual infant, animal, or program instantiates it."), and it goes against L8, where whether a candidate meets the conditions of an account depends on the candidate, its question and its target, not on anyone's view of them.
- **"It fixes what the classes are."** (GLM, line 8.) GLM's point holds, with one qualification. In the text's own idiom "fixed by" means determined by (L127, L151, L269, L277, L315), so the rival's content is not far from defining. But in L27 the verb stands against "decide", and in ordinary use to fix something (a date, a price) is to decide it; so the contrast the sentence exists to draw is blurred, and the verb leans toward settling, which S28 excludes ("A theory is never settled."). It also loses the tie to "what is defined" (L29) and "Everything else is defined" (L520), which "defines" keeps. A different claim, and not one that works as well.
- **"It says what the classes are."** (GLM, line 14.) GLM's point holds: "says what the classes are" is weaker than "defines", since it no longer places the classes among what the document defines in the dependence order (L29, L520, L526). A different claim, and not one that works as well.
- **"It defines them."** (GLM, line 20.) GLM's objection, the lost echo, is one of emphasis, not of content: "them" can only take "the classes" as antecedent, since "any human, machine, institution or lineage ... belongs" is grammatically singular. On content this is a rewording that changes nothing the theory needs. It is not clearer: repeating "the classes" shuts off a hasty reading of "them" as the humans, machines, institutions and lineages just named, a reading the whole line exists to exclude.

## The sentence itself

- **Does it do work?** Mimo's fault check says its work is a role, not new content, because "the classes defined" already attributes definition to the document. That does not show an idle part. The participle "defined" in the first sentence has no agent; read alone, it leaves open that the classes are defined somewhere else. C01 supplies the agent, this document, which L520 and L528 state more exactly (L35). And a section headed "What this document does not claim" (L19) needs to say what the document does, or the denial of deciding membership could be read as a disclaimer of any claim about the classes at all; every other paragraph of the section (L21, L23, L25) does the same. Dropping the sentence would lose both. Mimo itself claims no failure.
- **Contradiction, circularity, vacuity, conflict with a definition:** none found. The classes are defined at L528 in terms of (G), (EX), (RC) and (U3), not in terms of this sentence; the sentence conflicts with nothing in the dependence order (L526); and it agrees with L632 and L8 on the side it declines.
- **The owner's decisions.** "defines" is no word S23 forbids, and nothing in the sentence implies verification, settling, belief or ranking; it is not about what must happen to a candidate (S21); it holds no list, count or grade (S20); it does not bring in physical possibility (S25–S27; the physical realization in L528 is a condition of the class, not of this sentence); it settles nothing (S28).

## Ruling

**KEEP.**

- line: 27

**Between the readers.** Mimo's point holds on one thing: "It does define the classes." works as well for what the theory needs, so the sentence is easy to vary on this reading, and so, on content, is GLM's own "It defines them.". GLM's point holds on another: every wording that changes the verb or the object ("fixes", "says", "the class", "decides") either blurs the define/decide contrast, loses the place of the classes among what the document defines, or contradicts the line's first sentence and L632. No wording offered is clearer than the text's: the emphatic rival breaks the section's plain pattern (L21) and can read as a concession, the longer rival misdescribes L528, and "them" opens a misreading that "the classes" shuts off.

**What depends on the sentence.** Nothing in the text quotes or cites it (checked by search). The first sentence of L27, "It does not decide whether any human, machine, institution or lineage belongs to the classes defined.", depends on it in one respect: C01 gives the agentless "the classes defined" its agent, this document. With a KEEP nothing changes for either.
