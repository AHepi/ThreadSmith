## The sentence and where it stands

> Two responses to a violation are distinguished. A **selection response** extends the history \(H\) of a selected transport and lets \(\mu\) act: the transport is re-tuned within the population. A **construction response** introduces a new organization or a new transport with a construction trace. Only the second can be originative under Part X.

What the sentence does: it opens the two definitions that follow on the same line, and it says of them only that they are two and that they are told apart. ## Is the rival a rewording or a different claim?

A rewording that changes nothing the theory needs. Checked point by point:

- **Terms.** "two", "responses", "violation", "distinguish" are all kept; "the semantics" is the text's own name for itself, used as an agent throughout (for example L13, "the semantics keeps them apart"; L127, "The semantics never asks whether a component "is" a cause."; L584).
- **Exhaustiveness.** Neither wording says there are no other responses. "The semantics distinguishes two responses" singles out two, as "Two responses … are distinguished" does. Neither claims what must happen (S21) or closes anything (S28; L429, "Closing an episode is a choice").
- **Responder.** Neither names who responds; the definitions that follow name none either ("A **selection response** extends the history \(H\) …").
- **Dependents.** The two definitions on L225, C20's "the second" (fixed by the order of the two definitions, as Mimo's C20 step 3 says), L584's "The two responses" and L626's "Two responses." read the same after either wording.
- **Grounds.** Neither wording gives the grounds on which the two are told apart; L584 gives them in both cases.

So the rival does not make a different claim, and it is not weighed as a challenge.

## Is the rival clearer?

No, not in a way that changes what a reader takes from L225. For the rival: it names the distinguisher and so ties L225 to L584 at once, which removes the question "distinguished by whom?" (Mimo's step 3). Against its being clearer:

1. The question is answered by the text where it matters. The two sentences that follow are definitions in the text's own voice (bold terms), which is how a reader of a definitional section takes an agentless "are distinguished"; and L584 names the distinguisher together with the grounds ("by their traces, not by their outcomes").
2. The passive is the text's register for the same kind of sentence: L77, "They are distinguished by their histories."; L13, "The three are told apart by their histories, not their outputs". Changing L225 alone would make it differ from L77 without making it say anything else.
3. The active would state at L225 half of L584's clause ("the semantics distinguishes them") without its grounds, so the reader meets the same assertion twice and the grounds only the second time. That is no fault, but it is not a gain in clarity either.
4. No reader showed a misreading the passive produces. The one misreading Mimo considers ("that both responses are open for every violation") it answers itself from L225's "of a selected transport", and the rival would leave that reading exactly where the passive leaves it.

The rival is safe: applied, it would break nothing found here and move no case. Whether to prefer it for its tie to L584 is a choice for the person choosing (S21); on the reading rule's test ("FIX only if the rival is clearer"), it is not clearer, so KEEP.

## The variations that change the claim, and what each breaks

These are the wordings the readers tried that do change what the sentence says. Each is weighed here on its own argument; none is a repair of a fault in the sentence, and none is taken.

- `Two kinds of response to a violation are distinguished.` (Mimo, reply line 167). "Kind" is a term of the text with its own sense: L39, "A *kind* is what a level's admitted changes can distinguish."; L111, "## Kinds are edit-signatures"; L245, "there is no further condition about kinds to state (Argument 1)"; L558, "The word "kind" is therefore eliminable from the definition of an account". The two responses are told apart by their traces (L584), not by edit-signatures, so "kinds" would put the technical term where it does not belong. The break is real.
- `There are two ways to respond to a violation.` (Mimo, line 172) and `There are two responses to a violation.` (GLM, line 155). Both read as saying that a violation has these responses and no others. The text gives no argument for that, and it would say what happens after every violation, against S21 ("Notice I never once claimed what must happen."; "If the person decides the problem is a low priority, then this whole process may be abandoned.") and L429 ("Closing an episode is a choice"). Doing nothing, or leaving the episode to fade, is left open by the current wording and closed by these. The break is real.
- `A violation admits two responses.` (Mimo, line 177). "Admit" is a term of the text for edits and contracts: L141, "the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs the claim ranges over"; L257, "every edit the target admits". Using it of a violation collides with that sense, and "admits two" also leans towards the exhaustive reading above. The break is real.
- `A violation demands one of two responses.` (GLM, line 161). It says what must happen, against S21 ("Notice I never once claimed what must happen.") and L429 ("no argument makes the choice"). The break is real.
- `The two responses are learning and creating.` (GLM, line 167). It names the responses by their outcomes at the place they are defined, against L584, "the semantics distinguishes them by their traces, not by their outcomes."; and "The two responses" presupposes that there are exactly two. The break is real.

What the variations show between them: every change tried that alters what the sentence claims (its exhaustiveness, its modality, its vocabulary, or its grounds) meets a named line of the text or the owner's words; the one change that meets none, the rival, alters only the voice.

## Failures of the sentence itself

None found. Sought:

- **Exhaustiveness.** The sentence does not claim these are the only responses (see above); S21 and L429 are kept.
- **Vacuity.** It is not idle: it opens the pair that C20 ("Only the second can be originative under Part X."), L584 and L626 refer back to, and without it the two definitions would stand with nothing saying they are a pair to be told apart.

L223 defines violation "for every transport to the simulation layer", and L225 gives the selection response only for "a selected transport"; the sentence says two responses are told apart, not that both are available at every violation (Mimo's step 3 reaches the same point).
