## What the sentence says, in the text's terms

**"These".** The sentence follows L121 and its three bullets:

> What ordinary language calls a cause, a measurement, a rule or a constitutive status are families of signatures: (L121)

"These" takes in the bullets (L123–L125), each a description of how a signature responds to kinds of edit. L121 names four ordinary words for three bullets; a constitutive status is a status under a rule, and L347 gives its pattern in the rule-application form: "Its signature under (K) is invariant under interventions on \(Z\) and variable under edits to \(C_r\)." So every one of the four is covered, and each by a description of a pattern.

**"patterns in (K)".** "(K)" is the text's name for the signature, as in L520 ("Kinds, from signatures (K).") and L347 above. A signature is built from relations, not values: "A signature is built from a component's relation under each \((a,b)\in C\), not from the values its ports take in a solution" (L119). A pattern in (K) is thus a way a component's relation behaves across the pairs of \(C\).

**"not additional data".** "Data" in this text is what an interpretation supplies:

- L91: "For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies" the relation \(L_j(a,b)\) of L94.
- L528: "The base class: interpretations supplying these data with typing as declared".

"Not additional data" says that an interpretation supplies nothing beyond those relations for a component to count as a causal assignment, a measurement or a rule application: which family a component's signature falls in is read off the relations already supplied, on the contract. "Additional" ties the contrast to what is already supplied.

**What it does in the text.** The sentence is to the families what L107–L109 are to roles and L203 to representation:

- L107: "## Roles are defined, not supplied"; L109: "No role assignment is supplied."
- L203: "## Representation is defined, not supplied"

It is what lets the sentences after it, and some before it, use the families as defined patterns and not as supplied labels:

- L109 defines an observation through a family: "when the component assigning it has a measurement's signature (below)." That is consistent with "No role assignment is supplied" only if a measurement's signature is itself a pattern read from (K), which is what C10 says.
- L127.s2–s3: "The semantics never asks whether a component "is" a cause. It asks what its signature is."
- L127.s5: "Which of the two an account offers as producing an outcome is fixed by that signature, not by the account's wording."
- L37: "A declared label adds no discriminating power", and L57's "That is a difference in edit-signature (Part II)", both answer grievances by pointing to this section.
- L265: "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in \(C\). None inspects a label."
