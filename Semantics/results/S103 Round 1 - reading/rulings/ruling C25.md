## The sentence and where it stands

L385 (whole line):

> **Reason use.** A response uses a reason when a structural map from the represented objection into the response suborganization preserves role bindings, sends content-preserving recodings to the same transition, sends content changes to the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule, and lands on an active route. Using an objection does not give it bearing (K1) or make any argument from it usable (K2).

**The terms it uses, and where the text gives them.**

- **Active route**, L375: "An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components meet the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts." And: "A route that started and did no work, or that was already at rest when the result occurred, is not active for that result; whether a route is active is read from the history, not from the result."
- **Represented**, (R) at L205: "An occurrence \(o\) **represents** content \(c\) at grain \(\ell\) when there is a transport from the organization that \(o\) instantiates under the physical module, at grain \(\ell\), to \(c\), faithful on \(c\)'s contract, whose provenance is selected or constructed". The objection is represented as L383 says a criticism is: "An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents it as the premise of a criticism alleging a defect in a target."
- **Role bindings** of an objection: the roles of a criticism, L377: "A criticism has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), premise \(g\), and a connection." Roles in general are the text's own (L107, "## Roles are defined, not supplied"; L109, "No role assignment is supplied.").
- **Content**, L169: "A **content** is an organization together with its contract-relative commitments."
- **Content-preserving recoding**, L365: "A declared, invertible recoding of a carrier preserves the content when a reader who applies the declared convention recovers every pairing (Argument 8)."
- **Structural**: the text's word for what is read in the terms of an organization and not of strings: L413, "The relation is structural at the stated grain, not string equality or similarity"; L397, "read structurally as non-circular dependence reads identity (Part V) and not by logical equivalence alone"; L520, "the structural vocabulary of (O) and (Q)". "Structural map" itself occurs only at L385.
- **Rule**: a component with a rule application's signature, L125: "a **rule application** has a signature invariant under interventions on the world and variable under edits to the rule."; L103: "A changed rule is a changed component." **Operative**: in operation, as at L375 ("an operative result"), L487 ("the result can affect its operative use") and L606 ("takes over operative use"). "Deliberative" occurs only at L385.
- **Transition**: a step of a process, as (T1) writes one at L358 (`z\xrightarrow{a}z'`); "transition" occurs only at L385 and L409.
- **Suborganization**: part of an organization, as at L343 ("every intermediate product is a determinant suborganization whose hidden ports project away").

**What the sentence does.** It says when a response uses an objection as a reason, in terms of the response's own organization and history: the objection, as represented, is carried into the part of the system that makes the response, with its roles kept (clause 1); a change of wording that keeps the content leaves the response where it was (clause 2); a change of content moves the response as the rule the system is operating by in its deliberation lays down (clause 3); and all this ran, in the history (clause 4). It gives an exact form to L429's "a content-sensitive response" ("A complete critical episode contains a recognized difficulty, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a content-sensitive response."). L409 identifies a binding constructed in a subhistory by the same asks: "by responses that preserve its role bindings, send content-preserving recodings to the same transition and content changes to the changes the binding specifies, and lie on an active route, as reason use asks of an objection (Part IX)." The line's second sentence then separates use from bearing and from usability.

**Where they differ, and the ruling between them.**

1. *The closing lines.* They do not conflict. GLM tried drops and replacements, each of which changes what the sentence asks; it tried no wording that keeps every clause. Mimo's rival keeps every clause. GLM's HOLDS records that its own variations broke something, and nothing in GLM's section bears on Mimo's rival. The question is only whether Mimo's rival changes anything the theory needs, and whether it is clearer; both are weighed below.
2. *What L409 matches.* GLM reads L409 as paraphrasing every clause of L385; Mimo reads its third item as a different specifier in a different context. On the text, Mimo's reading of the third item stands, and GLM's holds of the form. L409 carries reason use over from an objection used by a response to a binding identified by its use, and its "as reason use asks of an objection (Part IX)" says that the asks are alike. Three items carry L385's clauses over word for word or nearly: "preserve its role bindings", "send content-preserving recodings to the same transition", "lie on an active route" ("lie" because responses, not the map, are now the subject, as GLM says). The third changes its specifier: L385 has "the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule", L409 "the changes the binding specifies". That is not a slip in either line. In a construction trace the constructed binding is itself the new organization that lays down how content changes go on (L628's example: "the binding of a persistence component to a continuity subnetwork"); in reason use the objection is what comes in, and what lays down the response's changes is the rule the system is deliberating by. So the two lines are not to be made word for word alike: putting "the binding" into L385, or "the rule" into L409, would be a different claim (Mimo's reply line 152 is the first of these; weighed below). The difference between the readers changes no drop's outcome: each drop from L385 leaves L409 asking of a binding's use something reason use would no longer ask of an objection, so L409's "as reason use asks" would misdescribe reason use in that clause. On that both readers are right.

## Is Mimo's rival a rewording or a different claim?

What the theory needs from the sentence:

1. a map from the objection **as represented** into the part of the response's organization that makes the response (L383's second sentence; L429's "when the system represents it");
2. that the map be **structural**, read in the organization's terms and not in the wording (L413, L397), which is what lets the text say, in S96's reading of N13, that "The parrot's responses follow wording, not content (the change list's reading, through reason use, l. 385)";
3. each of the four clauses (each drop breaks something; below);
4. the existential reading: some such map exists;
5. clauses that L409 can point back to ("as reason use asks of an objection (Part IX)");
6. the specifier of the response's changes is the operative deliberative rule, not the objection.

Mimo's rival keeps 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 word for word or in active voice ("the changes the operative deliberative rule specifies" for "the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule", with the same content). It keeps each of L409's asks (5), though not their order. It adds nothing and removes nothing the sentence claims. **A rewording.** The sentence is easy to vary on this reading, and that is recorded.

## Is the rival clearer?

**For it.** "there is … that" states the existential in the form the text gives its most formal existential definitions: L205 ("when there is a transport"), L119 ("Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one kind on \(C\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which"), L413 ("Write \(d\equiv_\ell c\) when there are transports from \(d\) to \(c\) and from \(c\) to \(d\)"). The last clause's active voice is a little shorter.

**Against it.**

1. **The existential is not in doubt in the text's wording.** The text uses the same form, an indefinite subject in the "when" clause, for existential definitions elsewhere: L441, "\(\operatorname{ProducedBy}\) is met when an active route (Part IX) runs from \(\Delta\) to the repair"; L453, "\(\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')\) is met when an active route that runs from \(\Delta\) to the repair of \(o\) contains the relevant binding of \(c\)." A universal reading of L385 ("every structural map from the represented objection into the response suborganization …") would ask all such maps, including any that exchange the objection's roles, to meet the four clauses; reason use would then fail almost wherever it is claimed, and no reader takes it so. Both readers read the text existentially; Mimo's own word is that the existential is "made explicit" (line 139). So the change gives the reader nothing the reader lacks, and making L385 match L205 would unmatch it from L441 and L453.
2. **The rival opens an attachment the text's syntax lacks.** In the rival, the relative clause "that lands on an active route, preserves role bindings, …" follows the noun phrase "the response suborganization", and a reader can attach it there: the suborganization would land on an active route and preserve role bindings. "sends … to the same transition" shows the map is meant, but only at the third verb. In the text, "a structural map" is the grammatical subject of all four verbs, and no such attachment arises. (The same cost falls on a narrower third wording that adds only "there is … that" and keeps the text's order; it was weighed and not taken. A third wording that writes "some structural map" avoids the attachment, but its only gain is the existential of point 1, which is not in doubt; not taken.)
3. **The reorder breaks the order that L409 points back to.** L409 gives its asks in L385's order (role bindings, recodings, content changes, active route) and then says "as reason use asks of an objection (Part IX)". A reader who compares the two lines, as L409 invites, finds them item for item now; after the reorder the first item of L409 would answer to the second of L385, and so on.
4. **The text's order has a sense the rival's loses.** It goes from the map's structure (role bindings), through its behaviour under changes (recodings, content changes), to where it ran (an active route, which "is read from the history, not from the result", L375). The rival puts the history first and the structure after.

Weighed together, the rival is not clearer than the text's wording. What holds in the challenge is that the sentence can be reworded without loss, not that the text falls short; the smallest change that meets that is none. KEEP.

## The variations that change the claim, and what each breaks

Each is weighed on its own argument; none is a repair of a fault in the sentence, and none is taken.

- **Dropping "content-preserving"** (Mimo, reply line 144: `… sends recodings to the same transition …`). The break is real. The sharper ground is inside the sentence: a recoding that loses content (by L365, one from which a reader applying the declared convention does not recover every pairing) is also a content change, so clause 2 would send it to the same transition while clause 3 sends it to the change the rule specifies; the two clauses would clash on it. L409's "send content-preserving recodings to the same transition … as reason use asks" would then ask less than reason use asks. Mimo's ground that the drop "breaks L365" is looser than put: L365 is not contradicted; the variation loses the tie to it.
- **Dropping "preserves role bindings"** (GLM line 66; Mimo line 149, described). Real. A map that sends the objection's premise to what the response treats as its target, or its target to a premise, would count; the response would then be using a different criticism from the one represented, whose roles L377 fixes ("A criticism has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), premise \(g\), and a connection."). And L409's "preserve its role bindings … as reason use asks" would ask of a binding's use what reason use no longer asks.
- **Dropping "sends content-preserving recodings to the same transition"** (GLM line 66). Real. A response keyed to wording, one that treats a paraphrase of the objection as a different objection, would count as using it: the pattern S96 reads off N13 through this line ("The parrot's responses follow wording, not content"). L409's matching ask would lose its counterpart.
- **Dropping "sends content changes to the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule"** (GLM line 66). Real. A response that gives the same transition whatever the objection says, or moves in ways its rule does not lay down, would count; L429's "content-sensitive response" would lose its exact form; L409's "content changes to the changes the binding specifies" would have no counterpart in reason use.
- **Dropping "lands on an active route"** (GLM line 66; Mimo line 149, described). Real. Reason use would be read from structure alone, not from the history: a response organization that never ran, or a route "that started and did no work" (L375), would count, against L375's "whether a route is active is read from the history, not from the result"; and L409's "lie on an active route" would lose its counterpart.
- **"… the changes specified by the role bindings"** (Mimo, reply line 152). A different claim, and a real break. Mimo's ground, the division of labour between clauses 1 and 3, holds, and there is a sharper one. The changes an objection's own bindings lay down are those it alleges: the defect \(\delta\) in the target \(z\), repaired. Under the variation, only a response that goes the objection's way would use it; a response that takes the objection in and finds, say, that its premise is not live for the person (L393's \(\operatorname{Live}_j\)), and leaves the target as it was, would not count as using it. That makes using an objection all but conceding it, against L71 ("A criticism has a target and is itself conjectural.") and against the point of L385's second sentence, which keeps use apart from bearing. In the text's wording, the specifier is the system's own operative rule, itself open to criticism (L73: "Any aspect of a system's practice, a question, a contract, a transport, a method, an attention policy, can become a target, and the result must be able to change how the system proceeds."); under the variation, the objection would lay down the response. S21's "Whatever happens is always the choice of the person." sits with the text's wording and not with the variation.
- **"a correspondence of the objection to the response"** for the head (GLM line 67). A different claim, and a real loss: it drops "represented" (against L383's second sentence and L429's "when the system represents it"), drops "structural" (so a pairing by wording or by likeness of strings would do, against L413's "not string equality or similarity"), drops "suborganization", and brings in "correspondence", which is the text's word for the relation between a representation and what it represents, with a provenance (L13: "Correspondence between a representation and what it represents is not an import here. It is a **relation with a provenance**."; L77: "Selection and construction both produce correspondences."). GLM's own ground, L612, is looser than GLM puts it: L612 ("Transporting all carriers, relations, transports, histories, contracts and declared inputs along structure-preserving bijections preserves (E), (G), (P), (EX).") does not name reason use, which reaches (G) only through L409's identification of a constructed binding, and a relation defined over the transported data would be carried along the bijections whatever its name. The variation is not taken on the losses named here.
- **"lies on" for "lands on"** (GLM line 68). A rewording of one verb. Not clearer: in L385 the map is the subject, and "lands on" says where its image falls; in L409 responses are the subject and "lie on" is apt there, as GLM says. Neither verb is a defined term (the text's active-route verbs vary: "runs from", L441 and L453; "form an active route", L307). Not taken.

What the variations show between them: every change to the claim (a clause dropped, the specifier moved to the objection, the head loosened) meets a named line of the text; the changes that meet none (Mimo's rival, "lies on") change how the definition is worded and not what it asks.

## Failures of the sentence itself

None found. Sought:

- **"the same transition" (Mimo's step 3).** Not a fault. "the same" is comparative, and its comparison is given by the map's own argument: the map is "from the represented objection", so "the same transition" is the one the map sends the objection to, and a content-preserving recoding of the objection goes there too. That is the reading Mimo gives ("it reads as the transition the mapped content reaches"), and the next clause shows the same by contrast: recodings go to the same transition, content changes to changed ones. The other reading one might try, "the same as one another", comes to the same thing, since a declared recoding followed by its declared inverse (L365's recodings are "invertible") gives back the objection's own carrier. L409 uses the same words ("send content-preserving recodings to the same transition"); a repair of L385 alone would leave the two lines differently worded, with no fault shown in either.
- **"operative deliberative rule" and "structural map" (Mimo's step 3; GLM line 70 for the first).** Both readers say only that these are "not defined in the lines given", and neither calls it a failure; GLM finds "no conflict". In the whole of file 99, "deliberative" and "structural map" occur only at L385. Their parts are the text's own: "structural" (L413, L397, L520), "rule" (L125, L103), "operative" (L375, L487, L606); "map" is used in its ordinary mathematical sense. "The operative deliberative rule" reads as the rule (a component with a rule application's signature) in operation in the system's deliberation, that is, in its response to the objection; "deliberative" singles out, among the rules operative in the system, the one by which the response treats the objection. No case or definition found turns on anything "deliberative" adds beyond that. Not a fault.
- **Vacuity of clause 3.** If "the operative deliberative rule" were whatever the response in fact does, clause 3 would be met by any response. It is not: a rule is a component with its own signature (L125), and the clause asks that content changes of the objection go to the changes that component lays down; a response whose changes do not go through it (another route, or none) does not meet the clause. Not vacuous.
- **Circularity.** The definiens does not use "use of a reason": "operative" means in operation, not "used as a reason". Not circular.
- **The owner's words.** S23: "reason" and "uses" carry no forbidden word or idea; the reason is the represented objection, the sentence says nothing of a reason *for* a claim (L8: "An argument is never a reason *for* a claim"), and the second sentence denies that use gives bearing or usability. GLM's point here stands. S21: the sentence says what counts as use, not what must happen; the rule is the system's own and open to scrutiny (L73). S25 to S27: clause 4 places reason use in a history with a physical interpretation (L375), which is where the text lets physical possibility in (L75: "Physical possibility enters the semantics where a content is instantiated in a carrier or transformed"; S25: "instantiation and transformation of information and knowledge"); nothing about a question, an account or a conflict is tied to it. S28: the sentence settles nothing, and its line's second sentence says so of use. S20: no list, count or grade.
- **Contradiction.** With L409: the third item's specifier differs by design (ruled above). With L429: reason use gives "a content-sensitive response" its exact form. With L383: "the represented objection" is an adverse signal represented as the premise of a criticism. With L277 ("how it came to be taken up is assessed elsewhere (Part IX)"): that points to Part IX's taking up and liveness (L393), and reason use is apart from it, as the second sentence of L385 says. None found.
