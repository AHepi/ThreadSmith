**1. The load-bearing attack: file 11 makes the deciding matter a declared input the case leaves unstated (file 11 SILENT).** The fixed verdict's first sentence states the ground: "Ownership here rests on the log, which shows the routine running inside the robot." File 11 answers with a different criterion and an express disclaimer of that one. f11 L465: "a capability is attributed to a system under a declared boundary (which processes and resources are the system's) … Both are declared before the attribution, not chosen after it." f11 L419 s2: "where the boundary is drawn decides, not where the process sits in the casing." f11 L514: "A verdict that depends on one of these is a verdict given the input; where the input is missing, the verdict is unsettled and the semantics says so rather than choosing the input from the verdict wanted." A log records occurrences; it can show only where the routine ran — the casing fact — not a declared index. On this reading the theory takes the deciding matter as an input the case never states, so the fourth mark rule fits (SILENT) before the fifth (AGREE), and the row becomes AGREE→SILENT through M37, M44, M45 and M48, a theory change away that fails (b). The pressure is real: the determination's reason 2 answers with the placement fact itself ("the case names the system and places the process in it"), which is exactly what f11 L419 s2 disclaims; and its reason 4 ("At most the theory adds 'given that the robot's boundary includes the routine'") and the text-lens note ("the same hazard stands as at O30") show the ruling resting on a conditional. The attack nevertheless fails on the case's text. The contrary reading's premise is that "the case gives only the routine's physical location" (1C A). It does not. The case's first sentence states whose processes the work is: "A repair robot keeps a log of every comparison it runs." The boundary is defined at f11 L465 as "which processes and resources are the system's"; the case answers that definition in its own words — the system's processes are "every comparison it runs" — and the second sentence puts the routine's work inside that set: the log is "of every comparison it runs" and "shows which routine … produced today's diagnosis", so the routine's production is among the robot's runs and within the robot. The input is therefore stated, not missing; the fill comes from the case's words, not "from the verdict wanted", so f11 L514's prohibition is not engaged. Nor is f11 L419 s2's disclaimer: the ruling does not infer membership from "where the process sits in the casing" but from whose work the case says it is. The disclaimer has its work where a fact separates the two — f11 L419 s2's own first clause ("Work supplied from outside that boundary … remains an outside contribution however it is executed inside"), or the remote author and outside instruction the determination names at O30 and O41. O17 contains no such fact, which is the defensible core of reason 3. The ruling is close in one respect and I state the condition it turns on: had the case said only that a routine ran "inside the robot", with no "every comparison it runs", the attack would succeed — bare placement is what f11 L419 s2 refuses — and the row would fall to file 11 SILENT. Note also that the casing/boundary contrast is not a SPLIT candidate: the ambiguity is not in file 11's sentences (they are unambiguous that the drawn boundary decides) but in whether the case states the input, which is the question just answered.

**2. A second attack: on the point as the fixed verdict words it, file 11 gives a different finding (rule 2, DISAGREE).** The fixed verdict's first sentence is a "what counts as what" finding: the log's showing of the internal running is the ground of ownership. File 11's ground sentence is f11 L467 s3: "Ownership is grounded in the processes and resources the boundary includes, never in the capability being attributed: 'owned because it can, and can because owned' grounds neither" — and with f11 L419 s2's "not where the process sits in the casing", file 11 can be read as replacing the ground the fixed verdict states, hence reaching a different finding on that point. This too fails, on the words of f11 L467 s3. Its ground is "the processes and resources the boundary includes" — the processes themselves, i.e. the routine's running, the very fact the fixed verdict cites — with the boundary as qualifier. That is the fixed verdict's finding with "a qualification, a limit or a more exact wording" under "Same finding", and the qualifier coincides with the fixed verdict's "inside the robot" on the case's facts (point 1). The remainder of f11 L467 s3 reaches the fixed verdict's last sentence in both halves: "'owned because it can, and can because owned' grounds neither" is "Strike the manual's sentence and nothing is lost" (not necessary) and "keep only the manual's sentence and there is nothing" (not sufficient). The boundary qualifier is in any case a limit of the kind the theory marks on itself: f11 L516 ("Indices, not primitives", Part XIV: "Grain ℓ, boundary β, continuity Ω, and the contract C are declared indices. Every claim is relative to them…"). An index the case's facts fix is a limit under "Same finding"; an index the case leaves open is the missing input of the fourth rule. Point 1 is what separates the two, and it separates them here in favour of the limit.

**3. A third attack: f11 L419 s2 supports two readings of a maker-supplied routine (SPLIT).** The paragraph that carries the ownership ground also says "Work supplied from outside that boundary … remains an outside contribution however it is executed inside" and then "a process that runs inside the boundary is the system's own today whoever wrote it". A routine surfaced by "the maker's manual" is plausibly the maker's supplied work (first clause: an outside contribution, ownership denied — a different finding from the fixed verdict's affirmation); read by the second clause it is the robot's own. Two readings, different findings, and the text might seem to leave the choice open. The attack fails because the clauses carry opposite premises and one instruction. The first is premised on work whose source is outside the boundary, and its list ("a diagnosis, a decisive question, an instruction about what to read") marks contributions to an episode, credited to their source; the second is premised on a process that runs inside the boundary and expressly cancels authorship — "whoever wrote it" is written for exactly this case, an internally running routine with an external maker. Both reduce to the third clause: "where the boundary is drawn decides". On this case's facts the second clause applies and the first does not: the only external presence is the manual, which affirms ownership and is struck down on its ground, not on membership, and nothing in the case supplies the routine from outside any boundary. One reading, not two.
