**1. File 11 gives SILENT on the first point of the fixed verdict, "This is a legitimate narrowing."** That point is an evaluation of the restriction. File 11 speaks to such evaluations twice, and each time withholds them. f11 L161 s3: "What makes a restriction appropriate to the question asked is a substantive, criticizable part of the claim; the semantics records the restriction and supplies no rule that certifies it." f11 L514: "some claims take stated inputs that the semantics records and does not supply: … the scope of a contract and what makes a restriction appropriate (Part III) … A verdict that depends on one of these is a verdict given the input; where the input is missing, the verdict is unsettled…" What makes a restriction appropriate is an input "the semantics records and does not supply," and the semantics "supplies no rule that certifies" a restriction. The mark rules ask what the theory "finds on each point, by its own definitions and conditions," and SILENT applies where "its sentences stop short of deciding it." On this point they record the claim's ground and abstain. SILENT precedes AGREE in the rules' order, so if it fits, AGREE is not reached. **2. The ruling's bridge step has no source.** Its reason runs: "The verdict given it is that the restriction is appropriate." But f11 L514 *classifies* verdicts ("A verdict that depends on one of these is a verdict given the input"); it does not manufacture one. The verdict must come from the theory's definitions and conditions, and on restriction-appropriateness there is none (point 3). Part XI shows the design exactly: of the declared inputs O and P the theory says "Their declaration makes no claim that the aims are worth pursuing, and (P) does not rank alternatives" (Part XI, Repair paragraph, f11 the paragraph opening at L427). It issues repair verdicts *given* O and P and never a verdict on their merits. Restriction-appropriateness is the same kind of item. This answers the ruling's reductio ("would do no work for Part III"): the clause governs the verdicts the semantics *does* issue that depend on the scope item — e.g. f11 L161 s2's "An account at a stated scope answers the question asked at that scope," a verdict given the stated scope — and it forbids back-filling ("rather than choosing the input from the verdict wanted"). On the ruling's reading the same clause would have to yield *worth* verdicts on O and P, which Part XI forbids in terms.

**3. The reading that yields "appropriate given the input" needs a decision rule that the same sentence and f11 L514 both deny the semantics has.** If "What makes a restriction appropriate … is a substantive, criticizable part of the claim" meant "a restriction is appropriate when the claim has such a part," then the semantics would be supplying exactly "a rule that certifies it" — denied in the next clause — and would be *supplying* "what makes a restriction appropriate" — denied by f11 L514's "records and does not supply." The clause must therefore be read as placement (appropriateness lives in the claim's criticizable content), and then it *explains* the disclaimer: because the ground is the claim's own criticizable material, the semantics cannot certify the restriction. The two clauses cohere only on this reading; on the criterion reading they contradict each other. With no decision rule, there is no verdict for "given the input" to qualify and no finding for "Same finding" to catch.

**4. "Same finding" is applied without its first condition.** The rule reads: "A verdict of the theory that *reaches* the fixed verdict's finding *and adds* a qualification, a limit or a more exact wording reaches the same finding." The ruling's reason compresses this to "A qualification is the same finding," then supplies only a qualification — "given the stated ground and open to criticism." But "recorded, uncertified, criticizable" is not a limit on a finding reached; it is a statement that no finding is established. The fixed verdict asserts legitimacy; the theory answers that appropriateness is a criticizable claim-part about which "the semantics … supplies no rule that certifies it." That is the abstention SILENT records, not AGREE with a NOTE.

**5. No definition or condition of the theory separates Greta's limit from the Tuesday limit, so none can find the first legitimate. **6. The case's own counterfactual shows "given the input" is an abstention in both directions.** The ruling's contrast reason ("O1 is the contrast: its input is missing, and it is SILENT") treats present-input as the positive half of a pair. A machinery that can never find a narrowing illegitimate is not finding "legitimate" here; it abstains whenever the question is the legitimacy of the restriction itself. That also explains O1 without the pairing.
