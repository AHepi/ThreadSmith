# Cross-examination report — O35 and O40

## 1. O35 — Four seconds

**1. What the rule fixes, and what remains live.** The plan's file-10 rule is a rule about the counted mark: S75's baseline stands "unless both 1K returns agree on a different mark and [the determiner's] own reading of file 10 confirms it". Both 1K returns are SILENT, which is the baseline. So the counted file-10 mark is SILENT under the rules in section 2 however the text reads, and the row can fall only through the file-11 mark or a comparison element. The text question stays worth testing, because 04 section 7.9 records that 01's pre-registered reading was AGREE and that "On 01's reading, O35 would have been a theory change away, and test (b) would have failed." That endpoint reading is the strongest contrary; I take it first.

**2. The endpoint reading of file 10 (AGREE) fails.** On it, (P) compares \(r\) at \(\xi\) and \(\xi'\), the tap runs at both, so \(P\) is preserved and the stop "is no loss of \(P\)". Three things defeat it.

(a) (P) is parametric in the content of its obligations: they are "claimed obligations \(O\) and protected obligations \(P\), fixed for the comparison" (f10 L438 = f11 L427), and the case states no condition. The situation says "the protected tap" and nothing else; nothing says what protection requires, or over which occasions. The formula fixes *when* \(r\) is evaluated, not *what* \(r\) says.

(b) File 10 itself admits history-valued conditions: "Values of ports may be paths, functions, fields, proofs, or histories" (f10 L122 = f11 L107). On \(r\) = "the tap has run throughout the work", \(r(\xi')\) is false at \(\xi'\) although the tap runs there, and (P) finds a loss with endpoints alone. The O2 group's O38 ruling shows the machinery doing exactly this "when the job sheet states such a condition": the same formula reaches "it was broken, for four seconds". The content decides; the formula does not.

(c) The fixed verdict's own standard is one the theory refuses: "The protected condition held **in every way that mattered**" chooses the condition's content by what mattered, and f10 L25 says the semantics "does not supply an objective aesthetics, a probability of truth, a merit function, or a ranking of thinkers. It supplies places where such things would go if anyone had them, and marks those places as empty."

Mark rule 4's first branch fits exactly: the deciding matter (the content and the occasions of \(P\)) is an input the case leaves unstated.

**3. SPLIT does not fit either.** The two candidate contents are two fillings of one unfilled input, not two readings of the theory's sentences: the theory says one thing — evaluate whatever \(P\) is, "fixed for the comparison" — and the case supplies no \(P\). Rule 3 needs "the theory's sentences [to] support two readings"; here they support one and defer to an input. (Even a SPLIT would rank with SILENT in direction and would sit on the same point — see 4 — so it could not make the row away; but the mark rules give SILENT, and rule 4's description, "takes the deciding matter as an input the case leaves unstated", is the precise fit.)

**4. File 11's SILENT holds, on the same point.** f11 L433 s1 makes the occasions part of the declaration: "\(O\) says what is to be repaired and \(P\) what is to be protected, each as a stated condition over stated occasions, and a protected condition is lost exactly when it fails on an occasion it covers." f11 L514 then supplies the missing-input rule: "where the input is missing, the verdict is unsettled and the semantics says so rather than choosing the input from the verdict wanted." Both contrary file-11 readings choose the input from the verdict wanted: reading the occasions as draws gives the thoughtful person's finding; auditor 2's blind DISAGREE (condition = "the tap runs") takes its content from the same silence and is equally forbidden. The two SILENTs concern one point: under f11 L433 s1 "held" and "not lost" are one fact (lost exactly when it fails on a covered occasion), and in file 10 both turn on the content of \(P\).

**5. The comparison elements hold.** Verdict SAME (two SILENTs, same point). Passage CHANGED (additive) is correct: the file-11 verdict rests on f11 L433 s1 and L514, both ruled CLAIM (M38, M48) and both listed in section 4 as having no file-10 counterpart. I checked whether the verdict could instead rest on the shared sentence f10 L438 = f11 L427 and so make the passage SAME; it cannot, because what makes file 11's SILENT a rule-governed verdict rather than a stop-short one is the occasions clause plus the unsettled-input sentence — precisely the CLAIM places. Direction and kind are unchanged.

## 2. O40 — Two hands on the test

**1. The point is claim-availability, and the determination reads it correctly.** "Neither can say 'mostly'." The verb "say" and the quotation marks around "mostly" mark the claim a party would make ("mostly mine"), not a share-finding ("neither has most"). The preceding sentence already carries the division — "The reinterpretation was theirs together" — and the third sentence draws its consequence for what either party can assert. The case family confirms the contrast: O21, O27 and O50 state shares in plain form ("mostly the expert's", "mostly the robot's"). Reason 1 of the ruling is right on the words.

**2. On that reading, file 10 reaches the finding.** A "mostly" claim needs a warrant: an established preponderance on the record. File 10 establishes the two contributions — f10 L414's construction witnesses, with "Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not" keeping the expert's fix the expert's rather than the robot's — and establishes that no weighing is in play: f10 L25, quoted above, with "those places" (a merit function, a ranking) "empty". The case's record contains the two seeings and no weights. So on the record neither party has a ground for "mostly", and the theory's verdict on the point is the fixed finding with the limit "absent a ranking". The Same finding rule counts "a qualification, a limit or a more exact wording" as the same finding.

**3. The SILENT contrary fails.** Mark rule 4 requires "the deciding matter as an input the case leaves unstated". For a claim-availability finding the deciding matter is what the record warrants, and that is settled by the record: an unrecorded convention cannot ground a claim anyone can *say*. The 04c text-lens argument concedes this in its own words — "whether either can say it is decided by what fills that place, and the case supplies nothing" — with the place empty in this application, the claim fails. "If anyone had them" (f10 L25) describes other applications in which someone supplies a ranking; it does not posit an unstated ranking in this one. Contrast O35, where the case *posits* a protected condition and leaves its content unstated: there the input exists with no stated value and the verdict is unsettled; here the warranting input is absent and its absence is the ground of the finding.

**4. Why the point cannot be a share-finding (the consistency test).** The determination rules O21, O27 and O50 SILENT in both files. Set beside f11 L433 s4 — "both are credited and the history supplies no division of credit that it does not contain" — and f11 L27 s3 ("It does not supply a division of credit among contributors beyond what a history establishes"), the theory's position on shares is "no division of credit is established here". That position leaves a share-finding open (SILENT on "mostly the expert's") and delivers a claim-availability finding (no established majority, hence no claimable majority) — exactly the two marks ruled. If the point were instead a share-finding, two packages follow and each costs more than it buys. (a) SILENT in both files: then f11's history clauses reach nothing in O40 — but "both are credited and the history supplies no division of credit that it does not contain" *is* a verdict about the credit the history supplies, and it reaches "theirs together" and "no majority". (b) SILENT under file 10, AGREE under file 11 (the third reading): it needs f11's clause to settle share-truth where f10 only abstains — an asymmetric reading of one point across the two files. On the claim-availability reading each file reaches the finding by its own route (file 10 through f10 L25's empty place; file 11 through L27 s3 and L433 s4), which is also what makes the verdict SAME and the passage CHANGED (additive) — the ruling's own comparison.

**5. File 11's AGREE stands, and the robot-credit contrary fails.** f11 L419 s2: "Work supplied from outside that boundary, a diagnosis, a decisive question, an instruction about what to read, remains an outside contribution however it is executed inside" — so the expert's proposal is the expert's however the robot executes it — and f11 L433 s4 credits each contribution the history establishes. The contrary that treats the expert's fix as an incoming carrier in the robot's construction witness, making the reinterpretation the robot's, is answered by f11 L419 s2 and by f10 L414's "relay is not".

**6. No reading moves O40 away, and I name the file-10 text I would want.** The three readings give AGREE in both files (this ruling), SILENT in both, or SILENT→AGREE (toward the thoughtful person). None makes the file-11 mark further from AGREE than the file-10 mark, so tests (a)–(d) and the standing verdict do not turn on the row; it decides only S75 correction versus theory change toward. Residual request: file 10's definition of \(\operatorname{ProducedBy}\) and any crediting rule beside it (its Part IX histories text, and Part XI between L444 and L447), together with anything file 10 says about the normative relation beyond f10 L519. These would close the only door left ajar by the share-finding reading — whether file 10 carries *some* credit-division provision that f11 L27 s3 and L433 s4 replace. On the reading I adopt, f10 L25 and f10 L414 settle the row without them.
