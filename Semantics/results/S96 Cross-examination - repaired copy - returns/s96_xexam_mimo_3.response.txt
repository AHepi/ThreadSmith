**2. Q5 · L413–L417 — \(\equiv_\ell\) is called an equivalence it has not been given.**
"Write \(d\equiv_\ell c\) when there are transports from \(d\) to \(c\) and from \(c\) to \(d\), both faithful on \(c\)'s contract at grain \(\ell\). The equivalence is structural at the stated grain…". As written the relation is reflexive and symmetric; transitivity is not supplied, because \(d\equiv c\) (faithful on \(c\)'s contract) and \(c\equiv e\) (faithful on \(e\)'s contract) give transports \(d\leftrightarrow e\) whose scopes match only when Part VIII's condition holds ("Composition of relations preserves both directions when intermediate scopes agree", L361). (N) survives, since it tests each \(d\) against \(c\) separately; the word does not.

```
Write \(d\equiv_\ell c\) when there are transports from \(d\) to \(c\) and from \(c\) to \(d\), both faithful on \(c\)'s contract at grain \(\ell\). The relation is structural at the stated grain, not string equality or similarity; it is transitive when the transports' scopes agree (Part VIII, Relational transport), which is all (N) needs, since (N) tests each \(d\) against \(c\) separately.
```
