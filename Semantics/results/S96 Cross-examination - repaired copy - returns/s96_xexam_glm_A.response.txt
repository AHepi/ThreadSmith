## Findings

### 1 · `Q5 · L233–L236`

**(F1) uses an undefined operation.** L233 says the counterpart relation is obtained "by imposing the constraints of \(\lambda(k)\) and projecting away its hidden ports," and L236 writes

\[
\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\!\big[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\big]=L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)). \tag{F1}
\]

But \(V_k\) is a footprint **of \(E\)** (L91, L231), while \(\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) is a set of valuations of \(\lambda(k)\)'s ports, which are ports **of \(D\)**. A projection onto \(V_k\) is therefore undefined as written: it exists only through the port translation that L186 says \(\lambda\) carries ("\(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\) with a port translation") and that L119 names explicitly ("its counterpart \(\lambda(k)\), read on \(C\) directly with its hidden ports projected away, **up to the port translation**"). The first conjunct of (E), on which the whole definition of an account rests, silently omits what L119 states. The repair is available inside the text; it should be made in (F1) itself.

Proposed wording for L233:

```
**Component fidelity.** For every active component \(k\) of \(E\) with counterpart subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\), and every \((a,b)\in C\), the relation obtained by imposing the constraints of \(\lambda(k)\), projecting away its hidden ports, and carrying what remains to \(V_k\) by the port translation of \(\lambda\) (write \(\operatorname{proj}^{\lambda}_{V_k}\) for that projection) equals the component relation of \(k\) under the translated edit:
```

Proposed wording for L236:

```
\[
\operatorname{proj}^{\lambda}_{V_k}\!\big[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\big]=L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)). \tag{F1}
\]
```

### 3 · `Q1 · L53`

**"Testable in principle" is in-principle verifiability in disguise.** Grievance 9 says of selection: "Whether it occurred is a claim about the physical module, fallible but testable in principle." "In principle" is exactly the absolute sense S23 forbids ("Anything that could be interpreted as needing verification or falsification in any absolute sense"), and it is out of step with the text's own treatment elsewhere: L275 holds that "A contrast that no one could produce, or that could not come about, is still a contrast of its question," and Part XII makes whether a test can be carried out itself something the physical module decides. The contrast the grievance needs — a physical history versus "a kind-label," which "is not a claim about anything" — survives without the assurance.

Proposed wording for that sentence of L53:

```
Whether it occurred is a claim about the physical module, fallible as any claim about it is; what bears on it is whatever tests and arguments bear on such a claim, and the semantics does not say that a test of it can be carried out (Part XII).
```
