# Findings

**1. Q5 · L17.** The two sentences that carry the constitutive conjecture do not go through on their natural reading. "A candidate that an argument not using these four **rules out as a non-explanation** of its question, and that these four cannot represent, would conflict with the conjecture; so would a candidate that meets all four and that such an argument **rules out as an explanation** on its question and contract." The defined idiom is "an argument rules out a claim" (L8, L397) and "a candidate is ruled out for \(j\) when the claim that it meets (E) is" (L315). Here the object is elliptical. On the natural reading — the argument rules the candidate out, calling it a non-explanation — the first conjunct names a case the conjecture *predicts* (a non-explanation the four cannot represent), so no conflict is shown and the central claim fails. Only the other reading (the argument rules out *the claim that* it is a non-explanation) makes both conjuncts cases of failure, as Part XV claims (A) sufficiency and (B) necessity require (L61). Also "and (iv) **their** physical realization" has no clean antecedent (transports? transports and questions? organizations?), and "a candidate that meets all four" makes realization something a candidate meets, which can be read as physics defining an explanation — against L75 and decisions S25–S27.

```
The **constitutive conjecture** is this: explanatory creativity is fully characterized by (i) organizations and the changes they admit, (ii) transports between organizations tested by change-fidelity alone, (iii) the provenance of those transports and of the questions they serve, and (iv) the instantiation and transformation of those contents in carriers, which is where physical possibility enters and nowhere else (Part I). A candidate would conflict with the conjecture in two ways. If an argument that uses none of these four rules out the claim that the candidate is a non-explanation of its question, so that the argument shows the candidate to be an explanation of that question, and the four cannot represent the candidate, the four are not necessary. If a candidate meets the four on its question and contract and such an argument rules out the claim that the candidate is an explanation there, the four are not sufficient. An argument that exhibits either rules the conjecture out for whoever can use it, while it stays usable.
```

Question-finding, "at least half of creativity" (L15), is under-represented against L15's own definition of a question.

```

```

```
A claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) for the question — its target, its contract and what it asks — with the trace; a construction of the contract alone, with the target and the query given, is a claim that the episode found the question's scope, not the question.
```

# Answers

**Q1.** The listed words are gone; "ruled out" is person-relative and tentative throughout ("A claim is **ruled out** for an assessor \(j\) when an argument usable by \(j\) (Part IX) rules it out", L315), and L8 bars the forbidden reading outright: "An argument is never a reason *for* a claim... A claim whose denial an argument rules out gets nothing more than that ruling out." Acceptance is always tentative (L8: "whatever is accepted is accepted tentatively"; L75: "to adopt something is to take it tentatively, open to replacement"); "aesthetic reasons" (L51) is appraisal vocabulary, not belief. But L49 and L105 use "mathematical argument" for what explains — the reasons-for shape S23 forbids (finding 7).

Q1: LEFT — L49, L105

**Q2.** L75 states the rule and the lines keep to it: physical possibility "enters the semantics where a content is instantiated in a carrier or transformed... and it also enters as the content of claims that a candidate can conflict with", and "does not define a question, its range of changes, an account or a conflict between candidates". Contracts (L159: "admitting a change is not a claim that it can be carried out or come about"), fidelity (L43: "turns on the transport and the target alone"), non-vacuity (L257) and conflict (L315: "it does not turn on what any physics admits") are all physics-free. L17's (iv) "their physical realization", read with L75, is instantiation, but as worded ("a candidate that meets all four") a reader can take physics as a requirement of explanation (finding 1).

Q2: KEPT OUT OF WHAT DEFINES EXPLANATION

**Q2 targets.** L159: "in whatever the target is: a garden, a mathematical structure, a melody or a philosophical claim"; L49 gives edits that "need not be a physical intervention" ("Removing a starting assumption, dropping a constraint or changing a dimension"); L105 admits "mathematical arguments or histories" as port values. Nothing makes any of the three wait on physics.

Q2 targets: ALL THREE CAN BE TARGETS

**Q3.** All three parts are said. No test needed: L315 "it is found by argument, with no test, and it does not turn on what any physics admits"; L8 "An argument need not cite a test". Bare claim enough: L315 "the bare claim that perpetual motion is impossible is enough for a conflict with a candidate whose organization gives perpetual motion", while "Nor is the conflict enough to do anything about it... and \(\chi\) alone does not say where the candidate is in error". Choice: L315 "which one the person goes on with is the person's choice (Part 0)", with L8's "for whatever reason" and L71 "A thinker may act on an appraisal that no argument has decided". The pendulum wrinkle (an edit \(\chi\) excludes) is handled.

Q3: SAID

**Q4.** L8: "A premise of an argument can be a claim tentatively accepted as given"; L397 makes the owner's S27 point explicit — "even with no thought given to it, by someone who holds no explanation of it", "neither it nor anything else in the semantics asks that the person represent (R) a premise's explanation, or any part of it, before using the premise to find an error" — and quotes the owner's "costly gamble" and "impossibly costly" words. No examined line requires containment.
