## C21 · L273

The sentence is `"\(p\) because \(p\)" fails non-circular dependence.` Its work: give the canonical circular case, name the conjunct of (E) it fails, and stand as the account-level twin of the argument-level rule in Part IX.

**Variations tried.**

- `"\(p\) because \(p\)" violates non-circular dependence.` — The text's verb for not meeting a condition is "fails": L275 "and fails non-circular dependence", L267 "it fails (F1)", L271 "fails (F2)". Part XV's summary says "conclusion-as-premise fails non-circular dependence" (L536); changing the verb only here splits the usage against L536 and L275 without gain.
- `An explanation whose premise is its own conclusion fails non-circular dependence.` — L397 says: "Such an argument, as one against that claim, has its conclusion among its premises, as in `"\(p\) because \(p\)"`". The identical formula in L273 and L397 is what makes the parallel between Part V's structural reading of identity and Part IX's structural reading of a premise-that-is-the-denial visible. Dropping the formula from L273 breaks that tie.
- `"The answer because the answer" fails non-circular dependence.` — loses the match with L397's verbatim quotation, and "the answer" is not the quoted case.

**Fault sought.** Non-circular dependence (L255) is a conjunction; "\(p\) because \(p\)" fails its first part ("The target's answer does not appear… as a component"), so "fails non-circular dependence" is correct as stated. No vacuity: the case is the one L536 names.

C21: HOLDS — every variation broke the verbatim echo at L397 or the "fails" usage at L275 and L536.

## C27 · L443

The sentence is `An explanatory aim requires an account, or the correction of a use through one, to be deployable.`

**Fault found.** "To be deployable" attaches, by the sentence's own grammar, to both disjuncts. But Deploy is defined only for contents: "Deploy_{β,ℓ}(s,c,ξ;U) is met when s at ξ holds a representation of c" (L403). A "correction of a use" is not a content; correction is repair, a relation on histories and contributions: "Repair_{O,P}(ξ,ξ';Δ) ⟺ …" (L438). So the second disjunct cannot be deployable, on the repair-event reading. On the charitable reading — that "the correction of a use" denotes a corrected content — (EX) still deploys the account, not a corrected use: `Account((c,p_c,t_c,Γ_c)) ∧ … ∧ Deploy_{β,ℓ}(s,c,ξ';U_c)` (L449), with U_c "the declared use task for c" (L453). Either reading leaves one disjunct ill-typed or mismatched to (EX). What (EX) actually requires, in both kinds of explanatory aim, is a deployable account that runs to the repair: `ProducesVia(Δ,c,o;ξ,ξ')`, "an active route that runs from Δ to the repair of o contains the relevant binding of c" (L453).

**Variations tried before the fault.**

- `An explanatory aim requires a deployable account.` — drops the correction-of-a-use coverage, which S20's "Once the explanation is rescued, the mistake shouldn't be able to creep back in" and (P)'s protected aims exist for.
- `An explanatory aim requires an account, or a corrected use, to be deployable.` — same type fault.

Repair wording:

```
An explanatory aim requires an account to be deployable, whether the aim is that account or the correction of a use through it.
```

This keeps both kinds of aim, keeps "deployable" where Deploy applies (L403), and matches (EX) exactly: the account is deployed (L449) and produces the repair of o (L453).

C27: FAILS — "to be deployable" cannot apply to "the correction of a use": Deploy takes a content (L403), a correction is a repair on histories (L438), and (EX) deploys the account (L449) — `An explanatory aim requires an account to be deployable, whether the aim is that account or the correction of a use through it.`
