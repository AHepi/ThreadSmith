## A note on what counts as a variation

A recasting that keeps every term and every dependency and changes only voice or synonym ("are distinguished" → "are set apart") is the same formulation wearing different clothes; it does not show the sentence easy to vary. A variation, here, is a wording that changes what the sentence says, relies on, or leaves out. Near-misses of the recasting kind are named where they arose and not counted.

---

## C13 · L161

**Rival wordings tried.**

(a)
```
A question can be wrong about its target, its baseline, or its requirements.
```
"Wrong about" grades the question by a correctness it does not locate; the original's three failures are structural — picking out, compatibility, combination — and each can be exhibited at the level of the question itself. "Wrong about its target" also presupposes a target to be wrong about; "fail to pick out its *alleged* target" does not, and "alleged" is doing work: a question about nothing still alleges a target.

(b)
```
A question may aim at a nonexistent target, demand a physically impossible change, or contradict itself.
```
"Physically impossible" imports into the theory of explanation exactly what the owner excluded: S25 — "It has nothing to do with explanation" — and S26 — "Physical possibility comes in only when something is instantiated or transformed". The text keeps this boundary at L159: "whether anyone could carry a change out, or whether it could come about, is a matter of instantiation and transformation (Part XII)". "Demand a physically impossible change" also conflates a single change with *requirements*, which are pairs in $C$.

**Fault sought.** "Assume an incompatible baseline" does not say incompatible *with what*. That is not a fault: non-vacuity at L257 ("$\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing$") gives one form the incompatibility takes, and the line deliberately leaves it unlocalized because, by its own next sentences, exposing it is another question — matching L159's stance that "the semantics records the restriction and supplies no rule that decides it". Spelling the incompatibility out would decide by rule what the theory says is decided by question.

C13: HOLDS — every rival either presupposes a target ("wrong about", "miss"), imports physical impossibility against S25/S26 and L159, or collapses the three distinct failure modes into one graded verdict.

## C14 · L161

**Rival wordings tried.**

(a)
```
Asking it still happens.
```
Drops "event", the text's running term: L397 — "A record leaf is a reference to an event with an interpreted claim" — L55 — "An assessment is an event with a frozen contract" — and L604 — "An assessment event with contract $C$". Something that merely "happens" is not yet the kind of thing a record leaf can reference or an assessment can be; the sentence would stop supporting the freezing machinery asserted two sentences later on the same line.

(b)
```
Its formulation is still an occurrence.
```
An occurrence is defined at L169 ("An **occurrence** is a physically located carrier") and histories are sets of occurrences (L375); but the record and assessment machinery speaks of *events* (L397, L604). Substituting the other defined word leaves it open whether a formulation can be a record leaf, and blurs the carrier/content line L169 draws.

(c) Deleting "still": "Its formulation is an event." The concession is the point — the previous sentence has just listed defects, and the work of this sentence is that defectiveness does not cancel eventhood. Without "still" it no longer answers the doubt the previous sentence raises.

**Fault sought.** "Its formulation" could be misread as the formulated text rather than the act; but "event" forces the act reading (events happen), so the ambiguity is not live. No circularity: the sentence claims eventhood, it does not define "event".

C14: HOLDS — every rival severs the tie to "event" at L397, L55 and L604, or drops the concession that a defective question still counts.

## C15 · L161

**Rival wordings tried.**

(a)
```
Exposing the defect is another question.
```
Breaks on "own contract". L151: "Two questions with the same $D$ and different $(C,\mathcal Q)$ are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other." Without its own contract, nothing marks the exposing question as a *different* question rather than a continuation of the defective one. It also breaks the line's own last sentence — "Supplying a meaning for a replacement query can make a coherent new question; it does not answer the original one" — which needs the exposing question contract-bound so that the replacement's coherence does not leak back onto the original; and L55's frozen contract needs a contract to freeze.

(b)
```
Exposing the defect is a further question about the same target.
```
Breaks L377: there the exposing question is "the question whether $z$ has $\delta$ in respect of $p$", whose subject is the criticized content $z$, not the original question's target $D$. Fixing the target misidentifies $p_\delta$.

(c)
```
Exposing an alleged defect is another question with its own contract.
```
"Alleged" is Part IX's word for criticism (L377, "alleged defect $\delta$"). Here, under the "may" of the previous sentence, the defect is actual when it occurs, and "the defect" ties each of the three listed failures to its own exposure. The variant redoes L377's job and leaves the three failures of the previous sentence without their link.

**Fault sought.** "Another" is not idle: it marks the exposing question distinct from the defective one, which the following sentence ("it does not answer the original one") presupposes.

C15: HOLDS — dropping "its own contract" breaks L151 and the line's final sentence; fixing the target breaks L377; shifting to "alleged" untethers the sentence from the defects just listed and duplicates Part IX.

## C16 · L217

**Rival wordings tried.**

(a) Dropping the qualifier:
```
For an edit–boundary pair $(a,b)\in C$:
```
Prediction and violation would be defined on pairs that never occur. This breaks L582, which restates this sentence — "Surprise is defined (Part IV) as a violation of a selected transport at an occurring $(a,b)\in C$" — so Argument 4's restatement would no longer match the definition. It also undoes what L159 keeps apart: admitting a change "is not a claim that it can be carried out or come about". A violation at a never-occurring pair would be a verdict on unactualized changes — a claim the semantics does not make.

(b)
```
For an encountered edit–boundary pair $(a,b)\in C$:
```
"Encountered" is the history term: L195, "a finite history $H\subseteq C$ of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered". But L223 says "Prediction and violation are defined for every transport to the simulation layer, surprise only for a selected one" — including declared transports (L199) held by no encountering system. "Encountered" would tie all three definitions to a selection history that only surprise requires.

(c)
```
For a physically realized edit–boundary pair $(a,b)\in C$:
```
Occurrence is the right notion, but this wording invites reading physical realization as a precondition inside the theory, against S26 ("Physical possibility comes in only when something is instantiated or transformed") and away from the phrase L582 quotes.

**Fault sought.** None: "actually" does real work, contrasting occurrence with mere admission — $C$ contains pairs whether or not they ever occur.

C16: HOLDS — removing "actually occurring" breaks L582 and L159; "encountered" breaks L223 and L195; "physically realized" crosses the S25/S26 boundary and mismatches L582.

## C17 · L219

**Rival wordings tried.**

(a)
```
- the **prediction** is what $S$ answers at the translated edit and boundary;
```
Loses the compositional form. L189 fixes the positions — "$\tau$ translates edits, $\sigma$ translates boundaries" — and (A) at L250 states "$\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)$" in exactly these positions. The verbal surrogate no longer pairs with (A), and question fidelity can no longer be read as: the prediction equals the question's answer when the transport is faithful.

(b)
```
- the **prediction** is $\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)$;
```
Breaks the whole structure of error. The two are equal only *under* fidelity (A), L250; fidelity can fail, which is what violation is (L220). This rival makes the prediction correct by definition and leaves violation nothing to be a failure of — against S20 (error correction is the next thing to check) and S23 (nothing verified, nothing absolute).

(c) Swapping the translations, $\operatorname{Ans}_S(\sigma(a),\tau(b))$: a type error against L189.

**Fault sought.** None. L177 — "whose queries are predictions: what a port of $P$ will take under an admitted edit" — matches the form exactly.

C17: HOLDS — the formula's positions are fixed by L189 and mirrored by (A) at L250; the verbal rival uncouples it from (A), and defining the prediction as $\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)$ abolishes violation.

## C18 · L220

**Rival wordings tried.**

(a)
```
- a **violation** is a wrong prediction;
```
Under-defines. Fidelity has parts: (F1) and (F2) at L233–L243, and (A) at L247–L251. L245: "(F1) prevents an assembled match from hiding a decomposition in error." A transport whose answer at $(a,b)$ is right while a component relation is wrong — (F1) failing — is violated on the original and unviolated on this rival.

(b)
```
- a violation occurs when reality falsifies the prediction;
```
Falsification in an absolute sense is what the owner forbade: S23, "Anything that could be interpreted as needing verification or falsification in any absolute sense". It also relocates the failure from fidelity — the defined relation (L189, L233–L253) — to an unmediated comparison with reality.

(c)
```
- a violation holds where fidelity fails somewhere in $C$;
```
Breaks the localization. L221 defines surprise as "a violation of a selected transport at $(a,b)\notin H$", which needs violation *at a pair*; Argument 4 (L580–L582) turns entirely on pairs of $C$ outside $H$.

**Fault sought.** None. In fact this sentence is what *does* the localization: fidelity is defined over all of $C$ (L233, "every $(a,b)\in C$"), and L221 and L582 use the pair-local notion this line creates.

C18: HOLDS — "wrong prediction" drops (F1)/(F2) against L245; "reality falsifies" violates S23; globalizing over $C$ breaks L221 and L582.

## C19 · L225

**Rival wordings tried.**

(a)
```
There are two responses to a violation.
```
Reads as exhaustive. Against S21 — "this entire episode may never resolve"; "Notice I never once claimed what must happen" — and L429: "Closing an episode is a choice". Abandoning, or doing nothing, is also possible; the theory marks two, it does not say only two exist.

(b)
```
A violation demands one of two responses.
```
Normative; same breaks (S21, L429: "an argument can rule out some ways of closing it… no argument makes the choice").

(c)
```
The two responses are learning and creating.
```
Inverts definition and ground. L584: "the semantics distinguishes them by their traces, not by their outcomes." Naming them by their outcomes in the defining sentence would make the distinction outcome-based — the very thing L584 denies. (The active recasting "The semantics distinguishes two responses to a violation" changes no term and no dependency; it is L584's own next move, not a variation.)

**Fault sought.** The agentless passive is not slack: it names no responder and claims no exhaustiveness, leaving the grounds (traces) to the following sentences and to L584.

C19: HOLDS — exhaustivity breaks S21 and L429; prescription breaks S21 and L429; outcome-naming breaks L584's "by their traces, not by their outcomes".

## C20 · L225

**Load-bearing parts.** "Only" (the exclusion), "can be" (the modality), "under Part X" (the index to (G)). The exclusion is sound, not a fault: the selection response "extends the history $H$ … and lets $\mu$ act: the transport is re-tuned within the population" (L225); a selection history contains no representation at all — L195, "No member of the history represents $t$, $H$, or the survival condition" — so no construction trace can exist there, and $\operatorname{Build}$ at L405 requires one ("contains a nontrivial binding construction … and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers"). L626 confirms the failure the selection response answers is "structural, not parametric".

**Rival wordings tried.**

(a)
```
Only the second is originative under Part X.
```
Breaks against (G), L422: $\operatorname{Origin}$ conjoins $\operatorname{Attempt}$, $\operatorname{New}$ and $\operatorname{Build}$. A construction response that rebuilds an already available transport fails $\operatorname{New}$ (L413–L416) and is not originative. The modality is load-bearing.

(b)
```
Only construction is creative.
```
Same modal break, and "creative" is not the defined term; L592 ties creativity to "(G)", not to the bare presence of construction.

(c) A rival that keeps everything:
```
Only a construction response can be originative under Part X.
```
It keeps "only", "can be" and "under Part X"; it names the response by the term defined earlier in the same line (L225, "A **construction response** introduces a new organization or a new transport with a construction trace"); it breaks nothing — L584 refers to the pair by content ("extend $H$ and re-tune, or construct a new transport"), not by position, and nothing else in the given lines cites "the second". Unlike a synonym recast, it changes what the sentence depends on: the original leans on the *order* of the two preceding definitions; this wording stands on the defined term alone, and so does the sentence's work at least as well.

C20: VARIES — Only a construction response can be originative under Part X.
