# Report on candidates C01–C12

## C01 · L27

**Rivals tried.** A first rival:

```
It fixes what the classes are.
```

"Fixes" sits next to "decides", the very act the first sentence disowns ("It does not decide whether any human, machine, institution or lineage belongs to the classes defined", L27); the whole work of the sentence is the contrast decide/define, and a verb that neighbours "decide" re-blurs it. A second rival:

```
It says what the classes are.
```

"Weaker: a class can be *said* by description. The document's mode is definition — the heading at L28–L30 names "what is defined", and membership is stated as definitional data (L528: "The base class: interpretations supplying these data with typing as declared"). "Says" would let a reader take the classes as described rather than defined, weakening what L528 does. A third:

```
It defines them.
```

This drops the deliberate echo: the first sentence already says "the classes defined", and the second sentence's restatement of "the classes" is what makes the affirmation land against the denial.

**Faults sought.** None. The affirmation is backed by L528 (the classes' membership conditions) and by L632 ("It is not a claim that any actual infant, animal, or program instantiates it."), which keeps the instance side open exactly as the sentence says. No circularity: the classes are defined by data and conditions, not by this sentence.

C01: HOLDS — every rival either blurred the define/decide contrast or downgraded the classes from defined (L528) to described.

## C02 · L41

**Rivals tried.** Replace "Survival" with "Selection":

```
Selection is how the transport got there; fidelity is what it is.
```

This loses grip on the grievance's own word. The grievance at L41 is "you have made fidelity a matter of **survival**", and the answer must deny that *survival* confers fidelity. It also misassigns: selection is the package — population, variation operator, history, survival condition (L195) — while survival is the condition the grievance names, and what Argument 3 isolates is survival's inability to distinguish ("survival on \(H\) does not distinguish \(t\) from \(t'\) there", L572). A second rival:

```
Survival concerns provenance; fidelity concerns the transport.
```

This miscasts survival: provenance is a classification of *transports* (L193: "has exactly one of three provenances"), not something survival has; and "concerns" is too weak to carry the point that fidelity is a property of the transport on its contract (L189: "A transport is **faithful on \(C\)** when it meets the component and global fidelity conditions of Part V"), which is what the underdetermination of Argument 3 turns on.

**Faults sought.** The clause "fidelity is what it is" risks the colloquial fatalist reading ("it is what it is"); the context blocks it — the preceding sentence fixes the sense: "turns on the transport and the target alone, not on whether it survived" (L41). The compression of "survival" for "selection by survival" is licensed by the opening "Selection produces a transport" (L41), which has already named the process. No fault stands.

C02: HOLDS — rivals lost the grip on the grievance's word "survival" or miscast survival against L193/L195; the compression is licensed in context.

## C03 · L47

**Rivals tried.** Widen the requirement:

```

```

This breaks L199 ("**Declared.** Neither of the above. The transport is entered into the model by its author.") and L13 ("Declaration is used only as a modelling convenience, and a claim about creativity cannot depend on it"): declared transports serve explanations; only claims about creativity cannot rest on declaration. Narrow the subject:

```
Construction is a separate provenance with a separate trace, and creativity requires it.
```

"Creative attribution" is the theory's unit, not loose creativity: (G) at L422 is \(\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\), the creative-episode class is "base interpretations with an instance of (G)" (L528), and the episode contains "an instance of (G) connected to its inquiry" (L429 excerpt). The requirement is on attribution; the rival overclaims about an unattributed notion. A third rival commits a category error:

```
Construction has a separate provenance with a separate trace, and every creative attribution requires it.
```

Construction *is* one of the three provenances (L193, L197: "Write \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\)."); it does not have one.

**Faults sought.** None. "Separate trace" is backed by L405 ("A construction trace identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting representation.") and L411 ("the traces differ"), and the requirement matches (G).

C03: HOLDS — rivals broke L199/L13 (declared transports), the attribution unit (G) at L422/L528, or the provenance classification at L193.

## C05 · L113

**Rivals tried.**

```
The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is given by
```

and

```
Define the **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) as
```

Both depart from the display convention by which the text marks a definition by form alone: "The compatible valuations are" (L97) before (O), "The answer profile is" (L141) before its display. The bare "is" is how a reader recognizes, without a keyword, that what follows is a definition rather than a derived statement; varying this one display breaks the uniform convention that governs the others. "Is given by" also quietly suggests a supplier, which the text reserves for the interpretation's data (L91: "the interpretation supplies").

**Faults sought.** None. The phrase "on \(C\)" is load-bearing and correctly placed: it is the idiom used at L554 ("a signature on \(\tau[C]\)") and L119, and it cannot be dropped without losing the contract-relativity the section goes on to state.

C05: HOLDS — rivals departed from the definitional display idiom shared with L97 and L141, or suggested a supply relation the text reserves for the interpretation's data (L91).

## C06 · L115–L117

**Rivals tried.** Restate as a function:

```
\(\operatorname{sig}_C(j):C\to\bigcup_j\{L_j(a,b)\},\quad (a,b)\mapsto L_j(a,b)\)
```

This breaks L556, which writes the signature in the set-builder form (K) instantiates: "\(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}^{\lambda}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\)" and "\(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)=\{(\tau(a),\sigma(b),L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)))\}\)", and argues "(F1) equates the third coordinates pointwise". Under a function form those displays no longer instantiate (K), and the pointwise-equality step loses its referent. Drop the first two coordinates:

```
\(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{L_j(a,b):(a,b)\in C\}\)
```

This breaks L119's comparison of components "read on \(C\) through \(\tau\) and \(\tau'\)": without edit–boundary coordinates there is nothing for the edit translations \(\tau,\tau'\) to act on, and it breaks L554's claim, which is stated over translated coordinates. Restrict to a baseline profile:

```
\(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=L_j(1,b_0)\)
```

This destroys the coarseness content of L119 ("a coarser contract identifies more components, and two components of one kind on \(C\) may separate on a finer contract"), whose whole point is which pairs \((a,b)\) the contract carries.

**Faults sought.** The comprehension is well-defined: \(L_j(a,b)\) is supplied for each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\) (L91), so (K) is total on \(C\). L526 records exactly its dependence ("(K) depends on (O) and a contract"), and L520 indexes it. No circularity: (K) uses only (O)'s primitives.

C06: HOLDS — the graph form is used verbatim at L556 and acted on by L119; function-form, coordinate-free, and baseline rivals each broke a named use.

## C07 · L123

**Rivals tried.** Swap the polarities:

```
- a **causal assignment** has a signature that is invariant under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and changes under observation edits;
```

This breaks L127's test — "change only the reading and the part it reports stays as it was; change the part and the reading follows" — which assigns the observation-edit variability to the *measurement*, and "Which of the two an account offers as producing an outcome is fixed by that signature, not by the account's wording." It also breaks L37: "A correlation has no component that responds to an intervention on its supposed input; a cause does." Substitute the input port:

```
- a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its input port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits;
```

An edit setting an input replaces the component *assigning that input* (L103: "An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port"), not the causal component, so the causal component's relation would not change under it; the clause would misdescribe exactly the responsiveness L37 makes the cause/correlation difference. Drop the replacement clause:

```
- a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and is invariant under observation edits;
```

Without it, a signature sensitive only positionally — because the output-setting edit replaces the occupant (L103) — would count as causal while the relation depended on nothing about the component itself; L127's verdict ("fixed by that signature, not by the account's wording") needs the assignment tied to the component, which is also what lets L57 pair cause with rule in component-sensitivity ("A changed rule is a changed component", L103).

**Faults sought.** The first clause might look automatic given L103 (any output-setting edit replaces the component), i.e. idle. It is not: it locates the sensitivity at the *output* port, which is what contrasts with the measurement's invariance at the *measured* port (L124) and carries L127's change-the-part test; and the pattern obtains only where \(C\) contains such pairs, which is the text's own position (L37: "where a separating change exists, fidelity finds it; where none exists, the label asserts a distinction the level does not contain"). No circularity: "observation edits" are fixed by L109 in terms of the *measurement's* signature, and the measurement bullet (L124) uses only interventions and edits, not "cause".

C07: HOLDS — polarity and port swaps broke L127, L37, and L103's replacement structure; dropping the replacement clause untied the assignment from the component.

## C08 · L124

**Rivals tried.** Swap polarities:

```
- a **measurement** has a signature variable under interventions on the measured port and invariant under edits to the measuring relation;
```

Breaks L127 outright: "change the part and the reading follows" is the invariance under intervention on the measured side, and the variability under edits to its own relation is "change only the reading and the part it reports stays as it was." Replace the idiom with value-tracking:

```
- a **measurement** is one whose reading follows what it reports and changes when its own relation is edited;
```

Breaks L109, which defines an observation port "when the component assigning it has a measurement's signature (below)": that definition needs the measurement's signature statable as an edit-response pattern in (K)'s coordinates, not as a tracking disposition. Use the defined term:

```
- a **measurement** has a signature invariant under observation edits and variable under edits to the measuring relation;
```

This creates a circle with L109: the observation port is defined by the measurement's signature, so the measurement cannot be defined by observation edits. The standing wording avoids the circle by using "the measured port" and "the measuring relation" as plain glosses fixed by signature conditions.

**Faults sought.** The circularity just probed is absent as the line stands; "measured port" and "measuring relation" are glosses inside a pattern description, which C10 marks as "descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data". Consistent with L109 and L127.

C08: HOLDS — polarity swap broke L127; tracking idiom broke L109's definitional use; "observation edits" would circle with L109.

## C10 · L127

**Rivals tried.**

```
These are derived from (K), not additional data.
```

"Derived" is on the owner's forbidden list (S23: "derived, derived from").

```

```

Overclaims formal status. The text's consequences are theorem-styled (L558: "**Consequence.** A condition 'each component's counterpart must be a component of the same kind' adds nothing to (F1) on any contract."); calling these labelled pattern descriptions consequences would put "causal assignment" on a par with proved claims, weakening what L11 requires of them — "a *kind* is nothing over and above how a component responds to the changes that level admits" — which must hold equally of cause, measurement and rule.

"Input" is a defined port notion (L109: "A port \(v\) is an **input** under \(A\) when \(A\) contains an edit that sets \(v\) directly."), so the sentence would collide with the port vocabulary. **Faults sought.** None. The sentence does real work: it is what keeps the bullets from being read as membership requirements, backing the eliminability result at L554–L558.

C10: HOLDS — "derived" is forbidden (S23), "consequences" overclaims against L558 and L11, "inputs" collides with the defined port notion at L109.

## C11 · L127

**Rivals tried.**

```
The semantics does not ask whether a component "is" a cause.
```

"Weaker than "never". The parallel at L11 — "The definition never asks whether a piece of the explanation 'is the same kind of thing' as a piece of the world" — uses "never" for a standing refusal that holds at every level of detail ("at any level of detail", L11) and for any contract; "does not" reads as one omission among others, and the echo of L11 is what tells a reader the two denials are the same denial.


Shifts the claim from the question asked to the vocabulary used. The predicate claim is made elsewhere and need not be repeated (L600 excerpt: "There is no residual, undefined predicate meaning 'explains,' 'represents,' or 'is a cause'"); here the scare-quoted "is" targets the question of being, and the "asks whether … " framing is precisely what licenses the reply "It asks what its signature is." — replacing it orphans that reply.

**Faults sought.** None. Consistent with L31's excerpt (no undefined predicate "is a cause" is an import) and with the ordinary-talk tolerance of the surrounding sentences.

C11: HOLDS — rivals weakened the L11 parallel and its level-relativity, or destroyed the ask/ask structure that carries into the next sentence.

## C12 · L127

**Rival that works.**

```
It asks what its signature on \(C\) is.
```

Signatures are contract-relative by (K) itself (\(\operatorname{sig}_C\)) and by L119 ("Kinds are therefore relative to the contract"), and \(C\) is in scope from L113; making it explicit states what context already fixes, breaks nothing, and loses no case: the contrast with the previous sentence survives, and the uses of signatures downstream (L233–L253, L281, L554–L558) are all explicitly contract-indexed already, so the plainer form fits them.

**Variations that break.** Dropping "signature" for something weaker — "It asks how it responds to changes." — would break the tie to (K) and to L11's "nothing over and above how a component responds", which is a claim about signatures, not an instruction; and "It asks what kind it is." would be circular against the section's order of definition, where kinds are equivalence classes *of* signatures (L119).

**Faults sought.** The implicit contract is fixed by the section's opening (L113) and by the display (K); the explicit rival merely records it. No vacuity: the sentence names the semantics' sole question about components, as L119 and L554–L558 bear out.

C12: VARIES — It asks what its signature on \(C\) is.
