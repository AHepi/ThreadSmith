## 1. The found rules under the theory

### 1.1 What the uploads say (the owner's outside report, unverified)

Two files: a read-me (2,631 bytes) and a final report (6,649 bytes). They link a figure, a rationale, the exported model, unit tests, run records, seals and receipts; none of these was uploaded, and nothing here was run. What they claim:

- **The world.** A finite synthetic visual world, 24 by 24 binary pixels, calibrated, with canonical panels.
- **The machine.** No neural network, no model service, no network access. A search the report calls "the finite arithmetic constructor": a "first-exact-fit search" over a fixed arithmetic grammar against supplied teaching measurements, reconstructed on replay as **29,174 proposals with their paired feedback**, in order.
- **What was learned** (the found rules, exported in a model file): `max(abs(x), abs(y)) <= 3`, `x*x + y*y <= 9`, `cur + (cur - prev) * lag`. The first two "became unnamed geometric concept slots" before two invented words, `tov` and `mip`, were attached (which word goes with which is not said); the third "was learned from raw-decoded motion demonstrations".
- **Four stages, two sealed confirmations** (seeds 20261007 and 20261011), each passing **788 of 788** checks over 33 test conditions: shape and partial-view integration 244, conditional object permanence 176, distinct-object tracking 96, spatial translation and planning 272. The report says these are not interchangeable accuracy trials: they include predictions, necessary UNKNOWN answers, state-history comparisons, causal interventions and exact pixel comparisons. Replay rebuilt every proposal and score and every one of 1,556 transitions per run; 43 unit tests passed; an exhaustive geometry check covered 8,852 legal single-object poses, 3,952 learned-bank templates (3,628 distinct rasters), and kept all 324 cross-concept alias masks as UNKNOWN.
- **Causal interventions**: the learned geometry bank cleared (geometric answers removed); the motion formula replaced by `cur` (a stale hidden location and an unmoved translated raster, perception intact); memory erasure; binding erasure and swap; sham controls; restoration; matched camera and object motion; same current frame with different histories.
- **What was supplied** (its own list of declared priors): the 24-by-24 calibrated world; canonical panels; the fixed arithmetic grammar and the first-exact-fit search; convex level-set support; finite radii, rotations, centres and object count; camera calibration; persistent tokens; one-to-one continuity and bounded matching; explicit commands and references; four cardinal actions; bounded breadth-first search. "Shape predicates and the motion formula were learned. Pose transformation, convexity, tracking, object enumeration, task selection, and planning machinery were supplied."
- **A development error caught**: 564 rotated-box edge templates violated the evaluator's fully-visible rule; the developers fixed it with "a generic, charged convex-support optimizer", leaving the learned formulas unchanged.
- **Its own limits**: it does not establish natural vision, unrestricted object identity, language translation, general intelligence, or that the priors were learned; the boundary between machine, world and scorer is "a logical boundary in trusted Python, not a hostile-code sandbox".

### 1.3 Their provenance by the theory, read

**The search is the selected shape, part for part.** D12.1 (L195) asks for a population of candidate transports, a variation operator, a finite history H of encountered pairs, and a survival condition requiring fidelity on H. Here: the population is the grammar's expressions; the variation is the step that proposes the next one; H is the teaching measurements; the survival condition is an exact fit; and the first expression that fits is kept, so fidelity on H decided which one survived (S84's graded advantage). The negative clause asks that nothing earlier in the history, inside the declared boundary, represents the rule, H, the survival condition or the world it carries to. At **the machine's boundary** (the machine as run: the scorer and the teaching measurements inside, their writers outside) the scorer and measurements are routines and records written outside and run inside: under Reading C they entered whole and represent nothing there (I200; L427: a routine written outside and run inside is the system's own process, and its content remains its writer's contribution), exactly as Avida's task-check code does at the S72 boundary (S129, MC1). So the rule is **selected** there, **within a named class**: the grammar names the class (S118's grade 2), and the target of each rule is supplied by measurements of a world the designers wrote, the shape S120 called a function "named in disguise" (plain file 120). Nothing here reaches S118's grade 3. At **a boundary taking in the designers and the teaching data**, the people who wrote the grammar, the scorer and the world represent the target before the rule is held, so the rule is not selected there; nothing at that boundary prepares it either, so it is **declared** (D12.3). The report's own word "constructor" does not decide this: the three provenances "are told apart by their histories, not their outputs" (L13), and calling a selection history construction is one of the things Part XV says would have to be shown without loss to collapse the two ((Prov), L542).

**The one fact the report does not give.** Whether the paired feedback only *filters* proposals that come in a fixed order, or *steers* what is proposed next. "First-exact-fit search" over a "fixed" grammar reads as the first (**reading S**, the main reading): a complete expression is drawn from an enumeration and kept if it fits, which is reply 04's own example of what does *not* supply a newly prepared binding: "selecting an already-complete answer" (S125's check of reply 04; R04 L72; the target row is R04 L71). If instead the feedback steers the next proposal while the measurements are held inside the machine (**reading K**), the search is read as a trace that prepares the rule with its target held, the way S129 (K8) and S131 (item 3) read training on a teacher's answers, and the rule is constructed. Prepares is a primitive (I56), read by hand; this is the judgement on which every provenance verdict below turns. The data to decide it exist: the report says replay reconstructs all 29,174 proposals and their feedback in order. **One check the owner could ask of the outside agent**: run the search on two different teaching sets and compare the proposal sequences up to the first exact fit; if they are identical until one fits, the feedback only filters (S); if they part before any fit, it steers (K).

**The interventions show a causal role, not a provenance.** Clearing the bank removed the geometric answers; swapping the motion formula for `cur` produced the predicted stale location; sham edits changed nothing; restoration brought the answers back. That is the shape of S126's knock-out and of constructor theory's elimination test as S110 gives it (O2): the held representation lies on the route to the answers (D12.5's faithful correspondence, doing work). It says nothing about how the rule came to be held: a selected and a constructed rule would both pass it. The intervention that bears on provenance (reply 04: "interrupting preparation should remove the newly prepared capability") would act on the search, not on the bank; the report does not describe one.

**Under S86 they are evolved knowledge and not explanations** (on reading S): a selected account is a representation, the owner's evolved knowledge, and "explanation" is kept for what was worked out (S86; S131's copies). **The two invented words** are labels the teacher handed in and the machine stored against a slot as given: declared at both boundaries, attached to selected content.

**One more point the theory makes of them (Argument 3, L570 to L576).** What a selection history leaves open, the population decides. On the integer pixel grid the disc rule as found, `x*x + y*y <= 9`, and `x*x + y*y < 10` give the same raster at every pixel from -5 to 5, yet they differ at 56 of 1,681 quarter-pixel points (computed, plain arithmetic). Which of two such expressions the machine holds is fixed by the grammar's order, not by the teaching measurements. Whether the outside machine's rotated poses ever sample between pixels is not said; the report's 324 alias masks kept as UNKNOWN are the same lesson met inside its own world.

## 2. Their use, toward the goal "build something that can construct first"

### 2.1 What they cannot do

- **They are not a constructor.** A formula held in a bank, used as given, prepares nothing.
- **Re-running the search on new teaching data is selection again.** It extends H and lets the variation act: the theory's *selection response* (L225), not a construction response; "only the second can be originative". The class is still the grammar's and the target is still supplied.
- **The machine holds no problem and no rivals.** D10.1 asks two rivals, each offered for one question in place of the other, neither ruled out for the one who holds them. The grammar's members are a population; the machine offers none of them for anything; and when a rule fails, nothing in it represents the failure as a difficulty (L429).

### 2.2 Their uses, each tied to a definition

**(a) The stock a constructor starts from.** "Construction may operate on selected material" (L201, Part IV), and "a small binding newly prepared inside received content is construction of that binding, and the rest of the content keeps its inherited provenance" (L405). Handed to a subject whole, the found rules enter its boundary whole and are **declared** there (Reading C; D12.3, I200); a repair the subject then builds is told apart from them part by part (D12.4). So under Reading C they are the inherited, declared layer against which a built repair can be seen. Computed on templates (section 4.6): a repair built in the notebook with its draft held before its test is constructed; the same repair handed over is declared; one kept by a scorer from varied candidates is selected.

**(b) Claimed aims that can fail (the main use).** In the theory every construction that counts begins in an episode whose **recognized difficulty is a failure of a claimed aim**, or a conflict that meets a claimed aim only by failing a protected one, when the system represents it (L429; D13.8's CompleteCritical); and a created explanation is a **repair of a declared aim produced via the account** (L443 to L453; D14.7). A found rule held as a claimed aim ("this rule forecasts where the object will be") is exactly such an aim, and a world in which it fails is where a constructor has to begin. Computed (MC2 (S133), the motion rule on the 72 cases, all three decisions on):

| World | Where the rule's forecast is wrong (what an observer sees, (A) fails) | Violation (D12.7: the rule's law fails the world's at the pair) | Surprise (D12.7: selected, outside H, violated) | Example: prev 3, cur 5, lag 2 (rule says 9) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| straight-line (the teaching world) | 0 of 72 | 0 | 0 | world 9 |
| a constant pull (velocity grows by one each frame) | **72 of 72** | 72 | 72 at the machine's boundary (reading S); 0 where declared or constructed | world 12 |
| a wall at position 7 that bounces | **12 of 72** | 72 | the same | world 5 |

A rule that meets each changed world (the world's own law under each change) meets (E) on the same question: the repair exists to be built. Two things the numbers show for design. First, **the bounce is the better case**: the rule holds on most cases and fails on a few, so the failure is a failure of one claimed aim, not chaos, and noticing it needs an expectation written down before each action. Second, the theory's **violation is about the law, the observer's evidence about answers**: under the bounce the rule's law fails the world's at every case (72), but its answers are wrong at only 12; a subject sees the 12 and must infer the rest, which is why the design reads fidelity from sealed part-level questions as well as from forecasts. And **D12.7's surprise belongs to the machine, not the subject**: at the outside machine's boundary the rule is selected, so its failure outside H is surprise (L221, L223); handed to a subject whole, the same rule is declared there, so its failure is a violation and not surprise, and "a violation the system represents can be a recognized difficulty" (L223). The signal the design measures is that: the subject's written expectation, failed, and recorded as failed.

**(c) The copy control.** S130's rule card, handed over whole and expected declared (S130 E.8): in version 2 the inherited rules are a rule card for the old world, and the repaired rules handed over are the copy control.

**(d) A test case for the theory itself, now encoded** (section 1.4). It shows the theory classing a search its makers call a "constructor" as selection on the main reading, and it exposes where the verdict is decided: Prepares (I56), a primitive read by hand, and the open question S131 left for the owner (learning from a teacher's answers): if the outside machine's feedback steers its proposals, its rules sit where a classifier trained on labels sits.

**The main use is (b)**, because the goal is a constructor and the theory has exactly one route to a created explanation: a represented failure of a claimed aim, a target held before its criticism, an objection, a content-sensitive response, a repair of a declared aim, and an account deployed and on the route to the repair (L429, D13.8, D14.7). The found rules supply a simple, exact, testable claimed aim whose failure can be engineered, sealed and timed. (a) and (c) are what makes (b) measurable; (d) is what they teach the theory.

## 3. The application of our experiments

### 3.1 S126's knock-out: the test of any built repair

**What S126 showed** (results, sections 1, 4, 5): in population 2, cutting NOT out of most programs (copying kept) let the execution environment's NOT store refill from about 290 to about 4,300 within 100 updates (4,288 at update 100 in seed 2050; 3,912 in seed 2150), against about 280 in the unchanged and sham arms; restoring the removed instructions at update 100 drew it down to about 275 within 100 updates, while the same population reloaded without restoring stayed near 4,000 for those updates. A matched harmless change did nothing. The strict tests written before running were **not** met: the cut was never complete, the store never stayed above half full, and the run brought the capability back by selection (about 1,000 updates in population 2; about ten in population 1, where ORN's store rose fourfold for eight updates only). Verdict: mixed; a capability-specific, transient effect, out and back, through a route Avida's designers wrote.

**What carries over.** The shape: remove the thing claimed to do the work, expect the work to vanish; a sham of the same size, expect nothing; restore, expect it back. The outside report used the same shape on its bank. It tests a **causal role**, not a provenance (section 1.3), so in version 2 it is paired with the provenance test, not used instead of it.

**How it would be run on a built repair in the subject's notebook**, by S132's replay harness (S132 1.6; the stretch design of 1.4, where only the notebook and the action log carry anything from one stretch of 10 actions to the next):

1. **The saved point** is the end of the stretch in which the repair is first written (the program finds it: the first notebook version whose rule for the changed part differs from the card's).
2. **Five replays of the next stretch and of the door task**, each from that point, each changing one carrier:
   - **knock-out, log kept**: the notebook set back to its version just before the repair (the card's rule for that part restored), the action log kept whole. Expected: the repaired forecasts vanish at first and the card's forecasts return; *and* the subject may rebuild the repair from the failures in its log. This is S126's "the run defends the capability" in a new form: re-construction, measured by how many actions it takes and whether the rebuilt repair matches the first.
   - **knock-out, log cut**: the same notebook, and the action log cut back to before the first failed expectation. Expected: the card's forecasts return and the door task fails as it did before the repair. This is the clean knock-out S126 could not make (there the cut was never complete).
   - **sham**: an edit of the same length elsewhere in the notebook that changes no rule (an unrelated entry reworded). Expected: no change beyond the spread of the unaltered replays.
   - **restore**: from the knock-out-log-cut state, the repair's lines put back. Expected: the repaired forecasts and the door's opening return.
   - **unaltered**: the same point, nothing changed (two replays), as the noise floor, since sampling cannot be fixed on this route (S132 1.6, 7).
   Beside these, S130's **altered** replay (the repair's binding changed, for example the changed part's table permuted) tests ProducesVia: the door task should fail or go as the altered binding says.
3. **What is measured**: in each replay, the share of written expectations about the changed part that follow the repaired rule against the card's rule, and whether the door opens within 12 actions.
4. **Written now**: the knock-out (log cut) counts as removing the repair if, of the first 10 written expectations about the changed part, at least 8 follow the card's rule again (read against the unaltered replays' spread) and the door fails; the sham counts as harmless if it stays within that spread; the restore counts if the repaired share and the door return to within the spread of the original.

The difference from S126 is the point of using it here: the carrier is one notebook, the cut can be exact and complete, and nothing re-evolves it; whatever brings it back is the subject itself, working again, which is exactly what we want to see.

### 3.2 S113 and S118's execution environments

**What they are** (S113 settled; S118 results): S113's GROWING LIST environment adds the next harder level of tasks once a task of the top level is common; it ended with the most common capabilities in every seed (65, 48 and 55 at update 50,000). S118's P1 was told no function, only "any function of the three numbers counts the same, and a common one counts less", and its population came to compute 17, 12 and 21 functions commonly by update 20,000: **grade 2**, the class still written down in the checking code (the 251 functions of Avida's 77 tasks); **grade 3 was never reached** and cannot be in stock Avida.

**What they can and cannot give the goal.** They are a source of evolved knowledge whose target is named no further than one level up, and a selector that holds no problem: no rival accounts, no represented target, no account deployed to repair an aim (S130 B; S132 section 5). So they can supply material (evolved programs) and the "evolved" comparison arm, never the constructor. **Should anything from them be used now? No.** The evolved comparison arm is made more cheaply by S130's blind search (Python, under 3 CPU-hours, no C++, no reopening of the Avida line S81 closed; S132 section 5), and evolved programs are not material for a box or pixel world. The one idea worth keeping is a schedule, not a system: GROWING LIST's rule (add a harder level once the current one is mastered) is the shape a later version could use to order world changes; it is not needed for the first run.

### 3.3 S120 and S122: anticipation and timing

S120's anticipation pilot paid programs for handing back the number they were about to read; programs that add one appeared within a few hundred updates and 95 in 100 did it by the end (plain file 120). S122's interval programs keep where an event came within a frame. Both are **selected memories**: one fixed short relation found and kept across generations (S130 B). S128's derivation (f) placed the forecaster: a forecast is a prediction (D12.7's Pred), a computed mismatch is the system's sign of a failure, but the forecaster has no object layer and no model; and the forecaster Astra and Claude wrote is declared, so its failures are violations, never surprise. So **Avida's programs give the violation signal's shape** (a forecast set against what came) **but no account**: nothing behind the forecast could be blamed, revised or used to repair an aim. In version 2 the subject's written expectation before each action is that layer; the account behind it, held, blamed and repaired, is what Avida never had.

## 4. The design, version 2: the constructor experiment

Built on S130's plan (sections C to F) and S132's mechanics (the seven pieces, the stretches, the novel-parts arm, the replay harness), changed by the owner's goal: the subject **inherits** found rules handed over whole, the world changes so that one fails, and the subject must repair it.

### 4.1 The subject

A fresh Claude Code session, Opus 5.5, in the sandbox S132 planned and checked (S132 1.4, 1.9): its only tools the box's, no project context, every stream kept, no retries and no best-of-many; the work in stretches of 10 actions, each a fresh call seeing only the brief, the notebook and the action log. A non-language-model subject would differ in what it is and in what its construction would show (section 5).

### 4.2 The world: chosen and justified

Two candidates:

| | The sealed box with the first box's rules handed over | A small pixel world like the outside experiment's |
|---|---|---|
| The inherited rules | the rule card of box 1: each of six parts' rules, exact, and the wiring | the found rules themselves (square, disc, straight-line motion) |
| The failing case | box 2 = box 1 with one part replaced by a random rule table, matched in size, checked to match no named kind (S132 3.3) | a hidden cause changes the motion or a shape |
| The relay trap | closed for the changed part: no textbook names it, no copy of it exists before the session (sealed seed) | open: square, circle and straight-line motion are textbook, and so are their natural failures (a pull, a bounce, a drift); a language model would recall "add gravity" or "reflect at the wall". Closable only by making the hidden cause a random table, which makes it a box in a pixel costume |
| Interventions on parts | native: remove, jam, restore a slot (F1 on parts, S130 E.3) | would need new edits (freeze an object, hide a region) and a renderer and perceiver, supplied machinery that adds nothing to the theory's test |
| Build | S132's seven pieces, already planned, plus about 250 lines (section 4.7) | a new world generator, renderer, simulator and question set, roughly the size of S132's build again |
| Continuity with the owner's uploads | through the logic (inherited rule, failure, repair), not the content | direct |

**Chosen: the sealed box, with the first box's rules handed over**, because only it closes the relay trap without new machinery, gives interventions on parts natively, and reuses the whole of S132's build. The found rules themselves stay where section 2 put them: the model case, the test case of the theory, and the plain example (the motion rule meeting a wall) the owner can hold in mind. A pixel arm with the owner's found rules as the card and a random hidden table as the failure can follow if the owner wants continuity with the outside experiment; it is not needed for the first run.

### 4.3 The material, and what is declared before anything runs

- **Box 1 and its card.** A box drawn by S132's generator (three buttons, a dial, four lamps, a bell, a door; six slots, each a part; textbook or novel kinds as in S132's arms). Its **rule card** states every part's rule and the wiring, exactly; checked by program to predict every readout of box 1 on the whole contract.
- **Box 2: the change.** Box 1 with **one** slot's part replaced by a random rule table of the same number of inputs and states, rejected if it matches any named kind (S132 3.3's list) or has unreachable or indistinguishable states. Drawn from a sealed seed; which slot changed is sealed too.
- **Requirements on the change, checked by the generator**: (i) the card mispredicts at least one action in five of a standard sequence on box 2 (the failure will be met); (ii) the card still predicts at least half of those actions right (the failure is of one claimed aim, not of everything); (iii) the door task on box 2 cannot be done by following the card (the declared aim is unmet at the start), and can be done by a rule that knows the new part.
- **Boundary β** (L473, L522): inside, the session (the model as run, its contexts, the notebook, its own action log, its tool calls); outside, the box, the generator, the seeds, the scorer, the experimenter, the card's writer, and the model's training. The card enters β whole: declared at β (I200), part by part (D12.4). Continuity Ω: the chain of stretches with one notebook and one log.
- **Aims** (declared inputs; D14.1, D14.5): claimed aims O: **o_door**, "open the door on this box within the action budget"; **o_ex** (explanatory), "the notebook holds an account of this box that answers the declared questions on the contract". Protected aims P: **r_seen**, "the account predicts every readout already observed"; **r_rules**, the budgets. o_door is unmet at the start by design (iii).
- **The question and contract**, fixed now (S130 E.4): target the box; query the readouts after a sequence of actions; contract C: every sequence of up to 8 actions from the start, with no part removed, one or two removed, or one jammed. History H: at most 60 actions and 6 panel uses.
- **Sealed questions**, 40 per box, drawn from C minus the shapes the budget can reach: **20 on the changed part** (with other parts removed or jammed, so the changed part's rule alone decides the answer: F1 on that part), **20 whole-box** (long sequences, two removals, including the changed part: F2 and (A)). Answered after the session is frozen, from the notebook and log, with no box. Any the subject happens to try is dropped and reported.
- **What the subject is told**: the box, the tools, the budgets, the aims, the card ("an account of this box you were given"), and "before each action, write in the notebook what you expect to see". It is **not** told that the box has changed, which part, or what is counted.

### 4.4 The phases of one session

1. **Brief and card.** Nothing else enters afterwards except the box's readouts.
2. **Work** (up to 60 actions, 6 panel uses, stretches of 10). Expectations are written before actions; the simulator logs the order, so "expectation before the action that tests it" is computed by program.
3. **Close**: the final account; the session frozen.
4. **Sealed questions** (40), with the notebook and log; and again with the notebook only (what the written account carries by itself).
5. **The door** on box 2, 12 actions, from the frozen notebook (deployment: the account held and used again without being worked out afresh, L403).
6. **Replays** (section 3.1): knock-out with the log kept, knock-out with the log cut, sham, restore, unaltered twice, altered.

What we watch for, in order: the first failed expectation on the changed part (by program); the failure recorded in the subject's own words (R4); the card's part named as at fault with a reason (R5); the card's rule for that part kept in view as what is being repaired (I201: held inside the boundary before its criticism); two rival repairs stated, neither yet ruled out, and an action that splits them (R7, R8: a problem, D10.1); the repair written as a change to that part, the rest of the card kept (R6; D12.4); the door opened through the repair.

### 4.5 Measurements

**The five witnesses** (S125, reply 04), each measured:

| 2. A represented target in the episode | the card's rule for the changed part is in the notebook (held, entered whole) and a draft repair with an expectation from it is written **before** the action that tests it (log order, by program) |

### 4.6 What counts for and against, written now

**For** (at least 11 of the 16 main sessions meet all):
1. **Noticed**: the failure recorded in the subject's words within 3 actions of the first failed expectation on the changed part, and the card's part named as at fault with a reason (R4, R5).
2. **Held and repaired**: the repair written as a change to that part, the rest of the card kept, and the protected aim r_seen met at the close (the account predicts every readout already observed).
3. **Reach**: on the changed-part sealed questions at least 85 in 100 right and at least 40 points above both the no-world baseline and the card's own answers; whole-box at least 90 in 100.
4. **Deployed via the account**: the door opened within 12 actions; not opened after the knock-out with the log cut (ProducesVia).
5. **Knock-out**: the pattern of 3.1 (knock-out removes, sham within spread, restore returns).
6. **Provenance**: the changed part classed constructed, the card's parts declared, by the written test.
7. **Controls behave**: handed-over classed declared; search classed selected; no-failure with complete critical episodes in at most 2 of 8; no-world within 10 points of chance on the changed-part questions; renaming within spread.

**Against** (any one): the failure not noticed (the subject keeps the card and fails the door); the repaired account no better than the card on the changed-part questions; the knock-out changes nothing (the written repair does no work: emitted text, Argument 9, L614 to L616); the provenance test cannot tell the handed-over control from the main arm; episodes as frequent in the no-failure control as in the main arm.

**Mixed**: anything between, reported with each criterion's numbers. The thresholds are set now and may be changed only before the first main session, with the reason written (as S130 and S132).

### 4.8 What it would NOT show

- That the subject is a constructor in general, or in the theory's recursive and universal classes (Part XIII), or that it "understands" the box beyond the narrow retained use the door task asks (L403).
- That the method (conjecture, test, revise) was created in the session: it came from training, entered whole, and the theory does not ask it to be new (L405, L201; S132 3.5). What can be shown is that **the binding of the changed part, and the repair of a held account**, were worked out there.
- Anything about language models in the theory (S43), or the owner's S57 question (when the amorphous thing in a creative agent is knowledge), or the owner's three properties (S59).
- Anything about the outside machine beyond what section 1 says: the experiment does not run it.
- That a machine without a language model can do it (section 5).

### 4.9 How it differs from S130's box, and which to run first

S130's box tests **building an account where none was held**: the subject meets a box with no card, and the recognized difficulty must arise from its own first draft failing (the trap), at a moment no one can predict; it is the theory's "a first representation may be constructed from an available problem without prior observation of what it represents" (L405). Version 2 tests **repair of a held account**: the card is held inside the boundary before its criticism (I201), the claimed aim it supports fails at an action the program can find, and the repair is of a declared aim through an account (D14.7). That is the theory's own shape of a created explanation: a creative critical episode, a repair of a declared aim, an origin, an account meeting (E), deployed, and the repair produced via it.

**Recommendation: run version 2, the constructor experiment, first.** Reasons: it is the shape the theory itself gives for a created explanation, so a positive result bears directly on the goal; its difficulty arrives at a known action, so episodes are found and scored by program rather than hoped for; its provenance test sets declared (inherited) and constructed (built) parts side by side in one notebook and differs from its handed-over control in one part only, which is the sharpest separation either plan offers; and it costs about the same (about 9,000 calls against 9,800) on the same build, so S130's box from scratch can follow with little more building. Against it: inheriting the card makes the task easier than building from nothing, so a positive result here is weaker evidence of building a first account; that is what S130's box would add afterwards.

## 5. A machine without a language model

The owner's outside machine has no neural network. What would such a machine need, for its rules to be worked out in the theory's sense? Proposal only.

**What it would need, by the theory:**
1. **Hold a problem** (D10.1): two rival accounts of the same question, each offered in place of the other, conflicting at some admitted change, neither ruled out *for it*. Concretely: two candidate repairs of the failed part kept side by side, each with its predictions written out, and its own record of which observations have ruled out which. A population scored by a fit is not this (L315).
2. **Hold the target inside its boundary** (D12.2 with T′; I201): its current model of the world, as data it holds, and a prediction from that model written **before** each probe (the prediction layer of D12.6 and D12.7). Supplied measurements used as a pass-or-fail filter are not this; that is the outside machine's case.
3. **A repair loop driven by its own recognized failures** (L429; D13.8): when a prediction it made fails, it represents the failure as a failure of an aim it claimed, blames a part of its model with a reason (a criticism with target, alleged defect, premise and connection, D9.10), and changes that part (a response that uses the criticism as a reason, D9.11), keeping its protected aims (D14.2). Not driven by supplied teaching data: what it fits is what it chose to probe after its own failure.
4. **Build the repair, not draw it** (D13.3): a search over accounts whose binding it **prepares** from the failure's content (the new part's table filled from the cases that conflicted, unseen entries left open as rivals, a probe chosen to split them) and **keeps for explanatory use** (answers what-if questions and plans the declared aim from it). A complete expression drawn from an enumeration and kept if it fits is reading S of section 1, and selected.
5. **Deploy it to repair a declared aim through it** (D14.7): the door, or a route, opened because the repaired account says how.

**The naming trap** (S118). Whoever writes its model language, its critic and its edit operators names the class it can build in: the most it reaches is **construction within a named class**, the class contributed by its writers (L427: a routine written outside the boundary and run inside it is the system's own process; its content remains its writer's contribution). **Does the theory count that as worked out? Yes, under S124's Reading 3, "constructed on selected material"**, if the five witnesses hold inside its boundary: the class and the linking are inherited (as a child's hypothesis class and linking rules are, S124's T5 and T3), and the particular binding to this world is newly prepared, owned, with a held target, for explanatory use, and not relayed. The class itself would not have been created; nothing in the theory asks it to be. What separates such a machine from the outside one is item 2 and item 4 together: its own failure, held inside, shapes what is built; in the outside machine the supplied measurements only filter what the grammar proposes. That is exactly the S/K hinge of section 1, made into a design.

**What is known to exist of this kind, from the record only** (no web search):
- S120's check of Astra's reply 03 (sixteen lines of work on selecting without fixed objectives: novelty search, minimal-criterion and coevolution methods, POET and its successors, learning progress, compression progress, empowerment, MAP-Elites, POWERPLAY, Avida's host-parasite and anticipation work): **none holds explanatory problems or directs criticism at a part, and no selector meeting grade 3 was found**; the reply called its search bounded, not a census. S118's table of ways to select without naming (rows 1 to 9) reaches grade 2, with grade 3 only by parasites (not run) or new C++ (not built).
- S124 (Pinker, from published sources): a child's grammar read as constructed on selected material (Reading 3); and Pontes and colleagues (2020), evolved associative learning in a modified Avida, where S125's check of reply 04 found learning reported and **Build not demonstrated**.
- S130 D.2: a sketch of "a search given a represented target" (a held model, a prediction before each probe, blame by its own structure, two rival models, probes that split them), designed, never built; its cost about 1,000 lines plus the critic and 1 to 5 CPU-hours for the box (S130 D.3), with "its construction is ours, by design".
- The owner's outside machine: a search with a scorer, selected on the main reading.
- **Not on the record**: any running machine without a language model shown to meet the five witnesses; any selector whose standard is free of written rules.

**Cost, against running the language-model subject first.** Building one for the box world: about 1,000 to 1,500 lines (its model language for part tables, the prediction log, blame by removal and jamming, a rival-keeping repair builder, a split-choosing probe policy, the door planner), on top of the shared generator, simulator, scorer and knock-out, and 1 to 5 CPU-hours; no model calls. Its result would be clean to read (every line is ours, so its trace is exact) but weak as evidence of construction in general, since we would have built the constructor for this class. Running the language-model subject first costs about 9,000 calls and the same shared build, and answers the prior question: whether the experiment's tests can tell a built repair from a handed-over or a searched one at all. Then the machine without a language model is a natural later arm **in the same world, with the same tests**: a subject whose construction is ours, beside one whose construction is not, and the outside machine's kind (the search control) beside both.

## 7. What is unsure

- **Reading S or K** for the outside machine is a reading of four words in the report ("first-exact-fit", "fixed arithmetic grammar"); its records could settle it (section 1.3). Every provenance verdict in section 1 turns on it.
- **The encoding is ours** (Θ by hand, I90): small finite worlds in place of the 24-by-24 world with supplied poses; the tags of the history; the words as a chain of records. The verdict on (E) does not depend on the size; the provenance verdicts depend on the tags and on Prepares.
- **Everything about the outside experiment is its own report**, unverified: the 788 checks, the interventions, the replays. Its linked files were not uploaded.
- **Violation at the level of the law against the answers** (section 2.2): the copy counts a violation wherever the component's law differs at the pair, so a bounce is a violation at all 72 cases though the forecast is wrong at 12. Which of the two a design should count as "the rule failed" is our choice (we score both).
- **The templates of section 4.5** show the provenance test separates arms as encoded; real notebooks may not fall cleanly into them, and coding them is by hand with a written sheet (S130 F, S132 1.8).
- **The knock-out with the log kept** may be confounded: a subject that rebuilds the repair quickly could look as if the knock-out failed; the log-cut knock-out is the clean one, and both are reported.
- **The change requirements** (one action in five mispredicted; half still right) are guesses at a level that makes the failure met but not overwhelming; the dry run should check them.
- **Budget figures** are S132's rates applied to new arms; the dry run measures the real cost.
- **Whether the inherited card helps or hinders** the subject's repair (it may anchor the subject to the old rule) is part of what the run would show, not a defect of the design.
