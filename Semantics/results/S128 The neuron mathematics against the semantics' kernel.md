### 1.2 Avida's stores are the report's fading trace: the rule from the source, and the confirming run

**The rule, from the code (47f13dad).**

- `main/cPopulation.cc` line 443: a global resource's decay is `1.0 - outflow`.
- `main/cResourceCount.cc` lines 35-37 and 336-343: the store is advanced in steps of `UPDATE_STEP` = 1/10,000 of an update; in each step it is multiplied by `pow(decay, UPDATE_STEP)` and `inflow × UPDATE_STEP` is added (tables precomputed for up to 100 steps, lines 342-343, and applied in blocks, lines 814-825).
- `targets/avida/Avida2Driver.cc` lines 107-114 and `cPopulation.cc` lines 5792, 5855: the store's clock advances by 1/(update size) for every instruction executed, so by one update per update **while programs run**; the loop stops at once if the world is empty, so **an empty world's stores do not move at all**. (This is why the run below injects one program that performs no task.)
- `main/cEnvironment.cc` `DoProcesses`, lines 1610-1740 (read from 1610): a task performance paid from a store takes `consumed = min(frac × R, max)` (lines 1674-1704; the pay, `consumed × value`, line 1729; quality factors aside) and the pay is `consumed × value` doublings for `type=pow`. In S113's COMMON TASKS PAY LESS (`tools/s113_run_the_avida_execution_environments.py`, `environment_text`): inflow 100, outflow 0.01, `frac=0.0025`, `max=1.0`, so a performance takes 1.0 and pays fully when R ≥ 400, and takes 0.0025 R, paying that fraction, below.

**The law that follows.** Between performances, with k = −ln(1 − outflow):

R(t) = R\* + (R(0) − R\*) e^(−k t),  R\* = inflow / k

For S113: k = 0.0100503 per update, **time constant 1/k = 99.50 updates**, half-life 68.97 updates, **R\* = 9,949.92** (the step form gives 9,949.92 as well; the per-update rule R ← 0.99 R + 100 that a reader might assume gives 10,000). S115's correction of reply 08 (pools at inflow ÷ (−ln 0.9) = 1,708, not 1,800) is the same law. Written as the report writes its trace: the **depletion** M = R\* − R obeys M(t + dt) = M(t) e^(−dt/τ), τ = 99.5 updates, and each performance adds min(frac R, max) to M. The pay threshold R ≥ 400 is the report's threshold: after use stops at R(0) < 400, the store pays less than fully exactly while M(t)/M(0) > θ with θ = (R\* − 400)/(R\* − R(0)), that is, while the time since the last use is under −τ ln θ = τ ln((R\* − R(0))/(R\* − 400)): **1.14 updates from R(0) = 290, 4.08 from 0**. The pay recovers within a few updates; the store remembers for about a hundred.

**Already in the record.** S126, one cut program alone in a world for 300 updates from stores at 10,000: 9,952 (the law gives 9,952.4). S118: the stores of functions no program computed at 9,950; S113 (Xa4): up to 9,950 for tasks never common. S115: 1,708.

### 1.4 The six candidate findings, judged

- **(i) Avida's stores are the report's decaying trace. Holds.** The depletion R\* − R fades exactly as e^(−t/τ) between uses (τ = 99.5 updates for S113's stores), with a threshold at R = 400; the rule is in the code (section 1.2) and the run matches it to Avida's printed precision. What differs from the report's trace: the store fades toward a full level, not toward zero; its "reset" is not to one but a draw of min(0.0025 R, 1.0) per use; and many programs draw on one store.
- **(ii) COMMON TASKS PAY LESS and S118's P1 are tiring. Holds, at the execution environment's level.** In balance, with n performances per update all below the threshold, the store sits at R = inflow / (k + 0.0025 n), so the pay exponent per performance is level × min(1, 100 / (n + 4.02)): no tiring at all until about 96 performances per update, then a hyperbolic fall (S126's NOT at about 280 gives n ≈ 139 and an exponent of 0.70; S113's 0.59 to 0.86). Rest restores it (S126). What differs: the unit that tires is a task's store, shared by every program; the stimulus is the program population's performances, not one input line. (Below the threshold the law is the same as a standard model of short-term synaptic depression, a used fraction and an exponential recovery; named from memory, not checked here.)
- **(iii) The growing list is a chain of set-only switches. Holds, with the S76 caveat.** `next_unlocked` in `tools/s113_run_the_avida_execution_environments.py` (lines 134-145) returns the same level or one more, never less: a level is added after a piece when any task of the top rewarded level is performed by at least 10% of the program population, at most one per piece, starting from level 1 (NOT and NAND). Seed 1: levels 2 to 9 at updates 2,000 to 9,000; seed 2: 2 to 5 at 2,000 to 5,000, 6 at 9,000, 7 at 10,000, 8 at 11,000; seed 3: 2 to 10 at 2,000 to 10,000 (S113 results after the cross-examination, section 2). Claude's runner switched them between pieces, not Avida (S76). There is no reset, so it is not bistable in the report's sense (set and reset pulses); it is a ratchet.
- **(iv) S126's return of NOT is a population-level likeness of rebound, not a unit's rebound. Holds as a likeness, and better called recovery from tiring.** Rebound needs suppression to charge a state that makes release a trigger. The store is such a state: under the cut it filled. Left alone, NOT came back by selection and the draw rose smoothly to the balance level (10 to 97 per update over about 1,000 updates), with no overshoot: recovery. When Claude's script put the instructions back, the draw was 1.3 to 2.2 times the balance for about 50 updates (section 1.2): the stored excess was spent faster than balance allows, which is the nearest thing in the record to rebound's burst after release. It was triggered by an intervention, in a population, not by a unit's own state.
- **(v) The add-one programs carry a one-step memory. Holds** (patched Avida, S120): the number read is held in a register, incremented and written out before the next read; nothing older is kept.
- **(vi) The interval programs carry a within-frame memory, once. Holds** (patched Avida, S122, one seed, the paid run only): A's place in the frame is held two or three reads; the processor is reset at each division (`cHardwareCPU.cc` 1836, checked in S125, check 01 row 7 and check 04 row 2; the brief names S120's reply 02 check, but the line was checked in S125), so the memory never outlasts a copy cycle.

### 2.2 Candidate by candidate

**(a) Memoryless against stateful; the window.**
*Report:* y = f(x) against s(t+1) = F(s(t), x(t), Δt), y(t) = G(s(t), x(t)); r > θ ⇔ elapsed < −τ ln θ.
*Semantics:* an organization D = (V, (X_v), J, (V_j), B, A, ·, 1, L) (D1.1, core 50; L88-L94); "Values of ports may be paths, functions, fields, mathematical structures or histories" (L105); FC11, "Roles are relative to A; families are relative to C"; Boundary (D), L302, D7.4 (core 283), over a declared family 𝒱 of organization edits.
*Derivation.* Write the stateful system as an organization whose input port takes the path x(·) (L105), or unrolled, with ports x_t, s_t, y_t and one component F_t per step. Let p ask for y at t, on a contract C. A memoryless candidate E₀ (y_t = f(x_t)) meets (A) on C only if no two pairs of C set the same present input and different earlier inputs while the target's answers differ: equal inputs to f give equal outputs (Argument 9's step, L616).
