## (b) The counterexample

**FC23 (b) · L273.** A lookup whose background component has the empty relation at both pairs, so \(E\)'s answer is undetermined at both points and NC2 has no contrast. It is a counterexample to the claim's own phrasing ("through NC1 only"), resting on U3 and on the claim's premise; it is not a counterexample to the text. L273 says the lookup fails non-circular dependence: this lookup meets neither NC1 nor NC2, so the sentence stands. No text change removes it, because it does not tell against the text; at claim level the premise "the other components are jointly satisfiable at both points" removes it, and with that premise the claim held on every model tried. Its lesson for the text: NC2 alone does not exclude a lookup, so the exclusion of "p because p" rests on the "does not appear" sentence alone — where the vagueness is.
