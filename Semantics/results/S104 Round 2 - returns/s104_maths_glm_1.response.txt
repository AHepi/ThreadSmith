## (d) Attacks on the claims that held

**FC01 · L100, L103.** Monotonicity is a set-theoretic identity from (O): z ∈ Sol_D(a,b) iff every restriction lands in the corresponding relation; enlarging relations cannot remove such z, for arbitrary and infinite domains alike. It cannot be broken while (O) and I03 stand; the only fork, I03's alternative (b), is closed by L103 itself. Hand extension of the witness: delete every component (G = J) and Sol_{D−J}(a,b) = X_D for all (a,b). So a target in which, per L339, the needed ports and components are absent answers every query over its full domain: under I03 absence is maximally permissive and cannot, through (O) alone, rule anything out. Hand model for the look: V = {p}, X_p = {0,1}, J = {c}, V_c = {p}, L_c(1,b) = ∅. Sol_D(1,b) = ∅, so a query on p answers ⊥; D−{c} gives Sol = {0,1} and the query is determined. This is the program's witness reproduced by hand. It tells against NC2's wording only (a line not given here), not against any sentence of this part.

**FC02 · L109, L325.** (b) cannot fail while D2.3 is as written — A does not occur in it; the substantive forks are inside I05 and are decided in (a) by the pole itself (hand model there: V = {u,v}, L_j(1,b) = {(0,0)} separates at-most-one from exactly-one, and exactly-one would strip c_L of its output). For (c), a hand attack on the claim's own wording: take A exactly as L325 describes — interventions on H and θ, plus 1. Then Set_L = ∅ (c_L's relation is not a slice), so asg(L) is undefined, and D2.5 carries nothing about L except L ∉ Input. Positively, L's tie to its component under this A rests on output status, not on asg — the opposite emphasis of the claim's last clause. The claim as computed rests on the pole encoding's fuller A (I65, I92; not given here). No text change beyond the L325 rewrite, which states the carrying without the overstatement. (a) is definitional; its witness uses non-identity edits and survives I80's exclusion.

**FC11 · L109, L113.** Both halves hold by construction and cannot be attacked as formal statements. The attack lands on the sentence behind them. Model one: the pole, C = {(1, b0)} — an agent contracting to do nothing. Observation(H) can hold through A (a recalibration of c_H is admitted) while every signature clause under C is vacuous; the "that is" clause equates something true with something C leaves unsettled, which is exactly FC11's content. Model two, the sharper one: the pole, C containing one intervention h that sets H, reading R-i. Then h alters L_{c_H} and leaves asg(θ)'s relation alone, so h ∈ Obs(H, θ); Causal_C(c_H) requires Changes under Set_H and Inv under Obs, i.e. L_{c_H} both changes and does not change under h — unsatisfiable whatever the relations are. So under R-i the round-1 L123 cannot hold in any contract containing an intervention on the output port. This is why the L109 rewrite in (c) adopts R-ii: with setting edits of a port excluded from its own observation edits, the contradiction disappears and FC11's separation is the whole story.

Reasons: by L103 a changed rule is a changed component, so any edit altering the component already replaces it and the clause was redundant once Changes under Set_{o_j} is required; and it named an edit class no other line of the group names. No wording proposed for L123 itself.

**Matter 5 · L109.** Yes, the text needs a change. The "that is" clause equates a role read off A with a signature defined on C ⊆ A × B, and FC11's own computation (Input(v) with ¬Causal_C(asg(v))) shows the two come apart. Replace the clause with a relativized gloss, folded into the full L109 rewrite of (c):

> L109 | No role assignment is supplied. A port \(v\) is an **input** under \(A\) when \(A\) contains an edit other than the identity that sets \(v\) directly — directly, by replacing only the component assigning \(v\) and leaving every other component as it is. A port is an **output** of component \(j\) when its value is determined by \(L_j\) at the identity edit, given the other ports of \(V_j\) across \(B\) — determined as at most one value. A port is an **observation** when \(A\) contains an edit that alters the relation reporting it without altering the port it reports in the same footprint; an edit that sets the port itself alters what it reports and does not count; under a contract \(C\) this role is read through the measurement signature of the component assigning it (below).

*(Writes in I05, I06, I80; the final clause closes matter 5 by replacing the equation with a contract-relative reading.)*

> L113 | Fix an organization \(D\) and a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\) (Part III). A signature changes under a class of edits when some edit of that class in \(C\) alters the relation from what it is at the identity edit at the same boundary, and is invariant under it otherwise; invariance holds vacuously where \(C\) contains no edit of that class.
