## (b) The counterexample

**FC20 · L219–L220, L250.** The counterexample (κ = {0:0, 1:0}, \(\operatorname{Ans}_p=\bot\), \(\operatorname{Ans}_E=0\)) tells against the inventions, not the text. The text nowhere says what a port translation does to values; value maps are I14's, the merging of 0 and 1 is I14's allowance of non-injective maps, and κ(⊥)=⊥ is U1, recorded nowhere. It also survives I81's alternative (a): a direct partial π merging the two values gives the same model, so it is not an artifact of port-induced π. Two remedies, in order of preference. First, tighten the invention: require I14's value maps to be injective. Reasons why this and not that: a merging κ makes two distinct target valuations one candidate valuation — it hides a difference, which is exactly the hiding L245 accuses (F1) of preventing; and under injective κ, FC96(ii) holds without its recoding caveat (between equal finite value domains, \(\rho=\kappa\circ\kappa'^{-1}\) always exists), and Violation and Violation⁺ coincide wherever π's action on the designated port is injective — FC20's own third search says so. Nothing in the text needs to change for this. Second, if the owner wants the text to settle it, add to L189 (this writes I14 into the text, and says so):

> L189 | where \(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) on the stated scope, \(\tau\) translates edits, \(\sigma\) translates boundaries, and \(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\) with a port translation that carries distinct values to distinct values.

with the I16 change below folded into the same line if both are adopted.

> L231 | An explanatory candidate for question \(p\) is an organization \(E\), a transport \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\) from \(D\) to \(E\), and an identified set \(\Gamma\) of active commitments in \(E\). The commitments \(\Gamma\) are the active components of \(E\), the components the candidate offers as doing the work, whether or not anyone has described their work; the boundary conditions of \(E\) and the components of \(E\) outside \(\Gamma\), including any of them that assigns an input, belong to the named background of Part VI.

(This writes I15 into the text.)

**I16 · L189.** Not settled: "on the stated scope" names no scope, and the arrow says total. It should be settled, because L315 needs pairs outside \(C\) carried. Proposal (writes I16 and part of I17 into the text):

> L189 | where \(\pi:X_D\rightharpoonup X_E\) is defined on the stated scope, at least on \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) at every admitted pair \((a,b)\) whose edit and boundary \(\tau\) and \(\sigma\) translate, \(\tau\) translates edits, \(\sigma\) translates boundaries, and \(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\) with a port translation.

**I17 · L315.** Partly settled: "in \(C\) or outside it" rules out reading 'translates' as 'is in \(C\)' (alternative (b)). The partiality of τ and σ and the meaning of 'translates' are not settled. Proposal, on the fragment as quoted (I rely on the rest of L315's sentence standing as it is):

**I18 and I84 · L242.** Not settled: the equation \(\tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1)\) presumes \(\tau\) total on composites, and with partial τ (I17) the clause's reach is open. The clause should ask nothing of composites outside the domain. Proposal, a sentence added after L242 (writes I84(a) into the text):

> after L242 | The clause \(\tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1)\) is required when \(a_2\), \(a_1\) and \(a_2a_1\) lie in the domain of \(\tau\); it asks nothing of other composites, and it is a condition on \(\tau\) as a whole, not at a pair.
