# Report, part 10 of 17: Conflict, rivals, conflict with a claim

## (a) The definitions and claims against the sentences

**D8.1 · L315.** The definition matches the sentence: meeting under hypothetical relations, R a relation on each component's footprint, (F1), (F2) and (A) all three at the pair. One part of the invention is not in the sentence: that the homomorphism clause is left out at a pair (I18, given in another part; I rely on the register's remark). See I19 below.

**D8.2 · L315.** Faithful. The two disjuncts mirror the sentence's "answers differ, or each could meet under some relations and no such relations let both", and the existence conditions ("∃R Meets(ℰ,R) ∧ ∃R' Meets(ℰ',R')") correctly carry the sentence's "each of them could meet … under some relations". Note that the second disjunct's "no such relations let both" already covers answer-difference when both can meet (meeting anchors the answer by (A)); the first disjunct does its work only where one or both can meet under no relations — which is what the sentence's "or" intends. The words should stand.

**D8.3 · L315.** Two small partings. (i) The sentence says rivals conflict "at some admitted edit–boundary pair"; D8.3 says only "some pair both translate". The maths should carry "admitted"; the words stand. (ii) The symmetry of the offer is settled by the words: "one of them has been offered as an answer to p in place of the other" — "one of them" is unbiased, so either direction makes rivals. I33's symmetric reading is the text's own, not an invention on that point; what is invented is only what an offering *is* (see I33).

**D8.4 · L315.** Faithful, and consistent with S27: the claim enters as what it allows, with no requirement that anyone carry its explanation; the premise that it speaks of the target is kept separate from conflict itself ("It is not enough for a creative agent to do anything about it though").

**D8.5 · L315.** This is where the maths and the words part company, and the words should stand. D8.5 lacks the existence condition its two neighbours have. D8.2 requires each candidate to meet under *some* relations; D8.6 requires *some* R to let both meet; D8.5's second disjunct — "every R with Meets_ab(ℰ,R) lies outside Allow_χ(a,b)" — is vacuously true when no R meets, and its first disjunct is vacuously true when no R yields the candidate's answer. So a candidate that can meet under no relations at a pair, or whose answer no relations give, *conflicts with every claim there*, including a claim that allows every behaviour. That contradicts the sentence: a claim that excludes nothing cannot exclude "what the candidate's organization and transport give there". The words, read with their presupposition ("every relation … under which it could meet" presupposes that it could), are right; the formalization dropped the presupposition. This is a choice no register entry records — section 6 lists none for this part, and this one bears directly on FC52. Exact change (it also makes the two disjuncts independent, as the sentence has them):

> A candidate **conflicts with** a claim \(\chi\) at an admitted pair of the target that its transport translates, in \(C\) or outside it, when \(\chi\) excludes what the candidate's organization and transport give there: its answer — some relations of the target's components would give that answer there, and none that \(\chi\) allows would — or it could meet (F1), (F2) and (A) there under some relations of the target's components, and \(\chi\) excludes every such relation.

**D8.6 · L315.** Faithful, existence condition included. The formalization correctly omits any requirement that each candidate can meet under χ alone; see I35.

**FC43 · L317.** The formal statement is mostly about Conf and Riv; the sentence quoted ("the rival is then easy to vary too") is about easy to vary, whose definitions (I37) live in another part and are not given here. On the lines given here the sentence is only partly formalized; the symmetry leg is sound (see (d)), the ETV leg cannot be judged from this part.

**FC44 · L315.** The formal statement says what the sentence says under I36; see (b).

**FC45 · L315.** Both parts faithful. Part (b) is holds-by-construction and needs no search; part (a) is arguably also by construction (any R meeting one meets the other through φ), so the search corroborates rather than tests — its real content is I20/I81's equivariance (see (d)).

**FC52 · L315.** The "result" is an artifact of the vacuity in D8.5: because meeting anchors the answer, answer-exclusion implies meeting-exclusion *only when the meeting-set is nonempty or the implication runs through vacuity*. Once D8.5 is repaired as above, the first disjunct no longer implies the second, and FC52 fails (model in (d)). This is right: the sentence offers two independent conditions and should keep both. The words stand; the maths changes.

**FC53 · L315.** Faithful on both legs, and matches the owner's words: conflict is identified with no test and no agent action (S27), ruling out is a choice needing the premise (S28: "That 'ruling out' is a choice that was made"), and nothing is settled. Note the computation rests on I90 (histories and provenance set by hand, per section 1) and I88's finite argument set; it is an encoding check, not a search.

**FC54 · L315.** Faithful, and the implication ConfG_χ(a,b) ⇒ ¬Conf(a,b) holds by logic, not by smallness of models: ConfG needs an R letting both meet, which equalizes answers and defeats both disjuncts of Conf at that pair. The concluding clause ("a pair of rivals given χ that conflicts at no other pair is not a pair of rivals") matches "for one who does not [tentatively accept χ], they pose none on that account".

## (b) The counterexample

**FC44's witness · L315.** The witness (one subnetwork {h_p0} counterpart to k in both candidates through different port translations, v := p0 and v := p1, different relations, both meeting (F1) at (1,b0)) refutes the reading "one counterpart = the same subnetwork, whatever the port translations". It does not refute I36's reading, under which FC44 held on all models tried. So it tells neither against the text's claim nor against I36; it tells against leaving the words as they stand, because the words do not fix the reading, and on the loose reading the sentence is false: the target component's one relation can project to two different candidate relations when the translations differ. Exact change (writes I36 into the text; named under (c)):

> Two candidates **conflict** at such a pair \((a,b)\) when their answers there differ, or when each of them could meet (F1), (F2) and (A) there under some relations of the target's components at that pair, each a relation on the component's footprint (Part II), and no such relations let both, as none do when two of their active components have one counterpart — the same subnetwork of the target, their ports carried by translations that agree up to a bijection of ports with matching value maps — and their relations there differ under that bijection.

No other entry in section 4 is a counterexample; FC52's "holds" is discussed in (a) and (d).

## (c) The inventions in full

**I19 · L315.** The text settles the range of R: "under some relations of the target's components at that pair, each a relation on the component's footprint" fixes R to assignments of relations on footprints, and "it does not turn on what any physics admits" rules out other choice (b); the subjunctive "could meet … there under some relations" speaks of hypothetical alternatives at that pair, not the target's relations at other pairs, which rules out (c). What the text does not settle is whether meeting at a single pair includes clauses of (F1) that speak beyond the pair (the homomorphism clause, I18, given in another part). If the part carrying I18 does not settle it, the text should say:

> Meeting (F1), (F2) and (A) at a pair under hypothetical relations takes those conditions as they hold at that pair alone; nothing they say of other pairs, or of whole edits, is in question there.

This writes I19's exclusion of the homomorphism clause into the text (with I18); I rely on the register's remark about I18, whose own entry I have not seen.

**I33 · L315.** The text settles symmetry ("one of them … in place of the other") but not what an offering is; the register notes L522 does not list offers, a line not given here. Consistently with S20 (no listing; the set need not be listable) and S21 (whatever happens is the choice of the person), the text should carry:

> Whether one candidate has been offered in place of another is a matter of record, not of the semantics: someone put it forward as an answer to \(p\) in place of the other, and the semantics carries that fact and nothing more; no list of offerings is supposed either.

This writes I33's choice (a record, not an event read through the physical module) into the text. Instantiating or transforming the record is physical, per S26; the offering itself is not (S25).

**I34 · L315.** The text settles one of the three shapes: answers-only exclusion (other choice (b)) cannot express "a candidate whose organization gives perpetual motion" — the sentence excludes behaviours, not only answers, so (b) is out. Between (a) (a sentence with its own semantics) and the invented shape (a set of allowed behaviours, given with the claim) the text should stay open: S27 puts the claim's use outside the process ("It's also a detail that exists outside the process") and allows an agent to carry "perpetual motion is impossible" as a given without its explanation. The premise that χ speaks of the target is already textual ("the premise that \(\chi\) speaks of the target under that pair's edit"). No wording proposed.

**I35 · L315.** The text settles it. "Two candidates that some relations of the target's components would let both meet (F1), (F2) and (A) at a pair, when \(\chi\) excludes every such relation, conflict there given \(\chi\)" conditions conflict-given-χ only on the jointly-meeting relations, and says nothing of what each can meet under χ alone — exactly I35. Other choice (a) is not the text's. No change.

**I36 · L315.** Not settled by the text; the witness under (b) shows the loose reading false. The wording proposed there writes I36 into the text, and should be adopted.

**I86 · L315.** A device of the search, not a reading of the text: the text's rivals require an actual offering, and the program's setting Offered to hold of every examined pair tests conflict-symmetry over all conflicting pairs. It should stay as it is, but see (d) on what FC43's result is then worth.

**I88 · L397.** The text settles that an argument has a step: "An argument is an argument tree: argument steps whose leaves are premises" rules out other choice (b), a bare premise as an argument. The finite range of X_j is a bound of the search and should stay open as such; no text change.

## (d) Attacks on the claims that held

**FC52 · L315 — broken by hand.** Take one target component j, footprint V_j = {v}, X_v = {0}, one pair (1,b0), one value of the fixed query. Let ℰ have k ∈ Γ with L^E_k(τ(1),σ(b0)) a relation containing a tuple with value 1 — no R_j ⊆ ∏_{V_j} X_v projects to it, so F1 fails for every R and Meets_ab(ℰ,R) is empty for all R. Let Ans_E(τ(1),σ(b0)) = q, and suppose some R0 gives answer q. Let χ be the permissive claim: Allow_χ(1,b0) = every assignment. Then D8.5 as written gives ConfCl(ℰ,χ;1,b0) — χ excludes nothing, yet conflicts — through vacuity. Now change χ to Allow_χ = all R except those with answer q: the first disjunct is true, and under the repaired D8.5 the second is false (no R meets at all), so FC52's implication fails. Both breaks rest only on the missing existence condition, well inside I77's bounds; the search missed them because no claim asked. The sentence needs no change beyond the wording in (a); the maths does.

**FC43 · L317.** The symmetry of Conf holds by logic, not by search: BothMeet is symmetric in its conjuncts, and the answer disjunct is an inequality. Riv's symmetry, under I86, is holds-by-construction — every examined pair was offered both ways — so the search tested nothing about it; what the sentence adds, "the rival is then easy to vary too", rests on I37 (another part) and on fixing the assessor: if easy to vary is relative to an assessor, the unqualified "too" needs the assessor to be the same one, and nothing in the lines given here fixes that. No break found on the lines here; flagged, not proposed.

**FC45 · L315.** No break found. The proof of (a) needs that meeting transfers under φ: Meets_ab(ℰ,R) ⟺ Meets_ab(ℰ',φ·R). That is exactly I20's and I81's equivariance (computed ports carried by the bijection); a non-equivariant query application under I20 would hide a counterexample beyond the families searched. I rely on those entries, given in another part, for the transfer; within everything given here the claim stands.

**FC53 · L315.** Beyond I88's bounds, an assessor could string χ into an argument lacking the Applies premise — but by the text's own sentence such an argument "rules out no candidate there", so no counterexample to the sentence is available that way; only the maths' claim ("no argument in X_j") is bounded by I88. The pendulum leg (b) rests on I90's hand-set histories; with a different provenance for χ in j's accepted set, an argument from χ might become available, which would affect (a) and not (b). No break of the sentences.

**FC54 · L315.** Holds by logic on any model, unbounded. The one genuine pressure point is I35: under its other choice (a) (each can meet under allowed relations, none lets both), rivals given χ could also conflict plainly, and FC54 would fail. But the text settles I35 as invented (quoted under (c)), so the pressure tells against the alternative, not against text or claim. A further consequence worth recording, not repaired: a χ excluding every behaviour at a pair makes any two candidates that could jointly meet there rivals given χ — including recodings of one candidate that conflict nowhere plainly. That is the text's own consequence (an agent's costly gamble, S27), and per S21 and S28 nothing should be said about what must then happen; no change proposed.
