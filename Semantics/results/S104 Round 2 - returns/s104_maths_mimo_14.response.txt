## (a)

**D12.7 · L220** — the scope of "fidelity fails". The words tie violation to a defined condition, fidelity (D5.7, not printed here; I rely on the maths' naming of (F1), (F2) and (A)). The maths carries two readings, Viol = ¬(F1 ∧ F2eq) (narrow, I50) and Viol⁺ adding ¬(A). Two partings. (i) The narrow reading drops the homomorphism clause of (F2), which I50 itself lists as a live alternative; if fidelity as defined includes hom, the narrow reading silently narrows a defined term. (ii) Viol⁺ imports (A), a failed prediction, which the words do not name at L220. The words should stand: a violation is the failure of the defined fidelity condition at the pair, hom clause included, and (A) is not part of it. Why this and not that: "fidelity" is defined elsewhere and its scope is fixed there, not at L220; and decision S27 has explanations conflict "at the level of explanation without ever having to be tested against reality", which a violation reading that needs a wrong answer against the world would cut across. No change of wording; the maths must record which condition it carries.

**D12.7 · L217, L221** — the binding of the selection parameters. The formula writes Surp(t;a,b) :⟺ Sel(t; 𝒯, μ, H) for some 𝒯, μ, H …; the words give t one history: `> L217 | Let \(t\) be a transport to the simulation layer \(S\), with contract \(C\) and, where \(t\) is selected, history \(H\).` and `> L193 | … exactly one of three provenances, determined by its history in the physical module`. The words stand: H is t's own selection history, fixed by its history. The existential reading is not a variant of the text but a different sentence. (The clause "(a,b) ∉ H" in the formula does no work, since Viol already puts (a,b) outside every history on which t survives; the words may keep it, it states content.)

**D12.8 · L225** — the two responses. Three partings. (i) The formula extends H by exactly the failing pair, H ∪ {(a,b)}; the words say only "extends the history H" and do not say by what. (ii) The formula's μ*, reflexive-transitive, lets t' = t; the words say "the transport is re-tuned", which does not fix whether μ must change t. (iii) The words disjoin a new organization and a new transport; the formula disjoins what is new about one transport t'' (t'' or its codomain) and so has no place for a response that introduces an organization with no transport. On (i) the words should change, because without the extension a "response" is not a response to anything; the change writes into the text the choice the formula made (recorded under H08), and says so. On (ii) leave open: nothing in this part turns on whether μ may return t. On (iii) the words stand.

```
> L225 | A **selection response** to a failure at \((a,b)\) extends the history \(H\) of a selected transport by \((a,b)\) and lets \(\mu\) act: the transport is re-tuned within the population. A **construction response** introduces a new organization or a new transport with a construction trace. Only the second response introduces content that is originative under Part X.
```

**FC83 · L225** (formal statement against sentence). The sentence predicates originativity of the response ("Only the second can be originative"); the formal statement predicates Origin of the content c' that t' carries to. These are different sentences. The words in their intent stand; the formula does not. The third sentence of the line above fixes the mismatch by predicating originativity of the content a response introduces.

Nothing to add: D12.1, D12.2, D12.6, D13.3, Pred's extension (I51).

**FC82 · L225.** Two things weaken this counterexample. It rests on the same one-history model as FC78, so it shows only that Sel and Con can hold together — the gap just dealt with. **FC83 · L225.** Here the counterexample survives exclusivity: nothing ties Build's subhistory to the selection history, so a transport reached by a selection response can carry content that meets Origin. ## (d)

**FC80 · L572, L574.** Three hand attacks. (i) Against (b), under I71's other reading of "value". Model: one port of two values, edits a1, a2, boundary b0, C = {(a1,b0),(a2,b0)}, H = {(a1,b0)}, 𝒯 = {t, t'} faithful on H and giving the same valuation at (a2,b0). Read "the value at (a,b)" as the value the pair takes in the target rather than the transport's own valuation: target D1 gives 0 at (a2,b0), D2 gives 1. The survivors agree with each other while the value at (a2,b0) differs between D1 and D2, so it is a function of (𝒯, H, D), not of (𝒯, H). (ii) Against (a) and (c), under I02's alternative, an action law. Model: valuations {0,1} on one port, edits a1, a2 with the law that the value at (a2,b0) equals the value at (a1,b0); H = {(a2,b0)}. Any two transports faithful on H agree at (a1,b0), so nothing is underdetermined there: an alteration at one pair forces the other, and the step "altered at one pair gives another admitted relation" (I02) is exactly what the small witness needs. The claim survives only with a condition: the orbit of (a,b) under the law must avoid H. (iii) Beyond the bounds: with composite edits and no law linking a composite's relation to its parts' relations (I02 again), two survivors can agree at every part and differ at the composite pair outside H — the witness's shape is not confined to the three-port models tried.

**FC81 · L580, L223.** Against (a) and (c), which are "holds by construction" only because Surp's formula puts (a,b) in C∖H. Under H07's "for some" binding, let t's own history be H = C (both pairs of C) and let there be a second population in which t survives on H' = {(a1,b0)}; t fails at (a2,b0) ∉ H'. Then Surp holds on the witness H', while t's own history exhausts C. So "A system can be surprised only if it holds a transport selected on a history \(H\) strictly smaller than its contract \(C\)" is broken by the binding, not by the words.
