## (d) Attacking the claims that held

**FC79 · L41.** The claim holds by construction because Faithful takes no 𝒯, μ or H; the text backs the separation in the same line. Two attacks. (1) Read a selected transport's contract as its history, C := H: then "faithful on changes it was never selected against" is vacuous, not false — the line survives by saying nothing. This is a reading a reader could take, and nothing quoted forbids it; if the text wants L41 to have content for selected transports, it needs a guard. Proposal, replacing L41:

> L41 | Whether that transport is faithful on changes it was never selected against turns on the transport and the target alone, not on whether it survived.

```
Whether that transport is faithful on changes it was never selected against turns on the transport and the target alone, not on whether it survived; a contract is never fixed by what a transport was selected against.
```

(This writes a new choice into the text — that contracts are not scoped by selection histories — and says so.) (2) Read "faithful" in I49's wide extent wherever survival is at issue; but the wide extent's added clause is still pairwise (I50), so it cannot import H. The attack fails; the claim stands on the words' own line, which is its own guard.

**FC95 · L211.** The witness computed rests on two transports sharing a domain and codomain: the carrier instantiates the content's target, the representing transport is faithful, the candidate's transport from that same target is not. Attack: if a transport were fixed by its domain and codomain, the two would be one and L211 would be contradictory, not true. Nothing quoted settles that transports may so differ; the sentence's own grammar ("the transport from carrier to content… while the transport from the content's target to the content fails") speaks of two, and I81's transport space allows them, but that is the maths, not the text. The text should carry it. Added after L211's first sentence:

```
The transport by which a carrier represents a content and a candidate's transport from the content's target to that content need not be the same transport.
```

(rests on transports being more than a domain and codomain; beyond the bounds searched, no small model can fail it, since it is what the witness is built on). A second attack — that Rep's Sel-or-Con clause drags the candidate's own provenance in — fails: (R) quantifies over transports and asks provenance of the representing one only, which is what the sentence's first half describes.

**FC77's held part** ("the trivial witness exactly when τ is a homomorphism", 8,640 models). Attack: it holds under I18's reading of (F2)'s homomorphism clause as a condition on τ as a whole; if the clause were pointwise, fidelity on ∅ would be vacuous for every t and the sub-claim would fail for all of them. I cannot check (F2) from the lines given here; the second check's hand work supports I18, and I found no reading that makes the clause pointwise while keeping τ(1) = 1 and the equation τ(a₂)·τ(a₁) = τ(a₂·a₁) at defined products. Its force beyond the models is anyway nil once (b)'s wording is in: with L481's equality and a competitor in 𝒯, the trivial witness needs a physics that admits exactly one transport and a variation that moves nothing — which the owner's S20 words refuse.
