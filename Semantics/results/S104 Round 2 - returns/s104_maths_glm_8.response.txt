## (b) Counterexamples

**FC63 (c-i) · L343** — This is a counterexample to an invention, not to the text. The Leibniz expansion is a polynomial in the entries: its terms are read off the matrix, not free inputs. "The full Leibniz expansion *with skewness substituted*" (L343) fixes that; so only term tuples some matrix gives can enter, and the first variant's sum component — relating all 3⁶ tuples of GF(3) term values to their sums — is not the expansion the sentence names. The hand check makes clear the failure is the encoding's, not the field's: for a 3×3 skew matrix each entry lies in exactly one even and one odd term, so t₀t₃t₄ = −t₁t₂t₅, and the all-ones tuple would need 1 = −1 — over the reals just as much as over GF(3). No real matrix gives that tuple either; only the encoding does. The variant (c-ii) is the sentence's own candidate and it meets (E).

A second failure rides with it: even (c-ii) needs computed ports, which I14's one-port translations exclude, so under the formal core as written the candidate cannot be written at all — while the text says "The expansion is an account" (L343). That tells against I14 (given in another part; I rely on its summary here), not against the text: the words require that the expansion be writable as a candidate, and the maths must be wide enough to write it. I81's computed ports are the widening; they should stop being an exception used "only for the term ports of the Leibniz candidate" and become part of the core, or I14 should say they are.

No change to the text is needed; if the owner wants the terms' reading made explicit:

> L343 | The full Leibniz expansion, its terms read from the matrix entries, with skewness substituted meets (F1) and (F2)
