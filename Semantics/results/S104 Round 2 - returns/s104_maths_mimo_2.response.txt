# (a) Definitions and claims against the sentences

**D2.4 · L109.** The maths carries two readings; the words carry one.
> L109 | A port is an **observation** when \(A\) contains an edit that alters the relation reporting it without altering what it reports, that is, when the component assigning it has a measurement's signature (below).

A setting of the reading alters the relation that reports it and leaves the relation of what it reports; nothing excludes settings, and L127 names "change only the reading" as the case. So R-i is what the words say and should stand; R-ii is a hedge the text does not warrant and must be recorded as a departure. The price of R-i is paid at FC07 below.

**D4.6 · L123** (and H02). The maths reads "its output port" as the port the component assigns. L109 defines "output" otherwise ("its value is determined by \(L_j\) given the other ports"), and on the pole that makes \(H,\theta\) outputs of \(c_L\) too (H02's own note). The maths should stand: L123 speaks of an *intervention* on that port, and interventions here are the setting edits of L103/L119, which replace the assigning component only; under L109's output, setting \(H\) would be an intervention on \(c_L\)'s own output, which the edit structure does not give. The words should name the assigner.

```
> L123 | - a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on the ports it assigns and is invariant under observation edits; a part that reads a port another part assigns has no such signature on any contract;
```
Replaces the causal bullet at L123. This writes I04 (the assigner read off the edits) and I102 (several ports: the union) into the text, and answers H02 in favour of the assigner.

**FC10 · L57.** I09 reads "changes under" existentially; L57's "changes when the rule is edited" reads universally. The maths should stand: L123 opposes "changes under …" to "invariant under …", and these are contradictories only when change is existential. L57 should carry the same.

```
> L57 | a rule's application can change when the rule is edited and not when the world is intervened on; a cause's assignment can change under intervention.
```
Replaces the sentence at L57.

**FC13 · L127.** The formal statement is honest ("of sig_C(j) and of how each edit of C is classed") but it is not what the words say.
> L127 | These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data.

The classification of an edit is read off the whole organization, not off j's own (K); see the hand model FC13 · L127 in (d). The maths should stand; the words should widen (K). Separately, "not additional data" does settle I04's alternative (a): a declared assigner would be additional data.

```
> L127 | These are descriptions of patterns in (K) and in how the edits of the contract touch the other components, not additional data.
```
Replaces that sentence at L127.

**FC09 · L121.** The maths maps a constitutive status onto Rule_C; the words name four things and give three bullets. The maths should stand: L347 gives the constitutive rule the rule family's shape and the text supplies no other. Wording under matter 4 in (e).

# (c) Inventions and unrecorded choices

**I06 · L109.** Settled in favour of R-i by L109 as quoted and by L127's "a part that reads or reports another part has a measurement's signature" plus "change only the reading". R-ii stays as a recorded departure. No text change.

**I07 · L124.** Settled: L109 ("No role assignment is supplied") plus L127's "another part" give the measured port as one another part assigns; L116's (K) is built from \(L_j\), and L103's "A changed rule is a changed component" gives "the measuring relation" as the component's relation. Read per port, as L127's "another part" is read one at a time. No text change; but asg(m) must be *defined* and different from j (this removes U5).

**I08 · L125.** The main line is settled: "variable under edits to the rule" can only be variability of the signature, which is \(L_j\), so the rule is the relation the component applies; I08's alternative (b) would leave the family empty, since settings of a port the component does not assign never alter \(L_j\). What is open is whether the component's own setting edits count as rule edits; L57's contrast ("the rule is edited" against "a cause's assignment changes under intervention") says not.

```
> L125 | - a **rule application** has a signature invariant under interventions on the world, that is, settings of the ports it does not assign, and variable under edits to the rule, that is, edits that alter the relation it applies other than by setting the ports it assigns.
```
Replaces the rule bullet at L125. This writes I08 into the text.

**I09 · L123.** The baseline comparison (identity edit at the same boundary) is not settled by the words and should stay open; the searched choice is the natural one, and vacuity is the natural reading of "invariant" (a contract holding no such edits is not thereby disqualified). The existential reading of "changes under" *is* settled, by L123's own contrast; no change there, but L57 needs "can" (above).

**I102 · L109.** The text presumes a unique assigner ("the component assigning that port", L103; "the component assigning it", L109), so a port with two assigners lies outside its scope; the several-ports case is carried by the L123 proposal. The clause that lets a port with no assigner count among "the ports j does not assign" must not extend to making m a measured port of j, nor to making its settings interventions on the world for the components they alter (L125's "the world" is set against "the rule"); trim the invention, no text change.

**U5 · L127.** Settled against it: L127's "another part" and L109's "the component assigning it" require the measured port to be assigned by another component. Fix in I07/I102: asg(m) defined and ≠ j. No text change.

**H01 · L119.** Not settled. L119's "only" answers "does it touch readers?", and L103's sentence is satisfied by a composite; the collapse of the families is a consequence of D2.1's single-edit restriction, not of the words.

```
> L119 | An edit that sets a port replaces the component that assigns the port (above), not the relations of the components that read it; an edit that sets several ports at once sets each of them.
```

Replaces the sentence at L119. This writes H01's first alternative into the text.

**H02 · L123.** Settled by the intervention wording (see (a)); the L123 proposal writes it in.

# (d) Attacking the claims that held

**FC06 · L119.** Two breaks, both in the claim's wording. (1) *Model:* \(X_p=X_q=\{0,1\}\), one boundary. \(c_1\): \(V=\{p\}\), \(L_{c_1}(1,b)=X_p\). \(c_2\): \(V=\{p,q\}\), \(L_{c_2}(1,b)=\{(p,q):p=q\}\). Edit \(a_1\): \(L_{c_1}(a_1,b)=\{p=1\}\), \(c_2\) unchanged; \(a_2\): \(L_{c_2}(a_2,b)=\{p=0\}\), \(c_1\) unchanged. Then \(Set_p=\{a_1,a_2\}\) and asg(p) is undefined, so "j ≠ asg(m)" has no truth value. (2) On the pole, if the composite \(e=set(H=1,L=1)\) is a setting edit of H (H01's alternative), then \(L_{c_L}(e,b)\neq L_{c_L}(1,b)\) with \(c_L\neq asg(H)\), and the universal is false. So FC06 is L119 read for single edits; repair the claim's wording ("j is not a component through which a sets m"), not the text. Rests on I04, H01.

**FC07 · L123.** Holds beyond the search, but not as worded. By I04 a setting of o alters only \(L_j\); by I06 (R-i) any such edit that alters \(L_j\) is an observation edit for \((o,m)\) whenever \(m\neq o\) is assigned by another part. Causal_C needs a change under \(Set_{o_j}\) and invariance under Obs; the change witness is itself an Obs edit, so invariance fails — unless no setting of \(o_j\) changes \(L_j\), in which case the change clause fails. Hence Causal_C(j) ⟹ j reads no port another part assigns, **on every contract**; the hypothesis "on a contract that sets every port" is idle. The same lines collapse the families: by FC06, Inv_C(j, \(Set_m\)) and Inv_C(j, World_j) hold automatically for non-assigners, so L124's and L125's invariance clauses do no work, and Meas_C(j,m) ⟺ Changes_C(j, Alt_j) (R-i), Rule_C(j) ⟺ Changes_C(j, Alt_j ∖ \(Set_{o_j}\)); under R-ii those two change clauses coincide. The only discriminating clause is L123's invariance under observation edits. Rests on I04, I06, I102.

**FC09 · L347.** The computation runs under I08; it cannot distinguish I08 from its alternative (b), because under (b) the rule family is empty by I04 (settings of a port the component does not assign never alter \(L_j\)). The claim is the invention, restated.

**FC10 · L57.** *Model:* ports r, z, o in \(\{0,1\}\); \(c_r\) assigns r, \(c_z\) assigns z, j: \(V_j=\{r,z,o\}\), \(L_j(1,b)=\{(r,z,o):o=r\}\); edit s sets o through j; \(a_1\) with \(L_j(a_1,b_0)=\{o=1\}\), \(L_j(a_1,b_1)=L_j(1,b_1)\); \(a_2\) with \(L_j(a_2,b_0)=L_j(1,b_0)\), \(L_j(a_2,b_1)=\{o=0\}\). \(C=\{(a_1,b_0),(a_2,b_0),(s_r,b_0),(s_z,b_0)\}\). Rule_C(j) holds, but \(a_2\) is an edit to the rule whose pair in C leaves the signature unchanged: L57's clause fails if "changes when" is universal. Rests on I08, I09(c).

**FC13 · L127.** *Model:* two organizations, \(X_m=X_o=\{0,1\}\), one boundary, \(C=\{(a,b_0)\}\), and in both \(c_o\): \(V=\{m,o\}\), \(L_{c_o}(1,b)=\{(m,o):o=m\}\), \(L_{c_o}(a,b)=\{(m,o):o=0\}\) — identical sig_C(\(c_o\)) and C. D1: \(c_m\) assigns m and a leaves it; then a is a setting edit of o and an observation edit, so Rule_C(\(c_o\)) fails. D2: a also replaces \(c_m\) by \(\{m=0\}\); then a is neither setting nor observation edit, and Rule_C(\(c_o\)) holds. Same (K), same C, different verdicts: the family is not a pattern in (K). Rests on I04, I08, I09.

FC14 · L127: the scan is weaker than the sentence (it cannot rule out an equivalent definition); what carries the sentence is that every family predicate is indexed by C.
