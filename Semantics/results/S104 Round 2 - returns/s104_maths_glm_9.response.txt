## (b) Counterexamples

**FC75 look · L375.** The look's history: i → m runs at step 1; m's product persists by an edge m → r with no process between; r occurs at step 9; intervening on i across the contrast (0, 1) changes r's value through the persisted product. D11.4 counts the route active (connected, chained, dependent); L375 says in so many words that a route "already at rest when the result occurred, is not active for that result". This is a counterexample **only to the invention** (I46 with I95), not to the text: the text excludes the route; the formalization fails to. The words should stand — they are the text's own content, and the sentence survived every revision. What the text leaves open, as H11 says, is only whether a finished route whose product persists is "at rest"; that must be fixed in words, not silently in the maths. The wording is in (c), under H11.

No claim in this part has the status *counterexample found* or *computed: not as claimed*.

> A route is **at rest for** a result when every process occurrence of the route is complete before the process occurrence that carries the result begins; a route at rest for a result is not active for it, whatever of its product persists and feeds the result.

## (d) Attack the claims that held

**FC37 · L305.** Holds for every finite Γ, by argument, so the |Γ| ≤ 5 bound is no weakness: if CB({d}; W), take M ∈ min S with M ⊆ W (finite descent from W ∈ S); d ∉ M would give M ⊆ W ∖ {d} ∈ S by upward closure, contra; so d ∈ ∪min S. Conversely, if d ∈ M ∈ min S, then M ∖ {d} ⊊ M is not in S, so CB({d}; M). For indispensability: if Γ ∖ {d} ∈ S, a minimal member of it avoids d; if d ∈ ∩min S and Γ ∖ {d} ∈ S, its minimal member avoids d, contra. The finiteness hypothesis is indispensable, and both equivalences fail together without it: take Γ = ℤ, S = {W : d* ∈ W and W ∖ {d*} unbounded above}. S is upward closed, Γ ∈ S, min S = ∅ (drop any element other than d*), so ∪min S = ∅ and, by the usual convention, ∩min S = Γ — yet {d*} is critical in every route and no d is globally indispensable (Γ ∖ {d} ∈ S for all d). This is not a counterexample to L305, whose hypotheses exclude it; it confirms the sentence is right to condition on finiteness, and confirms D7.5's decision to define contributory by (B), not by ∪min S.

**FC38, FC39, FC42 · L307–L309, L299.** Checked by hand directly on the displays: ∅ ∉ {{a},{b},{a,b}} makes B = W critical in FC42(a); {b} ∈ S makes {a} uncritical in W = {a,b}; S = {{a}} is not upward closed and Γ ∉ S. These are one-line verifications; nothing beyond the models exists to find.

**FC40 · L311.** Holds for all subsets of ℕ, not just eventually periodic ones, so I91's bound drops away: supersets of unbounded sets are unbounded (upward closure); every unbounded set has a proper unbounded subset (no minimal member, and no finite set is unbounded); W ∖ B fails to be a route exactly when its index set is bounded; a critical block contains all but boundedly many d_n, so is infinite; B = W is critical (∅ is bounded); and adding or removing one index leaves an unbounded set unbounded, which is FC40(c). Each clause is a fact about unbounded subsets of ℕ, with no finite representation needed.

**FC41 · L313.** Holds for arbitrary Γ by the arguments in (a) for the glosses, plus one step: if B is finite, every member of it does no work by itself, and CB(B; W), remove the members one at a time — each W ∖ {b₁}, W ∖ {b₁,b₂}, … stays in S by NoWork — reaching W ∖ B ∈ S after finitely many steps, contradicting criticality. At an infinite B the removal never terminates, and there the text's own example (L311: B = W critical, every d_n doing no work by itself) shows the 'when Γ is infinite' cannot be dropped. So the sentence as well as the claim holds, with no bound.

**FC75 · L375.** Clauses (a) and (b) follow from D11.4's own wording; (c) holds by construction, which is to say it was not tested. The attack that lands is the look, dealt with in (b). A second, softer one: D11.4 assesses dependence by intervening on i's port in Org_ℓ(h), while L375 says activity "is read from the history". If a history never varies i across a declared contrast, the intervention reading can call a route active that a reading from the actual occurrences alone (I46's other choice (b)) would not. "Under the declared contrasts" points to the intervention reading, and "read from the history" contrasts with the result, not with counterfactual variation; but the text should say which, and the wording in (c) under I46 says it. I could not break (a) or (b) under any reading.

**FC105 · L169.** Under I45, L604's "An assessment event with contract C" makes the contract a property of an occurrence inside the event, not of the set — readable, but only by that gloss; and L612's "lost event identities" (known to me only through the phrase quoted in FC105; I rely on lines not given here) reads naturally as identities over and above occurrence sets, which is I45's other choice (a). So the claim, untested, sits on an invention that at least one of the six uses resists. The definition proposed in (c) settles the reading one way; whether that is the owner's way is not for the maths to say.
