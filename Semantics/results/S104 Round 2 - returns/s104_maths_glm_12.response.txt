## (a) Does each formal statement say what the sentence says?

**D9.5 · L393.** The maths reads "applied within the contract … j has declared" as containment (C_u ⊆ C_j(u)) with equality of grain and boundary. "Within" supports containment; the text settles this much. Nothing to change.

**D9.7 · L397.** The text gives each *step* a ruling-out of a *case* (Prem(u) ∧ ¬concl(u)) and each *argument* a ruling-out of a *claim* via its conclusion. The maths keeps both levels (D9.7's first sentence and RO(α,φ)); since the text itself defines premises as leaves ("argument steps whose leaves are premises", L397), restricting the non-circularity check to leaves matches the text. They agree.

**D9.9 · L395 (K3).** Two partings, one small and one real.

*Small:* the text states (K3) outright; the maths makes part (i) conditional on the conditional being live for j and modus tollens admitted. The conditions are what (K2) itself demands — an argument using the conditional is usable only if the conditional is live — so the text's sentence presupposes them. The words should carry them (proposal under I43 below).

*Real:* the text's "and nothing narrower" most naturally says that *that argument's* ruling-out is of the conjunction and of no part. The maths reads it as: from those premises alone, *no argument at all* rules out T, B or I. That is a stronger sentence, and under the text's own allowance that admitted forms need not be classically sound (L397, "for someone who admits its inference form"), the stronger reading is false — see the model in (d). The words should stand; the maths should read "nothing narrower" as scoping the ruling-out the argument yields.

**D9.10 · L377, L380 (K1).** The formalization matches: Bearing(c,z,p) ⟺ Acc(ℰ_c), where ℰ_c's organization is the connection. The one parting is that the text *constructs* p_δ ("Let p_δ be the question whether z has δ in respect of p") while the maths *supplies* it with c — taken up under H12. The text should stand: it says how p_δ is got.

**D9.11 · L385.** Every operative word of the sentence ('structural map', 'role bindings', 'the same transition', 'operative deliberative rule') is undefined in the text, so the maths cannot part company with it; it fills a silence. What the sentence itself says — that reason use gives neither bearing nor usability — the maths says too, by keeping Acc and Usable out of UsesReason. The independence reading ("does not give" = use without bearing is possible) is the right one; a universal reading would be too strong, since nothing stops a system in which both happen to hold.

*Nothing to add:* D9.1–D9.4, D9.6, D9.8.

## (d) Attacks on the claims that held

**FC71 · L395 — counterexample to part (ii) by hand.** Assessor j with Accepted_j(ξ) = {¬O, (T∧B∧I ⇒ O)}; Forms_j = {MP, F}, where F is the free form (I89) with Prem = {¬O} and conclusion ¬T. D9.7 lets an admitted form be classically unsound, so F is admissible. Argument β: one step u, Form(u) = F, leaf premise ¬O. Prem(u) = {¬O} is live (accepted); Form_j holds; Scope is vacuous (I42, alternative b). So Usable_j(β), and RO(β, T): Incons(T, ¬T) holds and the leaf is ¬O, not ¬T. Hence β ∈ X_j(T) — from exactly the premises of the failed prediction, T alone is ruled out. Rests on I38 (forms need not be sound) and I89 (the free form). Verdict: it breaks I43's strong reading of "nothing narrower", an invention; it does not touch the text read as scoping the argument's own ruling-out, and the wording change proposed under I43 removes it. Note also that per L397 and decision S28, the ruling out by F is j's choice, not the premises' doing.

**FC56 · L393 — a near miss on "does not rule the conclusion out".** Model: j first accepts d; j then withdraws d, and the withdrawal is taken up as the claim W ("d is withdrawn"). Let Forms_j contain the free form G with Prem = {W}, conclusion ¬d. After ξ′, Accepted_j(ξ′) = {W}; the argument with leaf W and step G is usable and rules out d. So after withdrawing d, d is ruled out — though not by the withdrawal: by j's admitted unsound form on the withdrawal's record. The sentence "it does not rule the conclusion out", read without the choice clause the same line carries ("the ruling out is a choice the person using it made"), says more than (K2) gives. The fix is one word, and it belongs with matter 9's fix:

```
> L393 | Withdrawing a premise makes the step unusable when nothing else keeps that premise live; it does not by itself rule the conclusion out.
```
