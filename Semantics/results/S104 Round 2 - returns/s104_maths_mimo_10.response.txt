# (a) Do the formal statements say what the sentences say?

Throughout: an argument is S23's "reasons why this and not that"; and per S28 nothing here rules anything out — a ruling is the person's choice.

**D8.1 · L315.** Matches "each of them could meet (F1), (F2) and (A) there under some relations of the target's components at that pair, each a relation on the component's footprint (Part II)": R assigns one relation to each footprint, and Meets is (F1), (F2), (A) at the pair. One reliance I cannot check here: the maths drops the homomorphism clause at a pair (I18). That is faithful only if Part II's (F1) carries such a clause; Part II's lines are not before me (see (c), I19).

**D8.2 · L315.** Domain mismatch. The words fix the pairs: "at some admitted edit–boundary pair of the target that both their transports translate, in \(C\) or outside it." The maths says only "a pair (a,b) of the target that both transports translate", dropping "admitted"; the same drop recurs in D8.3. The words stand (a pair the target does not admit is not a place where anything happens); the maths must restrict to admitted pairs. No text change.

**D8.3 · L315.** Mismatch. The maths makes rivals of two candidates whenever one is offered in place of the other. The words add a second condition: "a candidate's rivals are among the candidates someone has offered, and a candidate that nobody has offered is no one's rival." Under D8.3, if A is offered in place of B and nobody ever offered B, B is still A's rival — which the words forbid. The words stand; the maths must require that both have been offered.

**D8.5 · L315 (with FC52 · L315).** Two things.

*Vacuity.* As written, "every relation … under which it could meet" is true of an empty set. A candidate that can meet under no relation, whose answer χ allows, then conflicts with χ. The words do not say that: "χ excludes what the candidate's organization and transport give there" — where nothing is given there, χ excludes nothing. The words stand.

*Redundancy.* Because Meets includes (A), the first branch entails the second, so FC52's implication holds and the words' "or" is not two routes. With the empty case excluded (below), the first branch becomes the route for a candidate that can meet under no relation, and the "or" is genuine.

Proposal — replaces, within L315, the sentence "when \(\chi\) excludes … (A) there.":

```
when \(\chi\) excludes what the candidate's organization and transport give there: its answer, or, where some relation of the target's components lets it meet (F1), (F2) and (A) there, every such relation.
```

**FC53 · L315.** The formal statement (a) asserts \(X_j(\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal E))\neq\emptyset\) — that an argument exists. The words say only what "that argument" does: "that argument rules out that the candidate meets (E) for any assessor \(j\) who can use it, and so who tentatively accepts \(\chi\) (K2)". The words stand; the maths should state (a) conditionally on an argument being there. (I rely on the earlier part of L315, which builds the argument and is not given here.)

**FC43 · L317.** Conf and Riv are symmetric by construction; that much the words force. The step "so Prob_j and its kind are symmetric, and ETV_j(\(\mathcal E\)) through \(\mathcal E'\) gives ETV_j(\(\mathcal E'\))" goes beyond the one sentence quoted, "the rival is then easy to vary too", and rests on the lines defining Prob_j and ETV_j, not given here. **I33 · L315.** Not settled. "offered … as an answer to \(p\) in place of the other" has no definition, and L522 (not given here) does not list offers among the declared inputs.

Proposal — added to L315 after "…in \(C\) or outside it.":

```
An **offer** is a record of what someone has offered as an answer to \(p\) in place of another; whether an offer has been made is not settled here.
```

**I34 · L315.** Partly settled. "\(\chi\) need not be an explanation or come with one", the perpetual-motion example, and "every relation of the target's components under which it could meet" together fix that a claim speaks of relations (so option (b), χ constraining answers only, is out). Not settled: what a claim is.

Proposal — replaces the sentence "\(\chi\) need not be an explanation or come with one, and it may be a claim about what is possible or impossible: …":

```
A claim \(\chi\) is given at each pair by the relations of the target's components it allows there; it need not be an explanation or come with one, and it may be a claim about what is possible or impossible: the bare claim that perpetual motion is impossible is enough for a conflict with a candidate whose organization gives perpetual motion.
```

**I35 · L315.** Settled by the words: "Two candidates that some relations of the target's components would let both meet (F1), (F2) and (A) at a pair, when \(\chi\) excludes every such relation" requires that some relations let both meet — which is I35 as written and excludes option (a). No change.

**I36 · L315.** Not settled.

Proposal — replaces "as none do when two of their active components with one counterpart have different relations there.":

```
as none do when two of their active components have one counterpart — the same subnetwork of the target, reached by port translations with the same image and a bijection of their variables that matches values — and have different relations there, that is, when carrying the one relation to the other's variables does not give the other's relation.
```

**I86 · L315.** A search choice, not content: the harness sets Offered for every pair it examines, which the words' "a candidate that nobody has offered is no one's rival" cannot honour. It should stay out of the text; FC43's symmetry holds for any content of Offered, so nothing turns on it.

**I88 · L397.** Partly settled: "An argument is an argument tree: argument steps whose leaves are premises" fixes a tree with steps; whether a bare premise is an argument is open, and finiteness is a bound of the search, not content. S23 ("something that can be strung together into a coherent structure") supports requiring a step.

Proposal — replaces the L397 sentence:

```
An argument is an argument tree: argument steps whose leaves are premises, which are record leaves or stated assumptions and definitions; a premise on its own is not an argument.
```

# (d) Attacks on the claims that held

**FC43 · L317.** Conf/Riv symmetry cannot be broken (it is the shape of the definitions). The sentence can. Model: one pair (a,b); \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) conflict there (different answers); \(\mathcal E\)'s active component admits two relations and still meets; \(\mathcal E'\)'s admits one. Under the reading of L317's "easy to vary" as "has a variation that still meets", \(\mathcal E\) is easy to vary and \(\mathcal E'\) is not, so "the rival is then easy to vary too" fails. It rests on that reading; the text does not fix the phrase here, and per S33–S34 I propose nothing about what hard to vary covers. The symmetry of Prob_j/ETV_j rests on lines not given.

**FC44 · L315.** I tried to break it under I36 with a bijection \(\beta\) of order 3 (three ports, three values, inside the bounds): relations that correspond under \(\beta\) but are compared the other way. It fails: I36 compares \(L^{E'}_{k'}\) with \(\beta_*(L^E_k)\), and if both (F1) hold under one R the two relations correspond exactly. A break survives only where a transport is partial (I17), so that "relations there" are compared on the translated part alone and a difference on the rest is invisible; that rests on I14/I17, whose lines are not given. The witness under the loose reading is the standing problem (b), fixed by the I36 wording.

**FC45 · L315.** Both parts hold as the words stand; (b) is by construction and no search could break it. Dropping "takes its transport with it" breaks (a): two isomorphic organizations whose transports differ on one port give different answers at (a,b) and conflict. The words are exactly as strong as the claim needs.

**FC52 · L315.** Hand countermodel to D8.5 as written: one pair; χ allows every relation; \(\mathcal E\)'s answer is allowed; no R makes \(\mathcal E\) meet. The second disjunct is vacuously true, so \(\mathcal E\) conflicts with χ although nothing χ excludes is given there. The proposed D8.5 wording in (a) removes it; FC52's implication then no longer holds in the empty case, which is what the words want.

**FC53 · L315.** (a) as computed is not general. Model: one pair in \(C\); χ = "nothing moves here", Applies(\(\chi\),a,b) true; premises = \(\{\chi,\ \mathrm{Applies}(\chi,a,b)\}\) as atoms (I87); admitted forms MP, MT, AND-intro/elim (I89); no premise linking χ to meeting. Then \(X_j(\mathrm{Acc}(\mathcal E))=\emptyset\) while ConfCl holds. The pendulum encoding supplies that link by hand (I90). So (a) is a property of the encoding, not of the words; per S28 even an existing argument is not a ruling.

**FC54 · L315.** Holds by construction given (A) ∈ Meets: if some R lets both meet, their answers agree and the second disjunct of Conf fails. Under I35's option (a) it would fail: two candidates that can each meet under some allowed relation, with no relation at all letting both, conflict plainly at that pair and are rivals given χ. The words exclude that (see (c), I35); so the claim stands only where the words stand.
