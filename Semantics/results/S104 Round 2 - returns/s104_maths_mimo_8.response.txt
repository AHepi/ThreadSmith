## (d) Attacks on the claims that held

**FC57 · L329.** Drop I64's "attainable": at y with empty fibre, |f[Z_y]| = |∅| = 0 ≠ 1, so identification fails, while f = f̄∘g on Z is unconstrained at y and holds. Rests on I64; the words use "attainable", so this is not a counterexample to L329, but it shows the word is load-bearing. Second attack: if f = f̄∘g is read on the domain of f rather than on Z, the ⟸ direction can fail. Take Z = {0}, domain Y′ = {0,1}, g(0)=0, g(1)=1, f(0)=0, f(1)=1. Identification on Z at y = 0 is trivial; the equation on Y′ forces f̄(1) = 1 as well, which is stronger than identification on Z gives. The maths' "on Z" avoids this; the proposal writes it in.

**FC58 · L329.** On a proper Z the kernel criterion is sufficient but not necessary. Z = {(0,0),(1,0),(2,0)} ⊆ ℝ², G = [1 1] (g = x+y), c = (1,−1) (f = x−y). ker G = span{(1,−1)}; ker f = span{(1,1)}; ker G ⊄ ker f. On Z, g takes 0, 1, 2 in singletons, so f is identified at every attainable y. Rests on I64's alternative (a). The sentence at L329 says "For linear g and linear feature c^⊤", which does not restrict Z; the proposal's "In the linear case Z = ℝ^n" removes the mismatch.

**FC59 · L331.** If the two readings repeat (both x + b_A), the kernel becomes span{(1,−1,0),(0,0,1)} and neither b_B − b_A nor x is identified. Not a counterexample: the setup is fixed at L331. Nothing rests beyond I64.

**FC60 · L331.** On (b), drop I39's structural reading of "among its premises": then the premise "b_B = 0" does not carry "x = m*" literally, and D9.7 says the argument rules out x ≠ m*. Rests on I39. L397's "read structurally as non-circular dependence reads identity (Part V)" is on the invention's side; no change.

**FC61 · L335.** (b) needs at least two states: on a singleton, z R* z always, so no pair (z,z') with I(z) = I(z') and not z R* z'. The existential "there are R, I, z, z'" remains true in a larger space; the search (I77's bounds) is over spaces of size ≥ 2. Beyond the bounds the statement stands.

**FC62 · L339.** Beyond the one-component setup: D with x and y both driving the answer, E with k = π(x) only. Delete {k}: y still drives the answer at both pairs, so the contrast is not lost and NC2 with G = {k} does not witness. Rests on I78's surgical single-component encoding. L339 says "those components" (plural) and "a deleted subnetwork"; the maths' G = {k} is a reading. The words need no change; the formal should let G range over the counterparts of all introduced components.

**FC63 · L343.** Over GF(2), n = 1, M = [1]: M^T = [1] = −[1], so M is skew-symmetric, det M = 1 ≠ 0. The theorem fails there. The contract at L343 holds field arithmetic fixed, and the question is about ℝ; this is against I66's choice (b) being excluded, not against the words. Beyond n ∈ {1,2,3} and GF(3), the argument (det M = det M^T = det(−M) = (−1)^n det M = −det M, so 2 det M = 0) goes through in any characteristic ≠ 2; no counterexample lies there.
