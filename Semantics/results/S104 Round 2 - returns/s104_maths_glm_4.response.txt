## (d) Attacks on the claims that held

**FC36 · L151 — attack succeeds for purpose-achievement.** Take two questions p, p′ with the same D, C, b0, Q — the same type of Q and the same shape of C — but O_p ≠ O′_p (L147 puts O_p in the tuple, so such pairs exist inside the theory). Purpose-achievement (AR, D14.8) is assessed against the aims; the respect asked differs between p and p′ while (Q, C) does not. So the sentence as it stands has a counterexample by hand: purpose-achievement is not fixed by the type of Q and the shape of C. It rests on L147 and D3.1 alone. Fix:

```
What a question asks, production, identification, obstruction, rule-status
or purpose-achievement, is fixed by the type of \(\mathcal Q\) and the shape of
\(C\), and for purpose-achievement by the aims \(O_p\), not by a label.
```
(replaces L151's "fixed by" sentence)
I cannot attack the rule-status half here: D4.6 is not given in this part, and the computation never touched it; the claim's "computed: as claimed" covers only the three respects, and "holds by construction" for renaming-invariance is written into the program, not searched — evidence of the build, not of the sentence.

**FC106 · L161 — attack on the held part.** "An incompatible baseline admits no account" held on 17,280 models, but the argument runs through NonVacuous (D6.7, not given in this part; I rely on it as summarized). If NonVacuous demands a determined answer somewhere in C other than at the baseline, then Sol_D(1, b0) = ∅ no longer blocks Acc, and a baseline-incompatible question could admit accounts on other pairs. Whether any of the 17,280 models had a determining pair outside the baseline is not reported. The claim is therefore hostage to NonVacuous's exact content — an invention-governed definition from another part — and should be re-searched once with a NonVacuous that demands determination only off the baseline. Where to look: a two-pair contract, baseline undetermined, one intervention pair determined.

**FC107 · L161 — attack on the definitional step.** p_δ ≠ p holds by D3.7 only if p_δ is a question of the kind (E) can assess, i.e. with a target that is an organization. The text says nothing that makes a question, or a defect, into an organization; L175's layers are organizations of things, and a defect is not a persistent thing with boundaries. So "Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract" is true but possibly unusable — the second question may have no target the theory recognizes. Not a counterexample, a gap. Minimal fix, if the owner means p_δ to be assessable:

```
Exposing the defect is another question with its own target, contract and
query.
```
(replaces L161's last sentence)
Whether a question can serve as a target is a decision for the owner, not something I propose to settle.

**FC34 · L151 — narrow reading.** Trivially consistent; no attack attempted beyond (b).
