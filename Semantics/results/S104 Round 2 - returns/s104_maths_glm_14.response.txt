## (b) The counterexamples

**FC81(d) · L223.** This tells against the **text**, through one gap. L193 fixes that a transport has "exactly one of three provenances, determined by its history"; L195's non-representation clause lists "t, H, or the survival condition" and omits the organization t carries to, which is exactly what L197's Con needs. So the text's own clauses let a transport be both selected and constructed, and then L223's "the failure is not surprise" fails, since surprise needs only Sel. The model is hand-built (I90) — it shows what the definitions allow, not what any physics does — but the allowance is the text's. Change, closing L193's promise at L195:
> L195 | A transport **survives on \(H\)** when it is a member of \(\mathcal T\) that meets the survival condition on \(H\); fidelity on \(H\) without membership in \(\mathcal T\) is not survival. No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), the survival condition, or an organization \(t\) carries to.

I flag the cost: a transport selected by trial and error whose target organization is itself held in a carrier (S26's first example) would then not be selected on that history. Whether that is a misclassification or the truth is FC78's question, in its own part; the wording above is the local closure of it, and FC81(d) holds under it.

**FC82 · L225.** As first computed, this is a counterexample **only to the model**: t′ = t, no violation (H08) — not a response to anything. The second check's stronger case (violation, μ(t) = {t′}) stands, but against the text only through (i) the same L193/L195 gap and (ii) the unsettled "new relative to what". Rather than amend L195 here too, close (ii) at L225, which also serves FC83:
> L225 | A **selection response** to a violation extends the history \(H\) of a selected transport and lets \(\mu\) act: the transport is re-tuned within the population, which already holds every transport \(\mu\) can return. A **construction response** to a violation introduces, with a construction trace, a new organization or a new transport — new relative to any population \(\mathcal T\) on which selection is running and to anything the system already holds. Only the second can be originative under Part X.

This proposal writes two choices into the text: "new relative to any population on which selection is running" (invention: the repertoire/population split FC82 exploits) and the population's prior hold on μ's results (the choice FC83's Look names). Under it no transport is the result of both responses, and the sentence holds without touching L195.

**FC83 · L225.** This is a counterexample **only to the claim's own formal statement** and to I90's hand-set conjuncts.
