**The test applied.**
- **CLAIM** follows the 1D and 2D briefs: a reader can conclude from one text something the other leaves unconcluded. It covers B asserting what A leaves unasserted, B dropping, narrowing, widening or qualifying what A asserts, and B adding or removing a requirement, condition, exception, quantifier or outside input.
- **WORDING:** both texts assert the same thing, and every conclusion a reader can draw from one, the reader can draw from the other.
- **ORDER** follows Claude-pre's policy. A new file-11 sentence is ORDER when file 10 states the same claim, about the same subject, at the same or wider scope somewhere. It is CLAIM when file 10 leaves the claim to inference.
- The line between the two, as drawn here:
  - **ORDER** when a file-10 sentence (or two adjacent ones) already says it of the same subject: contracts, narrowings, predicates of explanation.
  - **CLAIM (application)** when file 11 gives a verdict on a kind of situation that file 10 never names, and a reader of file 10 must first classify the situation under a general sentence. Examples are a reading part offered as producer, a measure with a reliable prediction, and a meaning supplied for a replacement query.
- "The whole text" is what a reader concludes from. A changed front-matter sentence is WORDING when the body of both files, unchanged, carries the same content.
- One brief instruction matters when weighing the readers. The 1D brief tells the testers: "Where a place could take either CLAIM or WORDING, mark it CLAIM and give the reason under WHY; the choice between them is settled later by a reader holding both texts." A tester's CLAIM on a close call is that instructed default. It is not a finding that the place is CLAIM.
- **Harm.** A CLAIM place is harmful when a case's verdict moves away from the thoughtful person because of it. The evidence is Claude's case rulings (file 01). Those rulings count 5 verdicts CHANGED. O18, O37, O41 and O48 move toward the thoughtful person. O35 moves away, and that change is traced to f11 L433 and L514, which lie outside R1.
