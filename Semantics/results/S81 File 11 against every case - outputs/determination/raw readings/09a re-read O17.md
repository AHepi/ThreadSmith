## 4. The ruling, by the plan's words

**FILE-11 MARK: AGREE.**

- **Points (ii) and (iii) are reached exactly.** The manual grounds ownership in capability ("because the robot can diagnose"). f11 L467 s3 says that ground "grounds neither", and L419 s3 and L518's last sentence say the same. So striking the manual loses nothing, and keeping only the manual gives nothing. No reading disputes this.
- **Point (i) is reached, with a qualification.**
  - f11 L467 s3 grounds ownership in "the processes and resources the boundary includes". The log is a record of processes ("every comparison it runs"), and it shows the process that produced the diagnosis. So the ground file 11 names is the ground the fixed verdict names: the processes the log records, not the manual's capability.
  - The boundary enters as a qualifier: "given the robot's boundary". Under the Stage 1 rule of "Same finding", that is "a qualification, a limit or a more exact wording". It is not a different finding.
- **The SILENT reading fails on the case as written.** That reading needs the boundary to be "missing" in the sense of f11 L514. It is not missing, for three reasons:
  - **The case gives the input L514 asks for.** f11 L465 defines the boundary as "which processes and resources are the system's". The situation names one system, "a repair robot". It gives that system's processes as the comparisons "it runs". It places the routine that produced the diagnosis "inside the robot", in the log of those comparisons.
  - **Only an added boundary could exclude the routine.** No boundary the situation states or implies excludes the routine. Excluding it would take a boundary the situation does not state, of the kind O41 and O51 state in words.
  - **So the input is not chosen from the verdict.** Taking the robot as the system is taking the input the case gives, which is what L514's "rather than choosing the input from the verdict wanted" forbids doing otherwise. This is the standard 04c applied to O35 and O30: an input is missing where the situation leaves open alternatives that give different verdicts. O17 leaves none.
- **The casing clause of f11 L419 s2 is not engaged.** "Where the boundary is drawn decides, not where the process sits in the casing" settles cases where a drawn boundary and the casing come apart. Its sister clauses show this: "however it is executed inside" (L419 s2) and "however close it sits" (L465).
  - The SILENT reading must treat "inside the robot" as a casing fact only.
  - The situation's own words do not allow that. "The robot" is the agent that keeps the log and runs the comparisons, and the case names no casing apart from it, as O51 does ("shell", "casing").
  - No fact of O17 separates the robot as a system from the robot as a body.
- **The same case words are read the same way under both files.** File 10 already makes the boundary a declared index: f10 L523, word for word with f11 L516, and \(\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}\) in f10 L476. The baseline and both 1K returns read "inside the robot" as supplying it, and 1K B does so explicitly. File 11's new sentences sharpen what follows when a boundary is missing, or differs from the casing. They do not change what the case's words state.
- **Mimo's added reason, "every comparison it runs", supports the ruling but does not settle it.**
  - It was not in the determination's reasons.
  - O51 uses "runs" of a module outside its declared boundary: "A controller runs a diagnostic module …".
  - So "runs" alone does not state membership where the case draws a boundary elsewhere. What decides O17 is that it draws none apart from the robot.
- **Derivation 9 does not apply** (f11 L606 = f10 L613: "use inferred from delivery logs"). The text is shared, and the log records the internal comparisons, not deliveries (1K A's HOLDS line).
- **Neither SPLIT reading holds.** File 11's sentences agree that the drawn boundary decides. The open question is whether the case states the boundary, which is answered above, so there are not two readings of the theory. The maker's-routine reading (Mimo's attack 3) fails on "whoever wrote it" in f11 L419 s2 and L465.
- **DISAGREE does not hold.** No sentence of file 11 gives a finding contrary to points (i) to (iii).
