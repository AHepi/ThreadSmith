## Marks, as the testers were given them (brief, lines 7-17)

- **Same finding.** "A verdict of the theory that reaches the fixed verdict's finding and adds a qualification, a limit or a more exact wording reaches the same finding. Write the addition under NOTE. A mark records findings; differences of wording belong in NOTE."
- Marks, where the first rule that fits wins: (1) **CASE DISPUTED**: the situation alone, read in ordinary terms, decides a point against the fixed verdict. (2) **DISAGREE**: on at least one point the theory reaches a different finding. (3) **SPLIT**: on at least one point the theory's sentences support two readings that reach different findings, and the text leaves the choice open. (4) **SILENT**: on at least one point the theory leaves the finding open, either because the deciding matter is an input the case does not state or because its sentences stop short. (5) **AGREE**: the theory reaches the same finding on every point.
- A word the theory leaves undefined is read in its ordinary sense (brief, line 10).

## Conventions of this file

- Line numbers are those of the files as read. Quotations are copied exactly, with bold markers left out.
- **PASSAGE SAME**: the sentence the verdict rests on is identical in both files, or file 11 rewords it without a new claim. **PASSAGE CHANGED**: the file-11 sentence the verdict rests on asserts something that no sentence of file 10 asserts. "(addition, consistent)" means the new sentence states outright what file 10 already gives through the file-10 sentences cited, so the verdict does not move.
- **KIND** = theory change only when the VERDICT is CHANGED and traces to a CHANGED passage.
- No case in this range seemed to me CASE DISPUTED. The closest candidates are noted where they arise (O44, O51).
