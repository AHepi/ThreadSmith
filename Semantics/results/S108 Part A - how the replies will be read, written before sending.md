## How the replies will be read

1. **Nothing is opened until all four calls end.** No reply, receipt, reasoning file, attempt file, stream file or guard log of Part A is opened until every job has ended its last allowed pass (a reply accepted, or failed with no pass left): the run log holds `s108_glm_loop: every pass ended; …` and `loop ended <UTC time>`, and the process in the pid file has exited. The progress lines of the log (start, sent, still running, accepted, not accepted, done) may be watched; they hold no reply text. If a call is still running 24 hours after the launch, the orchestrator may stop the runner by its PID and record the calls not ended as not returned.
2. **What counts as a reply.** Only `<tag>.response.txt`, which the helper writes last and only for a reply whose last non-blank line carries END OF REPORT. A failed call counts neither for nor against anything. Connection failures, timeouts, 429 and 5xx are not answers (the helper tries again); a job failed only on the connection after pass 3 may be sent again only under a new note written before sending; a job failed on answers after pass 3 is final and recorded as not done by that call. A receipt that shows the key in the output, or a sandbox not unchanged, is reported to the orchestrator before anything else is read.
3. **A reply is a set of variants and claims about them, not a result.** A trace a reply gives is its claim until computed; a small case marked "not run" is run; an edge a reply names is its claim until the computation settles it. Silence is not agreement: an item no variant touched is not thereby shown to be free of dependencies.
4. **Tabulation first, by one fresh Opus 5.5 agent** that built nothing of Part A and has read no reply. It tabulates the four replies before anything is implemented: for each variant, its section and id, the item or items varied, the old and the new statement whole, its kind (delete, weaken, strengthen, swap, re-order, other), the reply's trace (which candidates newly count, which stop, as meeting (E) and as explanations), its small cases and whether each was run, every edge it names (blocks, constrains, changes with, moves) with the item, the owner's decisions it names, and the inventions it rests on. It flags a variant that changes a FROZEN item or an item of another section (out of the agent's scope: recorded and not implemented), one that adds prose (S40), and one that would move where values are placed (the owner's question). It writes `results/S108 Part A - tabulation of the replies, before any ruling.md`, created at once and filled as it goes (lesson S28), and rules on nothing.
5. **Implementation and computation, by Opus 5.5 agents**, at most one per section, each in its own copy of the program (copied from the program after round 4 into `results/S108 Part A - computation/section <n> model/`). Each implements every in-scope variant of its section as a reading the program can switch on and off (the current definition stays the default), and **computes** the variant's actual effect on the explanation definition, Acc and Account ∧ ¬Dec(t), with the variant off and on, over:
   1. **the worked cases**: the encodings E1 to E9 and the text's cases the program builds (among them the table of observed answers, its encoded form, the reversed calculation, "p because p", the pole and its shadow, the skew-symmetric matrices, the student's declared formula, the weathervane, the shop sign with one part and with two, the bridge), and the case script of the written-in step;
   2. **the external examples** FC-E1 to FC-E5;
   3. **the creative transport case** CT1 to CT8;
   4. **the generated worlds**: the program's generators at scale 4, over every question and candidate they build, reporting the candidates whose result under Acc, or under Account ∧ ¬Dec(t) where provenance is computed, differs between off and on, how many of each kind, and the smallest witness of each;
   and the whole suite with the variant on (every claim whose result moves). A small case a reply gives is built and run. Where a variant cannot be implemented as written, the agent says why and implements the nearest reading it can state, recorded as its own invention with the other choices (S36). For every edge the reply names, the agent marks it **computed** (the computation shows it), **contradicted** (the computation shows otherwise), or **not settled by computation** (and what would settle it); and it adds the edges its computation shows that the reply did not name. Each writes `results/S108 Part A - section <n> - variants computed.md`, with its runs and a `.json` of its edges, created at once and filled as it goes. Nothing it writes is a change to the theory.
6. **The dependency map, by one Opus 5.5 agent**, from the tabulation and the four computations: `results/S108 Part A - the dependency map.md` and `.json`. Nodes: every item of the template (FROZEN and middle, sentences and definitions) and the parts of the explanation definition ((E)'s conjuncts and what each reads, Dec, Expl, (Suff), (Nec)). Edges: blocks, constrains, changes with, moves, each with what shows it (the variant, the computation and its result, or the reply's argument where no computation settles it) and its standing (computed, contradicted, claimed only). The gaps, listed: sections and items no variant touched, and edges the computation could not settle.
7. **The candidate definitions of explanation.** Every variant whose computation changes which candidates meet (E) or count as explanations is a discovered candidate definition of explanation. The map's agent lists them in `results/S108 Part A - candidate definitions of explanation.md`, each with its statement, what it admits and what it drops (computed), and the owner's decisions it does not appear to agree with, named, for the later step in which the owner answers yes or no (S52: "candidates that don't appear to fit decisions I've made in the past"). Nothing in the list is applied.
8. **One critical review, by a fresh Opus 5.5 agent** (S42, S52) that built nothing of Part A, reads the tabulation, the computations, the map and the list against this rule and the owner's words, reruns the program where it doubts a result (it may give the reruns to the harness), and writes `results/S108 Part A - critical review.md`. Nothing is final before it.
9. **The orchestrator decides contested points** (S13), with its reasons why this and not the other; it keeps the finding, or sends the contested points, all together, to at most one fresh Opus 5.5 second checker, which rules in maths and code. Either way it is recorded.
10. **A second round of Part A** follows if the map has gaps: sections or items no variant touched, or edges the computation could not settle. It goes under its own rule, written and committed before sending.
11. **Nothing in the theory's text or maths is changed by Part A.** It is an experiment on copies. Its outputs are the map and the list of candidate definitions. The text, the formal core, the claims and the program after round 4 are never written; nothing is written into `authority/`.
12. **Arguments, not numbers.** Four replies saying the same thing decides nothing; neither do the length or the confidence of a reply.
13. **The owner's words bind every finding as content**: no list, count, grade, ranking or record doing the explanation's work (S20); nothing about what must happen to candidates (S21); no word S23 forbids in any statement, name or gloss, "argument" as reasons why this and not that, every accepting tentative (S23); physical possibility only where information or knowledge is instantiated or transformed, and as content (S25 to S27); nothing settled, a ruling out by a claim taken as given a choice (S28); nothing about what hard to vary covers in general (S33, S34); every invention recorded (S36); no new prose (S40); the four answers (S41, with S47); LLMs not part of the semantics (S43); a written-in answer never stops a candidate being an explanation (S44, S45); the maths asks for nothing (S47). A variant that explores what one of these would reject is computed like any other, and its entry in the list names the decision.
14. **Quotations are compared.** Each quotation a reply or an agent relies on is compared with the text or the maths; where it is not found, that is recorded and the point is read on what the text says.
15. **Records, by Opus, and a plain-words file** (S48): after the review and the orchestrator's decisions, one Opus agent writes the records (what Part A found, the log entry, status and lessons), and then `plain words/108 Part A - what the variations found, in plain words.md` for the owner, which says "candidate" or "explanation", never "model", for what the theory judges (S43).
