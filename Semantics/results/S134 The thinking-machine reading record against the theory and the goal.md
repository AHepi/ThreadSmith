## 3. The bounded constructor: would it construct or select?

### 3.1 The machine, in the theory's terms

- **Its own boundary, β_M** (declared now, under Reading C): inside, the machine as run: its elementary operations, the constructor, the event record, the ledger, the working records, the registered operations, the observation policy and the limits; outside, the world (the figure and the sensor's physics), the evaluator's knowledge of how figures are generated, the designers, and the teaching words. **A boundary taking in the designers, β_D**: the same plus the designers who wrote the grammar, the operations, the constants, the order, the revision policy, the calibration, the limits and the task.
- **Its question** is declared by the designers: target the figure; query the sensor's reading at a position (and, in the teaching phase, a word for a history); the contract, the positions and changes admitted (BPC L3, L25). Its claimed aims (predict readings; distinguish; later, predict movement and plan) are declared too.
- **It learns by four routes**, which the theory reads differently:
  - **(a) First enumeration**: short typed expressions in a fixed order, each kept while compatible with the ledger (BPC L9, L13).
  - **(b) Repair of a failed candidate**: replace subexpressions of the failed one, smallest first, each replacement checked against every retained observation, the place of the failure guiding which parts are tried first (BPC L15).
  - **(c) Registration**: a subprogram of a survivor becomes a callable operation (BPC L17).

- **Against the outside machine** (S133 material; S133 section 1.3): that machine searched a fixed arithmetic grammar by "first-exact-fit" against supplied teaching measurements, a supplied scorer acting on its proposals (29,174 proposals with paired feedback); S133 reads it, on the main reading, as route (a) alone. ### 3.3 The naming trap

The designers write the operations, the typing, the constants, the composition rules, the enumeration order, the replacement order and the limits (BPC L5, L9, L23). Everything the machine can reach is in the closure of that grammar; registration makes members of the closure cheaper, not new (the proposal says so: no unexplained source of new powers, TMT L60). So:

- **The family is named one level up** (S118's grade 2, for the family): no single formula is written in, but the family is. **Which member** is fixed by the world's readings, not by any code (S118's grade 3, for the member, as its row 5, prediction of what the world does next).
- **What the machine can do is construction within a named family**: S124's Reading 3 (constructed on selected material), with the family *declared* by its writers rather than selected by a history (L201: construction may operate on selected material; L405: a binding newly prepared inside received content is constructed and the rest keeps its inherited provenance; D12.4, part by part). S130 D.2 said it plainly: whatever such a system constructs, we built the constructor for this family.
- **The shortness bias is part of the naming.** Short expressions first, with multiply and compare among the primitives, makes the disc rule a few symbols long; the outside machine found its rules within 29,174 proposals. A family chosen so that the expected answers are short is a prior that names their neighbourhood (the critique's "its candidate generator offers the two completed definitions", IDC L3, in a milder form). The test is a world whose right relation is **long** in the declared grammar, or has no short form at all, the analogue of S132's novel-parts arm.
- **The naming trap in S130's narrow sense** (an outside checker acting on the subject during the work, C.6) is **closed** for routes (a) to (c): the check is the machine's own comparison against its own readings. It is open for route (d): the supplied word is a teacher's answer.

### 3.4 The provenance of its question

- **The question is the designers'.** Target, query, contract and aims are declared (ρ_p declared, L153). The machine never finds a question (QF, Argument 5): it has no step that makes a new contract, and the ellipse case (TMT L96) asks it to add a relation within the same question, not to ask a new one.
- **The problem is the machine's.** When two survivors in its ledger predict different readings at a reachable, unvisited position (BPC L21), it holds, in D10.1's sense, two rivals offered for the same question, conflicting at an admitted pair of the contract, neither ruled out by anything it can use (its check against retained readings), a problem of the first kind; moving there and recording the reading is a test (L317). Reading the machine as an assessor (its admitted forms, its accepted premises) is by hand (Θ, I90), as S130 read a subject's notebook.
- **When its survivors all agree**, it keeps inspecting while resources last (BPC L21); a problem of the second kind (rivals that agree on the contract and differ only outside it) it cannot find, since it has no step that widens the contract.

**Plainly, by the owner's word.** Such a machine would **find** its first rules, as the outside machine did. When one of them fails and it repairs it by changing the failed formula and checking the new version against everything it has itself seen, the repaired rule would be **worked out at the machine's own boundary on the theory's present reading, but only inside the family of formulas its builders wrote**; at a boundary taking in the builders it is still the machine's repair, and the family is still theirs. It would never work out the family itself, nor find a question. Whether that repair counts as worked out in the owner's sense turns on the same open question as a learner trained on a teacher's answers (S131 item 3), and on whether its repair is prepared from the failure or only filtered by it, which the interventions below decide for an actual run.

**What would settle it, by intervention** (the audit's own suggestions first):
1. **Remove the newly constructed relation** (IDC L13; S126's form): knock out the repaired subexpression or the registered operation, a sham removal, then restore; the ability goes, stays, and comes back. Run once with learning paused (TMT L102) and once with it on, recording whether and how it is rebuilt (a reconstruction is construction, L405).
2. **The same-final-observation test** (IDC L5; TMT L74): two histories ending in the same reading with different answers, position, duration and order varied apart from the answer; resetting or scrambling the relevant working record destroys the distinction (BPC L19); a count-only rival checked to fail.
3. **A withheld compositional structure** (IDC L13): simpler relations learned first, a task needing a new combination of them; the new relation inspected and then removed.
4. **Order permutation** and **same ledger without the failure** (3.2, witness 3): decide reading S against reading K for this machine.
5. **Part-level "what if" questions** (3.2, witness 4): decide explanatory use.
6. **A long-form world** (3.3): decide how much the family's shortness carried the result.

## 5. What should enter the constructor experiment's design

For the owner and for S133's design (S133's file is not edited here). Each with its reason.

1. **A clock-only rival in the box generator's checks.** Keep a box only if an account using only the number of actions so far and the present settings mispredicts at least one action in five (as the memoryless trap does), and include sealed pairs of equal-length histories that end in the same settings with different readouts. *Reason*: S122's read-counting finding; the critique's clock (IDC L5; TMT L76).
2. **"Undetermined" allowed, scored, and seeded.** A few sealed questions whose answer no history within the budget's shapes decides (the generator can check this), credited only when answered "undetermined". *Reason*: a compulsory choice hides ignorance (IDC L9; TMT L98); D10.1's problem of the second kind.
3. **Out-of-account questions with "supported" or "a guess"** (section 4, the MISSING test).
4. **A designed fork.** In some sessions the brief gives an opening record (actions and readouts already observed) on which two simple accounts both fit and differ at one available action; score whether the next action splits them, against the share of available actions that would. *Reason*: IDC L15; it makes D10.1's problem appear at a known moment, as S133's change makes the difficulty appear at a known action.
5. **A withheld composition**, as a later arm: boxes in a first stretch show two part behaviours separately; the main box combines them in a way never shown; the knock-out deletes the notebook line holding the combination. *Reason*: IDC L13; Origin's newness against the repertoire (L413 to L423).
6. **A ladder of box sizes** (4, 6 and 8 slots) with the subject and brief fixed, cost per box recorded. *Reason*: IDC L17; exhaustive search can hide the absence of construction, and the cost curve is the proposal's hardest unknown (TMT L124) measured.
7. **Reason use on the evidence as well as on the account.** S132's replay alters the notebook's binding; S130 A.4 row 6 also asks for the *objection* altered. In version 2, rerun the repair stretch with the readout that broke the card altered (a different wrong value) and with it consistently recoded: the repair should follow the altered readout and not the recoding. *Reason*: it is the S/K question for the subject (a repair prepared from the failure follows its content), and D9.11's own test.
8. **Build the bounded constructor as the machine-without-a-language-model arm, in the same box world, beside the language-model subject.**
   - **Two variants**: with its repair step (route (b)); and with the repair step switched off (first enumeration only, which is S132's blind search on the same grammar and budget). The difference between them, on the same boxes and budget, measures what holding the failed candidate adds: reading K against reading S, as a number.
   - **What it would be**: a typed model language for part tables and wiring, the enumerator, the ledger, repair by replacement, registration, the observation policy (where survivors disagree) under the box's own budgets (60 actions, 6 panel uses), the limits with "unresolved", and an export of its trace to S133's provenance encoder. **Cost, rough**: about 1,500 to 2,500 lines of Python with tests on top of the shared generator, simulator, scorer and knock-out (S133 section 5 put 1,000 to 1,500 for its sketch; the audit's version adds registration, the limits and the trace export); on the order of **1 to 5 CPU-hours** for 32 boxes at a declared budget of about 100,000 candidate evaluations each, more if the budget is raised (above 3 CPU-hours needs the owner's word); **no model calls**.
   - **What it shows that the language model cannot**: no training to relay from, so **the relay trap closes completely** (only the designers' grammar entered, and it is listed); **the trace is exact**, so whether a repair was prepared from the failure or filtered by it is read off the code and confirmed by order permutation, and the theory's warning about emitted text (Argument 9) does not arise; and it is **a thing built**, which is the owner's goal (S88). **What it cannot escape**: the naming trap; its best result is "worked out within a family we wrote" (S118's grade 2 for the family). On boxes most of its repairs may be table entries changed (parametric, a selection response); only a change of a part's number of states or of the wiring is structural (Argument 10).
   - **When**: built with the shared pieces, and **run first, before the 9,000 model calls, as the calibration of the provenance test**: on this subject the right answer is known from its code, so the test must class the repair variant constructed and the no-repair variant selected; if it cannot, it cannot read notebooks either. This changes the order S133 recommended (the language model first), not its design, and is the owner's choice.
9. **The world.** **The sealed box, for both arms.** For a language-model subject the owner's 24-by-24 world leaves the relay trap open: circle, square and straight-line motion are textbook, and so are their natural failures and repairs (an ellipse, a pull, a bounce; S133 section 4.2 found the same). The box's random rule tables have no textbook form. For the non-language-model arm the relay trap does not exist, so the owner's world would be fair there, but its grammar makes the expected rules short (the naming trap at its strongest), its interventions on parts would need new edits, and its code is not in this project. If the owner wants continuity with the outside experiment, a later arm for the bounded constructor only, in the pixel world, with a hidden change that has no textbook form (a displacement drawn from a random table by position), is the fair version.

- **Held or represented.** Witness 2 holds on the formal core's reading (Held, D12.2 with T′) and fails on reply 04's stricter one (an occurrence that represents the target with non-declared provenance); the text's L197 says "available as a represented target", and the core reads it as Held (the second check of round 2, Q1 ruled).
