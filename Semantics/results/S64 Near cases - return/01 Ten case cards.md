## O15 — Sam's inherited marks

**What I expect:** The missing arrow history will leave the required starting stock incompletely grounded while leaving the described account of Sam’s own cutting intact.

**Written by: an outside author.**

**Aimed at:** E, row 57, completeness of the history required for a stated starting stock.

**The situation:** Sam starts from two diagrams. Every line on both has a recorded preparation: who drew it, from which trial, on which day. One symbol appears on both, a small arrow meaning "the blank catches here", and nobody recorded who first drew it or from what. Sam cuts his new blank by following those arrows.

**The thoughtful person's verdict:** Sam's account of his own work is complete. Where the arrows came from is a hole in the history of the diagrams, and it is a hole in his account, because his cut followed them.

**The theory’s verdict, condition by condition:**

| Condition | Earlier-version assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Component fidelity | Empty as an Account test: no candidate account of the lock’s workings and its responses is supplied; detailed preparation records do not by themselves establish component fidelity. |
| Question fidelity | Empty as an Account test: the case asks how much of a representation history is accounted for, and supplies no explanatory answer profile to compare with a lock question. |
| Non-circular dependence | Empty as an Account test: there is no proposed explanatory derivation whose starting premises can be checked; the missing provenance is assessed separately under E. |
| Non-vacuity | Empty as an Account test: no explanatory change list is supplied, although the two diagrams and the cut provide an ordinary historical situation. |

**Under the earlier version with R2 and the S62 clauses:** The earlier Part X requires a construction witness to identify incoming carriers and constructed bindings; R2 E adds an earlier starting stock and a grounded source for inherited representation. Sam’s own activity can be fully described at its local scope while the history of a content he used is incompletely supplied. Recording the preparation of lines on both diagrams does not also establish the origin of the arrow meaning used on both. The theory therefore withholds the claim that every required starting representation has been accounted for. It does not infer that no arrow history ever existed or that Sam did no work. The S62 clauses supply no missing provenance and do not change this result.

**Best reply on the theory’s behalf:** A witness for Sam’s local contribution need not make him the originator of everything he received; separating his contribution from the inherited stock is exactly what E permits. But an explicit claim to complete grounding of that stock still owes the arrow’s source.

**Does a difference survive that reply?** There is no surviving disagreement on these two scopes: completeness about Sam’s own actions can coexist with incompleteness about a required inherited representation. The result is local and does not prove that selected histories ground every starting representation.

**How I know each claim:** The situation explicitly supplies both diagram records, the missing arrow origin and Sam’s use. The narrower-versus-inclusive reading of the thoughtful verdict is the assessment made here, using earlier Part X and R2 E.

**Stage B row, its own pair, side, agreement and mark:** Row 57, Amendment E, <u>every required starting representation</u>; <u>complete grounding of representation</u>.

> For a specified starting stock of two diagrams, supply the required history for both rather than only one. Remove the history for either diagram: that particular stock is no longer wholly accounted for.

The case lands on the second side: a required part of the specified stock lacks its supplied history, even though the omission is a shared symbol within both diagrams rather than the whole of one diagram. That is the row’s relevant incompleteness, not an additional demand that Sam invent the diagrams. This agrees with the exact thoughtful verdict above. **PLACED.**

## O16 — The worn key and the diary

**What I expect:** The key will remain an independent physical witness while the diagram-derived dates add no independent date evidence; the original independent-history versus solely circular pair may not place that mixed record.

**Written by: an outside author.**

**Aimed at:** E, row 52, a purported provenance supported by a later representation of that provenance.

**The situation:** Sam's present diagram matches a worn trial key found in his drawer. To date the trial key, Sam wrote a diary last week, working the dates out from the stages visible in the diagram.

**The thoughtful person's verdict:** The worn key is evidence on its own. The diary is the diagram talking about itself. Sam has one independent witness, and the diary is a second copy of the first.

**The theory’s verdict, condition by condition:**

| Condition | Earlier-version assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Component fidelity | Empty as an Account test: matching a worn key with a diagram does not supply a candidate account and a component-by-component change list. |
| Question fidelity | Empty as an Account test: the case concerns the sources of historical evidence, with no complete explanatory answer profile proposed. |
| Non-circular dependence | Empty as an Account test: the circular diary support is a provenance issue under E, not an established failure of a supplied Part V explanatory derivation. |
| Non-vacuity | Empty as an Account test: no explanatory change list is stated; the worn key and the diary are the actual evidence whose independence is being assessed. |

**Under the earlier version with R2 and the S62 clauses:** Earlier Parts IV and X distinguish physical history from a modeller’s declaration, and earlier Part IX keeps an evidence reference distinct from the event it supports. R2 E prohibits manufacturing provenance solely from the later diagram’s supposed representational status. The key is present independently of the diary, whereas the diary’s dates were worked out from the diagram and therefore are not a second independent dating source. The supplied physical key does not establish when the trial happened or by itself prove which drawing or key copied which. This record supports a partial independent witness and a dependent reconstruction; it supplies neither a wholly grounded history nor a history resting solely on the diagram. None of the S62 clauses repairs that evidential gap.

**Best reply on the theory’s behalf:** E says solely, so it need not reject a record because one part of it is dependent. It can retain the key as evidence and decline to count the diary as fresh support without treating either document as worthless.

**Does a difference survive that reply?** The broader theory can agree with the thoughtful separation of independent and repeated evidence. The original pair, however, does not say how its two sides apply when an independent object and a dependent chronology coexist; that missing near contrast survives.

**How I know each claim:** The worn key, its matching pattern, the diary’s creation last week and its dependence on diagram stages are all supplied. No independently established date or copying direction has been added.

**Stage B row, its own pair, side, agreement and mark:** Row 52, Amendment E, <u>no binding can acquire a provenance solely because its own later representation presupposes that provenance</u>.

> Add independently described earlier trials or construction that produced the diagram. Remove those events and argue only that they must have occurred because the diagram now counts as represented: the supposed provenance supports itself.

The case sits outside both complete changes: its key does not supply the independently described earlier trial history of the first side, and its surviving key prevents the whole record from being supported only by the circular argument of the second. The pair therefore does not earn the full thoughtful verdict, even though R2’s prohibition is compatible with it. **BORROWED: JUDGEMENT.**

## O17 — The robot's log and the maker's manual

**What I expect:** The recorded execution of the diagnostic routine will survive deletion of the manual’s circular sentence, so the extra bad argument will not defeat the independent history.

**Written by: an outside author.**

**Aimed at:** I, row 94, grounding ownership without grounding it in the capability it is supposed to explain.

**The situation:** A repair robot keeps a log of every comparison it runs. The log shows which routine, running inside the robot, produced today's diagnosis. The maker's manual says the routine is the robot's own "because the robot can diagnose".

**The thoughtful person's verdict:** Ownership here rests on the log, which shows the routine running inside the robot. Strike the manual's sentence and nothing is lost; keep only the manual's sentence and there is nothing.

**The theory’s verdict, condition by condition:**

| Condition | Earlier-version assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Component fidelity | Empty as an Account test: the log describes an executed comparison history rather than a supplied explanation of the diagnosed fault under changes. |
| Question fidelity | Empty as an Account test: ownership of the diagnostic process is the question here, and no separate explanatory answer profile is proposed. |
| Non-circular dependence | Empty as an Account test: the manual’s circular attribution is assessed under I’s ownership rule, not counted as a Part V proof of the diagnosis. |
| Non-vacuity | Empty as an Account test: no full explanatory change list is given; the independent execution record and the redundant manual sentence supply the ownership contrast. |

**Under the earlier version with R2 and the S62 clauses:** Earlier Part XII requires owned realization and says that a theorist’s description does not amount to the system’s possession of a protocol. R2 I supplies the independent process-and-resource requirement. Here the log identifies the routine actually executing the comparisons that produced today’s diagnosis; the manual supplies a separate circular assertion. Removing that assertion leaves the described process history. The positive evidence is the logged execution of the robot’s routine, not the mere position of an otherwise external thinker inside its shell. Keeping only the manual would remove this independent basis. This is local ownership of the described work, not proof of retained capacity on every input or universal understanding. The S62 clauses do not alter that attribution.

**Best reply on the theory’s behalf:** Location inside a casing is insufficient under I, but the case does not merely locate a person or an unexplained box inside the robot: it identifies the robot’s performed comparisons and their producing routine. The case therefore supplies the kind of process evidence the manual fails to supply.

**Does a difference survive that reply?** There is no surviving disagreement about the supplied history. The circular sentence is dispensable because there is another stated basis; the assessment makes no general inference from enclosure to ownership.

**How I know each claim:** The log’s scope and the producing routine are stipulated in the situation. The deletion contrast is the outside author’s fixed verdict, evaluated here against earlier Part XII and R2 I.

**Stage B row, its own pair, side, agreement and mark:** Row 94, Amendment I, <u>Do not define that ownership by the very Can claim whose owned realization it is meant to ground</u>.

> Identify the controller’s actual processes and resources, then use them in arguing for its ability. Justify ownership only by “it can do this” and ability only by “this history is owned”: neither receives an independent basis.

The case lands on the first side because an actual producing process is independently identified; the presence of an additional circular sentence does not make the account rest only on that sentence. The manual-only contrast belongs to the second side. The pair agrees with the exact thoughtful verdict above. **PLACED.**

## O18 — The one tooth

**What I expect:** The changed tooth will support a local construction attribution while the unchanged teeth retain their inherited origin.

**Written by: an outside author.**

**Aimed at:** E, row 58, relay combined with a later recipient construction.

**The situation:** A learner receives a complete key diagram from Sam. At a new lock she finds a catch the diagram never covered, files one tooth to fit it, and keeps every other tooth as the diagram gave it.

**The thoughtful person's verdict:** The one tooth is hers. The rest is relay. She built something, small, and it is exactly the tooth.

**The theory’s verdict, condition by condition:**

| Condition | Earlier-version assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Component fidelity | Empty as a complete Account test: the catch and alteration identify a local content-sensitive relation, but the case does not supply a full explanatory account and all its changes. |
| Question fidelity | Empty as an Account test: the assessed question is who contributed which binding, not whether a complete proposed explanation answers every admitted lock change. |
| Non-circular dependence | Empty as an Account test: no explanatory derivation is supplied; the new tooth’s history and the other teeth’s inherited history are assessed under E. |
| Non-vacuity | Empty as a complete Account test: the new obstruction is a real contrast, but no full explanatory change list is stated. |

**Under the earlier version with R2 and the S62 clauses:** Earlier Part X explicitly distinguishes reconstruction from relay and requires a nontrivial binding for construction; R2 E permits an inherited representation followed by the recipient’s own new binding. Finding a catch absent from Sam’s diagram and filing the tooth to fit it gives a content-sensitive preparation at precisely that location. Small physical size does not reduce the change to typeface or relabeling. The learner receives local construction credit for that tooth-obstruction relation; she does not thereby originate the diagram’s other relations. This is the construction attribution in the ordinary case, not proof of every wider capacity or of an exhaustive explanation of the lock. The S62 clauses add no contrary threshold.

**Best reply on the theory’s behalf:** One tooth is enough if it supplies a previously absent relation that the obstruction guides; E does not demand that the whole inherited design be remade. Conversely, successful filing without the described connection would not establish the same construction history.

**Does a difference survive that reply?** No difference survives: the attribution follows the prepared relation while preserving the copied parts as inherited. The outside verdict’s small contribution is not inflated into authorship of the entire design.

**How I know each claim:** The received complete diagram, its failure to cover the new catch, the learner’s filing and her retention of all other teeth are supplied facts. The distinction uses earlier Part X and R2 E.

**Stage B row, its own pair, side, agreement and mark:** Row 58, Amendment E, <u>Relay alone supplies neither Build nor Origin</u>.

> Let a recipient inherit Sam’s diagram and then prepare a previously absent correspondence to another obstruction: that later preparation supplies a separate construction candidate. Change only the received diagram’s typeface and award the recipient its original binding: copying supplies no such construction.

The case lands on the first side: after receiving the diagram the learner prepares a previously absent correspondence to another obstruction. Retaining the other teeth does not turn that preparation into the second side’s typeface-only relay. The pair agrees with the exact thoughtful verdict above. **PLACED.**

**REVISED ONLY:** the anchored explanatory grade invoked by R2 H and its S62 restatement belongs to the revised theory; no such grade is used here to decide the earlier version’s physical repair attribution.

**Does a difference survive that reply?** The broad cautious verdict agrees with the outside author, but the original pair opposes a record identifying joint contributions to a record of mere overlapping attendance. **How I know each claim:** Both acts, their one-second window, the complete-record description and separate sufficiency are supplied. No finer order, common closing instant, hidden valve route or division of causal credit has been invented.

The thoughtful verdict is compatible with H but not placed by this pair. **BORROWED: JUDGEMENT.**
