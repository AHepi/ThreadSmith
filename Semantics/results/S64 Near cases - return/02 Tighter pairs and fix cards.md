## H, row 90 — O20: actual stopping and a sufficient later act

Earlier Parts IX and XI, amended by R2 H, require contribution, while this pair shows why the two isolated sufficiencies alone do not earn the whole thoughtful verdict.

## H64 — Fix card for H's remaining attribution judgement

**The proposed or changed clause.** Append the following three sentences to the applicable attribution portion of R2 H and the S62 H clause. They replace no outside verdict and supply no numerical credit rule.

> [yours] Distinguish an act's <u>ability to stop the failure on its own</u>, its <u>participation in the actual repair</u>, and a claim to <u>exclusive attribution</u>.
>
> [yours] Independent sufficiency alone establishes neither exclusive attribution nor its impossibility; <u>complete records need not yield a unique division of credit</u>.
>
> [yours] Credit the contribution established by the <u>historical argument</u>, and leave a proposed exclusive or divided attribution unsettled when that argument does not establish it.

**The case that forced it.** O20's complete record contains two acts each sufficient in isolation. The original pair contrasted documented joint contributions with mere overlapping attendance. It did not test whether complete evidence can leave exclusive attribution unavailable, or whether isolated sufficiency settles actual participation. H64 preserves those separate questions instead of classifying O20 as an ordinary shortage of records.

**Two cases it must leave alone.** O13, written by an outside author, must retain Ben's actual repair, Ana's correct unused explanation and the absence of repair through that explanation. O9, written by an outside author, must retain the float's producing role and the dial's reporting role; a merely available response cannot become an actually operating part through this clause.

**Its weakest sentence, read as another author's.** “Independent sufficiency alone establishes neither exclusive attribution nor its impossibility; complete records need not yield a unique division of credit.” The second half could be used to evade a history that actually settles priority. The attack below holds that risk against a case.

### H64 attack — The earlier closure

Written by: me

**What I expect.** A recorded priority difference will support a specific attribution even though both valves are sufficient in isolation.

**The situation.** Mara closes a supply valve at noon and the leak stops. At one second past noon, Joel closes a second valve on the now dry pipe. Each valve would have stopped the original flow alone. The record shows the two times and the moment the leak stopped.

**The thoughtful person's verdict.** Mara stopped this leak. Joel closed a valve that could have stopped it, after there was no leak left to stop.

| Audit condition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Component fidelity | The described valve closures and flow record supply the contrast between actual stopping and a later sufficient alternative. |
| Question fidelity | The question concerns what stopped this leak, not which valve could stop a leak by itself. |
| Non-circular dependence | The priority and flow facts support the attribution without inserting Mara's exclusive credit as a premise. |
| Non-vacuity | Both closures are possible, and reversing their order would alter the stopping history. |

**The earlier theory's verdict with applicable R2, S62 and H64.** Mara's closure contributes to this repair; Joel's later closure is not the act that stopped the already absent flow. Earlier IX's actual history and XI's production requirement support the attribution; no stronger anchored grade is used.

**Do they differ.** No.

**Best reply on the clause's behalf.** H64 says sufficiency alone does not decide the question and that records need not yield a unique division; it does not say records never settle it. This record adds a decisive temporal dependence.

**Does the difference survive the reply.** No; the concrete history prevents the permissive sentence from becoming a reason to suspend every attribution.

**How I know each claim.** The situation is written for this audit, and the assessment follows earlier IX and XI with R2 H. It is a test of the clause's limit, not an independent finding about real valves.

**What it gives up.** H64 gives up deriving exclusive credit, or denying its possibility, from isolated sufficiency alone. It supplies no proportion, unique tie-breaker or full causal theory of overdetermination. O20 therefore remains BORROWED: JUDGEMENT for its whole comparative attribution, while its two sufficient acts are retained.

**Whether the earlier version already said this.** Earlier Part IX, “Histories,” says:

> A history \(h\) is a set of occurrences with an acyclic causal precedence \(\prec_h\) and a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated. An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts.

Earlier Part XI, “Repair,” says:

> (P) does not rank alternatives. Losses outside \(P\) must be exposed.

The actual-history requirement was already present. (P)'s refusal to rank repair alternatives does not itself decide how causal credit is divided. The explicit separation of isolated sufficiency, participation and exclusive attribution is this clause's elaboration; R2 F and H already distinguish physical contribution from reason use. Nothing here treats the old represented-input definition as a requirement that a valve must think before it can stop water.

## I64 — Fix card for I's remaining comparative credit judgement

**The proposed or changed clause.** Append these sentences to R2 I's division-of-contributions paragraph; retain the permission to learn from instruction and receive questions.

> [yours] In assisted inquiry, identify separately who <u>supplied the alternatives and separating test</u>, who <u>applied it to evidence</u>, and who <u>chose the answer</u>, without inferring who originated a contribution merely from who sent it.

O18, written by an outside author, must retain the learner's one newly fitted tooth without transferring authorship of all the received teeth. Neither instructional dependence nor small size cancels a contribution.

### I64 attack — The misleading test

Written by: me

**What I expect.** A supplied test will remain the expert's input while the learner's work correcting its application prevents automatic expert dominance.

**The situation.** An expert sends Rani two possible pump faults and tells her to compare two pressure readings. Rani notices that one gauge was installed backwards. She works out how to correct its reading, checks the correction and then chooses the fault. Without her correction the supplied comparison would select the wrong fault.

**The thoughtful person's verdict.** The expert supplied the alternatives and test. Rani supplied the correction that made this diagnosis work. The expert does not own her correction merely because he sent the test.

| Audit condition | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Component fidelity | The reversed gauge and Rani's correction supply the described contrast in how the diagnostic comparison responds. |
| Question fidelity | The attribution concerns this corrected diagnosis, so merely supplying the uncorrected test does not answer whose contribution repaired its use. |
| Non-circular dependence | The wrong uncorrected result and corrected comparison support Rani's contribution without assuming a percentage of credit. |
| Non-vacuity | Correcting or retaining the reversed reading changes the chosen fault in the described case. |

**The earlier theory's verdict with applicable R2, S62 and I64.** The expert supplies the test and Rani performs a content-sensitive correction and choice; no whole-achievement dominance follows automatically. Earlier X's construction witness and R2 I's contribution distinction preserve her contribution.

**Do they differ.** No; no proportion is needed to preserve either contribution.

**Best reply on the clause's behalf.** The second sentence allows a supplied test to be a substantive contribution, not to override all later work. The third requires a case-specific argument instead of counting instructions or treating sending as origination.

**Does the difference survive the reply.** No, but the clause still does not supply a general rule for which contribution matters most.

**How I know each claim.** The situation is written for this audit. Its dependencies are stipulated and the attribution is worked out from earlier X and XII with R2 I, not observed in a real diagnostic trial.

**What it gives up.** I64 does not derive comparative importance from a permitted-assistance label, the number of steps or an output's correctness. It neither excludes taught understanding nor calls every supplied test the difficult part. O21's division is retained; its mostly judgement remains BORROWED: JUDGEMENT.

**Whether the earlier version already said this.** Earlier Part X, “Construction,” says:

> \(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an actual subhistory owned by \(s\) and delimited at \(e\) prepares a represented organization for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers. A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting representation. Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not.

Earlier Part XII, “Owned capability,” says:

> \(\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)\) requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one under the same continuity and resource contract. A theorist's description of a protocol is not the system's possession of it.

The earlier text already separates incoming carriers from owned construction. R2 I explicitly makes assistance relative to the capacity attributed. I64 adds the intermediate diagnostic division and exposes comparative importance as a further judgement; it does not claim the source already defined mostly.
