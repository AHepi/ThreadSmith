**Where the replies agree or conflict.**
- **Replies 06, 01, 05 and the S114 check agree** on every mechanism of save and reload: sequences, cells and averages are kept; CPU state, random state, resources, generation counts and task records are not. Reply 06 adds that pieces ending at update label 1,000 run 1,001 updates each (S113's 50 pieces: 50,050 updates, 0.1% more; too small to matter). Reply 06 calls the reload's effect "unknown"; the S114 audit has since measured it for two environments. **No reply conflicts with the S114 audit.** The one difference with the S114 check is emphasis: reply 06 stresses that the 2.14.0 tag is 133 commits older than our build; S114 found no difference in any file that matters.
- **Replies 01, 05 and 08 propose the same kind of probe** (every saved program rerun on fixed inputs, all tasks at zero reward). Reply 01's scripts implement it; replies 05 and 08 add retention rules and a never-rewarded arithmetic set. One probe serves all three.
- **Replies 03 (P0), 06 and the first reply (S114) all put the restart question first**; it is answered for FIXED GRADED and COMMON TASKS PAY LESS (S114 audit) and for saving alone (here).
- **Replies 05 and S114's first reply both propose replaying GROWING LIST's schedule** on another run, to separate "the list responds to the programs" from "the list changes".
- **Replies 03 (P5) and 04 (Experiment B) both ask whether what is inside a founder matters for what it learns later**; 04 with hand-made founders, 03 with evolved ones and a knocked-out NAND.
- **The one tension is with the first reply (S114):** its proposals count progress by new problems met; reply 03 says a finite list (77 tasks, or 9 paid tasks) cannot show open-ended learning however the rewards move, and reply 08 says the same of its own designs. The plan below therefore measures kept capabilities and never-rewarded ones, not only counts.

## 4. The two patches (replies 07 and 06): batch 1, done

S113's runner (PID 2411) exited at about 16:09 UTC. Each patch was then applied to its own fresh copy of the source (the stock checkout and build untouched) and built with the stock build's settings (Release, command line only), two compile jobs at a time: reply 07's in 236 s, reply 06's in 225 s, both with upstream warnings only.

- **Reply 07** (`RUN_BOUNDED`): the four expected output files matched exactly; with copy, division, insertion, deletion and point change rates all set to 1 the rows did not change; the reaction-pause test gave identical traces before and after (bonus 64); a 400-update ordinary run with the patched binary gave data files identical to the stock binary's. The tool works as described; it is ready for batch 2 if full output streams are wanted.
- **Reply 06** (the four miniatures): all six hand-made cases gave the reported credits; the twelve 5,000-update runs (three seeds each) finished in 0.4 to 2.8 s each. In these worlds of 16 to 48 cells, C's judges were never scored (every scoring invalid), B2's problem bank was invalid at 42 to 50 of 51 scorings, B3 had 6 to 25 usable graphs, B1's target moved among 13 to 21 values. The mechanisms run; in worlds this small the problem-setting and judging hardly start. A larger version would be a new design and needs the owner's word (it moves the choosing into the programs, S63's "environment made of the machines").
