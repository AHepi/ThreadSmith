# 50 Corpus - outside sources for the skill test (phase 0 of plan 49)

Compiled 20 September 2026. Nothing here has been sent to DeepSeek. No reply has been read.
Every file was fetched by program (`fetch.py`) from the address in its header and trimmed by the rule in plan 49.
Each was checked once, by its opening lines, for one thing only: that it is the document named and that it explains something. No weak point was looked for or written down.

By field: science 19, computability 7, philosophy 6, fiction 6, economics 5, design 2, rules 2, instructions 2.
By standing: contested 27, settled 11, open 11. Standing is recorded here and is never shown to the reader.

**Pairs (5):** E2a with E2b; S1 with S2; S14 with S14b; S3 with S4; S7 with S8. A pair is a claim and its published critique, or two rival accounts of one thing. Pairs are what the *build the best rival*, *check the patches* and *testing against cases* parts of the skill need, and a single document cannot supply.

## Known gaps in this corpus
- **Written instructions** has only two sources, and both are reporting guidelines from the same family (CONSORT and PRISMA). A third of a different kind would be better.
- **Why is there none of it** has two, both from astronomy.
- No source tests the skill's **router** by itself; that is the job of the run mode, not the corpus.
- No source is in a language other than English, and none is a spoken or conversational explanation.
- Nine sources are capped at their word limit, so the reader sees the front of a longer work: S13, C2, C3, C6, C7, F3, F5, I1, P7. Each header says so.

## What the corpus cannot do
- It cannot show the skill is right. It can show where the skill's words fail to reach.
- It is chosen by Claude, which wrote the skill. The choosing is the fitting risk here: a corpus picked to suit the skill would flatter it. Against that: every source is someone else's document, written for its own purpose, and the standings come from published disputes, not from this project.
- Nine sources are truncated, so a report that faults a paper for something answered in its later pages is the corpus's fault, not the reader's. Those nine are listed above.

## Traps
- Sending a file with its provenance header. The header names the source and the trim; strip it before any reader sees the text.
- Showing a source's standing, its pair, or this file to the reader.
- Reading these texts for their weak points before the marking plan is frozen.
