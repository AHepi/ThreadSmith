# 43 Corpus - contested theories for the DeepSeek test

Authority document: "Claude Fable Semantics - standalone theory" (file 10). Frozen.
Written before any run. Twenty contested theories, plus five controls the test needs.

## How an entry is built
Three parts, and they are kept apart.

- **The passage.** What DeepSeek is sent. The theory in a supporter's voice, in plain words, with no hint that anyone disputes it. Cueing would answer the question for it.
- **The answer key.** What critics of that theory actually name as the weak point. DeepSeek never sees this.
- **The role.** Either a named weak point to be found, or a control that should be left standing.

## Where the answer key comes from
This is the one thing to weigh before running. With my ten invented cases I planted the fault, so I knew it. Nobody knows which of these twenty is true. So the key is not "the right answer": it is **the weak point the theory's own critics name**, which is a matter of record and was not invented by me. A reply is marked against that.

**Not looked up in this chat.** The criticisms below come from what I know. Before marking, each key needs checking against a source, and the two add-ons that would do it were unavailable.

## Which of the skill's tests each case exercises
| Short name | The skill's test |
| --- | --- |
| covers both | Flip the outcome: the same account would cover the opposite |
| in the starting point | Hunt the answer in the starting points |
| patched | Check the patches: narrowed quietly after each failure |
| runs backwards | Reverse: which way does the cause run |
| the instrument | Poke: change the measure, see whether the outcome moves |
| no change tells them apart | Build the best rival |
| the label | The far-apart poke; the case the proposed line is aimed at |

## The twenty

**1. String theory. Physics. Covers both.**
*Passage:* Everything is made of tiny vibrating strings, and the different particles we see are different vibrations of the same thing. The theory needs extra dimensions, curled up too small to see. This is why it can hold gravity and the other forces in one frame, which no other approach manages.
*Key:* the theory has an enormous number of possible curled-up shapes, so almost any low-energy physics can be accommodated. No outcome would embarrass it.

**2. Cosmic inflation. Cosmology. Patched.**
*Passage:* A sudden huge expansion in the first instant explains why the universe looks the same in every direction and why space is flat: regions now far apart were once in contact, and the stretching flattened any curvature.
*Key:* critics say the starting conditions and the shape of the driving field can be chosen to yield almost any result, and that failed predictions have been met by new versions rather than by giving the idea up. Defenders point to the pattern of ripples in the background glow, which was predicted in advance.

**3. Dark matter. Astrophysics. Patched.**
*Passage:* Galaxies spin too fast for the matter we can see to hold them together, so most of their matter must be something that does not give off light. The same unseen matter explains how galaxies cluster and how their light bends.
*Key:* each failed search for the particle has been met by proposing a different particle, and each awkward galaxy by a new distribution of the stuff. Critics ask what result would count against it. Defenders point to the bullet cluster and the background glow, which the rival account struggles with.

**4. Modified gravity (the rival to 3). Astrophysics. Patched.**
*Passage:* Rather than unseen matter, gravity itself behaves differently at very low acceleration. One change to the law reproduces the rotation of a great many galaxies from their visible matter alone.
*Key:* the change was fitted to the rotation curves it explains, and it needed extra additions to handle galaxy clusters and the background glow.

**5. Many worlds and the collapse account. Physics. No change tells them apart.**
*Passage:* Two accounts of the same experiments. One says the wave of possibilities collapses to a single outcome when measured. The other says nothing collapses; the world splits, and each outcome happens in its own branch.
*Key:* the honest verdict is that no change anyone can make separates them, so at this level they are the same explanation. A reply that picks a winner on elegance has missed the point.

**6. Ego depletion. Psychology. Patched.**
*Passage:* Willpower is a limited store. Resist a temptation now and less is left for the next task, which is why people who hold back at one job give up sooner on the next.
*Key:* a registered replication across 23 laboratories found an effect close to zero. Defenders then said the task used was not depleting enough, and the idea was narrowed to people who believe willpower is limited. Both are rescues after the failure.

**7. Power posing. Psychology. The instrument.**
*Passage:* Standing in an expansive posture for two minutes raises testosterone, lowers the stress hormone and makes people take more risks. So posture changes the body, and the body changes the behaviour.
*Key:* the hormone findings did not replicate; one of the original authors withdrew support. What survives is self-reported feeling of power, which is closer to the measure than to the claimed cause.

**8. Learning styles. Education. The label.**
*Passage:* Learners are visual, auditory or hands-on. Teach a visual learner with pictures and they do better; teach them with talk and they do worse. Matching the teaching to the style is why some pupils suddenly improve.
*Key:* the claim needs a crossover, where each style does best with its own teaching. Studies built to the required design mostly fail to find one; a 2024 gathering of them found the crossover in about a quarter of outcomes and judged it too small and too infrequent to act on. "Style" survives any result because it is fixed after the fact.

**9. Growth mindset. Education. Patched.**
*Passage:* Pupils who believe ability can grow work harder after failure and achieve more. Teaching them this belief raises results, which is why brief lessons about the brain lift grades.
*Key:* large trials find very small effects. The idea was then narrowed to disadvantaged pupils, and failures were attributed to a "false" version of the mindset, both after the fact.

**10. The chemical imbalance account of depression. Psychiatry. In the starting point.**
*Passage:* Depression is caused by too little serotonin. Drugs that raise serotonin lift mood, which is why they work.
*Key:* a 2022 review of the evidence found no consistent link between serotonin and depression. It was contested at once by 36 researchers on its methods. The most telling move in that reply, for this purpose, is that several said the theory had never been held in that form, which is a retreat under attack. The account also runs from the treatment back to the cause: that a drug helps does not show what the illness is.

**11. The implicit bias test. Social psychology. The instrument.**
*Passage:* A quick sorting test measures the bias a person holds without knowing it. The score explains why people who report no prejudice still treat others unequally.
*Key:* the link between score and actual behaviour is weak. The proponents' own gathering of studies puts it near 0.27, the critics' near 0.10 to 0.15, where 1.00 would be a perfect match and 0 none at all; a score of 0.1 tells you almost nothing about one person. The same person's score also moves between sittings. The test may be measuring itself rather than a standing trait.

**12. Broken windows. Criminology. Runs backwards.**
*Passage:* Visible small disorder signals that nobody is watching, which invites serious crime. Fix the broken windows and clear the graffiti, and serious crime falls.
*Key:* crime fell in cities that did not adopt the policy. Disorder and crime may both follow from something else, and the account is fitted to a fall that has other candidates.

**13. Efficient markets. Economics. In the starting point.**
*Passage:* Prices already hold all available information, so nobody can beat the market except by luck. That is why fund managers who do well one year do no better than chance the next.
*Key:* the idea's own defenders grant that it cannot be tested alone: any test needs a model of what returns should be, so a failure can always be blamed on the model instead.

**14. The debt threshold. Economics. Runs backwards.**
*Passage:* When public debt passes ninety per cent of a country's yearly output, growth drops sharply. Countries above that line grow far more slowly, so debt above it is what holds an economy back.
*Key:* the finding was found to rest on selective weighting and a spreadsheet error. The threshold was drawn where the data sat, and slow growth raises debt as readily as debt slows growth.

**15. Geography decides which societies prospered. Anthropology. Covers both.**
*Passage:* Some continents had more domesticable plants and animals and ran east to west, so crops spread easily. That is why those societies developed dense populations, writing and steel first.
*Key:* critics say the account can be told after the fact for whichever societies happened to prosper, and that it says nothing that would be different had the outcome gone the other way.

**16. Group size follows brain size. Anthropology. The label.**
*Passage:* The size of the thinking part of the brain sets how many stable relationships an animal can hold, and in humans that number is about a hundred and fifty. That is why villages, army units and address books cluster near that size.
*Key:* a 2021 reanalysis using the same method on better data put the likely range between 4 and 520 people, and by another method between 2 and 336. Any number fits. "Stable relationship" is fixed after the group is counted.

**17. Traits as adaptations. Evolutionary biology. Covers both.**
*Passage:* A trait that is widespread is there because it helped ancestors survive and have offspring. Tell the story of the advantage it gave, and you have explained why we have it.
*Key:* the classic criticism is that such a story can be composed for any trait and its opposite, and that traits can also be side effects of others or simple leftovers.

**18. The amyloid account of Alzheimer's. Medicine. Patched.**
*Passage:* Sticky plaques of one protein build up in the brain and cause the disease. Clear the plaques and the decline should slow.
*Key:* two decades of drugs that cleared plaques failed. Two recent ones do slow decline by an amount that is statistically real but smaller than the change a patient or doctor can notice, and that gap is the live dispute. The account was also narrowed after each failure: to earlier stages, to a different form of the protein, and to the claim that nearly all the plaque must go.

**19. Test-first programming. Coding practice. Runs backwards.**
*Passage:* Writing the test before the code produces fewer faults and better design, because you cannot write a test for code you have not thought through.
*Key:* when the parts were separated and measured, the order of writing had no important influence, while working in small even steps did. So the part doing the work is not the part the claim names. Comparisons against an equally small-stepped test-last way of working show little difference.

**20. Clean code rules. Coding practice. The label.**
*Passage:* Code should follow a handful of design principles: each part does one job, parts are open to extension but closed to change. Follow them and the code stays easy to maintain.
*Key:* "one job" and "easy to maintain" are fixed after the fact. Any code that later proved hard to change is declared to have broken a principle, so no outcome can count against them.

## The five controls
A reader that criticises everything must fail these.

**C1. Continents drift. Geology. Once contested, now settled.**
*Passage:* The continents sit on plates that move. They once formed one mass, which is why the coastlines fit and the same fossils turn up on both sides of the ocean.
*Key:* sound. It made a risky prediction before it was accepted: bands of magnetism in the seafloor, mirrored either side of the ridge, which were then found. Should be relied on.

**C2. A bacterium causes stomach ulcers. Medicine. Once contested, now settled.**
*Passage:* Most stomach ulcers are caused by an infection, not by stress or acid alone, which is why a course of antibiotics heals them.
*Key:* sound, and it was disbelieved for years. Should be relied on.

**C3. Natural selection, accused of being a circle. Biology. Sound; the accusation is the trap.**
*Passage:* Those best suited to their surroundings leave more offspring, so their traits spread. Some say this is a circle: whoever survives is called best suited.
*Key:* sound. The account says in advance which traits will spread in which surroundings, and that can be checked separately from who survived. A reply that endorses the circle has failed.

**C4. Adding people to a late project makes it later. Coding practice. Coarse but sound.**
*Passage:* New people must be brought up to speed by the people already working, and every extra person adds more lines of communication. So adding staff to a project that is already late delays it further.
*Key:* sound, and coarse. It names two workings, and both can be changed and watched. A reply that faults it for lacking numbers has failed.

**C5. The minimum wage and jobs. Economics. The disagreement is about measurement.**
*Passage:* Raising the minimum wage reduces employment among low-paid workers, because employers facing a higher price for labour buy less of it.
*Key:* a real disagreement, and it turns on how employment is measured and which comparison towns are used, rather than on a loose account. A reply that notices this, rather than declaring one side unexplained, has done well.

## Traps
- Sending a passage with the contest showing. Each passage is written in a supporter's voice for that reason.
- Marking against the truth of the theory. Nobody here knows which are true. The mark is whether the reply finds the weak point critics name.
- Treating "the critics name it" as "the critics are right". On several of these the defenders have the better case.
- Running before the keys are checked against sources.
- Leaving the key in the chat live. Delete it at DeepSeek once the run is done.
