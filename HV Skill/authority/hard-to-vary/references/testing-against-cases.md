# Testing against cases

Open this before you check a claim against cases you go and find (research), or against a test you set up yourself.

The idea that runs through all of it: **your check is itself an explanation**, of why you now believe the result. Apply the same measure to it. If the check would have come out in your favour whatever the world was like, it is easy to vary, and it holds nothing in place.

Contents: 1 Before looking. 2 While looking. 3 A test you built yourself. 4 Afterwards. 5 Traps.

## 1. Before looking

**Case.** A cook who believes salt improves every dish tastes ten salted dishes and likes them all. Nothing was risked. The check that could have caught the belief is a dish tasted both ways, with the guess written down first.

- **Write down what would count against each part.** "If a case does job J well without part P, then P is loose." "If a case has P and still fails J, P is not enough." A part that no finding could count against is a label.
- **Name the repeats.** A case a part was fitted on cannot confirm that part.
- **For every borrowed part, name its best rival first.** Search only for the idea and you will meet its friends.
- **Pick some cases because they might break you.** For any rule drawn from one case, add a near neighbour where it should stay quiet, and one where a different rule should fire.

## 2. While looking

- **Tag how you know each thing:** *seen* (you watched it happen; say whether on your own cases or someone else's), *claimed* (a source says so), *recalled* (memory), *worked out* (follows from tagged things). Recalled claims are fitted; they carry no verdict alone.
- **What a thing says about itself is a claim.** It becomes *seen* when you look at what it actually does.
- **"Found nowhere" means unknown.** It shows neither "unneeded" nor "impossible". "I did not find X" describes your search.
- **Keep findings that fit no part.** They are the likeliest sign of a missing part or a wrong requirement. Report them; bending a part to absorb one hides it.

## 3. A test you built yourself

A home-made test has three layers: **the thing under test**, **the rig** around it, and **the inputs** as you prepared them. A pass, or a fix, can come from any layer.

- **Fix the cases before the rig exists.** Include one where the right answer is "nothing".
- **Develop on one case, then hold the rig still** and run the others against it first.
- **Keep the first result as it came.**
- **Every later change is a patch.** Say which layer it changed, and **what it gives up** as well as what it fixes. A false alarm on a new case is often an old patch's bill arriving. Before each new case, say how your latest patch is likely to fail on it.
- **After every patch, rerun everything.**
- **A clean sweep on your own cases is a reason to attack.** Add cases you expect to fail, and label them as added later.
- **If each new kind of case needs a new piece of rig,** you have a list, one item per kind, and should say so.
- **Preparing the cases is where fitting creeps in,** even when the cases were fixed beforehand. Where you can, have someone else prepare them.
- **When an outside case finds nothing, ask what kind of thing it is** before patching. If it differs in kind from what you built on, the miss belongs to the job list.

## 4. Afterwards

- **Go down your written expectations and mark each tested or untested.** One you designed around was never tested.
- **Part by part: did any finding move the mark?** "Unmoved" is a fair result. List the parts that never met a hard case.
- **Every change to the claim is a new claim,** recorded with the finding that forced it. The old claim stays as it was.
- **A failed expectation knocks down the bundle:** the claim, your reading of the case, and how you chose cases. Say which you are changing and why that one.
- **Say whose cases they were.** Cases you wrote with the answer in mind can show that a thing *runs*. They cannot show that it *works*.

## 5. Traps

- Treating a search as a hunt for support.
- Confirming a fitted part on the cases it was fitted to.
- Letting *claimed* or *recalled* pass as *seen*.
- Tuning a home-made test until it passes, or stopping at a clean sweep.
- Saying "it works now" without saying which layer moved.
- Logging what a patch fixes and leaving out what it gives up.
