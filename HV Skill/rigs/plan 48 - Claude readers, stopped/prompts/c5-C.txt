The case. Someone offers this explanation:

Why does a person who has just resisted a plate of cookies give up sooner on an unsolvable puzzle? Self-control draws on one shared store. Every act of restraint, whatever its domain, draws the store down, and it refills with rest and with glucose. So restraint at one task leaves less for the next, whatever the next task is. The store is one thing, not many: resisting food impairs a later persistence task, and holding back emotion impairs a later handgrip task, across domains. A sugar drink between the tasks removes the drop; a sweetened drink with no sugar does not. Remove the first act of restraint and the second task runs at full length. Add a rest and it recovers. The account also says what should not matter: which domain the first task was in.
