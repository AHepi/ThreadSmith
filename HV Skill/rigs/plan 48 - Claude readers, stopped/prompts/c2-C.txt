The case. Someone offers this explanation:

The sky looks the same in every direction to one part in a hundred thousand, and space is flat to within a percent. Neither is a coincidence. In the first fraction of a second a field with energy stored in it drove space to expand by a factor of ten to the twenty-six or more. Regions now on opposite sides of the sky were then in contact, so they had time to reach the same temperature: that is the uniformity. The stretching flattened any initial curvature, the way inflating a balloon flattens its surface: that is the flatness. The same stretching blew tiny quantum jitters in the field up to the size of galaxies, seeding the ripples we see in the background glow, with a spectrum nearly but not exactly the same at every scale, tilted slightly toward the large. That tilt was predicted before it was measured, and it was found. Take the field away and you need the uniformity and the flatness as starting conditions. Put it in and both follow, and the ripples with them.

(2) For each part, one sentence: what holds it in place, or what could take its place just as well. (3) The weakest part, and one change to the situation that would show whether it does any work. (4) Your verdict, one of: RELY ON IT / RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION, and say which / SET IT ASIDE.
