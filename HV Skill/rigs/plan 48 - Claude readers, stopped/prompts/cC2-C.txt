They describe a way of judging an explanation. Use it on the case below.

The case. Someone offers this explanation:

Why do stomach ulcers come back after the acid is reduced, and why do they clear with antibiotics? A bacterium lives in the stomach lining, tolerating the acid by making an alkaline shell around itself. It inflames the lining, the lining weakens, and acid then eats a hole. Reducing the acid lets the hole heal but leaves the bacterium, so the ulcer returns. Killing the bacterium removes the inflammation and the ulcer does not return. Nearly everyone with a duodenal ulcer carries the bacterium; a healthy volunteer who swallowed it developed an inflamed stomach within days; a course of antibiotics cures what acid reducers only suspend. Remove the bacterium and the ulcer stays away. Leave it and the ulcer recurs.

Reply in everyday words, in under 400 words, in this shape. (1) The parts of the explanation that are claimed to do the work. (2) For each part, one sentence: what holds it in place, or what could take its place just as well. (3) The weakest part, and one change to the situation that would show whether it does any work. (4) Your verdict, one of: RELY ON IT / RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION, and say which / SET IT ASIDE.
