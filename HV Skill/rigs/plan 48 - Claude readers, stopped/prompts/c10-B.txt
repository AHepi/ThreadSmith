They describe a way of judging an explanation. Use it on the case below.

The case. Someone offers this explanation:

Why do people with Alzheimer's lose memory, and why does it begin where it does? A protein fragment, amyloid-beta, is produced in the brain and normally cleared. In the disease production outpaces clearing, the fragments stick into plaques, the plaques set off a second protein, tau, to tangle inside nerve cells, and the tangled cells die, first in the memory regions, where plaque forms first. Every inherited form of the disease is a mutation in the production or processing of this fragment, and people with an extra copy of its gene get the disease in middle age. So plaque is upstream. Clear the plaque and the tangling and the cell death should slow. Antibodies that clear plaque now exist, and in trials they slow the decline. Remove the mutation and the inherited disease does not occur. Add the extra copy and it does.

Reply in everyday words, in under 400 words, in this shape. (1) The parts of the explanation that are claimed to do the work. (2) For each part, one sentence: what holds it in place, or what could take its place just as well. (3) The weakest part, and one change to the situation that would show whether it does any work. (4) Your verdict, one of: RELY ON IT / RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION, and say which / SET IT ASIDE.
