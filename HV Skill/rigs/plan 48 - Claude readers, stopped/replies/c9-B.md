**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

A. The price is a summary of everything known.
B. Anyone who knows something not yet in the price trades on it, and the trading moves the price until the edge is gone.
C. So an edge lasts only until you trade on it.
D. Managers who did well one year are no better than chance the next: it was luck.
E. New information moves prices at once, not over days.
F. A cheap index fund beats most active funds after costs.
G. Take away the informed traders and prices drift from what businesses are worth; put them back and the drift closes.

**(2) What holds each in place, or what could replace it**

A. Not a separate part: it is B's result written down as a starting point, so it holds nothing on its own.
B. Held: it is the engine. Trades visibly move prices, and you can poke it (add or remove traders, add a cost).
C. Follows from B; it adds nothing B does not already say.
D. "Luck" is a label unless you say what pattern luck would not make. Swap in "skill that others copy within a year" and the same no-repeat result comes out, so D does not pick this story over that one.
E. Loose: "at once" could be "within hours" or "within a week" and the rest survives. And prices do sometimes move for days after news, so this part is already leaking.
F. Held, but by arithmetic, not by B: active funds together are the market, so after fees most must lose to it even if the market were sloppy. A different engine would give F just as well.
G. This is the real test of B, stated as a promise. Nobody has run it in the plain form.

**(3) The weakest part, and one change that would show whether it works**

A is the weakest: "all available information" can swallow any failure by calling an unexplained gain "payment for risk" or the information "not really available." To see whether B is doing work rather than A absorbing it: pick half of a set of stocks at random and ban betting against them for a year. The story says the banned half sit above what they are worth and fall more slowly on bad news; the free half do not. If both halves behave alike, the trading engine is not what holds prices in place.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: why nobody beats the market for long using information that is widely known, and why cheap index funds win on average after costs (the second held by arithmetic, not by the story). Do not rely on it for "every winner was lucky" or "prices adjust at once."
