**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. A huge, fast stretch of space very early on.
- B. A field with stored energy that drove the stretch.
- C. Opposite sides of the sky once touched, so they evened out: the uniformity.
- D. Stretching irons out curvature, like a balloon: the flatness.
- E. Tiny jitters in the field, blown up, seed the ripples, with a slight tilt toward large scales, predicted and then found.

**(2) What holds each in place, or what could replace it**

- A. Held: take it out and uniformity and flatness go back to being assumptions, and nothing predicts the tilt. One part, three jobs.
- B. Loose in its details: swap this field for one with a different energy slope and the sky is just as even and flat; only the tilt moves. What is held is "some push that stretched space fast, then faded slowly".
- C. Borrowed from ordinary heat physics; held once A is in.
- D. Borrowed from geometry; held once A is in.
- E. The strongest part: it was risked before it was seen, and the rival "things just started that way" says nothing about a tilt. But the tilt is the one number holding the field's shape, and a shape can be picked afterwards to give almost any tilt. "Ten to the twenty-six" is loose too; what is held is "enough".

**(3) The weakest part, and one change**

The field's shape. Keep the stretch and swap in a field with a different slope: uniformity and flatness do not move, so they cannot tell fields apart. Only the ripples can, and so far one number fixes one knob. The change: demand a second reading the same shape must fix, unretuned. The stretch should also have shaken space itself, leaving a swirl pattern in the glow whose strength the field's energy sets. Found at that strength, the field does work. If the shape must be adjusted to fit, it is a knob, not a part.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: that an early fast stretch happened and produced the uniformity, the flatness and the ripples together. Do not yet rely on what the field is or its shape. Note too that "the field starts high on its energy hill" is itself a starting condition: two assumptions traded for one.
