**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. Each pupil has one stable channel: seeing, hearing or doing.
- B. A short questionnaire can find that channel.
- C. The channel is how that pupil's memory encodes best.
- D. Matching the teaching to the channel makes material stick; mismatching makes it slide off (the crossover).
- E. The fixed quarter who fall behind are the mismatched ones, and they catch up when the channel is swapped.

**(2) What holds each in place, or what could stand in for it**

- A. Only the fact that pupils will say they like pictures or talk; a liking for how a lesson feels is not the same as a channel the mind must use, and plain taste or habit would explain the same answers.
- B. The questionnaire is self-report, and the same pupil often gives a different answer a month later, so it may be measuring mood or last week's lesson rather than a fixed trait.
- C. Nothing shown here supports it; the rival is that memory holds meaning, so a diagram helps anyone with a shape and a spoken walk-through helps anyone with a step-by-step rule, whoever they are.
- D. The crossover has been looked for many times and not turned up; the rival is that some content simply suits some formats, and it suits them for everyone.
- E. A quarter falling behind and catching up on a second pass is explained just as well by a second exposure, extra time, and the same idea shown a new way, which helps every pupil, not just the flagged ones.

**(3) The weakest part and one test**

D is the weakest, because everything else is only useful if the crossover is real. The test: give the questionnaire, then ignore it and randomly assign every pupil to picture-taught or talk-taught for the same algebra lesson, and test all of them the same way a week later. If the channel does work, "visual" pupils score higher with pictures and lower with talk, and "auditory" pupils show the mirror image. If everyone scores higher with pictures, or with pictures plus talk, then the format did the work and the channel did none. Also check whether the quarter who fell behind in the ordinary class are actually the ones the questionnaire flagged as mismatched; if they are not, part E falls with D.

**(4) Verdict**

SET IT ASIDE. The one thing worth keeping, that showing an idea in more than one way helps most pupils, needs no channels, no questionnaire and no crossover to explain it.
