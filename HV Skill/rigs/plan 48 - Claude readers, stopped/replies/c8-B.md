**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. Small disorder (a broken window, graffiti, fare-dodging) sends a signal: no one is watching.
- B. Would-be offenders read the signal and act; the people who would stop them stay indoors.
- C. Fixing the small things reverses the signal.
- D. Serious crime fell because the likely offenders were the ones reading the signal.
- E. The fall began in the districts where the policy was applied first.
- F. Take the policy away and crime should rise again.

**(2) What holds each part in place, or what could stand in for it**

- A. Nothing in the story shows anyone reading a signal; "more officers on the street, seen and felt" does the same job with no signal at all.
- B. Swap in "arresting fare-dodgers picks up people carrying weapons or wanted for worse" and every fact fits just as well.
- C. A clean-up crew and a police patrol both "reverse the signal," and the story cannot say which one matters.
- D. This is the thing to be explained, restated as its own cause; it holds nothing.
- E. The only part that could do real work, but a plain "more police, more arrests" story predicts the same districts, and nothing says those districts were otherwise alike.
- F. A real test, but the rival predicts the same, so it cannot choose between them.

Two more things. Had crime risen instead, the same story could have said the signal was not yet reversed enough, so it could cover either outcome. And crime fell by about as much in the same decade in cities that never adopted the policy; the explanation has to say what was different there, and it says nothing.

**(3) The weakest part and one change that would test it**

Weakest: B, the signal-reading chain. Change: in some districts, fix the windows and clear the graffiti with clean-up crews only, no extra officers, no arrests; in others, add officers and fare-dodging arrests but leave the windows broken. The signal story says crime falls in the first set. If it falls only in the second, the signal was never doing the work; the policing was.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: "did policing small disorder lower serious crime in the districts where it was applied, compared with districts that got it later?", and only if those districts were alike in other ways. Set aside the signal chain and the claim to explain the citywide fall by half.
