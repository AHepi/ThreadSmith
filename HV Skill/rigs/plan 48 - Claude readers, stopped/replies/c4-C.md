**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

A. The equation never picks, and it is followed all the way up to the apparatus and to you.
B. So you and the apparatus split into branches, and each branch keeps going.
C. Branches stop touching once they tangle with the surroundings, too fast to catch.
D. You see one result because the you who sees it is in one branch.
E. Nothing is added: no collapse, no observer, no special role for measurement.
F. It matches every interference experiment, including interference coming back when small branches are brought together again.
G. Take branching away and you must add a collapse with a threshold nobody has measured.

**(2) What holds each part, or what could replace it**

A. Held, but borrowed: it rests on the equation's record, which every rival shares.
B. Held by F for small things only; at your size, "the other branch was erased" fits every change anyone has run just as well.
C. Held: without it the branches would keep mixing and no result would look settled. But collapse stories use the same tangling, so it does not pick a side.
D. This is the part that answers "why one," yet no change on the list tells "you are in one branch" from "only one branch is left." Until something does, it is a label.
E. Loose: something is added, a rule saying how the weight of a branch turns into how often you see each result. The explanation says nothing about it.
F. Held, and it rules one thing out: collapse at small sizes. It says nothing about collapse at large sizes.
G. Fair, but the same unmeasured threshold means the two stories cannot yet be told apart at your own size.

**(3) The weakest part, and a change that would test it**

D. Take a system that has been measured by something large, shield it from its surroundings, and bring the branches back together. If interference returns at every size you can shield, the other branch was still there and D does work. If it stops returning at some size even when the shielding is good, something did pick, and D does none.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: why no collapse is needed for anything up to the sizes where interference has been made to return, and why a result looks settled once the surroundings are involved. Do not rely on it for "why this result, this often," which needs a part it does not give, nor for whether the other branch exists at your own size, since nothing yet tells that from collapse.
