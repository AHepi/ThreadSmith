**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. Mood is carried by serotonin signalling.
- B. In depression that signal is too weak (too little released, or cleared too fast).
- C. Blocking the clearing leaves more serotonin, the signal strengthens, mood lifts.
- D. It takes weeks because the receptors adjust.
- E. Taking away serotonin's building block lowers mood in recovered people.
- F. Remove the block and the drug does nothing; block another transmitter and the effect is smaller.
- G. Therefore depression is a serotonin deficit and the drug corrects it.

**(2) What holds each in place, or what could replace it**

- A. Held only loosely: swap "serotonin" for "serotonin among several transmitters" and nothing in the story breaks, so the held part is broader than named.
- B. Not held by anything here: it is worked out backwards from the drug helping, the way one might call a headache an aspirin shortage; nothing in the story measures the signal before the drug.
- C. Held for the drug's first step: block the clearing and serotonin rises; remove the block and it does not. That is a real, tried poke.
- D. A patch: the drug raises serotonin in hours but mood moves in weeks, and "receptors adjust" was added only to absorb that gap. It does no other job and could be swapped for "the brain rewires" or "new cells grow" just as well.
- E. Held, but for a narrower claim: it shows the drug's benefit depends on serotonin. It says nothing about why the person got depressed, since the same test barely moves never-depressed people.
- F. The first half is held (it is the same poke as C). The second half is loose: drugs that leave serotonin alone and act on noradrenaline help about as much, so "smaller" could be "the same" without cost.

**(3) The weakest part, and one change that would test it**

G, the jump from "the drug raises serotonin and helps" to "depression is too little serotonin." Change the situation: give a drug that speeds up the clearing, so there is less serotonin in the gap. The deficit story says mood must fall, or at least not lift. If such a drug lifts mood as well as the blockers do, the deficit part was doing no work and the story was only ever about the drug, not the illness. Flip check: had the blockers lowered mood, "too much serotonin" would have been told just as smoothly.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: how these particular drugs act in the first step, and that their benefit depends on serotonin. Do not rely on it for why people become depressed.
