**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. One shared store of self-control, not several.
- B. Every act of restraint, in any domain, draws it down.
- C. Rest refills it, and so does sugar, but not sweetness alone.
- D. What should not matter: the domain of the first task.

**(2) What holds each part, or what could take its place**

- A. Its only job is the carry-over across domains, and a rival does it as well: after any hard first task, people feel they have done their share and spend less effort on the next; no store is needed, so A is loose.
- B. The remove-the-first-task and add-a-rest results hold B, but someone who feels they have earned a break gives those same two results, so B is held only if something tells a used-up store from a chosen let-up.
- C. The sugar-versus-sweetener contrast is the one job a "feeling" story cannot easily do, so C is the best-held part, but it rests on one kind of check and could be replaced by "sugar in the mouth signals reward, so effort seems worth spending".
- D. This is the right "would not matter" half of a poke, but the rival predicts it too, so it rules nothing out.

**(3) The weakest part, and one change that would show whether it works**

The weakest part is A, the store that gets used up: "less self-control because self-control was spent" is the answer sitting in the starting points under a new name. Change to try: after the cookies, tell half the people they will be paid for every minute they stay on the puzzle. An empty store cannot be refilled by money, so the drop should stay. If persistence returns with pay, the "store" is a choice about spending effort, not a fuel tank. A cheap second change: rinse sugar water and spit; if persistence returns, nothing reached any store.

**(4) Verdict: RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION**

Rely on it for what comes out: restraint on one task, then shorter persistence on the next, lifted by sugar and by rest. Do not yet rely on it for how it works, a single store drained and refilled. Every result here, except perhaps the sweetener contrast, is matched by the "earned a break" rival, and nothing on the change list yet tells them apart.
