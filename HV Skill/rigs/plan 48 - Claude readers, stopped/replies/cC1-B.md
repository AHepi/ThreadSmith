**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. The two coasts were once one landmass and have moved apart.
- B. The continents ride on plates that move a few centimetres a year.
- C. The Atlantic opened along a ridge where new crust wells up.
- D. The moving is driven by slow churning in the mantle.
- E. New crust records the earth's magnetic field, which reverses now and then.

**(2) What holds each part in place, or what could take its place**

- A. Held by the two given jobs together: "chance fit" leaves the fossils unexplained, and "a land bridge that later sank" leaves the fit unexplained, so only "once joined" does both.
- B. Held, but only roughly: the number "a few centimetres" is loose (two or ten would do as well for the fit and fossils); what is held is "slow, steady, still going", which the stripe widths against the dates of reversals pin down.
- C. Held by the added job, the stripes: rifting without new crust, or continents ploughing through the seabed, would not give a mirrored pattern that gets older away from the ridge. This is the part that the prediction actually tested.
- D. Loose. Swap "churning in the mantle" for "the cold edge of the plate sinking and pulling the rest along", or for any engine at all, and the fit, the fossils and the stripes come out exactly the same. Nothing in this case reaches the engine.
- E. Borrowed. It rests on rock magnetism, which is checked separately in lava flows on land. Held if that holds, and it does.

**(3) The weakest part, and a change that would show whether it works**

D, the mantle churning. It is a label here: the explanation would say everything it says without it. To make it do work, compare plates that have a long sinking edge with plates that have none. If the churning drives them, speed should follow the heat rising underneath and not care about sinking edges. If speed tracks the sinking edges instead, the churning is not the engine, and the rest of the account is untouched either way.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: that the two coasts were joined and have spread apart from the ridge as new crust forms. That much is held by the fit, the fossils and the stripes, and the stripes ruled out the "ploughing" rival. Do not rely on it for what drives the plates; nothing here holds that part in place.
