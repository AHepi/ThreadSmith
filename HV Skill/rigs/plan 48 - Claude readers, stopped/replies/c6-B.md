**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. Each pupil has a preferred channel: seeing, hearing or doing.
- B. The channel is stable over time.
- C. A short questionnaire finds it.
- D. The channel matches how that pupil's memory stores things best.
- E. Match the teaching to the channel and it sticks; mismatch and it slides off (the crossover).
- F. That is why a fixed quarter fall behind in a one-way class and catch up when the content is offered another way.

**(2) What holds each in place, or what could replace it**

- A. Loose: swap "channel" for "prefers examples before rules" or "already knows the words used" and the classroom story runs just as well; and "doing" is a bin that can take any pupil who fits neither of the other two, with no reading that would show it is swallowing cases.
- B. Loose: swap "stable" for "depends on the day" and nothing in the story about that class changes; only the questionnaire needs it.
- C. A reading, not the thing: reword the questionnaire and the label moves while the pupil's learning does not; nothing here shows the label tracks anything.
- D. The answer hidden in the starting points: "it sticks because that is how it sticks" is the conclusion under another name.
- E. Held if the crossover is real; it is the one part that sticks its neck out, and nothing in the case shows it was seen rather than expected.
- F. Two routes: a rival with no channels does both jobs. Any second pass over the same content, or one better-made set of materials, lifts the stragglers, whoever they are. Flip it: had a different quarter fallen behind, the same story would have called them the other type.

**(3) The weakest part, and one change that would show whether it works**

The match itself (E). Sort a class by the questionnaire, then split each group in half: one half taught with pictures, the other by talk, same content, same time, tested a week later. The explanation says visual pupils taught by pictures beat visual pupils taught by talk, *and* auditory pupils the reverse. If pictures beat talk for everyone, or for no one, the pupil-channel pairing does nothing, and the catch-up was a second exposure. The would-not half: rewording the questionnaire should not change who gains. If it does, C was measuring the asking, not the pupil.

**(4) Verdict**

SET IT ASIDE. What survives, that re-teaching missed content another way helps the pupils who missed it, is done just as well by the rival without channels, questionnaires or memory types. Until the crossover is shown, those parts are decoration.
