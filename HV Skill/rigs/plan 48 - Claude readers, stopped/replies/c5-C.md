**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. There is one store that all self-control draws on.
- B. Every act of restraint, in any domain, draws it down.
- C. It refills with rest and with glucose.
- D. So the second task gets less, whichever domain it is in.
- E. The domain of the first task should not matter.

**(2) What holds each part, or what could replace it**

- A. "One store" is held only by the cross-domain transfer (food then puzzle, emotion then grip); but "the first task leaves people fed up and less willing" transfers across domains just as well, so the transfer does not pick out a store over a mood.
- B. "Draws down" is the outcome under another name: we know the store went down because the second task got shorter, and nothing else shows the level; swap it for "the first task sours people on effort" and every result reads the same.
- C. Rest is held by nothing, since a break also lifts mood and willingness; glucose is the one detail that separates the store from its rival, because a mood account has no reason to care whether the sweet drink has sugar in it.
- D. This is A and B restated, so it stands or falls with them.
- E. This is a real "should not matter" half, and it is the strongest part: it is the kind of claim that could be caught out.

**(3) The weakest part, and one change that would test it**

Part B, "draws down", is the loose one. The store is only ever seen through the thing it is meant to explain, so had the cookie-resister persisted longer, the account could have said "resisting was not hard enough" and survived. That is a catch-all with no gauge.

The change: after the cookies, offer half the people a small payment for each extra minute on the puzzle, with no rest and no sugar. A drained store cannot be paid back, so the account says the paid group still quits early. A willingness account says they run at full length. The two come apart here and nowhere in the cases listed. A second, cheaper poke: sugar water rinsed and spat out, never swallowed. The store says no effect; if it restores the puzzle, the sugar detail is doing its work through something other than refilling.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: what we can tell from what we see. A demanding first task shortens the second, across domains, and a break or a sugar drink undoes some of that. Do not rely on it for what produces the drop; "one store that drains" is not yet held apart from "less willing", and the payment test would settle which.
