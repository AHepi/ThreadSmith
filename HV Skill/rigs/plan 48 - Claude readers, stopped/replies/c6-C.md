**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. Each pupil has a stable preferred channel (seeing, hearing, doing).
- B. A short questionnaire finds that channel.
- C. The channel is how that pupil's memory encodes best.
- D. Matched teaching sticks; mismatched teaching slides off.
- E. The fixed quarter who fall behind, and catch up when re-taught another way.
- F. The crossover: visual pupils do better with pictures, auditory pupils with talk.

**(2) What holds each in place, or what could replace it**

- A. Nothing in the case pins a stable trait: "some pupils need a second pass at anything, in any form" covers the same facts just as well.
- B. The questionnaire reads what pupils say they like; if changing only the wording changes who counts as "visual", it measures the reading, not the thing.
- C. "Encodes best" is the conclusion under another name; no change is offered that would tell it from "diagrams are simply a clearer way to show algebra to everyone".
- D. This restates F; it is not a separate part.
- E. Any class taught one way has a tail, and any second exposure helps the tail. The quarter is only evidence for channels if it is a *different* quarter under pictures than under talk, and the case does not say it is.
- F. This is the one part that sticks its neck out. It is held only if the crossover has actually been seen; the catch-up story alone does not produce it.

**(3) The weakest part, and one change that would show whether it works**

Weakest: B and C together, the idea that a questionnaire label tracks something in memory. The change: sort a class by the questionnaire, then teach half of each group through their matching channel and half through the other, same content, same time, same test for everyone. The explanation says the matched halves win and the mismatched halves lose, in both groups. The rival says whichever lesson is better designed wins for everyone, label or no label. The two cannot both be right.

**(4) Verdict: SET IT ASIDE**

What the case actually shows, that a second presentation in a different form helps pupils who missed it first, is real but needs no channels, no questionnaire and no encoding to explain it. Those parts are the ones that could be swapped or removed with no loss. If the crossover in (3) has been seen, bring it back; until then the explanation is carrying passengers.
