**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

A. Mood is carried by serotonin signalling.
B. In depression that signal is too weak: a shortage.
C. The drug blocks clearing, more serotonin stays in the gap, mood lifts.
D. The weeks of delay are because receptors adjust.
E. Taking away serotonin's building block lowers mood in recovered people.
F. Without the drug nothing happens; blocking another transmitter does less.

**(2) What holds each in place, or what could take its place**

A. Held only by E, and only for people who have been depressed; if the same food trick leaves never-depressed people's mood untouched, "serotonin level is mood" is loose and something narrower is doing the work.
B. Nothing listed holds it: every fact here fits just as well if serotonin is normal and the listening cells are sluggish, or if extra serotonin masks a fault sitting elsewhere. Aspirin lowers a fever; fever is not an aspirin shortage.
C. Held: take the drug away and the effect goes; take the building block away while on the drug and the effect should go too. Both can be tried.
D. A patch. Added only to cover the delay, it does no other job, and would cover a delay of days or months just as smoothly.
E. This is the evidence that holds C, not a working part of its own.
F. "Nothing happens without the drug" is too strong: a dummy pill lifts mood in many people and the story says nothing about why. "Less with another transmitter" is asserted; even if true, it shows serotonin is one route, not that the illness is a shortage.

**(3) The weakest part, and one change that would show whether it works**

B, the shortage. The explanation runs backwards, from "more serotonin helps" to "too little serotonin was the cause". Change: measure serotonin signalling in people who are depressed and have never taken the drug, against people who are not. The story says it must be lower. If it is not, B does no work, and the drug is a lever on mood rather than a repair of a fault. Flip it the other way: had mood lifted without any change in serotonin, the same story would have said "the receptors adjusted", so the story as written can swallow either result.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: "how does this drug act?" Through serotonin, held by the take-it-away tests. Not "why do people become depressed?" The shortage is reached by running the cure backwards, and nothing offered holds it in place.
