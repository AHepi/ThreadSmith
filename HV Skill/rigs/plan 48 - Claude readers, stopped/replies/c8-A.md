**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. Disorder is a signal: an unrepaired window tells would-be offenders that nobody is watching, and tells ordinary people to stay home.
- B. Fixing small things and arresting fare-dodgers reverses the signal.
- C. Serious crime fell because the people reading the signal stopped acting on it.
- D. The fall began in the districts where the policy started, so the policy caused it.
- E. Take the policy away and crime should rise again.

**(2) What holds each part in place, or what could stand in for it**

- A. Held up mostly by how sensible it sounds; but offenders may simply respond to seeing more police on the street, no "signal" from windows needed.
- B. Held up by the fact the city did it; but arresting fare-dodgers also directly catches people carrying weapons or wanted for other things, which removes offenders without any signal at all.
- C. Held up by timing; but crime fell by similar amounts in cities that did nothing like this, so the end of the crack trade, a strong economy, more people in prison, more police hired, and an ageing population can each take its place.
- D. Held up by the order of districts; but the policy went first to the worst districts, and the worst districts had the most room to fall anyway.
- E. Held up by nothing yet; nobody has cleanly removed the policy and watched.

**(3) The weakest part, and one test**

The weakest part is A, the signal itself. Everything downstream works just as well under the plainer story "more police, more arrests, more offenders off the street," which needs no reading of windows by anyone. To find out whether the signal does any work: in some districts repair the windows, paint over the graffiti and clean the stations, but keep police numbers and arrest rates exactly as they were. In other districts do the arrests but leave the mess. If crime falls where only the cleaning happened, the signal is real. If it falls only where the arrests happened, the arrests are doing the work and the signal story is decoration.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: that visible order together with heavy policing of small offences went along with a fall in serious crime in that city. Do not rely on it for the claim that the signal chain is why crime halved, or for the prediction that stopping the policy would bring crime back.
