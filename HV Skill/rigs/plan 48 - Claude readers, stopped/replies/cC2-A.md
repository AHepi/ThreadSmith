**(1) The parts doing the work**

- A. A germ lives in the stomach lining and survives the acid by making an alkaline shell.
- B. The germ inflames the lining and weakens it.
- C. Acid then eats through the weakened spot; that is the ulcer.
- D. Acid reducers heal the hole but leave the germ, so the ulcer comes back.
- E. Antibiotics kill the germ, so the ulcer stays away.

**(2) What holds each part in place**

- A. Held in place: the germ is found in nearly everyone with a duodenal ulcer, and the shell explains how anything could live there at all.
- B. Held in place: the volunteer who swallowed it got an inflamed stomach within days, which links germ to inflammation directly.
- C. Weakly held: the acid-eats-the-weak-spot step is a sensible story, but nothing offered here tests it; the germ could damage the lining directly, with acid only along for the ride.
- D. Held in place: acid reducers stop the ulcer while taken and it returns after, which fits "hole healed, cause untouched." A rival that fits just as well: acid reducers simply wear off and the underlying cause, whatever it is, was never addressed.
- E. Held in place: antibiotics cure where acid reducers only suspend. But antibiotics do other things too (they can calm inflammation, kill other germs), so "this germ was the cause" is one reading among a few.

**(3) The weakest part**

Part E carries the conclusion and is the most open. "Antibiotics cure" does not by itself say the cure came from killing this particular germ. The change to make: give antibiotics to ulcer patients, then check afterward who still carries the germ. If the ulcer stays away only in those where the germ is gone, and comes back in those where the antibiotics failed to clear it, the germ is doing the work. If the ulcer stays away regardless of whether the germ is gone, something else about the antibiotics is helping, and E collapses.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION. Rely on it for: this germ is present in ulcer patients, it causes inflammation, and clearing it prevents recurrence in a way acid reducers do not. Do not yet rely on the precise mechanism (the alkaline shell, the acid-eats-the-weak-spot step), which are plausible fill-ins the evidence here does not test.
