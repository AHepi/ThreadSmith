**(1) The parts claimed to do the work**

- A. The continents sit on plates that move a few centimetres a year.
- B. Slow churning in the mantle underneath is what pushes them.
- C. New crust wells up along the Atlantic ridge, so the two sides move apart.
- D. The matching coasts and shared fossils are leftovers of one landmass that split.
- E. The seafloor should carry mirrored magnetic stripes, older with distance from the ridge, and it does.

**(2) What holds each part in place, or what could stand in for it**

- A. Held in place: satellites now measure the drift directly, at about the rate claimed, so nothing else can take its place.
- B. Loosest part: the plates could just as well be dragged by their own cold edges sinking at trenches, and every fact in the account would look the same.
- C. Held in place: rock dredged from the seafloor gets steadily older as you move away from the ridge, which chance or a fixed floor cannot produce.
- D. Coast fit alone could be luck or sea-level trimming, but matching fossils plus matching rock belts on both sides leave luck a poor substitute.
- E. Held in place: the stripes were predicted before they were looked for, and no rival story makes them mirror-image and age-ordered around the ridge.

**(3) The weakest part, and a test**

The weakest part is B, the mantle churning as the engine. The fit, the fossils and the stripes all show that spreading happens; none of them shows what drives it. Pushing from below and pulling from a sinking edge give the same coasts, fossils and stripes.

One change that would tell: compare plates that have a long sinking edge with plates that have none. If the ones being pulled at a trench move clearly faster, the sinking edge is doing most of the work and the churning is at best a helper. If speed has nothing to do with sinking edges, the push from below is earning its place.

**(4) Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION. Rely on it for "the two coasts were once joined and have moved apart along a spreading ridge," which the fit, fossils, dated seafloor and stripes all support. Do not rely on it for "mantle churning is what moves the plates"; the evidence given never tests that part.
