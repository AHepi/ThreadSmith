**1. The parts claimed to do the work**

A. One kind of string, many vibrations: each particle is a note.
B. The graviton is one of the notes, so gravity comes free rather than bolted on.
C. Consistency forces extra curled-up dimensions, and the shape of the curling fixes the particle table.
D. No free constants: every measured number can be read off the geometry.
E. Nothing else gives gravity and the other forces together, so that is the reason to believe it.

**2. What holds each in place, or what could take its place**

A. Held by the fact that the math of a vibrating string closes consistently, but "one object, many states" is also what ordinary quantum field theory already gives, so this alone singles out nothing.
B. Genuinely held: a closed string always has a massless spin-2 note that must act like gravity, though that shows strings contain gravity, not that our particles are the other notes.
C. Ten dimensions are indeed forced, but the shape is not: there are astronomically many allowed curlings, so "geometry sets the table" really means "some geometry could give this table."
D. The equations carry no adjustable number, yet the measured values depend on which curling and which field settings you pick, which is the free constants moved to a different room.
E. This is a claim about the ideas people have had so far, not about the world; a rival that lacks unification could still be right if unification is not required.

**3. The weakest part**

C together with D: the claim that geometry fixes the table. The change: ask whether there is any particle table the framework could not produce. Take the curling that yields our particles and compute one number we have not yet measured, such as a new particle's mass or a forced relation between two couplings, then go measure it. If any table can be matched and nothing new falls out, the geometry is doing no explanatory work; if a forced number lands, it is.

**4. Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: "Can one consistent quantum framework contain gravity alongside the other forces?" Yes. Not for "why exactly these particles, with exactly these numbers."
