**1. The parts claimed to do the work**

a. The two coasts were once one landmass and have moved apart.
b. Continents sit on plates of crust that move a few centimetres a year.
c. New crust is made at a ridge in the middle of the Atlantic.
d. The moving is driven by slow churning in the mantle.
e. The field reverses now and then, so new crust should carry mirrored stripes that get older away from the ridge (the risky prediction).

**2. What holds each in place, or what could take its place**

a. Held: take it out and the fit is chance and the fossils need a vanished land bridge; a bridge does the fossil job but not the fit, chance the fit but not the fossils.
b. Held, but coarsely: swap "plates" for "continents ploughing through the sea floor" and the fit and fossils survive, but no ridge makes crust and the stripes go unexplained; the speed is pinned only to a range by the fossil ages and the ocean's width.
c. Held by the stripes: crust made all over the sea floor at once would give no mirror and no ageing with distance.
d. Loose: swap the mantle's churning for a sinking plate edge dragging the rest, or pushing at the ridge, and every job here comes out the same.
e. Borrowed: rests on the separate finding that the field reverses, dated from lava on land; it makes the prediction risky without being part of the drift story.

**3. The weakest part**

The mantle churning. Nothing in the case would look different if something else pushed the plates. One change that would show whether it does any work: compare plates with a sinking edge against plates with none. If churning drives them, speed should follow the heat below, not the edge; if plates with a sinking edge move much faster, the sinking does at least part of the work and "churning" is a label for "something moves them".

**4. Verdict**

RELY ON IT FOR A NARROWER QUESTION: were the coasts joined, and did the ocean open from the ridge? Yes. Parts a, b, c and e are held by the fit, fossils and stripes together, and the account said what would break it before looking. Do not lean on it for "what drives the plates": part d is loose here and needs its own test.
