# L66 Reader brief - the read-back test

You are a reader. You will be given the printed reports of a checker on some passages of prose, and nothing else: not the passages, not the ledgers the checker read, not the rules. The question is whether the reports, on their own, tell you what was found.

For each report, write:
1. **What the checker found**, in one plain sentence per finding, in your own words. If a report says NO FAULT FOUND, say so, and say what it did check, if the report tells you.
2. **Which sentence of the passage each finding points at.** The reports name lines; lines name sentences. Give the sentence number, or say that the report does not let you tell.
3. **What the writer is committed to that they did not say**, where the report lists consequences ("never said; follows from lines N, M"): one plain sentence each, and the sentences of the passage it rests on, or that you cannot tell.
4. **What you could not make out**, and what one sentence added to the report would have settled it.

Rules: read the report only; guess at nothing about the passage; where the report is unclear, say so rather than fill it in; do not judge whether the writer was right. Name yourself at the top. Close with: READING COMPLETE.
