Your Mondays paragraph: two steps hold, then the chain never reconnects

The checker followed a seven-step argument, found where it first lets go, and found that its one supposition undoes itself. It could not see the thing that makes the paragraph funny, which is words quietly changing meaning. It saw that only through the splits I was forced to make.

The chain

THE CHAIN, step by step:
  line 5: Monday lacks authority SINCE it is a beginning  ->  holds
  line 9: no duty to obey SINCE Monday lacks authority  ->  holds
  line 13: Monday is a suggestion SINCE we have no duty to obey it  ->  NO CONNECTION
  line 16: it ceases to exist if ignored SINCE it is optional  ->  NO CONNECTION
  line 21: the week is one day SINCE Tuesday becomes Monday  ->  NO CONNECTION
  line 23: time is unemployed SINCE the week is one day (and one day cannot be a week)  ->  NO CONNECTION
  line 27: we save civilization SINCE leisure is the basis of culture  ->  NO CONNECTION
THE FINAL CONCLUSION, the one argued for last: saves_civilization(sleeping_in).
2 of 7 steps hold. The first step that fails is line 13. The final conclusion DOES NOT STAND on this chain.
  • Steps one and two really do follow. Beginnings are arbitrary, Monday is a beginning, the arbitrary lacks authority: so Monday lacks authority, and we have no duty to obey it. Granted its starting points, the argument is sound this far. The joke needs that.
  • It lets go at “Monday is actually a suggestion”. The line before it says an unheeded command is a suggestion. Nobody said the command goes unheeded, only that we need not heed it. And the thing that would be a suggestion is Monday’s command, not Monday.
  • After that, no step connects. Optional does not lead to ceasing to exist. Tuesday becoming Monday does not lead to a one-day week. A one-day week does not lead to unemployed time. And “civilization” and “save” appear in no earlier line at all.

The supposition undoes itself

CONTRADICTION about exists(monday).
These lines cannot all hold (each one was taken out in turn; the contradiction went away every time):
  - line 17 [said, sentence 1]: [SUPPOSED] Monday no longer exists
  - line 18 [said, sentence 1]: Monday being gone MAKES Tuesday become Monday
  - line 19 [usual case, sentence 1]: if something becomes Monday, then there is a Monday
NOTE: this finding leans on lines you did not write: 19. If they are wrong, ignore it.

Suppose Monday is gone. The paragraph says that makes Tuesday become Monday. So there is a Monday. The checker took each of the three lines out in turn and the contradiction went away every time, so all three are needed. One of them is a usual-case line of mine: if something becomes Monday, there is a Monday. If you reject that, ignore the finding.

What it could not see

To write the ledger at all I had to split two words, because rule 2 allows one name per thing:

  • “command”: having the right to be obeyed (“cannot command our obedience”), and issuing an order (“commands us to wake up”).
  • “Monday”: the day, and the order it gives.

Had I not split “command”, the ledger would say Monday cannot command and Monday commands, a flat contradiction at step two. So the choice of whether the argument breaks at step two or step three was mine, not the checker’s. That is the pull between properties 2 and 10 in its sharpest form yet, and it is now a rule: every split goes in the report by name.

Also lost: must, obligated, can, optional. The language has no way to say them, so they went in as plain descriptions. The paragraph’s own headline, that we must abolish Mondays, is an ought, and nothing in the chain ever arrives at one. The checker cannot report that, because it cannot write it.

How the rig itself did

  • Unchanged, every step was judged correctly, and the contradiction was found. All eight predictions about the steps held.
  • The last patch failed exactly as predicted. It had been fitted to the two-step Markus paragraph. On seven steps it named all seven conclusions as “the point”, and it judged each step alone.
  • My fix for that then broke the Markus report, telling me none of its steps held. I found it only because every earlier paragraph is rerun after every patch. The corrected patch now treats two reasons for one conclusion as two routes to one job: the conclusion stands if either holds.
  • One piece of the patch has never fired: “connects, but only by leaning on a conclusion that already failed”. In your paragraph nothing reconnects after the break, so it had no chance to.

What this means for the language

  • Chains work, with the final conclusion taken as the one argued for last.
  • Must, may, can, ought have moved from “a known gap” to “the gap this kind of argument turns on”.
  • Word-splitting needs a second pair of eyes. It is the strongest case so far for a translator who is not me.
  • SUPPOSED lines are only labelled. The checker treats a supposed line as if it held.

Traps

  • Reading “2 of 7 steps hold” as a score. One broken step is enough; the count is only a map.
  • Thinking the checker found the equivocation. I did, while translating. It shows up only as a split.
  • Treating a supposed line as claimed.
  • Trusting the contradiction without reading its NOTE. It leans on a line I wrote.
