## The source check of the two modern passages (plan step 4)
Done at the level of titles and abstracts, through PubMed, on 22 September; the full texts were not read. According to PubMed, the five works SOURCES.md names that PubMed indexes are as described: Wilson and Wilson 2007, "Rethinking the theoretical foundation of sociobiology", which says group selection was rejected in the 1960s and "revived based on a more careful formulation" and offers multilevel selection as the foundation ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/522809)); Marshall 2011, "Group selection and kin selection: formally equivalent approaches", whose abstract says "the mathematical equivalence of these two approaches has long been known" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2011.04.008)); West, Griffin and Gardner 2007, "Social semantics", on the confused vocabulary of kin selection, group selection and altruism ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2006.01258.x)); Nowak, Tarnita and Wilson 2010, "The evolution of eusociality", arguing that "standard natural selection theory in the context of precise models of population structure" is simpler and superior to inclusive fitness ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09205)); Abbot and others 2011, the reply of 137 authors that Nowak's arguments rest on "a misunderstanding of evolutionary theory" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09831)). What this establishes: the against passage's central claim (sentence 6: any group accounting can be rewritten in gene terms with the same answer, so the group adds no cause) is the equivalence Marshall states; the for passage's central claim (sentence 8: the group structure is a cause, not a way of speaking; sentence 9: kin accounting is arithmetic done afterwards) is the position Wilson and Wilson and Nowak's group argue from, put in a supporter's voice. Both passages are fair statements of their side at this level. Not checked: any sentence against the texts of Williams, Hamilton, Dawkins or Wilson and Sober; the migration claim of the against passage's sentence 7 (the corpus maker's memory of Williams and the haystack models). The dispute conclusion this test can keep is therefore only this: the checker finds no contradiction inside either side, the two sides share two stated facts, and the difference between them is a MAKES against a SHOWS on one sentence, which 38 has no rule to weigh. That is what the plan expected and what the run showed.

## What this test shows
1. The checker cannot see most crude plants in lyrical argument, and the translator can. Four of six plants were flagged by the translator as things it would not repair; one reached the checker as a failed what-if; one (P15) became a general line that lacked its instance. The blind test as designed measures the translator more than the checker.
2. Reported arguments are the language's blind spot, again: the for and against passages' chains were tested only where the worker wrote SINCE and BECAUSE in the actual ledger, and the against passage's chain does not connect on the ledger (two NO CONNECTION), which a reader of the passage would call a fair result about an argument whose bridges are probabilistic and binned.
3. The read-back fails exactly where the report omits the sentence number and succeeds exactly where it prints it. One line in the driver.
4. The what-if machinery trusts unestablished BECAUSE claims and does not say so (finding D).
5. Every passage is out of scope under L64's proposed bin threshold, and the gauge counts the wrong thing. The threshold and the gauge are the owner's to set.
