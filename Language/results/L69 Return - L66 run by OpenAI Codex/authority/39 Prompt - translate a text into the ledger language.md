# Translate a text into the ledger language

Two attachments come with this message: the language definition ("The ledger language") and the text to translate. You are the translator. Your whole job is to write down what the text commits its writer to, as ledger lines. A separate checker does all the judging later.

## What a good ledger is
A ledger is complete when another reader, given only your ledger and your bin, could rebuild what the writer committed to, and could see which parts were the writer's and which were yours.

## Work in this order
1. **Number the sentences.** For verse, number each sentence, or each line where a line stands alone.
2. **Name the things.** Give each thing one plain lower-case name and use that name throughout. When the text uses one word in two senses, give each sense its own name and record the split.
3. **Write the lines, sentence by sentence.** Every line gets all five parts from the language definition: number, standing, content, source mark, sentence.
4. **Fill the bin.** Everything the language has no form for goes here, each with its reason.
5. **Run the final checks** at the end of this prompt, then print your output and stop.

## How to write the lines (the translator's rules, as things to do)
- **Standing.** Mark what the sentence asserts as CLAIMED, what it takes for granted as GIVEN, what it only supposes as SUPPOSED in a named case, and what a story, report, note, letter, speech or dream says as TOLD in a named world of its own.
- **Source mark.** Mark what the writer wrote as "said". Mark every line you add as "filled in" (your reading, or a missing step) or "usual case" (something everyone assumes). Give each line one mark; where part was said and part is yours, split it into two lines, or mark the whole line "filled in" and say in the line which part you added.
- **Things.** Give every thing in a happening a thing line. Where the text is silent on how a thing answers a press, write "not stated".
- **Happenings.** Write who did what to what, and which way, using the text's own verb. Keep the push, the movement and the link between them; send words of degree such as "so hard" to the bin.
- **Links.** When one happening depends on an earlier one, name the earlier happening and which of its results is needed.
- **General lines.** Mark each as ALWAYS or USUALLY, and as MAKES or SHOWS. When the text leaves that open, choose, and mark your choice "filled in".
- **Tendencies.** Write a tendency line when the text has its own words for it ("she was already heading downhill").
- **BECAUSE and SINCE.** Use BECAUSE where the text says one thing produced another, and SINCE where it gives a reason to expect or believe. Read the meaning; words like "so" are a clue. When a sentence claims both, write both lines.
- **Pointing.** Lines about other lines point at them by number.
- **Exceptions.** Attach each exception to a USUALLY line, and say whether the line did not apply or the opposite happened.
- **Stages.** When a thing's state changes in the text, put the stage in the fact.
- **NOT.** Put NOT in square brackets around exactly what is denied.
- **Figures of speech, irony, tone.** Write as lines the plain facts the sentence still states. Send the image itself to the bin with a one-line note saying what the image is. Where you are unsure whether words are literal, write both readings in the bin and mark any line you wrote from them "filled in".
- **Quoted commands and questions.** Send them to the bin, recorded as mentioned.
- **Who believes, wants or concludes what.** Send the attribution to the bin, and write the content as TOLD in that person's world.

## Print your output in exactly this form
**1. The ledger**, as a table with these columns: Number | Standing | Content | Source mark | Sentence.

**2. The bin**, as a list. Each entry: the words from the text, and the reason they are here.

**3. Words I split**, each with both senses.

**4. Readings I chose**, each as: the sentence, the readings open to me, the one I took. Include your readings of any slips of spelling.

**5. The count:** lines said, lines filled in, lines usual case, sentences that lost something to the bin.

Close with: TRANSLATION COMPLETE. Then wait.

## The parking place
Judgements about whether the writer's reasoning is good, and ideas for procedures, graphs, algorithms or scoring, go in a PARKED list at the very end, one line each. Then return to the line in hand.

## Final checks before you print
- Every line has all five parts.
- Every thing has one name, used throughout.
- Every line you added carries "filled in" or "usual case".
- Every sentence of the text appears in the ledger, in the bin, or in both.
- Every image, quoted command and attributed belief is accounted for in the bin.
- The output has all five sections and the closing line.

## Traps, and what to do in each
- **When a sentence could be literal or figurative:** write both readings under "Readings I chose" and mark what you wrote from it "filled in".
- **When you feel a missing step is obvious:** write it as its own line, marked "filled in".
- **When the text tells a story inside the story:** give the inner story its own named world and mark its lines TOLD.
- **When a poem's line states a plain fact inside an image:** write the fact as a line and send the image to the bin.
- **When you want to say the argument is weak:** put that one line in PARKED and carry on translating.
