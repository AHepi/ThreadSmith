1. A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds, adored by little statesmen and philosophers and divines.
2. With consistency a great soul has simply nothing to do.
3. He may as well concern himself with the shadow on the wall.
4. Speak what you think now in hard words, and to-morrow speak what to-morrow thinks in hard words again, though it contradict everything you said to-day.
5. "Ah, so you shall be sure to be misunderstood."
6. Is it so bad, then, to be misunderstood?
7. Pythagoras was misunderstood, and Socrates, and Jesus, and Luther, and Copernicus, and Galileo, and Newton, and every pure and wise spirit that ever took flesh.
8. To be great is not to be misunderstood.
9. I suppose no man can violate his nature.
10. All the sallies of his will are rounded in by the law of his being, as the inequalities of Andes and Himmaleh are insignificant in the curve of the sphere.
