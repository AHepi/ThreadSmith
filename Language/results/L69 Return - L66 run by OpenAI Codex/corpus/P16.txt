1. If the injustice is not part of the necessary friction of the machine of government, let it go, let it go: perchance it will wear smooth, certainly the machine will wear out.
2. If the injustice has a spring, or a pulley, or a rope, or a crank, exclusively for itself, then perhaps you may consider whether the remedy will not be worse than the evil; but if it is of such a nature that it requires you to be the agent of injustice to another, then, I say, break the law.
3. Let your life be a counter friction to stop the machine.
4. What I have to do is to see, at any rate, that I do not lend myself to the wrong which I condemn.
5. As for adopting the ways which the State has provided for remedying the evil, I know not of such ways.
6. They take too much time, and a man's life will be gone.
