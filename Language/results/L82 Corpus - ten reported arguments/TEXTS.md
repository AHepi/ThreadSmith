## Passage 1

The farm foreman kept notes on his evening rounds. He wrote that the shepherd had concluded the flock must be in the top field, since the gate stood open, and the shepherd always left that gate open only once the flock had been driven through it. The foreman added, from what he saw himself, that the lower field was empty of grass. The note ended with the time the rounds had finished.

## Passage 2

The laboratory technician described the morning's routine in her notebook. She listed the samples in the order she had tested them, from the water tank first to the soil trays last. She recorded the temperature of the incubator at nine, at noon, and at four. The notebook also described the new labels the supplier had sent, each printed in blue ink. At the end of the page she noted that the fume cupboard had been cleaned.

## Passage 3

The dockyard clerk read through the harbourmaster's log for the week. In it, the harbourmaster had written that the trawler crossed the bar early because the tide had turned. The log went on to record the names of the crew and the weight of the catch. The clerk also noted, from her own count, that six boats had tied up before noon. The rest of the entry concerned repairs due on the north pier.

## Passage 4

The incoming nurse read the handover notes left by the night sister. The notes said the sister planned to move the patient nearer the desk so that his breathing would be easier to monitor, since a bed near the desk let the night staff watch his chest rise without walking the ward. The notes also recorded that his temperature had been normal at midnight. They ended with a reminder to chart his medication at six.

## Passage 5

The governors' minutes recorded the head teacher's explanation for the rise in attendance. She said attendance had risen because the new bus route now reached the estate. The minutes noted that the school had also repainted the entrance hall over the summer. Later in the meeting, she said she did not think the rise in attendance was because of the new bus route. The governors moved on to discuss the canteen budget.

## Passage 6

The dockyard clerk read through the harbourmaster's log for the week. In it, the harbourmaster had written that the trawler crossed the bar early because the tide had turned, and she added that a loaded boat at that harbour always crosses the bar early once the tide turns before dawn. The log went on to record the names of the crew and the weight of the catch. The clerk also noted, from her own count, that six boats had tied up before noon. The rest of the entry concerned repairs due on the north pier.

## Passage 7

The shopkeeper told the stocktaker what had come in that week. She described the new delivery of tinned fruit, stacked on the middle shelf by the window. She listed the items that were running low: candles, matches, and writing paper. She mentioned that the delivery van had arrived later than usual on Tuesday. The stocktaker wrote down the shelf numbers as she spoke.
