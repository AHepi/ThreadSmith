## The findings, in order of weight
1. **The near miss never reached the driver, on either translator, on both rule texts.** The corpus was built so that the sound sides (B06, B01) carry the rule the faulty sides lack; both translators wrote the rules as general lines, and neither wrote the bridge from the rule's terms to the case's (that the trawler is a loaded boat at that harbour; that a gate standing open is the shepherd's having left it open). The language matches by atom identity; the prose joins by sense; 39 leaves the join to filled-in lines, and both translators, twice each, declined to fill it while filling in elsewhere (Atria's B04 route). The checker therefore reports the same fault on the sound side as on the faulty side, and the prose reader, on three of the four reports, points at the rule the report omits. This is the owner's question answered from the other direction: a translation faithful to the words on which the check reports a fault the prose's reader would not. Charged to nothing by the plan's cell; to be decided at the language and the guide (whether 39 should require the bridge, or the driver should search for it).
2. **The report does not name the general line it considered and could not use.** On B06 and B01 the JUMP and NO CONNECTION blocks list the cause's or reason's lines and the effect's, and say `It would take a line nobody wrote`, while a line of that kind sits in the ledger with conditions no line meets. The prose reader saw the gap. A report-layer change: print the general lines examined and the condition that failed.
3. **The ledger is unstable at the line grain; the report is stable at its own.** Byte-identical sentences translated in separate calls by the same model get different ids, atoms, Thing lines and, sometimes, different facts; every pair comparison fires B3 and B12's sameness half on both translators, with no OPPOSITE line anywhere. Yet the report kinds inside the world are identical on every rewording pair and agree across translators on twelve of thirteen texts. Derivation 3's prediction at the line grain, and file 11's grain argument at the report's. B3 and B12 as written measured the matcher's coarseness as much as the translators' movement (second marker, finding 6); a version of the variation test that reads report kinds and claim lines by sense is the next instrument.
4. **The hedge's false finding is real and reproducible.** B10 met on both translators: the language's silence on modality turns "may have been left open" into a bare cause and the checker reports a JUMP against a writer who claimed a possibility. C is short a change (L81 section 5): a modal marker on a claim, or a rule that a hedged cause is not asked about.
5. **The same shape on attitude and inference words.** "did not think" (B05, B10), "had concluded ... must" (B01, B08, B16): the prose reader labelled the ledgers' flat denials and claims as hedges made facts on every such report; the told world keeps who said it and not how strongly. Outside the plan's override, charged by its letter to the translator, which followed 39; for the owner with finding 4.
6. **The pipeline facts.** Both providers cut streams mid-answer often (29 of 38 translation receipts carry a -2 attempt; the retry provision carried the run); Mimo's content filter replaced three answers with a refusal sentence returned as a 200; Mimo reached the 100,000 cap twice, Atria never reached 65,536; two successful Atria answers carried no finish reason. The plan's Not tested named some of these; the second marker's list names them all.
7. **Nesting.** B16's conclusion inside a note inside the passage is flattened to two sibling worlds by both translators; the language has one level of world, and the prose reader noticed on both reports.
