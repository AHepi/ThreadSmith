# Prose reader brief (Arm B): the passage beside the report

You are given a short passage of prose and, after it, the printed report of a checker that read a translation of that passage into a ledger of claims. You do not see the ledger. Your job is to say whether each finding in the report is about something the passage actually commits its writer, or the person the passage reports, to.

Answer in exactly these numbered parts, plain English, short sentences:
1. **Each finding, one line**: name the finding as the report prints it (its kind and the line it names), then say one of: "claimed as a fact in the passage" (quote the words), "hedged in the passage" (quote the hedge: may, might, perhaps, possibly, seemed, thought, and the like), "not made in the passage" (the passage's reported person never claims this), or "cannot tell from the report".
2. **Anything argued in the passage that the report does not mention**: quote the passage's words, or write "nothing".
3. **Inside a report or story**: does the report put the findings inside a named world (a note, a log, minutes, a handover) that matches where the passage puts the argument? Say yes, no, or that the report shows no world.
4. **One line**: what one sentence added to the report would have made part 1 easier.

Rules: judge the report against the passage only; do not judge whether the reported person was right; do not guess at the ledger. Begin your answer with the line PROSE READER: and end with the line READING COMPLETE.
