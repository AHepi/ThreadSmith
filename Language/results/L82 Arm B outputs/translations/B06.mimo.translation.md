## 2. The bin

- **Sentence 1: "for the week"** — a span of time (which week the log covers). Time beyond a chain's order and a stage named in a fact has no form in the language.
- **Sentence 2: "In it"** — a place ("in the log"). The language has no form for where things are, inside or outside. The pointer "it" is read as the log (see Readings).
- **Sentence 2: "the harbourmaster had written that"** — attribution. The log's first account is the harbourmaster's writing; the rule is to send the attribution here and write the content as TOLD in that writer's world, so the account follows in log_world.
- **Sentence 2: "and she added that"** — attribution of the general rule to the same writer ("she" read as the harbourmaster). The rule follows in log_world.
- **Sentence 3: "went on to"** — the image of the log as a clerk who goes on down the page (a document made into an actor). The plain facts kept are that the log records the crew's names and the catch's weight.
- **Sentence 4: "The clerk also noted, from her own count, that"** — attribution of the note to the clerk and of its basis (her own count: who knows what, and a count is a number). The content follows in clerk_world. The phrase also implies the clerk made a count; that act is part of the attribution and gets no line.
- **Sentence 4: "six"** — a number (how many boats). Numbers and amounts are outside the language; the line keeps the boats that tied up and drops the count.
- **Sentence 5: "The rest of"** — a portion of the entry ("the rest" sits with some, most, few). The language has no form for portions, so the line is written about the entry as a whole.

## 4. Readings I chose

- **Sentence 1:** the verbs "read through", "crossed", "turned", "records", "tied up", "concerns" have no entry in the shape book. Readings open: write them as happenings of an unknown shape, or as facts carrying the text's own verb in the description. Taken: facts (as the tomato example wrote "the cold reached the balcony plants"), since the text states no press and rig 1's vocabulary records events as holds() facts; each such verb will be reported "not checked".

- **Sentence 2:** "In it" — "it" = the log, or "it" = the week. Taken: the log (the only document named). The place words themselves are binned.
- **Sentence 2:** "she added" — "she" = the harbourmaster (the sentence's subject), or another woman. Taken: the harbourmaster.
- **Sentence 2:** the kind of "the trawler". Readings open: the text's word "trawler" alone, or a wider kind "boat" so the log's rule can reach it. Taken: the text's word as its kind, plus a separate usual-case line "the trawler is a boat", so said and added do not share a mark.
- **Sentence 2:** the rule's strength and force. Readings open: ALWAYS or USUALLY ("always" is in the text); MAKES or SHOWS (the wording leaves it open). Taken: ALWAYS (said) and MAKES (the same sentence treats the tide's turning as what produced the crossing). Because the MAKES/SHOWS word is mine and cannot be split off the line, the whole line is marked filled in and says which part I added.
- **Sentence 2:** the stages "early" and "before dawn". Readings open: bin them as "when" words, or keep them as the stage named in the fact. Taken: keep them — dropped, the rule would cover turnings at any hour and crossings at any time, which the writer did not say.
- **Sentence 2:** whether the rule's conditions hold for the trawler (loaded, at that harbour, the tide turning before dawn). Readings open: add them as filled-in missing steps, or leave them unstated. Taken: leave them unstated, so the gap the writer left stays visible to the checker.
- **Sentences 3 and 5:** where the documents' contents go. Readings open: put everything the log and the entry hold inside log_world, or keep the sentences that describe the documents as claims about the documents. Taken: the log's own claims (sentence 2) are TOLD in log_world; the descriptions of what the log and the entry hold (sentences 3 and 5) are CLAIMED facts about the documents.
- **Sentence 3:** whose crew and whose catch. Readings open: the trawler's (the nearest subject of the entry), or unstated. Taken: unstated; no link line added.
- **Sentence 4:** "noted" — observed and remarked, or wrote into a note. Taken: the content is what the clerk reports under either reading, so it is TOLD in clerk_world; the difference between observing and writing is not one the language can state, and the act sits in the binned attribution.
- **Sentence 4:** "from her own count" — "her" = the clerk or the harbourmaster; "count" = the act of counting or a tally kept. Taken: the clerk's own count (as against the log's), read as a tally she made; the attribution and the count both go to the bin.
- **Sentence 4:** "before noon" — bin it as a "when" word, or keep it as the stage named in the fact. Taken: keep it as the stage; only the number "six" is binned.
- **Sentence 5:** "the entry" — the week's entry in the harbourmaster's log (what sentence 1 says was read), or an entry the clerk is making. Taken: the log's entry; the clerk's note is set apart in the text ("from her own count") and no other entry has been named.
- **Sentence 5:** "due" — the repairs are due (falling due: a state of the repairs), or the repairs are owed (an obligation). Taken: the state reading, kept inside the fact's description; under the obligation reading that word would go to the bin.
- **Sentence 5:** "The rest of" — name "the rest of the entry" as a thing of its own, or bin the portion and write about the entry. Taken: bin the portion (it sits with some, most, few) and write the line about the entry as a whole.
