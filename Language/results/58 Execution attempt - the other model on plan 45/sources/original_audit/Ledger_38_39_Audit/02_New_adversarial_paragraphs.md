# Sixty additional text inputs in thirty contrast families

The inherited corpus was preserved before these examples were written. These are new, targeted examples, not a blind test set. Every paragraph is original to this audit. The paired texts sometimes reverse a relevant fact, sometimes clarify an unresolved reading, and sometimes preserve a question-relevant meaning while changing its expression. The expectation says which relation is intended; no blanket paraphrase claim is made. Counts are inventory, not confidence.

## N01: The explicit kingfisher contrast

**Question.** Does the text assert literal combustion?

**Text A.**

The kingfisher caught fire in the sunset. The narrator explains that this describes the colour of its unharmed feathers, not burning.

**Text B.**

The kingfisher caught fire when a spark struck its feathers. The narrator explicitly describes burning, not a colour comparison.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** A colour reading is explicit in the first text; burning is explicit in the second. Neither requires a biological mechanism.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R01.

## N02: A kingfisher without the answer written in

**Question.** Is either burning or intact plumage settled?

**Text A.**

The kingfisher caught fire above the water. A moment later there was only the dark branch.

**Text B.**

The kingfisher caught fire above the water, she wrote, meaning the flash of its colour. She adds that it did not burn.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first is not a warrant to invent a spark, flames, unharmed feathers or an exclusive optical explanation. The second resolves that local uncertainty.

**Status.** CHOICE_GAP. Related candidates: R01,R03.

## N03: The pear touched red

**Question.** Is a physical touching event asserted?

**Text A.**

The pear was touched red beneath its yellow skin. No hand or painting action is described.

**Text B.**

The painter touched the pear with a red-stained finger. The text states the contact.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** A colouring description is not automatically TOUCHES. The contrast states contact. The first paragraph is newly authored, not a line-by-line expansion of Stevens.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R01.

## N04: A fruit that really flowers

**Question.** Is the unusual development literal within the story?

**Text A.**

Green flowered over the pear: the narrator means a change of colour and explicitly denies petals.

**Text B.**

In this tale petals literally grew through the pear skin. The narrator explicitly rejects a colour metaphor.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Do not equate literary unusualness with nonliteralness. Preserve the explicit reading in each text without supplying botany or a force class.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R01.

## N05: The unheld belief

**Question.** Does the source attribute acceptance of ripe?

**Text A.**

Nora does not believe that the pear is ripe. The writer attributes neither belief in ripeness nor belief in unripeness to her.

**Text B.**

Nora says she believes that the pear is ripe. The writer reports that affirmative belief.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Blindly transferring embedded ripe into a personal TOLD world in the first text is not faithful. The whole negative attribution can remain binned, with its complement merely mentioned.

**Status.** INSTRUCTION_CONFLICT. Related candidates: R02.

## N06: A wish is not a report

**Question.** May the desired condition become a reported belief?

**Text A.**

Nora wants the pear to be ripe. She describes it as unripe and does not claim that it is ripe.

**Text B.**

Nora reports that the pear is ripe. The outer narrator describes it as unripe.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first has an excluded desire plus an affirmative unripe description; do not assert ripe as Nora's reported belief. The second legitimately has conflicting report and outer descriptions in distinct scopes.

**Status.** INSTRUCTION_CONFLICT. Related candidates: R02.

## N07: A report that was never made

**Question.** Is open attributed as something Nora said?

**Text A.**

Nora did not say, “The gate is open.” No such report is attributed to her.

**Text B.**

Nora said, “The gate is open.” Her statement is reported without outer endorsement.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** A quotation under denial is mentioned content, not an affirmative reported assertion. General NOT can protect this, but the broad extraction wording must not override it.

**Status.** EXTRACTION_RISK. Related candidates: R02.

## N08: An if inside a note

**Question.** Does an inner conditional establish its antecedent?

**Text A.**

The note says, “If the gate opens, the bell rings.” The narrator does not say that the gate opens.

**Text B.**

The note says, “The gate opened and the bell rang.” The narrator does not endorse the note.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Keep the conditional within the note, or preserve it unanalysed if the scope combination is unavailable. Never turn it into actual or independently TOLD antecedent occurrence. Full nested-case execution is not demanded.

**Status.** DECLARED_BOUNDARY. Related candidates: R02.

## N09: Worlds do not borrow silently

**Question.** Is an outer rule automatically active inside a tale?

**Text A.**

In the actual account every press rings the bell. Nora tells a story about a press, saying nothing about ringing or that rule.

**Text B.**

Nora tells a story in which every press rings the bell and the button is pressed.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The isolated-world rule blocks inheritance in the first. The second supplies the rule locally. This is an existing declared boundary, not a new defect.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R02.

## N10: The same speaker at two stages

**Question.** Is this a same-stage contradiction?

**Text A.**

In Nora's story the pear is unripe at the first stage and ripe at the next.

**Text B.**

In Nora's story the same pear at the same stage is ripe and not ripe.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Stage indexing prevents the first clash and must not conceal the second. No theory of maturation or elapsed time is required.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R02.

## N11: A fictional contradiction is still a contradiction

**Question.** May fiction be used to conceal an explicit local clash?

**Text A.**

In this tale the gate is open and not open at the same stage and in the same respect.

**Text B.**

In Nora's tale the gate is open. In Bea's separate tale it is not open.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** 38 explicitly reports conflicts inside a told world. It does not merge independent tales. Do not exempt every fictional source from internal checking.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R02.

## N12: An exemption the translator cannot attach

**Question.** Can the author's stated exemption be represented without changing the rule?

**Text A.**

The author gives an unqualified ALWAYS rule: a press of this button rings the bell. The author then calls this press exempt, without giving an outcome.

**Text B.**

The author gives a USUALLY rule: a press normally rings the bell. The author then says this rule did not apply to this press.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** 38 promises an ALWAYS-exemption report but the translator instructions allow exception attachment only to USUALLY. Do not repair the conflict by silently demoting ALWAYS.

**Status.** INSTRUCTION_CONFLICT. Related candidates: R04.

## N13: The countercase remains visible

**Question.** Does calling it an exception erase the conflict?

**Text A.**

The source says every press rings, without exception. This button was pressed and the bell did not ring; the source calls this an exception.

**Text B.**

The source says daytime presses ring. A silent night press is explicitly outside that scope.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Facts can expose the first contradiction even without an Exception line. This is the strongest existing-rule defence against overstating N12: the exception-attachment defect is not total inability to detect countercases.

**Status.** CONTROL_ON_FINDING. Related candidates: R04.

## N14: The cause nobody supplied

**Question.** May a Result require an invented producer?

**Text A.**

The box moved away from the wall. The source supplies no producer or preceding happening.

**Text B.**

Ada pushed the box, and the source explicitly says her push made it move away from the wall.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first must not acquire an as-a-result-of happening solely to satisfy the Result shape. A descriptive Fact is a safe but less typed workaround. The second must retain its explicit cause.

**Status.** ENCODING_GAP. Related candidates: R05.

## N15: A result with a refused cause

**Question.** Can movement and denial of its proposed cause coexist?

**Text A.**

Ada pushed the box. The box moved, but the source denies that Ada's push produced the movement.

**Text B.**

Ada pushed the box. The source says that her push produced its movement.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Do not put the denied causal attribution back into the Result line. Conversely, the repair must not strip a cause that is explicitly stated.

**Status.** ENCODING_GAP. Related candidates: R05.

## N16: Making something already tending that way

**Question.** Does an existing tendency alone refute a production attribution?

**Text A.**

Nora was already heading toward the exit. The account says the moving walkway made her continue toward it and was the producer of that movement.

**Text B.**

Nora was already heading toward the exit. The account says the moving walkway produced her continued movement toward it.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The two formulations offer the same production attribution. Applying the blanket MAKES-only-without-tendency rule to one and allowing BECAUSE for the other creates a lexical-path divergence unless the chosen force-dynamic sense is made explicit. This is a diagnostic of the stipulated reading, not a quantitative-mechanics verdict.

**Status.** SEMANTIC_READING_CHALLENGE. Related candidates: R06.

## N17: Help and nonachievement

**Question.** Does assistance alone establish the goal?

**Text A.**

The account says the handle adjustment assisted an attempted opening. It explicitly states that the gate stayed shut.

**Text B.**

The account says the handle adjustment opened the gate.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first must not become an achieved opening. Unlike the old helped-move case, the attempted aspect is explicit here.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R06.

## N18: An unsupported relation and its negation

**Question.** Can lack of support hide a direct contradiction?

**Text A.**

The source asserts that the fleaming produced the opening and also asserts that that same fleaming did not produce that opening. No meaning or productive rule for fleaming is supplied.

**Text B.**

The source denies that fleaming produced the opening. It supplies no positive production assertion and no productive rule for fleaming.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** In the first, the opposed relational commitments clash irrespective of whether either can be verified. In the second, missing productive support is not itself a contradiction. Read 38's negative-BECAUSE fine-unless rule consistently with its general contradiction promise.

**Status.** RULE_PRECEDENCE_CONFLICT. Related candidates: R07.

## N19: A sound endpoint and a circular offered route

**Question.** Does independent support validate the particular circular reason?

**Text A.**

A source-supplied rule supports ringing from a recorded press. A separate offered argument uses an inferred flag rise as its only reason for ringing, while that flag rise was inferred only from ringing.

**Text B.**

A source-supplied rule supports ringing from an independently observed flag rise. The flag observation did not come from ringing.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first endpoint may have support from the press while the offered flag route remains circular. Do not let any-route success erase route dependence.

**Status.** ROUTE_REPORT_GAP. Related candidates: R07.

## N20: The conclusion comes before the lemma

**Question.** Is the last supported sentence necessarily the main conclusion?

**Text A.**

My final conclusion is that the gate opened. It follows from the bell's ringing under the stated rule. I finish by defending that premise: the bell rang because the logged press produces ringing.

**Text B.**

The logged press supports ringing under the stated rule. Ringing supports opening under another stated rule. My final conclusion is that the gate opened.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Reordering the exposition leaves the expressly named final conclusion unchanged. Last argued for must refer to the designated argument target, not simply the last claim encountered on the page.

**Status.** TARGET_AMBIGUITY. Related candidates: R07.

## N21: A relevant action in the wrong direction

**Question.** Does touching a dependency show a workable plan?

**Text A.**

The goal is that the pear stays cool. The source says this depends on the lid staying shut and that opening the lid makes the pear warm instead. Nora opens the lid so that the pear stays cool.

**Text B.**

The goal is that the pear stays cool. The source says closing the lid keeps it cool. Nora closes the lid so that the pear stays cool.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first action affects the right dependency in the wrong direction on the supplied account. The SO THAT relevance test alone cannot certify feasibility. The second supplies the favourable connection; goal achievement remains distinct from source validity.

**Status.** PLAN_VERDICT_GAP. Related candidates: R08.

## N22: A relevant action with no direction given

**Question.** What is established when an action changes a dependency but its effect is unknown?

**Text A.**

Keeping the pear cool depends on the setting of a dial. Turning the dial changes that setting. Nothing says which setting keeps the pear cool or what turning produces.

**Text B.**

Keeping the pear cool depends on the dial being set to cool. Turning this dial sets it to cool, according to the supplied rule.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first supplies relevance but not a supported route to the goal. The second adds a directional connection. No numeric temperature model is needed.

**Status.** PLAN_VERDICT_GAP. Related candidates: R08.

## N23: The source-dependent invented bridge

**Question.** Does the delete-all-said test detect every answer-smuggling addition?

**Text A.**

The source says only that the ribbon is red. To establish opening, the translator adds that whenever this ribbon is red the gate opens, with no independent reason.

**Text B.**

The translator adds both that the ribbon is red and that a red ribbon establishes opening, without any source premises.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Deleting all said lines stops the first derivation but leaves the second. The fixed diagnostic catches the second, not the first. Rule 9 can still reject the first; do not call diagnostic silence validation.

**Status.** DIAGNOSTIC_LIMIT. Related candidates: R09.

## N24: The opposite bridge

**Question.** May changing the desired answer select an equally unsupported rule?

**Text A.**

The source says the ribbon is red. Asked about opening, the translator adds red means open. Asked about non-opening, it adds red means not open.

**Text B.**

The same source-supplied red-means-open rule is retained whichever target is asked about.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The paired unsupported bridges lack discrimination. This is a translator-level criticism under existing Rule 9, not proof that the described fixed checker has evaluated every possible rival.

**Status.** CONTROL_ON_LIMIT. Related candidates: R09.

## N25: The photograph of the actual world

**Question.** Does independent evidence survive an intervention as an invariant fact?

**Text A.**

A photograph independently records the box moving after the push. Asked what happens without the push, the proposed assessment imports that movement as a fixed premise.

**Text B.**

The source explicitly supplies an applicable no-push account under which the box does not move; the old photograph remains a record of the actual case.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first confuses independent observation with warranted carryover. The second separates the old record from the changed case. The test does not require two interacting causes.

**Status.** CARRYOVER_AMBIGUITY. Related candidates: R10.

## N26: Withdrawal is not physical negation

**Question.** Does removing a report close the gate?

**Text A.**

Withdraw the report that the gate opened. No physical change of the gate is stipulated.

**Text B.**

Make it the case that the gate did not open. The old report remains a historical record.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The specified interventions differ. 38 already distinguishes them; this is a control against an overbroad counterfactual repair.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R10.

## N27: Keep the paired interpretations together

**Question.** Can locally possible assignments be recombined arbitrarily?

**Text A.**

The source leaves two readings: Ada moved red and Bea moved blue; or Ada moved blue and Bea moved red. Neither reading has both moving red.

**Text B.**

The source expressly says Ada and Bea both moved the red box.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Do not create a third reading by joining slots from different alternatives. A conditional selected ledger may remain legal, but it is not an exhaustive reading of the source.

**Status.** CHOICE_GAP. Related candidates: R03.

## N28: The sentence crosses the line

**Question.** Does the first printed line form a completed rain statement?

**Text A.**

The rain stopped
no one
from leaving.
The source makes no claim that rain ceased.

**Text B.**

The rain stopped.
No one left.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Line breaks in the first cannot supply the period in the second. Preserve the complete construction; the quantification can remain binned rather than turned into a cessation claim.

**Status.** CONTROL. Related candidates: R01.

## N29: Repetition is not another shove

**Question.** How many event occurrences does the source identify?

**Text A.**

Ada pushed the box once. The next sentence retells that same push: Ada pushed the box. It explicitly denies a second push.

**Text B.**

Ada pushed the box. Then she pushed it again. The source expressly identifies distinct pushes.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** Preserve distinct source occurrences without inventing a second physical event in the first. Event identity and line identity need an explicit relation when later checks remove an event rather than a report.

**Status.** IDENTITY_GAP. Related candidates: R11.

## N30: The verb without an object

**Question.** May an intransitive happening acquire a fictitious patient?

**Text A.**

The bell rang. The source names no object that it rang and no doer that caused it.

**Text B.**

Ada rang the bell. The source names Ada as the agent.

**Discriminating expectation and present adjudication.** The first can be retained as a descriptive Fact, but a Happening template that mandates DOER VERB THING has no direct intransitive form. This is a bounded representation-extension candidate, not evidence that all intransitives were promised by the original grammar.

**Status.** EXPLICIT_EXTENSION_CANDIDATE. Related candidates: R12.
