# Audit of the supplied ledger language and translation prompt

## Verdict

The kingfisher pair does not, by itself, refute the supplied language. File 39 explicitly permits a nonliteral reading, requires uncertain interpretations to be marked, and retains excluded imagery in the bin. Its literal-burning counterpart must still retain the stated event. Losing the poem's full literary effect is an admitted limit, not an in-scope failure.

There are nevertheless concrete conflicts and unresolved choices in 38/39. The most direct is the instruction to attach exceptions only to USUALLY while the language promises findings for exceptions aimed at ALWAYS. Another is the blanket instruction to put the content of a belief, want or conclusion into a personal TOLD world: under negation, desire, doubt or quotation this can promote merely embedded content into an assertion. A third concerns checking a causal claim and its explicit negation: lack of a verified productive route must not make the direct conflict disappear.

These are findings about the supplied documents, not observed outputs of a program. Some are instruction conflicts with a defensible safe reading; others are capability-preserving encoding gaps or underdetermined reporting rules. They are not all counted as demonstrated inevitable failures. In particular, full nested worlds, general psychology, modal reasoning, quantitative mechanics and jointly acting causes remain outside this audit's required success conditions.

## The hard-to-vary question

Hold fixed the source passage, the assessment question and the admitted subject matter. Does translating the passage under 38/39 retain its admitted commitments without inventing their occurrence, scope, source or support? When a proposed amendment is substituted, does it preserve those same jobs under relevant and irrelevant changes?

The job is not to turn literary interpretation into a computable truth test. The translator's judgment remains explicit and fallible. The fixed checker is criticised only for the distinctions and findings its description actually claims. A missing algorithm is not, by itself, a semantic objection.

The archived hard-to-vary skill and its depth reference were read earlier in this conversation. This audit uses removal, substitution, opposite outcomes, paired relevant/irrelevant changes, strongest defences and group-removal tests. No hardness score is assigned.

## F01. The ALWAYS exception conflict

**Source.** 38, content kind 9, line 51, promises a report for an exemption aimed at ALWAYS and a contradiction for its countercase form. But 38, translator rule 12, line 128, and 39, line 25, permit attachment only to USUALLY.

**Case.** N12-A gives an unqualified press-always-rings rule, then an explicitly claimed exemption without an outcome. The translator must preserve the author's exception claim without silently weakening the rule.

**Strongest defence.** When the source also supplies a press and silence, the translator can record those facts and a general contradiction check can find the conflict. N13-A demonstrates this defence. Therefore the defect is not inability to detect every universal counterexample.

**Finding.** The exception relation itself has no consistent instruction across the two documents. The exemption-without-outcome case is not repaired by encoding a negative outcome the source never supplied. R04 permits the relation and preserves the general rule's original strength. This is a confirmed text-level conflict, not a measured runtime failure.

## F02. Embedded content is not automatically TOLD content

**Source.** 39:30 says to bin who believes, wants or concludes what and write the content as TOLD in that person's world. 38:30 and 105 describe TOLD as what a story/report/note/dream says. 38:35 protects negation and 38:136 excludes psychology.

**Case.** “Nora does not believe that the pear is ripe. The writer attributes neither belief in ripeness nor belief in unripeness to her.” An unqualified personal-world ripe line does not record that source faithfully. N06 adds a desire for ripeness alongside a description of unripeness. Neither case requires deciding whether Nora's psychology is correct.

**Strongest defence.** A careful translator can preserve the whole attribution in the bin under the exclusions and NOT rules. That safe reading exists. It conflicts with the blanket content-transfer instruction rather than showing that every possible translator must produce a bad ledger.

**Finding.** The instruction needs a guard: transfer only what the source actually presents as asserted in a report/world. Preserve denied, desired, doubted, conditional and mentioned contents under their operators. Do not flatten them into a personal belief-world. R02 leaves excluded psychology in the bin rather than inventing a new belief calculus.

## F03. A chosen reading is not a resolved source

**Source.** 38:9,131 marks guesses; 39:17 and 39 records choices. 39:6 requires reconstruction from the ledger and bin, whereas the separate chosen-readings section can contain distinctions absent from either.

**Cases.** T39 has an unresolved pronoun. C03 has agreement among two explicitly incomplete readings. T69 and N27 contain correlated alternatives whose slots must not be recombined independently. C04 protects a separate explicit movement from being erased by local uncertainty.

**Strongest defence.** A single selected interpretation is allowed if marked filled in, and the existing report note already makes dependent findings conditional. A failure to enumerate every imaginable interpretation is not itself a defect.

**Finding.** Keep the condition on the reported result and preserve consequential alternatives where the reader can recover them. Do not present a selected ledger as the uniquely settled source, and do not infer exhaustiveness from agreement among retained readings. This is a coverage/reporting gap, not a proof that 38 already mandates an unsound consensus rule. R03 preserves both local certainty and local uncertainty.

## F04. A result need not come with an asserted producer

**Source.** 38:47 defines a Result with “as a result of happening NAME”. 38:35 and 55 also admit denying a causal attribution while retaining the effect.

**Cases.** N14-A states movement with no producer. N15-A states movement but denies that the named push produced it. T57 states an unknown operation and separate movement.

**Strongest defence.** The free-name Fact form can retain an uncaused movement description. This defeats a claim of total unrepresentability. It does not automatically give the description the Result-specific checking that the language promises for typed results.

**Finding.** This is a capability-preserving encoding gap. The display should separate outcome and stage from causal attribution. R05 requires a separate BECAUSE assertion when the source states production. A repair that simply deletes every causal association fails N14-B and N15-B.

## F05. The relation and its achievement need separating

**Source.** 38:52,75,81 describes causal-word checks against tendencies. Unlike its explicit goal rule at 89, it does not clearly settle every distinction between enabling, assistance, prevention and achieved endpoints.

**Cases.** T21 explicitly withholds passage despite enabling it. N17 explicitly says assistance concerned an attempted opening and the gate stayed shut. T23 prevents movement toward the wall without asserting movement away.

**Strongest defence.** The definitions can be read conservatively: a tendency is not an actual result and a source's explicit no-result clause remains. That reading already handles the tests.

**Finding.** Do not report a forced current false positive. State the endpoint distinctions so implementations do not infer success from enabling, or reversal from prevention. R06 does that without a general theory of counterfactual causation.

## F06. A force-dynamic reading must not be every meaning of “made”

**Source.** 38:81 permits MAKES for people/groups/rules only when the thing was not already heading toward the result. Elsewhere, 38:49 and 85 use production as the meaning of MAKES and BECAUSE.

**Case.** N16 offers the same continued movement as “made her continue” and “produced her continued movement”, while preserving an existing tendency.

**Strongest defence.** MAKES may be intended as a narrower force-dynamic construction, with ordinary production represented instead by BECAUSE. On that reading the existence of a prior tendency does not refute every production claim.

**Finding.** The narrow reading must be declared and the two translation routes kept consistent. Otherwise a synonym-level change can alter the finding without altering the offered causal relation. This is a semantic-reading challenge, not a claimed refutation of qualitative mechanics and not a request for a multi-force model. R06 retains an explicit narrow construction or an ordinary-production route; it does not silently choose an entire replacement theory of causation.

## F07. Endpoint support does not validate every offered route

**Source.** 38:85-87 describes BECAUSE, SINCE and chains, including “any route” and “any one step”. It also promises to name a disconnected reason.

**Cases.** N19 has an independently supported ringing conclusion but a separately offered circular flag-based route. N20 states the final conclusion first and defends a premise last.

**Strongest defence.** “Any route” can be read as support for the conclusion, not vindication of every reason. “Last” can refer to the argument's terminal target rather than the final sentence. Those readings are coherent and should be retained.

**Finding.** Make the readings explicit. Report the target's support separately from the offered route and identify the source-designated conclusion rather than using page order. These are unresolved reporting choices, not proof of an observed bad verdict. R07 handles the distinction and retains independent routes.

## F08. The added-lines test is a sufficient warning, not a complete safeguard

**Source.** 38:92 removes every said line to test additions. Translator rule 9 at 125 separately rejects answer-restatements, substituted reasons and equally permissive opposite-answer additions.

**Case.** N23-A adds a baseless red-ribbon-implies-opening rule to the source's red-ribbon fact. Removing the source fact destroys the conclusion, so the diagnostic is silent. N23-B puts both fact and rule among additions and triggers the diagnostic.

**Strongest defence.** Rule 9 can reject N23-A. A note on added premises also keeps the finding conditional. The document does not say the delete-all-said test is a complete proof of Rule 9 compliance.

**Finding.** This is an operational diagnostic limit, not a newly discovered permission to invent premises. R09 says explicitly that diagnostic silence does not validate the bridge. It preserves legitimate conjecture rather than banning all additions.

## F09. Independent observation is not invariant counterfactual background

**Source.** 38:103 preserves what led up to a change and sets aside results tied to the changed thing. The document already declares a separate multiple-cause trap at 147.

**Cases.** C15, U23 and N25 ask about a camera/photograph of actual movement when the question concerns the no-push case. The observation is independent testimony, not observation of the altered case.

**Strongest defence.** A result with the proper dependency link is already set aside, and “what led up to” can be read to exclude downstream outcomes. That defeats a blanket claim that 38 necessarily retains every actual observation.

**Finding.** Clarify unlinked observation and support records: preserve the actual record as actual history without treating it as an altered-case premise. Where carryover is not supplied, leave the changed result unresolved. This is not a disguised retest of the expressly excluded two-cause problem. R10 retains the declared what-if limits.

## F10. Only-ways does not manufacture production

**Source.** 38:50 rewrites Only-ways as denial when all alternatives are denied; every general line must have ALWAYS/USUALLY and MAKES/SHOWS at 49 and 123.

**Case.** T26 supplies entry and an unspecified open-gate alternative. T27 has both gates open. Neither establishes a particular gate or exclusive choice.

**Strongest defence.** In ordinary classical reading, the negative-all-ways condition expresses the same necessity constraint. Its inability to pick an alternative is not a defect. No new disjunctive calculus is required by this audit.

**Finding.** The compilation should be an ALWAYS SHOWS constraint in the original scope, not an unstated productive mechanism. A separately stated causal absence claim remains separately recordable. R05 includes this clarification. This item is not a finding that necessity was reversed in the supplied language.

## F11. One event can have more than one source occurrence

**Source.** 38:29 ties a happening's name to its line identifier, and 33 supplies one sentence anchor. 39:11 writes sentence by sentence.

**Cases.** T41, T65 and N29 explicitly repeat one push, as against a second push. C09 and U14 repeat a witness report, as against independent observations.

**Strongest defence.** A canonical happening and a source note/bin entry can preserve repetition. Line counts are not claimed to be statistical evidence weights.

**Finding.** The distinction is an identity/provenance clarification, not proof that the existing grammar necessarily duplicates events. R11 preserves source occurrences and explicit same-event identity. A general event-co-reference procedure is not supplied or promised.

## F12. Mention-only meaning needs a nonasserting endpoint

**Source.** 38:37 explicitly says goals and what-if targets are merely mentioned; 55 and 39:24 require numbered references, while the displayed SO THAT shape directly embeds a goal.

**Case.** T48 and N22 need a goal/question target without assuming its truth.

**Strongest defence.** The prose meaning already makes the intended nonassertion clear. A sympathetic reader can understand it without a parser grammar.

**Finding.** This is a notation clarification, not a mathematical claim that goals must become facts. R05 supplies a MENTION wrapper and a source-stage rule to make examples auditable. It adds no new subject matter or new truth status.

## F13. A clean limited check is not complete coverage

**Source.** 38:17 says a clean report means the lines fit; 44 makes Facts free names; 47 leaves BECOMES unchecked; 113 and 144-151 provide capability and bin warnings.

**Cases.** T61 stores a whole event description as an opaque Fact and calls the movement checked. T76 answers a source-attribution question in place of a production question.

**Strongest defence.** The document already warns against reading “not checked” as fine and against an enormous bin. The question can have genuinely narrow scope.

**Finding.** Keep the report target and coverage visible. The proposed wording says no clash was found by the applicable checks, rather than certifying every source commitment. R01, R03 and R09 jointly address this. No numeric coverage score is introduced.

## F14. Intransitive happenings are a bounded extension, not a hidden demand

**Source.** 38:46 shows DOER VERB THING. Unknown verbs remain legal, but a patient-free occurrence has no explicit Happening shape.

**Case.** N30 contrasts “The bell rang” and “Ada rang the bell.” Supplying a made-up patient or agent would alter the first.

**Strongest defence.** A Fact can retain ringing. The displayed Happening grammar may itself define the admitted narrower structural scope.

**Finding.** Do not count this as an in-scope contradiction of that grammar. R12 is an optional representation extension permitting only the roles actually stated. The companion translation prompt uses the conservative Fact/bin fallback unless the extension is explicitly adopted.

## F15. Unsupported opposed claims can still clash

**Source.** 38:35 admits scoped negation of a cause; 55 admits both positive and negative BECAUSE lines; 88 says NOT [E BECAUSE C] is fine unless the ledger makes C produce E. 111 promises contradictions among commitments.

**Case.** N18-A contains both the assertion that fleaming produced opening and the explicit denial of that same relation, without any independently defined fleaming mechanism.

**Strongest defence.** The general contradiction check can take precedence and report the pair without needing a mechanism. This is the correct safe reading.

**Finding.** The local “fine unless” sentence must not be read as overriding that direct clash. Relational support and contradiction are different questions. R07 explicitly retains direct positive/negative conflicts even when a route is unsupported, while N18-B prevents the repair from treating every unsupported causal denial as a contradiction.

## Candidate status after re-audit

The repair dossier contains twelve candidates. R01-R05 and R07-R10 are retained as bounded clarifications or changes on the recorded cases, with their exact qualifications. R06 remains a declared choice about force-dynamic versus ordinary production readings. R11 is a representation clarification whose automated co-reference is not supplied. R12 is an optional extension, not silently adopted. None is certified by an external checker or independent panel.

The amendments deliberately do not add nested what-if execution, a general belief logic, numerical force inference, a general spatial calculus, arbitrary default priorities or multiple-cause semantics. Cases needing those remain honest boundary reports. A more permissive language would be a separately declared research proposal.

Only the abstract was retrieved. Its usable contribution is a distinction between justified interpretive disagreement and arbitrary meanings, not a supplied gold interpretation of these poems. This informed the local-definition controls, not a theory that every synonym or reflow is meaning-preserving. ## Limit

The same assistant constructed and criticised the new examples and candidates. The 324 inherited items were not a statistically representative or independent corpus. Several old controls turn out to be scope-limited or to add information rather than preserve meaning; those corrections are retained in the coverage record and self-audit. No finite set of these cases shows that there is nothing left to audit.
