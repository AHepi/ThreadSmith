1. Natural selection acts on whatever is copied faithfully across generations, and in living things that is the gene.
2. A group is not copied; it dissolves and reforms with every generation, so it cannot be the thing that selection preserves.
3. Helping spreads when the gene for helping is more likely to be found in the recipients of the help than in the population at large, because then the help lands on copies of itself.
4. Relatives are the usual case: a helper's kin carry the gene with a known likelihood, and the help pays for itself when the benefit to them, weighted by that likelihood, exceeds the cost.
5. What looks like selection between groups is this same process seen from a distance, because groups of relatives are exactly where copies of the gene are clustered.
6. Any accounting in terms of groups can be rewritten in terms of the gene's likelihood of meeting itself, and gives the same answer, so the group adds no cause of its own.
7. Where a supposed group advantage cannot be so rewritten, it does not spread, because within each group the non-helper still outbreeds the helper and migration mixes the groups faster than the group advantage can act.
8. Wherever cooperation has spread, as in the social insects and the cell, the members were kin or the copies were bound together so that they could not defect.
9. The group description is a cause that produced the outcome, not merely a way of showing it.
10. Groups are therefore not selected; genes are, and groups are among the places where their copies happen to meet.
