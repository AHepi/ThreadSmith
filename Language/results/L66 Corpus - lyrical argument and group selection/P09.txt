1. But yet creation's neither crammed nor blocked about by body: there's in things a void.
2. There's place intangible, a void and room.
3. For were it not, things could in nowise move; since body's property to block and check would work on all and at all times the same.
4. Thus naught could evermore push forth and go, since naught elsewhere would not yield a starting place.
5. But now through oceans, lands, and heights of heaven, by divers causes and in divers modes, before our eyes we mark how much may move, which, finding not a void, would fail deprived of stir and motion; nay, would then have been nowise begot at all, since matter, then, had staid at rest, its parts together crammed.
6. Then too, however solid objects seem, they yet are formed of matter mixed with void: in rocks and caves the watery moisture seeps, and beady drops stand out like plenteous tears; and food finds way through every frame that lives; the trees increase and yield the season's fruit because their food throughout the whole is poured, even from the deepest roots, through trunks and boughs; and voices pass the solid walls and fly reverberant through shut doorways of a house; and stiffening frost seeps inward to our bones.
7. Which but for voids for bodies to go through 'tis clear could happen in nowise at all.
8. Again, why see we among objects some of heavier weight, but of no bulkier size?
9. Indeed, if in a ball of wool there be as much of body as in lump of lead, the two should weigh alike, since body tends to load things downward, while the void abides, by contrary nature, the imponderable.
10. Therefore, an object just as large but lighter declares infallibly its more of void; even as the heavier more of matter shows, and how much less of vacant room inside.
11. That which we're seeking with sagacious quest exists, infallibly, commixed with things: the void, the invisible inane.
