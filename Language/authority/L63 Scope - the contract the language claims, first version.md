# L63 Scope - the contract the language claims, first version

A draft for the owner to strike through. Written 21 September 2026 from the record (logs 01 to L62), file 38, the authority document (file 10, Parts III and V), and the hard-to-vary skill's building module. Provenance of this contract, in the authority document's terms: **constructed**, in this episode, with the record as its witness; the parts marked *fixed* and *given* below are the owner's and are not up for test here.

What this file is for: to say, in one place and in advance, which texts the language claims to handle, which changes the checker claims to track, which findings it promises, what it leaves out and how anyone would see it leaning on the leftovers. Every later test is run against a version of this file and says which. This version is never edited; the owner's strikes make L64.

How each claim is known: **seen** (a run in the record), **read** (a line of 38 or 10), **worked out** (follows from those), **unknown** (no test bears on it yet).

## 1. The question, in the authority document's form

**Target.** A piece of everyday reasoning prose in English by one writer: an argument, a plan, an account of something that happened to bodies, a reason to expect something, a story told inside it one level deep. Length seen so far: up to seven steps of argument in one sentence (Mondays, 27 lines, log 22) and about a dozen sentences (the audit cases). Longer texts: **unknown**; the authority document's T2 says nothing about the long run can be claimed without a rule for how error grows, and none has been measured.

**Query.** Two, and this version adds the second:
1. *Faults.* Which of the writer's commitments cannot all hold, which steps are not connected, which parts do no work, which words do not fit what they are applied to. (Decision 1, given.)
2. *Consequences.* What follows from the writer's commitments, and what would follow under each admitted change. (The owner, 21 September, given; **unknown**: nothing built.)

**Contract: the admitted changes.** These are the changes the checker makes to a ledger and works out afresh what follows. They are the whole of what "consequence" means here.
- Take out one line, or a group of lines (**seen**: every BECAUSE test; the added-lines test).
- WITHDRAW a line, as if unsaid (**seen**, patch 11).
- MAKE a fact NOT SO, as if it did not happen (**seen**, patch 11; the two-cause limit and the F09 gap noted in section 6).
- Change what fills a slot: response class, direction, strength level (**seen**, rig 2, log 17).
- Add supposed lines in a named case, one level deep (**seen**, patches 12 and 13).
- Open a told world and look at it alone (**seen**, patch 15).
Excluded from the contract, openly: any change to amounts, times beyond order, places, beliefs, purposes, modality, or numbers. See section 5 for where each goes and its gauge.

**Grain.** A thing is one name; a description is a free name the checker compares only for sameness (**read**, 38 kind 2); a verb is its shape and nothing more (**read**, 38 short lists). Two commitments the contract's changes cannot separate are one commitment at this grain (**read**, file 10, Derivation 2). This is the level; it is not a loss.

The bin is the one catch-all. Its gauge is already printed in every report. What is new here is the threshold: a number the owner sets, above which the run is *outside scope* and is reported so, rather than as clean.

## 6. The four sources of error, and what each may cost

| Layer | Provenance (file 10, Part IV) | Error that is acceptable, even wanted | Error that is not | What success looks like | What is given up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Which layer failed** is never read off a failed run: a failed test refutes the bundle (file 10, K3; the skill, section 10). F09 is left open for exactly this reason: the fix in the what-if rule would give up "after" as a plain fact; the fix in the translator would give up an honest translation of "after".

## 7. Pairs that pull, catch-alls, and where the parts came from

**Pairs that pull** (the skill: designs fail at the joints).
- *More consequences* against *exact read-back*. A ledger of twenty lines under six kinds of change yields more derived lines than a reader can use; which to show is a rule, and that rule is a catch-all needing its own gauge. Where the line is drawn is not decided; the prototype (step 2) is to find out whether the pull is real.
- *Translator freedom* against *checker fixedness*. Every marked guess makes a finding conditional. The gauge is the count of findings that lean on filled-in lines, already printed.
- *Scope width* against *the bin*. Widening scope empties the bin and adds promises to section 4. Each promise added is a forcing case owed.
- *Meaning preservation* against *usefulness*. The owner's word: this thread does not prioritise preserving meaning. The bin may hold what the semantics audit would keep; what matters here is that the lines that are written are checkable and the leftovers visible.

**Catch-alls and their gauges.** The bin: entries per sentence. "Cannot tell": count. "Not checked": count of unshaped verbs. The consequence filter, when it exists: derived lines shown against derived lines total. Each gauge must appear in every report.

**Where the parts came from** (the skill: fitted and borrowed first to check).
- *Fitted*: patches 4 and 5 (each forced by one paragraph; the log says so); the "final conclusion is the one argued for last" rule (patch 7).
- *Borrowed*: s(CASP) (rival: any other answer-set or Prolog engine; never set beside it); the shape book with four shapes (rival: none, or many; never set beside it); the pressing-slot theory (from the research at log 12).
- *Built*: the five parts of a line; the fourteen kinds; the laws; standing; told worlds; the what-if forms.
- *Asserted*: the exact read-back; the sameness test as a property.

## 8. What would show this scope is wrong
- A text of a kind in section 3 that the checker mishandles, and no patch fixes without breaking a job in section 4.
- A gauge that reads under threshold on a text built to hide a contradiction in the bin. (A hostile test owed; none run.)
- A finding in section 4 that is never reached on any text the owner brings, over a season: the promise is idle.
- Consequences that a reader cannot use: the filter swallows the ones that would have caught the writer out.
- Two ledgers of one text, by translators who have read no ledger, that differ in said-content on a text inside section 3.

## Traps
- Editing this file. Strikes and changes make L64.
- Reading a text as in scope because it is short. Section 3 is by kind, not by length.
- Reading the gauge without the threshold. A number with no line is decoration.
- Treating the excluded list as permanent. Each exclusion is a stated limit; removing one is a new claim with new forcing cases owed.
- Treating "constructed" as "correct". This contract can be wrong (file 10, Part III); exposing the defect is another question with its own contract.
