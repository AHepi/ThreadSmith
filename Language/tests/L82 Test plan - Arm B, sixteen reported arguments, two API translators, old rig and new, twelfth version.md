## Who does what, and what each is blind to
- **Text writer**: a Sonnet agent, from the brief in `tests/L82 Arm B corpus brief/` and a second brief (in the log, L83); it wrote ten passages in four matched pairs and two controls, then six more (a hedged pair, a narrator's-own passage, three rewordings), with a map of which is which; both maps are sealed (`MAP.enc`, `MAP2.enc`) and the orchestrator has not read them (the writer once rewrote the first map after it was sealed; its hash matched the seal and it was deleted unread). The pairs are visible to anyone who reads the texts (each pair shares its opening sentence), so the seal checks the writer's intent at marking, not the marker's blindness; every expectation below is stated by class and by observable features of the texts, never by the map.
- **Translators**: Atria (`Atria-Dawn-Preview`, 30 requests a minute) and Mimo (`mimo-v2.6-pro`, 100), each called once per text through `tools/translate_via_api_2.py`, which sends 39, L80, the encoding guide with three examples, and the text; a ledger that fails validation is sent back once with the failure. Neither sees the other's work, the plan, the map, or, within the run, any earlier ledger of these texts. Two earlier ledgers exist, made by the probes after the first run failed (P4): Atria's of B01 and Mimo's of B03, kept in `results/L82 Probes ...`. Each call is fresh and no probe output enters the run, so nothing carries over from them; the exposure is asymmetric (Atria saw B01, Mimo saw B03) and is stated beside the rows it touches, below the table.
- **Runner**: the orchestrator, by the script `tools/L82_run_2.sh` (P3, P11) with `tests/L82 Arm B corpus brief/PAIRS.txt`: translations in eight lanes per provider, streamed; both drivers on every valid ledger; `consequences_2.py` on every valid ledger; `sameness_2.py` within each pair per translator and across translators per text; a COUNTS file per ledger; every output kept.
- **Reader**: Atria, one fresh call per report through the reader brief in the brief folder, on all reports (old and new driver, every valid ledger; 64 if all 32 ledgers validate) in a shuffled order under neutral names, eight lanes. It is given the report with its heading line replaced, every world name replaced by `world_1`, `world_2`, ... in the headings and in the line texts' standing prefixes, and the leftover bin's quotations cut from the GAUGE line (P11). It never sees a passage file or a ledger file; it does see what the report prints, which includes the texts of the lines the report names, in the translator's words. It can tell an old-driver report from a new one by shape (the new has an OUTCOMES block and four extra gauge counts); the shuffle hides which ledger and which translator, not which driver.
- **Prose reader**: Mimo, one fresh call per new-driver report through the prose reader brief, given the passage and the report together, never a ledger, eight lanes; world names are left as the translator wrote them, because its part 3 asks whether the world matches where the passage puts the argument. - **Markers**: the orchestrator against this table; then a fresh Opus agent given this plan, the outputs and the opened maps, and nothing else; both markings kept. The maps are opened after both markings of B1 to B13 are drafted.

The marking rule, verbatim: "A difference between two translators, or between a translation and the prose, counts as an error only under one of the four, or on a prose reader's verdict that the ledger commits the writer to what the prose does not (a hedge made a fact; a claim the reported person never made). Everything else is variation, recorded in the sameness diagnostic and not scored. A shared error of the two translators shows only in the prose reader's verdict, never in the comparison between them; that is why the role exists." What counts as variation: "wording, ... the form of a line, ... standing where the text does not settle it, or ... extra Thing lines". On the hedge: "A translation that writes the hedged clause as a fact is wrong about the prose even though the hedge is marked in the bin ... This is a false finding produced by the language's silence on modality". Section 5, verbatim: "A text on which two ledgers give the same findings under all five changes in force and the prose reader says one is wrong about the prose: then the grain is too coarse and C is short a change, and the fix is a new claim at a new index (Part III), not an edit to this note. The hedged pair is built to reach this. Or a text where the variation test passes on both ledgers and the reports still disagree in kind: then the report, not the translation, is the variable, and the driver is charged."
- P8. `sameness_2.py` prints six headed buckets, each heading carrying its count: `BOTH SAY (n):`, `ONLY THE FIRST SAYS (n):`, `ONLY THE SECOND SAYS (n):`, `SAME CONTENT, DIFFERENT STANDING (n):`, `NEAR, JUDGE BY HAND (n, overlap >= 0.50):`, `OPPOSITE (n, a near pair with NOT on one side only):`, then a line `BIN ENTRIES: first n, second n (compare by reading)`. ## The marking rule, and where it lives
The rule is P7's, L81 second version section 3, applied by both markers: a difference is an error only under one of the four cases or on the prose reader's verdict; everything else is variation, recorded and not scored. Three things this plan adds to it, stated here and not only in the cells:
1. **The hedge override (B10).** On B12, a prose reader verdict of "hedged in the passage" against a because claim the ledger carries as a fact is charged to the language, not to the translation: 39 and L80 give the translator no way to keep a modal hedge (L64 sends "may" to the bin; L81 section 3, "a false finding produced by the language's silence on modality"). The override reaches only a hedge the language sends to the bin; a translator that keeps the hedge out of the world's claim by some other means is recorded, charged to nothing.
2. **Order within a row (B12, B11).** Where a row has a sameness half and a report half, the sameness half is read first and decides the charge of the report half: clean sameness with differing report kinds charges the driver (L81 section 5); unclean sameness charges the translator and the report half is recorded, not charged.
3. **Where two rows meet on one outcome (B4 and B13).** On a control, B4 governs: the ledger, not the prose reader's answer, decides the layer. A "not made in the passage" answer on a control where the ledger holds the unstated claim line confirms B4's translator charge; where the ledger holds no such line, the finding is the driver's under B4 and the prose reader's answer is recorded as agreeing with the ledger's reading, not as a second charge.
Thin or absent ledgers: a ledger B5 lists as unmarkable is removed from the numerator and the denominator of every row that counts it, and each row says what it was marked on.

## The probes' exposure, beside the rows it touches
Atria translated B01 and Mimo translated B03 once each in the probes (P4), on fresh calls whose outputs do not enter the run. A probe effect, if one existed, would be a translator doing differently on a text it had met than on one it had not, and the rows where that shape would show are: B1 (B03 is an F-side; Mimo's B03 ledger), B2 (B01 is an S-side; Atria's B01 ledger), B3 (the pairs B08/B01 for Atria and B03/B06 for Mimo compare a probed text against an unprobed partner within one translator), B7 (B01 and B03 are two of the thirteen texts, one per translator, which is the axis B7 measures) and B12 (B03/B11 for Mimo). No expectation changes; the marker lists, for each of these rows, whether the probed ledger sits with or against the others, and if a row fires only on a probed text the record says so and the charge stands with that note. Under Not tested: whether a fresh call is affected by an earlier call on the same text (nothing in either provider's interface carries state between calls).

## What is scored and what is only recorded
By L81: differences of wording, line form, standing where the text does not settle it, and Thing lines are recorded from the cross-translator sameness runs and not scored. What is scored is the report kind inside the world, the variation within pairs, the controls, the reader's trace, the prose reader's verdicts.

## Near-miss cases that must not fire
The four S-sides (B2), the two controls (B4), every old-driver report (B8), the three rewordings against their originals (B12), and B15 against B12 in the prose reader's answers (B10).

## Not tested
The world half of the split; rig 2; a reader other than Atria and a prose reader other than Mimo; the outside-line count at a nonzero value (no passage has a general line outside its world); which translation is right. Limits of the blinding: the reader sees the lines' texts the report prints (the translator's words for the passage), the neutral world names and the gauge counts; it can tell an old-driver report from a new one by shape; the prose reader sees the world names as written. A translator's bin entry or line text that names the passage would reach the reader through the line texts; the key records what was cut. Whether an earlier call on the same text affects a fresh call (the probes' exposure, above). The runner's start guard cannot see a caller in its own process group (P11), so the run is started from a launcher whose shell has launched none; a caller started from the same shell after the run began would not be seen.

- Reading B7's misses as the language failing. They charge 39's room for choice; B1 and B2 are where the language and the driver are charged.
