# L85 Note for the owner - what Arm B says about vaguely incorrect translations, and the seven decisions it leaves

Written 23 September 2026 after L84, for the owner's review. Facts are from `results/L84 Test results - Arm B ...` and the two markings beside it; the readings marked "Claude's" are mine and are not the record.

## The question, and the answer the run gives
The owner asked, at decision L11, to keep in view whether vaguely incorrect translations are acceptable and when. L81 (second version) gave the rule Arm B was marked by: a translation that differs in wording, line form, unsettled standing or Thing lines is variation; one that is as vague as the prose is right; one that makes a fact of a hedge, or a claim of what was never claimed, is wrong. Arm B tested it on sixteen passages with two outside translators and found three things.

1. **Vague translations are the normal case, and the checker's report absorbs them.** The same model, given identical sentences in separate calls, wrote different lines every time (different ids and atoms, Thing lines added or dropped, sometimes a different fact from the same sentence); yet the checker's finding kinds inside the world were identical on every rewording pair and the two models agreed on twelve of thirteen texts. At the line grain the ledger is unstable; at the report's grain it is stable. So a vaguely different translation is acceptable when the report does not move, and the report did not move. (B3, B7, B12; findings 3.)

2. **A translation as vague as the prose is right, and the language then reports a fault the prose does not carry.** Both translators kept the hedge "may have been left open" out of the claim, as the language tells them to (the word goes to the bin, the claim is written flat), and the checker reported a JUMP against a writer who claimed only a possibility; the prose reader saw it on all four reports. The same shape appeared on "she did not think it was because of" and "he had concluded the flock must be": the told world keeps who said it and not how strongly. This is L81 section 5's case: the grain is too coarse and the contract is short a change. (B10, B13; findings 4 and 5.)

3. **A translation faithful to the words can fail the check for want of a join the prose makes by sense.** On the two texts built to show the checker a rule that makes the argument sound, both translators wrote the rule as a general line, and neither wrote the bridge from the rule's terms to the case's (that the trawler is "a loaded boat at that harbour"; that a gate "standing open" is the shepherd's having "left it open"). The language matches atoms; the prose joins senses; the guide leaves the join to the translator's filled-in lines, and both models, twice each, did not fill it while filling in elsewhere. The checker then reported on the sound side what it reported on the faulty side, and the prose reader pointed at the rule the report never mentioned. (B2, B13; findings 1 and 2.)

Claude's reading, not the record: (1) says the report, not the ledger, is the unit the calibration should be stated at, which is what file 11's grain argument says; (2) and (3) are the two places the language is silent where the prose is not, modality and sense-identity, and both are contract changes (Part III) rather than translator errors.
