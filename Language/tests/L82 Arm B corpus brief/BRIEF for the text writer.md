# Brief for the text writer of Arm B (L82)

You are writing the texts for a test. You have not seen this project and must not look for it. Write ten short prose passages in plain modern English, each three to six sentences, numbered 1 to 10, as ordinary prose a reader might meet in a report, a letter, a news item or a memoir: not puzzles, not fixtures, no proper names reused across passages. The passages test whether a checker can find, inside a **report of someone else's argument**, whether that argument connects. So every passage contains a report: someone (an inspector, a committee, a neighbour, an account, a witness, a memo) is said to have argued or concluded something, and the report gives their reasoning. Four matched pairs and two controls:

- **Pair A (a cause claim).** Two passages alike in setting. In A-faulty, the reported person claims that one thing happened BECAUSE of another, and the report gives no line of theirs that would make the cause produce the effect (the claim is bare). In A-sound, the same report, but the reported person also states a general rule of their own that makes the cause produce the effect ("whenever ..., ..."; "... always ..."). The two passages differ in that one sentence and nowhere else that matters.
- **Pair B (a cause claim, denied version).** B-faulty: the report says the person claims X because Y, and later the same report says the person denies that X was because of Y (the same X and Y). B-sound: the report says the person claims X because Y, and later denies that X was because of Z, a different cause. Same setting.
- **Pair C (a reason to expect).** C-faulty: the reported person concludes something SINCE something else ("she concluded the visitor had returned, since the lamp was burning"), and nothing in the report connects the reason to the conclusion. C-sound: the same, plus the person's own stated general connection ("a lamp burning in that house always meant someone was home").
- **Pair D (a plan inside a report).** D-faulty: the report says the person planned an action so that an aim would follow, and gives no route from the action to the aim. D-sound: the same, plus the person's stated route ("closing the valve stops the flow, and the flow is what floods the cellar").
- **Controls E1 and E2.** Two passages with a report in them that contains no argument at all: the reported person describes, lists, or narrates; no because, since, therefore, so that, or plan.

Rules: the argument is always the reported person's, inside the report, never the narrator's own; the narrator may add one plain fact of their own per passage. Keep each passage self-contained. Do not label which passage is which pair or side in the passage text. Do not use the words "faulty" or "sound" anywhere in the passages. Vary the settings (a harbour, a school, a farm, a court, a laboratory, a kitchen, a railway, a hospital, a library, a shop). Plain words; no metaphor that would need unpacking.

Output, exactly: a file `TEXTS.md` with the ten passages under headings `## Passage 1` to `## Passage 10`, in a shuffled order you choose; and a separate file `MAP.json` with, for each passage number, its pair letter (A, B, C, D or E) and side ("faulty", "sound", or "control"), plus, for the faulty side, the number of the sentence carrying the bare claim, the denial, the SINCE, or the plan. Nothing else in either file.
