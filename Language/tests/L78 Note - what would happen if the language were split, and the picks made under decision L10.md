# L78 Note - what would happen if the language were split, and the picks made under decision L10

The owner's words on the three questions of L77 (decision L10): on the first, "pick something and see what happens. You keep a record of historical versions, so reversal only costs a lesson"; on the second, "This is a scope question. What would happen if the language was split." This note answers the second and records the picks made on the first and third. Written 22 September, before anything is built; the premises it leans on are quoted with their files.

## What "split" means here
One ledger, two questions. The ledger language (38) writes down what a text commits its writer to. The checker now asks two kinds of question of that one ledger and prints them in one report as if they were one kind:
- **the commitment question**: what has the writer committed to, and do those commitments hang together? Contradiction, connection (SINCE), circle, added lines alone, a plan as stated, what a what-if leaves standing *when the writer's own causes are taken at their word*, and what follows from the lines (consequences.py). Rig 1 asks these.
- **the world question**: could the stated cause produce the stated effect, under the laws the language holds (the laws of pressing, rig 2's `laws.pl`) and the general lines the writer gave? JUMP as check 2 prints it ("nothing in the ledger produces"), the strength and direction slots, "cannot tell" on a thing whose response is not stated, and a what-if answered by what would in fact happen. Rig 2 asks these, and rig 1's check 2 asks half of one.
File 11 says these are different questions and an answer to one is not an answer to the other: "Two questions with the same D and different (C, Q) are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other. A measure that identifies an outcome, with a reliable prediction from it, answers the identification question; whether the measured part also produces the outcome is the production question, and the first answer is not the second" (**read**, `Semantics/authority/11 ...md`, Part III, "The respect is the query"). The commitment question is the identification question of the writer's ledger; the world question is the production question.

## What would happen, item by item
1. **Finding 2 stops being a fault and becomes a declared scope.** In the commitment language a what-if answers: "taking your stated causes at their word, does the asked fact survive the change?" The tie on P14 is then not an error but the definition, printed as such ("set aside on your line h, which the world check above did not establish"). In the world language the same what-if answers "cannot tell" until a law or general line produces the effect. One what-if, two verdicts, each labelled. That is the honest form of what P14 showed, and it is what Astra Pro's audit proposed in one sentence (L74, finding D).
2. **F09 is resolved instead of chosen.** L64 section 6 left F09 open because the fix in the what-if rule gives up "after" as a plain fact and the fix in the translator gives up an honest translation of "after". Under the split, "the box moved after the push" stands in the commitment language (the writer said it) and reads "cannot tell" in the world language (nothing ties it to the push). Neither layer changes; the report says which question each answer belongs to.
3. **Finding 1 gets a clean rule.** The commitment language asks every one of its checks inside a told world and a supposed case, looked at alone, because a report's commitments are commitments (the account says the window caused the dryness: does the account's own argument connect?). The world language never enters a told world: a story has no physics the language can vouch for, which is what 38 line 149 already warns ("a told world that leans on everyday background ... is looked at alone, so general lines from the actual ledger are not available inside it"). So the pick on question 1 follows from the split rather than being a guess.
4. **JUMP is split in two.** "No line you gave produces this" is a commitment finding (the argument does not connect on your own lines). "No law produces this" is a world finding. Today check 2 prints one word for both and rig 2 prints its own. The report would say which.
5. **L64 changes shape, not content.** Sections 3 and 4 partition the promised findings by question; section 6's checker row becomes two rows; the four sources of error become five, or the checker row is read twice. The threshold and gauge questions are untouched.
6. **38 splits into two parts with one line format, or two documents; 39 does not change.** One ledger feeds both questions. The translator's task is the same; only the TOLD content is marked as belonging to the commitment question alone.
7. **The report carries two sections, and the read-back test is run on both.** The pull that creates: a reader wants one answer. Under file 11 the two answers are two questions and giving one as the other is the identification-as-production error (Part V, "a reversed calculation"). The read-back test decides whether readers can carry two.
8. **The instruments sort themselves.** consequences.py belongs to the commitment question and may pool nothing across worlds; the variation test of L64 line 143 is the translator's; the gauge serves both; sameness.py compares ledgers, which are the same under both.
9. **What is given up.** One verdict per finding. A shorter report. And the world language will be silent on most prose, because most prose states no laws; that silence is already rig 2's and would now be labelled as what it is rather than folded into NO FAULT FOUND.
10. **What would show the split is wrong.** A text on which the two verdicts cannot be stated apart without rewriting the ledger; or a read-back run in which a reader given the two-section report traces fewer findings than with the one-section report; or a finding kind that belongs to neither question.

## The picks (decision L10: "pick something and see what happens")
- **Question 1.** Inside a told world and inside a supposed case, every commitment check is asked, the world looked at alone. A finding inside a world that would need a line from outside the world is not reported; it is counted, and the count is printed in the gauge. Reversal costs: the count and the extra report lines, and any false alarms the run shows.
- **Question 2.** The split is adopted for rig 1 as the commitment language: a what-if answers the ledger with the writer's stated causes taken at their word, and prints every line set aside, the BECAUSE that tied it, that BECAUSE's own verdict from the checks above, and the note wherever a line used was filled in. World what-ifs are rig 2's and are not built in this round. Reversal costs: the two-section report.
- **Question 3.** NO FAULT FOUND is replaced by named outcomes: for each check, "asked, nothing found", or "not asked: the ledger has no line of that kind", or "ran out of time"; the header line goes. Reversal costs: readers who liked one line.
Each pick is a new version beside the old; the old stays runnable; a reversal is a lesson and a further version, as the owner said.

## Traps
- Reading "split" as two rigs. There are already two rigs; the split is by question, and rig 1's check 2 straddles it.
- Building the world half now. This round builds the commitment half and labels the other; the laws are rig 2's and untouched.
