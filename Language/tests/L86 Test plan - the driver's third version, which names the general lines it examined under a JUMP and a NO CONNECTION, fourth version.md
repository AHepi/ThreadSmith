## What is built (a new file beside the old, with a give-up line)
`rigs/rig 1 - arguments/patched/run_check_3.py`, made from `run_check_2.py` (P1) with these changes and no others; its header names this plan's hash and each D with its give-up line. "General line" and "general clause" below are this plan's terms and are not the code's `general_lines` (P1: the actual ledger's ALWAYS and USUALLY lines that a world lacks, lines 200 to 202), which is unchanged.
- **D1. A general clause, defined for the driver.** From the ledger's `.pl` text as the checker is given it (P1's `ledger_text`), with every `%` comment removed to its line's end first, a clause is the text up to a full stop followed by white space or the end of the text, joined across line breaks. A clause `Head :- Body` whose head is `produced(T)` or `holds(T)`, whose body contains a goal `line(<id>)` and at least one goal other than it, is a general clause of line `<id>`; it is a MAKES clause when the head is `produced` and a SHOWS clause when it is `holds`. Body goals are the body split at commas outside parentheses. Inside a world, or with lines removed, a clause of a removed line is not a general clause of that run (its `line(<id>)` fact is gone from the text the checker sees: P1's `run_query`). (39, the guide and both translators write MAKES and SHOWS this way: P2; ledger A's line 5 and ledger E's u1 and u2: P4.)
- **D2. Terms and matching.** A term is a name, a variable (a token beginning with an upper-case letter or an underscore), or `name(term, ...)`; both the checker's effect string (printed without spaces, `in_field(flock,top_field)`) and a clause's head term are parsed as terms. A head term matches an effect when the two unify: equal names and arity throughout, a variable binding to the sub-term at its position, a constant equal only to itself; the bindings are the head's variables. A goal with the bindings applied is printed as written in the ledger's clause, spaces and all, with each bound variable replaced by its sub-term printed as the checker prints it.
- **D3. Peeking without leaking.** The build adds `peek(query, removed)`, which calls `run_query(ledger_text, query, removed, world_removed)` with no `extra` (so a JUMP's supposed cause is not granted to the goals) and the same `world_removed` the battery was given, and returns the answers and whether the checker timed out; the goal peeks of D5 pass `removed=direct` (the list the verdict's `produced` asks used, P1 lines 284 and 310), those of D6 `removed=direct` likewise (line 342's `routes` ask), and the route peek of D6 `removed=direct` too; it touches none of `slowest`, `report`, `first_raw`, `out_of_time`, `current` and the raw log. So the gauge's slowest question, the OUTCOMES rows and the `RAN OUT OF TIME` line are what the second version prints.
- **D4. The goal line.** For each body goal other than `line(<id>)`, with the bindings applied: if a variable remains, `<goal>: open (<the remaining variables in order of first appearance, joined by ", ">)`; else if `peek("<goal>", direct)` timed out, `<goal>: the checker ran out of time`; else if it returned an answer, `<goal>: holds (line <ids>)`, the ids being the first answer's lines in the checker's order (the order `ask` gives, P1: `sorted(set(lines), key=str)`), joined by `, `; else `<goal>: no line says so`. Judged in that order.
- **D5. Under a JUMP**, appended to `text` immediately after the `listed_lines(meta, "Effect's direct lines", direct)` statement (P1, line 318) and before the abduction that may print `A single line that would close the jump, which nobody wrote:` and `Lines that would also close it but are ruled out, ...` (lines 320 to 329): the block `\n  General lines that could produce <effect>, and what each needs:` followed, for every general clause whose head term matches the effect, in the order the clauses stand in the ledger text (a line with several matching clauses gets one entry per clause, each with the same `- line` header), by `\n    - line <id> [<mark>, sentence <N>]: <text>` (the `describe_lines` row, P1) and one `\n        <goal line>` per body goal (D4), and, for a SHOWS clause, one more line `\n        a SHOWS clause: it shows <effect> and does not produce it`. When no general clause matches: `\n  General lines that could produce <effect>: (none)`. No blank line inside the block.
- **D6. Under a NO CONNECTION**, appended to `text` immediately after the `listed_lines(meta, "Expected's direct lines", direct)` statement (line 355) and before the `It would take a line nobody wrote` line (359): first `\n  Reached without the reason:` followed by `\n    - lines <ids>` per route (the ids in the checker's order, joined by `, `; an answer of `peek("holds(<expected>)", direct)` whose lines contain none of `reason_lines`; routes with the same ids are one; listed in the order of their joined strings), or `\n  Reached without the reason: (no route)` when there is none; then the D5 block with `reach` for `produce`, over general clauses of either kind whose head term matches the expectation (no SHOWS note), each clause's goals followed by `\n        the reason's atom, <reason>, is among these conditions` when the reason's term equals, with spaces removed, the inner term of any body goal whose goal is `holds(T)` or `produced(T)` (or the whole goal otherwise), else `\n        the reason's atom, <reason>, is not among these conditions`. When no clause matches: `\n  General lines that could reach <expected>: (none)`.
- **D7. Indentation and the world.** The block's lines are written with two, four and eight leading spaces as above; inside a world section `indented()` (P1, line 176) adds two more, as it does to every finding. The blocks are printed inside told worlds and supposed cases as under the actual ledger (the check runs there too, L79's D2); the goal peeks there use the world's `world_removed`. The count line `N findings inside '<world>' would not stand if the actual ledger's general lines were available` (P1, line 603) is computed on the findings with the two blocks removed by the rule of `L86_strip.py` (P7), applied to each finding text before the two runs are compared, so the count keeps the second version's meaning; the blocks themselves stay in the printed section. The blocks are built in both of a world's `battery` runs (the second, `restored`, run builds them and they are stripped before the comparison; the cost is the goal peeks of that run).
- **D8. Nowhere else.** Neither block is printed under FOLLOWS, FOLLOWS ONLY IF THE CAUSE IS GRANTED, CIRCLE, TO BE EXPECTED, CANNOT TELL, a plan verdict, a what-if, or in an OUTCOMES block.
- **Give-up lines.** D1: a general clause with another head (`depends`, `changes`, `denied`) is not examined, nor a bare fact; a full stop inside a quoted atom would split a clause (none of the 112 ledgers has one). D2: a head that is not a term the parser reads (none in hand) is not examined. D3: one checker run per goal (time, E6). D4: a goal the checker does not know returns `no line says so`. D5, D6: report length. D7: none. D8: none.
