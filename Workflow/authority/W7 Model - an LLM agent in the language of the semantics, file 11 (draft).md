## 1. The question the model answers, and the indices it is relative to

**The question** (W3 section 1). Which of the theory's objects does an agent built on a large language model instantiate at one call, which across calls, which only with a record or a harness around it, and which never; so that the skills the workflow needs, and the boundaries between them, can be drawn from it. The owner's six unknowns Q1 to Q6 are quoted in W3 section 1 and are the given jobs; each builder's added jobs are listed in section 2 under its slice, tagged as the builder's own, and a part held only by an added job is held if that job is real (file 33, Step 2).

**The grain, the boundary, the continuity: declared before any attribution.** File 11, Part XIV: "Grain \(\ell\), boundary \(\beta\), continuity \(\Omega\), and the contract \(C\) are declared indices. Every claim is relative to them; none is a predicate that could be true or false." (10 and 11). Part XII: "A capability is attributed to a system under a declared boundary (which processes and resources are the system's) and a declared continuity \(\Omega\) (what makes it the same system through change). ... Both are declared before the attribution, not chosen after it." (11 only). The grain is text (part A2 says what organization that adopts). The boundary: the system is the reader together with the program that builds and hands it its context; the record, the corpus and the owner are outside it; the skill's text once in the context is inside, and the work of writing it is outside (part B1). The continuity: what persists from one execution to the next, which differs by arm of phase 4 and is declared there (part B1).

**The unit.** One request to the language model is one execution; the context is what it reads and the emission what it writes (part A1). An agent turn with tool calls and a session are several executions; "across calls" below means across requests.

### A1 (M1 restated, first clause) A call is one execution, delimited by the context it reads in whole

**Statement (builder A, the call).** One request to the language model is one execution: the smallest unit at which what is read is a fixed set of passages. The context is the occurrences that execution reads; the emission is the occurrences it writes. An agent turn with tool calls, and a session, are not executions in this sense, because each re-posts a changed context and so is several executions; the rig's own record counts them separately.

- rests on [10 and 11; Part XII — The physical module]: "A substrate is a physical system; an attribute a set of its states; a task a permitted input-to-output attribute transformation with explicit resources and side effects."
- rests on [10 and 11; Part IV — Layers, transports, and provenance]: "An **occurrence** is a physically located carrier."
- rests on [10 and 11; Part XIV — The class collected]: "Grain \(\ell\), boundary \(\beta\), continuity \(\Omega\), and the contract \(C\) are declared indices. Every claim is relative to them; none is a predicate that could be true or false."

**Construction.** construction, built from: Part XII's task sentence (which gives an execution as one input-to-output transformation) and Part IV's occurrence sentence (which gives what is read and written). File 11 contains neither "call" nor "context window"; the join is the model's.

**Mark.** held, jointly with A2. **Provenance.** built. **Holder.** "A substrate is a physical system; an attribute a set of its states; a task a permitted input-to-output attribute transformation with explicit resources and side effects." ### A5 (M2 restated under file 11) The weights' organization admits a selected transport whose population is the set the physics and the stated construction admit

**Statement (builder A, the call).** The organization the weights instantiate admits a transport with selected provenance on the first three clauses of Part IV's definition: the population is the set of transports the physics and the stated construction admit, which for a language model is the architecture and training recipe as stated; the variation operator is the training update; the survival condition is fidelity on a finite history of context-and-continuation pairs actually encountered. ### A10 (added) The primitive layer and the simulation layer at the grain of text: a construction, and unknown

**Statement (builder A, the call).** Part IV's primitive layer has a counterpart at the grain of text: an organization whose ports are the items a document keeps identity as through rewording and displacement — a claim, a part, a speaker, a quotation — whose components are their continuity relations, and whose admitted edits are the textual counterparts of displacement (move a passage), occlusion (withhold one) and re-identification (exchange two labels). The simulation layer's counterpart is the organization whose queries are predictions about what such an item will take under an admitted edit, which is what the skill's poke asks for. Whether a reader instantiates either layer is not settled at this grain; what can be seen is whether the emission moves under the three edits, and the record shows it failing at re-identification.

- rests on [10 and 11 (reworded); Part IV — Layers, transports, and provenance]: "The **primitive layer** \(P\) is an organization whose ports are persistent things with boundaries and identity, whose components are their continuity relations, and whose admitted edits include displacement, occlusion and re-identification."
- rests on [10 and 11; Part IV — Layers, transports, and provenance]: "The **simulation layer** \(S\) is an organization over \(P\) whose components are dependencies among things and whose queries are predictions: what a port of \(P\) will take under an admitted edit. \(S\) is where expectation lives."
- rests on [10 and 11; Part IV — Layers, transports, and provenance]: "Neither layer is presupposed to exist in any particular physical system. The semantics describes what it is for them to exist and to be connected."
- (and 2 more sentence(s) in the builder's return)

**Construction.** construction, built from: Part IV's two layer paragraphs, Derivation 10's exact form with its four edits (displace, set velocity, occlude, swap identities), and Part X's port sentence, which says that adding the re-identification edit to the account is itself construction.
