### 4.3 A send, an answer, a fault
- **A send** is one call of the client's `call`: one request, whatever number of attempts the client makes inside it (the clients' README, "How to count requests"). Its tag is `W16-<family>-<lens>-s1`, and `-s2` for the one re-send.
- **An answer** is a send that ends with HTTP 200, a `content` that is not empty once whitespace is stripped, and either finish reason `stop`, or no finish reason at all with the stream's closing `data: [DONE]` line among its raw lines (both clients keep that line; the second case is recorded as such under G4). Only `content` is the answer; `reasoning_content` is kept and never read as points.
- **A ceiling fault** is a 200 whose `content` is empty, or whose finish reason is `length` (the clients' `truncated`) or `repetition_truncation` (MiMo's `repetition_truncated`). It is never read as an answer, not even in part. The request is re-sent once as `-s2`, at the same maximum ceiling, since neither ceiling can go higher, and the fault is recorded with its finish reason, its usage and the length of its `reasoning_content`.
- **Any other fault** (a 200 with no finish reason and no `[DONE]` line, which is a stream cut off for a cause the record does not show; an HTTP status the client does not retry; the client's seven attempts spent; a `content_filter` finish; a refusal for size): never read as an answer; recorded with the record the client carries out on the exception, or the record it returned, and re-sent once as `-s2`.
- **Two sends at most per request.** A request with no answer after `-s2` has none; it is named, and W16.1 fails on it. Nothing further is sent under this plan.
- A re-send is made only after its first send has ended, and counts against its family's cap like any send.

## 5. How each point is disposed of

### 5.1 The unit
A point is a numbered item of an answer, as the examiner numbered it; its id is `<family>-<lens>-<n>`. An answer that is not in numbered points is split by the first disposer into points, one per claim, and the split is recorded as the disposer's. A point that makes several claims is disposed of claim by claim, and the point stands if at least one of its claims stands.

### 5.2 The quotation check, by program
Before any disposition, a program, `questioning/quotes_w16.py`, looks for each point's Target quotation in the draft as questioned and records the draft line it sits on. Both texts are normalised the same way first: Unicode NFKC; every `*` and backtick removed; the curly quotation marks made straight; the en dash, the em dash and the minus sign made a hyphen; every run of whitespace made one space. The quotation marks round the quotation, and a trailing ellipsis, are dropped. A quotation is found when it is a substring of the normalised draft. A quotation with an ellipsis (`...` or `…`) inside it is split there, and is found when every piece of twelve characters or more is found, in order, within one line of the draft. A point with no Target quotation is not found. The line of the draft on which a found quotation begins is the point's target line (W16.8, G2).

### 5.3 The first disposition
One Opus 5.5 worker per lens, at effort xhigh (decision W15), armed with the skill, all eight files, and the qualified Derivation 3 (W18), disposes of every point under its lens from both families: three workers. With the three of 5.4, never more than five run at a time. Each worker has the repository, the marks, the independent check's outputs and `stage-B/results-w15/` (in the working tree at freezing, not yet committed). Every point gets one of three dispositions, with what each requires:
- **stands**: the claim was checked and holds. With the evidence (a file and line, or a program run on the marks, its code and output kept in `dispositions/programs/`) and the edit it forces in W15, written as the words to change.
- **refuted**: the claim was checked and does not hold. With the evidence that shows it (a file and line, or a program and its output).
- **not checkable**: with which of three reasons: the file the check needs does not exist; the claim is not about W15's text (it is about the theory, the skill, or a frozen text as such, and is recorded as a question for the owner); or nothing in hand could tell the claim from its denial.

A point that attacks a claim W15 does not make, or asks for what W15 already says, is refuted by quoting W15's sentence. A point arguing that a frozen text (W14, W10, the marking plan, `criteria.json`) is wrong stands only as far as it shows that W15 misreads or misstates that text; a change to the frozen text itself is outside this round and is recorded as a question for the owner. Where a standing point touches a count, the count is recomputed by a program on the marks, and the disposer says whether the edit moves a certification or a prediction verdict (W16.2). Each disposer writes `dispositions/first-L<n>.json`, one object per point (id, family, lens, target quotation, found, target line, claims, disposition, evidence, edit forced, what it moves, and for G3 whether the point bears on a place its lens text names), with a readable `.md` beside it, and counts nothing.

### 5.4 The second disposition, blind
Three more Opus 5.5 workers at xhigh, armed the same way, one per lens, dispose of the same points by the same rule without seeing the first disposition, and write `dispositions/second-L<n>.json` in the same form. Added by this plan, and held by one job: W16.3 and W16.4 count the first disposer's verdicts on criticism of the project's own draft, so a first disposer who refuted too readily would make W16.4 pass and W15 look tight. The skill's trap "passing your own test" names it, and W16.7 is its count.

### 5.5 Disagreements, and the governing disposition
A program lists every point the two dispositions mark differently. Where they agree, their disposition governs. Where they differ, the orchestrator rules after reading both with their evidence, and writes each ruling in `dispositions/rulings.md` as the orchestrator's, with its reason. Points that a disposer judges to make the same claim on the same target line are listed together, and a set of such points disposed of differently is ruled once.

### 5.6 The tally, and W15's final text
A program, `questioning/tally_w16.py`, runs only once all six disposition files and the rulings exist. It writes `tally.txt` and `tally.json`: every count of section 6, per family, from the governing dispositions, with W16.3, W16.4 and W16.7 also given from the first and from the second disposition alone, beside. Then W15 is finished. Only the edits of standing points are made. Its final text carries a section, "The questioning (plan W16)", which lists every standing point by id with its target and the edit made, ticks W16.1 to W16.8 from `tally.txt`, and names the draft as questioned (2.1) as the text the points were raised against. The draft as questioned stays in the rig. Log entry W12 records the round.

def sha(b):

def blocks(plan_text):
    """Every fenced block whose opening fence is FENCE + 'w16:<name>', taken from the line after
    the opening fence to the line before the closing fence, exactly."""
    out, lines, i = {}, plan_text.split("\n"), 0
    while i < len(lines):
        if lines[i].startswith(FENCE + "w16:"):
            name = lines[i][len(FENCE):].strip()
            j = i + 1
            while j < len(lines) and lines[j] != FENCE:
                j += 1
            if j == len(lines):
                refuse(f"the block {name} is not closed")
            if name in out:
                refuse(f"the block {name} appears twice")
            out[name] = "\n".join(lines[i + 1:j])
            i = j + 1
        else:
            i += 1
    if sorted(out) != sorted(BLOCKS):
        refuse(f"the plan's blocks are {sorted(out)}, not {sorted(BLOCKS)}")
    for name, text in out.items():
        if not text.strip() or FENCE in text:
            refuse(f"the block {name} is empty or carries a fence")
    return out


def read(repo, path):
    p = os.path.join(repo, path)
    if not os.path.isfile(p):
        refuse(f"{path} is not in the repository")
    return open(p, "rb").read()


def with_newline(text):
    return text if text.endswith("\n") else text + "\n"

    system, users, messages, sources = build(repo, open(plan, encoding="utf-8").read())
    for name in KEY_ENV:
        v = os.environ.get(name, "")
        if len(v) >= 12 and (v in system or any(v in u for u in users.values())):
            refuse(f"a built message contains the value of {name}")
    got = {"system": sha(system.encode("utf-8"))}
    for L in LENSES:
        got[f"user-{L}"] = sha(users[L].encode("utf-8"))
        got[f"messages-{L}"] = sha(json.dumps(messages[L], ensure_ascii=False).encode("utf-8"))
    rows = [("system", system)] + [(f"user-{L}", users[L]) for L in LENSES]
    for k, text in rows:
        print(f"{k:12} {len(text):>9,} characters {len(text.encode('utf-8')):>9,} bytes  sha256 {got[k]}")
    for L in LENSES:
        m = json.dumps(messages[L], ensure_ascii=False)
        chars = len(system) + len(users[L])
        print(f"messages-{L:3} {len(m.encode('utf-8')):>9,} bytes as posted  sha256 {got[f'messages-{L}']}"
              f"  (system + user: {chars:,} characters; about {chars // 4:,} to {chars // 3:,} tokens"
              f" at four to three characters a token)")
    print("both families: the same message list per lens, by construction (one list per lens)")
    if EXPECTED:
        bad = [k for k in got if EXPECTED.get(k) != got[k]]
        if bad or sorted(EXPECTED) != sorted(got):
            refuse(f"built messages differ from the plan's frozen hashes: {bad or sorted(EXPECTED)}")
        print("every hash equals the one the plan froze")
    else:
        print("no frozen hashes in this copy: nothing compared")
    if out:
        os.makedirs(out, exist_ok=True)
        files = {"system.txt": system}
        for L in LENSES:
            files[f"user-{L}.txt"] = users[L]
            files[f"messages-{L}.json"] = json.dumps(messages[L], ensure_ascii=False)
        files["manifest.json"] = json.dumps({"plan": plan, "sources": sources, "sha256": got},
                                            ensure_ascii=False, indent=1) + "\n"
        for name, text in files.items():
            with open(os.path.join(out, name), "w", encoding="utf-8", newline="") as f:
                f.write(text)
        print(f"written: {len(files)} files to {out}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
