## 2. The one claim change, and what it does to M2

File 11's Derivation 3 now claims: "Let \(t\) be selected from a population \(\mathcal T\) on a finite history \(H\subsetneq C\). For every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) at which some \(t'\in\mathcal T\), also surviving on \(H\), has a different value from \(t\), the value of \(t\) at \((a,b)\) is underdetermined by \(H\): survival on \(H\) does not distinguish \(t\) from \(t'\) there. The presence of an unseen pair alone does not establish that such a \(t'\) exists; it must be admitted, realizable, a member of \(\mathcal T\), and a survivor of \(H\)." Its consequence: "A correspondence produced by selection is faithful where it was tested and, wherever its population admits an alternative, unconstrained where it was not." And what the theory itself gives up: "What the qualification gives up is the guarantee of a differing survivor at every unseen pair, and with it the blanket claim that every untested value is unconstrained; a population restriction, a physical relation, or another stated constraint may already fix a value that the history never tested." Part IV now adds to the definition of a selected transport: "The population \(\mathcal T\) is part of the claim: what \(H\) leaves open about \(t\) is what \(\mathcal T\) leaves open (Derivation 3)." And Part XII: "The population is the set of transports the physics and the stated construction admit; a transport that would need a part every member of the population is built without is not in it."

This is the qualification the W2 file-10 judge said M2 needed ("a parametric family with tied weights is exactly the shape file 10 names as what would refute Derivation 3"), and the Semantics record reached it from its own cases (O48, logs S70 to S75) and noted the convergence with that judge's finding (log S73). **M2 is restated:**

**M2 (under file 11). The organization the weights instantiate admits a selected transport, and its population is the parameter family the architecture admits.** Part IV's definition, now with the population as part of the claim. Training fits the first three clauses as W3 says (the parameter settings reached; the update; the loss condition); the fourth clause stays *unknown* with its check named. \(H\) and \(C\) are as W3 says. The population \(\mathcal T\) is the set of transports the architecture's parameter family can instantiate; a behaviour the family cannot carry at any setting is not in it. *What follows* is the qualified Derivation 3: at an unseen pair, the reader's value is unconstrained by its history where the family admits a differing survivor there, and fixed by the family where it does not. So the model no longer says a reader is unconstrained off its history; it says the history settles nothing there, and the family may. What phase 4 measures across arms is then read two ways: a difference between arms at an unseen pair is a fact about the history or the family, and the plan cannot tell which at the grain of text; agreement is a fact about the contract (Derivation 2), as W3 says. The paragraph in W3's M2 on blindness is cut: file 11's Part 0 says a selected correspondence is "produced by variation and survival on a history of encountered changes", and the word is gone. Attack point (D) is now: "a selected transport whose value at an unseen change is determined by its history although its population admits a differing survivor there (against Derivation 3; a population with no such survivor is the theorem's own qualification, not a refutation)". *Mark:* held if (the fourth clause; the independence premise, which file 11 keeps as the proof's first sentence), provenance built. *Would count against it:* a member of a training history shown to represent the survival condition; or a showing that the parameter family fixes a value at an unseen pair that phase 4 reads as the history's.

What this gives up: W3's ground for saying nothing about a reader off its history was the unqualified consequence; that sentence is gone from the theory and from the model. Q2's and Q4's answers rest on retention (M5) and on the carrier (M7), not on Derivation 3, and are untouched.

## 3. Sentences of file 11 that hold parts W3 had marked as constructions or held if

Each is quoted with its Part, and the mark it changes. None of these changes what W3 predicts; each changes what holds a part.

- **The declared indices (W3 section 4, before M1).** Part XII, "System boundary and continuity": "A capability is attributed to a system under a declared boundary (which processes and resources are the system's) and a declared continuity \(\Omega\) (what makes it the same system through change). A replaced part that preserves the declared continuity leaves the same system; a process run inside the boundary is the system's whoever wrote it; a process run outside it is not the system's however close it sits. Both are declared before the attribution, not chosen after it." And Part XIV, "Declared inputs", names "the system boundary and continuity of an attribution (Part XII)" among the inputs the semantics records and does not supply, with: "A verdict that depends on one of these is a verdict given the input; where the input is missing, the verdict is unsettled and the semantics says so rather than choosing the input from the verdict wanted." W3's two declarations now rest on these sentences rather than on Part XIV's list of indices alone. Mark unchanged (declared inputs); the passage that requires them is now quoted.
- **M5's construction.** Part XII now says: "Ownership is grounded in the processes and resources the boundary includes, never in the capability being attributed", and continues that owned-because-it-can and can-because-owned grounds neither. So the stateful/stateless definition (the constructor attribute as the states carrying the represented targets of unfinished work) stays a construction, and the boundary it is read under is a declared input, not a fact about the agent. Mark unchanged: construction, held if by P4.1 and P4.2.
- **M9 and the premise of the skill map ("a skill is a carrier of the theory's operations").** Part X, "Ownership": "The subhistory in Build is owned by \(s\) when its processes run inside the system boundary and resource contract declared for \(s\) (Part XII). Work supplied from outside that boundary, a diagnosis, a decisive question, an instruction about what to read, remains an outside contribution however it is executed inside; a process that runs inside the boundary is the system's own today whoever wrote it; and where the boundary is drawn decides, not where the process sits in the casing. Ownership is not defined by the capability it is meant to ground (Part XII)." A skill's router table is "an instruction about what to read". So M9's claim that where an attention policy sits is decided by where the boundary is drawn is now on the theory's own words, and the skill map's premise has a passage: a skill carried as text inside the boundary is the system's own today whoever wrote it, and the same skill supplied from outside is an outside contribution. Mark of M9 unchanged (held if, by P4.6); the premise of section 6 stays a construction, now with this passage among those that permit it.
- **M4, M6 and arm (c).** Part IV: "A carrier keeps its provenance when present access to it is lost, and a later record derived from the carrier is not a second, independent witness to its history." A summary written from a transcript is a record derived from the carrier; it does not witness the history the transcript did. That is the theory's own reason for M5 (ii)'s prediction that arm (c) loses what needs a target across steps, and it is quoted in phase 4's plan. Mark of M5 (ii) unchanged.
- **M7 and the receipts row of section 6.** Part IX: "A record reconstructed from the claim it is meant to support is not a receipt for that claim." Part IX also now says of an active route: "whether a route is active is read from the history, not from the result." The record-keeping row of section 6 ("no skill holds the derivation tree over leaves") gains this: whatever holds it must keep a receipt from being reconstructed from its claim. P1.1's "the judges report zero" is the same rule applied to a search report. Mark unchanged.
- **M10's obligations.** Part XI: "The obligations are declared inputs: \(O\) says what is to be repaired and \(P\) what is to be protected, each as a stated condition over stated occasions, and a protected condition is lost exactly when it fails on an occasion it covers." This is the sentence the HV word list's row for *fixed* would rest on; the ask in W3 section 12 is unchanged and now names it.
- **M1's declared organization.** Part X: "A dimension of variation mentioned in passing is not thereby a port of the account; it becomes one when the account admits changes to it, and adding it is construction." So the ports of the organization M1 declares are the passages the admitted edits reach, and a passage of the context no edit reaches is not a port. Mark unchanged (loose, asserted); the declaration is narrower and says so.
- **Section 6's Part VI gap.** Part VI now says of the finite monotone theorem: "The theorem applies only where its assumptions hold; an addition to \(\Gamma\) that destroys a support is the interference case below, and there upward closure fails." And of criticality: "the supports assessed are the ones actually written, not a support someone could write in their place." The gap named in W3 (the theorem's condition has no row in the skill) is unchanged, and the HV ask in section 12 now has the sentence to quote.
- **Section 9's first condition.** Part XIV: "The order is well founded: a representation justified only by its own construction, or an ownership and a capability justified only by each other, has not supplied its place in it, and a separate proof that would supply it counts only when the account uses it." - **The skill's "watch the part as well as the outcome".** Part II: "a part that reads or reports another part has a measurement's signature: change only the reading and the part it reports stays as it was; change the part and the reading follows."
