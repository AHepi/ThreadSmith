# Does the Time Traveller's account of time travel hold up?

## 1. The question, frozen

What is being explained: how movement through time — forward or backward, at a rate the traveller can vary — is possible, as the Time Traveller argues at dinner and then claims to have done himself. The range runs from his opening argument (four dimensions, a uniform default rate, the balloon comparison) through the vanishing of the toy machine to his own bedraggled return and first-person account of the sensations of travelling. This is a "what produces it" question — what makes the vanishing and the return possible — not a question of what the evidence merely lets us infer, and not a matter of taste.

## 2. The explanation in parts

1. Time is a fourth dimension, of the same kind as length, breadth and thickness.
2. Ordinary consciousness moves along that dimension in one direction only, at a fixed rate, "through a natural infirmity of the flesh."
3. Just as people gained partial freedom from gravity, after ages of being stuck to the ground, by building balloons, a machine could let a person alter their rate, or reverse their direction, along time.
4. The toy machine's vanishing (breath of wind, blown-out candle) is offered as evidence that it moved into time rather than simply disappearing.
5. If it had gone into the past rather than the future, we would still not see it sitting in the room, because of "presentation below the threshold" — moving too fast through the present to register.
6. The Time Traveller's own state on return — filth, a half-healed cut, hunger, a limp, hair that seems greyer — is evidence that something real happened to him.
7. His narrated sensations while travelling (day and night flickering, then blurring into grey; the sun becoming a streak; buildings rising and dissolving) are what the theory predicts a rider would experience moving fast along the time dimension.

## 3. Part by part

| Part | Mark | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Time as a fourth dimension | Loose | Swap it for any other mechanism ("folds space," "a rip in the world") and the toy still vanishes the same way, and the traveller still comes back in the same state. Nothing in the demonstration or the return depends on which theory is true. |
| The balloon comparison (partial freedom from a constraint, won by machinery) | Held if | It works only if "moving in time" is already the same kind of act as "moving up," which is the very point in dispute. So this part is held only by an assumption the Time Traveller asserts, not one the story tests. |
| The vanishing of the toy (wind, candle) | Loose | Swap in a hidden draught or a trapdoor and you get the identical show. The detail cannot tell "time travel" apart from "conjuring trick" — exactly the rival the Medical Man names ("like that ghost you showed us last Christmas"). |
| "Presentation below the threshold" | Asserted; catch-all with no gauge | Introduced only to answer one objection (why wasn't the machine seen sitting in the room over the past week, if it went backward). The text itself flags it as convenient — it "helps the paradox delightfully." It would answer the objection whichever way the machine actually went, so it cannot tell forward travel from backward travel either, and no reading is offered that could catch it swallowing the objection wrongly. |
| Physical state on return (dust, cut, hunger, limp) | Held if | Solid evidence that something arduous happened to him. Not by itself evidence that the arduous thing was time travel rather than, say, a rough few days spent hiding and starving on purpose — a rival the Editor and Journalist openly raise ("Has he been doing the Amateur Cadger?"). It is held only if his own narration of the cause is also accepted, which is the very claim under test. |

| The uniform, one-directional default rate of ordinary life | Loose, but structurally needed | It sets up the contrast "we already move in time, only without choosing to." No test is offered for why the everyday rate must be fixed rather than already variable. |

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

**Flip.** Had the toy simply sat still and reappeared unchanged, the same theory could have absorbed that outcome too, by saying it "didn't travel far." As staged — disappearance, a gust, a cold candle — the demonstration is compatible with several outcomes the theory could equally have claimed afterward.

**Add a job.** The wider story (why the far future looks decayed, why the Eloi are frail and incurious) is a second job this excerpt sets up but does not discharge with the fourth-dimension theory at all. That account, when it comes, will rest on a different kind of explanation — social and biological change — and lies outside this theory's reach.

**Pairs that pull.** The harder "presentation below the threshold" is stretched to cover both future and past travel alike, the less naturally it also explains why the Time Traveller himself is instantly visible and solid the moment he steps back into the present. Pushed hard, the patch and the return scene sit at odds.

**Where the parts came from.** The fourth-dimension claim and the fixed-rate claim are asserted. The balloon comparison and the sensory description of high-speed travel are built — worked out from the premise and exposed, in front of listeners who object, to what it commits the Time Traveller to. The threshold patch is asserted, on the spot, in reply to one specific objection.

## 5. What would tighten it most

One poke the story gestures toward but this excerpt does not yet supply: a physical object brought back from the future that a faked ordeal could not produce. That would give the threshold patch, and the larger claim, a real gauge — something a skeptic could examine that mere exhaustion and dirt cannot fake.

## 6. What this does not show

None of this shows time travel is impossible, or that the Time Traveller is lying. It shows which parts of his own argument carry weight — the internally worked-out description of what fast travel through time would look like — and which are placeholders that several rival stories would fit just as well: the fourth-dimension metaphysics itself, and the threshold patch used to rescue it.
